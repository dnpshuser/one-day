# GS-I: Indian Heritage, History, Society & Geography

### 1. Indian Culture, Art Forms, Architecture & Literature

* **Harappan Urbanism & Architecture:** Town planning, citadel vs lower town, drainage networks, dockyards, contemporary urban inputs.


* **Mauryan Art & Epigraphy:** Ashokan edicts and pillar inscriptions as historical sources; Mauryan polish, palace architecture, transition from wood to stone.


* **Sculptural Traditions (Gandhara, Mathura, Amaravati):** Greco-Bactrian and Roman stylistic influences in Gandhara; indigenous synthesis in Mathura and Amaravati.


* **Stupa Architecture & Iconography:** Structural evolution (Anda, Harmika, Chhatri); narrative relief panels, folk motifs, and propagation of early Buddhist ideals.


* **Temple Architecture – Nagara Style:** Panchayatana layout, Rekha-Prasad/Phamsana/Valabhi spires; Khajuraho/Chandella temple sculptures, vigorous iconography, and social motifs.


* **Temple Architecture – Dravida Style (Pallavas & Cholas):** Pallava rock-cut mandapas, rathas, and shore temples; Chola Brihadisvara vimana architecture, gopurams, and bronze casting (Nataraja).


* **Temple Architecture – Vesara & Regional Synthesis:** Hoysala star-shaped layouts; Vijayanagara architectural syncretism (amalgamation of Chalukyan, Hoysala, Chola, and Islamic arches/domes, Kalyana mandapas, Raya gopurams).


* **Rock-Cut Architecture & Cave Art:** Mesolithic cave paintings (Bhimbetka fauna, socio-aesthetic reflections); Ajanta and Ellora rock-cut monasteries, Chaityas, murals, and religious coexistence.


* **Numismatics as Historical Source:** Artistic and economic apex of Gupta coinage (metrology, royal portraiture) vs post-Gupta debasement.


* **Ancient & Early Medieval Literature:** Sangam literature as a socio-economic mirror of ancient South India; Vedic literature (Rig Vedic pastoralism to Later Vedic agrarian stratified society).


* **Medieval Literary & Epistolary Traditions:** Persian court histories, chronicles, and *tazkiras* reflecting medieval administrative ethos.


* **Bhakti & Sufi Movements:** Social radicalism vs religious synthesis; Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's devotional orientation; Sufi silsilas (Chishti, Suhrawardi) and limits of structural social modification.


* **Foreign Accounts in Historiography:** Accounts of Megasthenes, Fa-Hien, Hiuen Tsang, Al-Biruni, and Ibn Battuta in reconstructing socio-political history.


* **Medieval Syncretism & Ideology:** Sulh-i-Kul, Din-i-Ilahi, and Akbar's religious policies; Krishnadeva Raya's intellectual patronage (Amuktamalyada).

### 2. Modern Indian History & Freedom Struggle

* **Colonial Economic Policies & De-industrialization:** Drain of Wealth theory, one-way free trade, collapse of traditional handicrafts and cottage industries; commercialization of agriculture.


* **Colonial Famines & Agrarian Distress:** Spurt in famines from mid-18th century; export of foodgrains, lack of state relief, colonial administrative culpability.


* **Colonial Educational Policy:** Orientalist-Anglicist controversy; Macaulay’s Minute (1835) and its institutional impact on colonial administrative apparatus and elite English education; Wood’s Despatch (1854).


* **Early Subaltern, Civil & Tribal Resistance:** Pre-1857 uprisings; Santhal, Munda, and Kol rebellions against the Diku encroachment, money-lenders, and colonial forest laws.


* **The 1857 Revolt:** Structural culmination of century-long local resistances; administrative transformation under the Act for Good Government of India 1858 and the Queen's Proclamation.


* **19th Century Socio-Religious Reform Movements:** Brahmo Samaj, Young Bengal Movement, Prarthana Samaj, Arya Samaj; Jyotirao Phule and Satyashodhak Samaj subaltern reforms; 19th-century women's reforms (Sati abolition, widow remarriage, age of consent).


* **Viceroyalties & Administrative Watersheds:** Lord Dalhousie (Doctrine of Lapse, Railways, Telegraph, Postal system); Lord Curzon (Partition of Bengal, Universities Act, police reforms, and radicalization of nationalism).


* **Moderate vs Extremist Nationalism:** Ideological limits of constitutional agitation and prayer-petition politics; Swadeshi Movement (1905), passive resistance, and ideological divergence at Surat (1907).


* **Gandhian Strategy & Mass Mobilization:** Non-Cooperation Movement, Civil Disobedience Movement, and Quit India Movement; mass mobilization across classes, women, and peasants; Constructive Programme (Khadi, village industries, untouchability removal, basic education).


* **Ideological Divergences in Freedom Movement:** Gandhi vs Tagore (Nationalism, cosmopolitanism, machinery, and education); Gandhi vs Subhas Chandra Bose (Militarism vs Satyagraha, INA tactics); Gandhi vs B.R. Ambedkar (Poona Pact, caste abolition strategy, separate electorates).


* **Left-Wing, Revolutionary & Women's Mobilization:** Growth of the Congress Socialist Party, CPI, Kisan Sabhas; revolutionary societies (HRA, HSRA, Surya Sen's Chittagong Armoury Raid); women leaders across Gandhian mass movements.


* **Transfer of Power Impasse (1940s):** Cripps Mission, Wavell Plan, Cabinet Mission; communalism, partition dynamics, and imperial policy in complicating power transfer; Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny of 1946 as the final imperial breaking point.


* **Government of India Act, 1935:** Provincial autonomy, federal provisions, emergency powers, and structural blueprint for the 1950 Indian Constitution.

### 3. Post-Independence Consolidation & Reorganisation

* **Integration of Princely States:** Sardar Patel and V.P. Menon’s instrument of accession, administrative dilemmas in Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir.


* **Linguistic Reorganisation of States:** Dhar Commission, JVP Committee, State Reorganisation Commission (Fazal Ali Commission); popular pressure from below (Potti Sreeramulu, Samyukta Maharashtra, Maha Gujarat movements).


* **Planned Economic Model & Regional Parity:** Adoption of Soviet-inspired Five-Year Plans to eliminate colonial geographic/regional imbalances and industrialize backward pockets.


* **Agrarian Land Reforms:** Abolition of Zamindari intermediaries, tenancy reforms, land ceiling legislations, and Acharya Vinoba Bhave's Bhoodan and Gramdan movements.


* **Strategic Foreign Policy & Wars:** 1965 Indo-Pak War and the Tashkent Agreement; 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War and geopolitical compulsions; evolution of Non-Alignment (NAM) and the Gujral Doctrine.

### 4. World History

* **Industrial Revolution:** Pre-conditions in 18th-century Britain; domestic socio-economic changes vs colonial drain in India; Japanese late-comer industrialization model (Meiji Restoration).


* **American Revolution:** Commercial/economic rebellion against British mercantilism (Navigation Acts, Stamp Act); ideological contribution to global republicanism.


* **French Revolution:** Jacobinism, Reign of Terror, Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen; long-term global impacts on sovereignty, constitutionalism, and human rights.


* **Imperialism & Partition of Africa:** The 1884–85 Berlin Conference, "Scramble for Africa", artificial boundary delineation, and lingering ethnic post-colonial conflicts.


* **Decolonization Struggles:** Elite-led anti-colonialism in West Africa; Malay Peninsula decolonization and the Malayan Emergency.


* **World War I:** Collapse of the balance of power system, secret alliances, Treaty of Versailles, and breakdown of imperial empires.


* **Inter-War Democratic Crisis & Fascism:** Failure of the League of Nations; hyperinflation, Great Depression (1929) and New Deal economic policy responses; ideological collapse of parliamentary democracies and rise of Nazism/Fascism.


* **Cold War Geopolitics & Crisis Points:** Suez Canal Crisis (1956) and the collapse of Anglo-French imperial status; socio-economic impact of railways globally.

### 5. Indian Society

* **Diversity & Regional Disparity:** Cultural diversity vs socio-economic marginality; geographic and developmental roots of regional disparity; "pockets of small India" across urban centers; Unity in Diversity persisting despite communalism and regionalism.


* **Caste System – Dynamics & Urban Evolution:** Dual nature of caste (simultaneously fluid and static); emergence of new caste associations and political lobbying; whether caste is eroding or morphing into residential/marital endogamy in urban India.


* **Family, Marriage & Socialization:** Breakdown of the traditional joint family into nuclear/individualized households; changing perception of marriage (sacrament vs civil contract); Work From Home (WFH) and domestic boundary strains; digital socialization and mobile screen immersion replacing early child cuddling.


* **Tribal Dynamics & Knowledge Systems:** Tribal development along the dual axes of displacement and rehabilitation; uniqueness of tribal ethno-medicinal and ecological knowledge systems; demographic variations (favourable sex ratio among STs vs SCs).


* **Disability Jurisprudence & Policy:** Conceptualization of disability beyond the medical impairment model to the social barrier model; structural deficits in public infrastructure accessibility, inclusive education, and employment frameworks under the RPwD Act.


* **Women, Patriarchy & Workforce Dynamics:** Patriarchal barriers facing middle-class working women; feminization of agriculture; suicide trends among young women; gender dimensions of the gig economy; gender equality vs equity vs empowerment.


* **Population Dynamics & Demographic Transition:** Demographic Dividend versus "Demographic Winter" (declining total fertility rate below replacement level); aging demographics and healthcare challenges; internal labor migration patterns and urban vulnerability.


* **Urbanization & Socio-Spatial Segregation:** Ghettoization, informal settlements, and marginalization of the urban poor; Smart Cities mission performance on distributive justice and pro-poor infrastructure; Tier-2 cities as engines of consumptive middle-class growth.


* **Globalization & Cultural Shifts:** Cultural homogenization vs cultural heterogenization/strengthening of local identities; proliferation of fast-food chains amid rising lifestyle health disorders; impact of globalization on youth aspirations, gig work, political values, and consumerism.


* **Digital Technologies & Social Empowerment:** Role of EdTech, mobile banking (UPI), and Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) in bridging social inequalities vs digital divide exclusions.


* **Secularism, Regionalism & Communalism:** Indian composite secularism (principled distance, sarva dharma sambhava) vs Western strict separation; transformation of personal religiosity into aggressive communal mobilization; regional cultural assertiveness vs developmental imbalances generating statehood demands.

### 6. Physical, Regional & Human Geography

* **Geomorphology – Endogenic Processes:** Plate tectonics and crustal movements altering shapes and sizes of ocean basins and continents; Mantle plumes, hotspots, and intra-plate volcanism; Orogeny and global fold mountain alignment along plate boundaries; Primary (igneous) rock characteristics.


* **Geomorphology – Exogenic & Glacial Processes:** Formation and geomorphology of fjords; Aeolian processes, sand-dune movement, and wind erosion causing desertification and land degradation; Western Ghats drainage and reasons for absence of river deltas.


* **Climatology – Atmospheric Dynamics:** Thermal and chemical stratification of the troposphere; Air masses, frontogenesis, and macro-climatic changes; Temperature inversions (valley, frontal, radiation) and smog formation.


* **Climatology – Planetary & Local Winds:** Planetary desertification on western continental margins in the sub-tropical high-pressure belt ($20^\circ\text{–}30^\circ\text{ N/S}$); Comparative analysis of local warm winds: Loo (Indo-Gangetic plain), Chinook (Rockies snow-eater), and Foehn (Alps).


* **Climatology – Monsoons & Oscillations:** Dynamics of the Indian Monsoon (ITCZ shift, Somali jet, Tibetan heating, Tropical Easterly Jet); regional wind systems ("Purvaiya" easterlies in Bhojpur); El-Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO), Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), and human-induced land-use change impacts on rainfall.


* **Extreme Geophysical & Atmospheric Phenomena:** Tropical cyclones (SST threshold triggers, track prediction, IMD color-coded warnings); Fujiwhara Effect (interaction, binary orbit, and steering of two adjacent tropical cyclones); Twisters/tornadoes formation in the Gulf of Mexico/Tornado Alley; Cloudburst mechanisms in orographic mountainous terrains; Auroras (Australis and Borealis) and magnetospheric solar wind interactions.


* **Oceanography:** Factors driving thermohaline circulation and surface ocean currents; impacts on navigation, marine life, and desert coastlines; Spatial variations in oceanic salinity; Ocean dead zones and marine hypoxia.


* **Biogeography & Fragile Biomes:** Tundra regions (ecological fragility, permafrost thaw, economic resource exploitation); Mangrove ecological functions and depletion drivers; Coral bleaching and thermal ocean stress.


* **Natural Resources – Energy:** Global spatial distribution of offshore oil/gas reserves vs onshore basins; Arctic hydrocarbon exploration and geopolitical/environmental stakes; Shale gas reserves and exploitation constraints in India; Solar energy potential, spatial variation, and ecological costs; Wind energy spread limitations.


* **Natural Resources – Land & Water:** Ganga River Basin population carrying capacity vs land-water degradation; Deccan Trap basaltic weathering and black soil potential; Freshwater crisis and depleting Gangetic groundwater aquifers; Water resources as both an economic asset and a source of transboundary conflict in South Asia (Indus, Teesta, Ganges).


* **Industrial Location:** Factors governing global iron and steel industry migration away from raw material belts; Decentralization of the cotton textile industry; Spatial shifts in the sugar industry toward Southern India; Agro-based food processing clusters in North-West India; Petroleum refineries located near coastal demand centers rather than crude wells.


* **Human Geography & Geospatial Applications:** Drivers of human-induced land-use changes in India and ecological consequences; Satellite-based remote sensing and GIS applications in climate-smart agriculture and crop yield forecasting; Geopolitical transition of global shipping and trade routes from the Atlantic to the Indo-Pacific.

# GS-II: Polity, Governance, Social Justice & International Relations

### 1. Indian Constitution & Political Philosophy

* **Basic Structure Doctrine & Amending Powers:** Scope and substantive/procedural limits of Article 368; Parliament's inability to expand its amending power into absolute power (*Minerva Mills*, *I.R. Coelho*).


* **Constitutional Morality:** Evolution from Dr. Ambedkar’s vision to Supreme Court jurisprudence (*Navtej Johar*, *Sabarimala*, *Manoj Narula*); role as a check on executive arbitrariness and institutional accountability.


* **Article 21 – Right to Life & Bodily/Identity Autonomy:** Expanding frontiers of personal liberty; Passive Euthanasia and Living Wills/Advance Medical Directives (*Common Cause* jurisprudence); Prenatal DNA testing, bodily integrity, and child legitimacy disputes; Self-determination of gender identity and the 2026 amendments to the Transgender Persons Act, 2019.


* **Article 19 – Fundamental Freedoms & Digital Speech:** "Sole arbiter of truth" doctrine; Fact Check Units under IT Rules vs *Anuradha Bhasin* minimal impairment and *Shreya Singhal* overbreadth tests; Section 152 of Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (sovereignty and subversive speech) vs political dissent.
* **Electoral Franchise & Citizenship:** Legal status of the Right to Vote (Constitutional right under Art. 326 vs Statutory right vs Fundamental right); Powers and jurisdictional limits of the Election Commission of India in updating electoral rolls and investigating voter citizenship.


* **Gender Justice & Affirmative Action:** Historical evolution of women’s political reservation from Panchayati Raj (73rd/74th Amendments) to Parliament/Assemblies (106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023); non-minority/non-backward status of women as an affirmative action class; Uniform Civil Code (Article 44) vs religious personal laws.



---

### 2. Federal Architecture & Centre-State Dynamics

* **Asymmetrical Federalism:** Constitutional mechanisms accommodating regional/cultural diversities (Articles 370/35A abrogation and its aftermath; Article 371 special provisions for North-East; 5th and 6th Schedules); demands for Sixth Schedule status in Ladakh.


* **Gubernatorial Discretion & Article 200:** Constitutional position of the Governor under the cabinet system; scope of assent to State Bills (withholding assent, returning bills, reserving for President); binding nature of the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 163); re-promulgation of ordinances (*D.C. Wadhwa*, *Krishna Kumar Singh*).


* **Centralizing Trends & State Encroachment:** Union statutory dominance during national crises (Disaster Management Act 2005, Epidemic Diseases Act 1897, Farm Laws 2020 via Concurrent List Entry 33); reduced invocation of Article 356 post-*S.R. Bommai* (1994); constitutional thresholds and consequences of Financial Emergency under Article 360.


* **Centre-State Financial Relations & Fiscal Federalism:** Impact of the 101st Constitutional Amendment (GST) on state tax sovereignty and compensation cess disputes; horizontal and vertical tax devolution debates under the 15th/16th Finance Commissions; Fiscal Health Index (FHI) and conditional borrowing limits on states.


* **Inter-State Dispute Resolution:** Article 131 original jurisdiction limitations; functioning, design flaws, and delays in Inter-State River Water Dispute Tribunals under Article 262; role of the Inter-State Council (Article 263).


* **Union Territories Governance:** Dual control dynamics in Delhi (Article 239AA, 69th Amendment, GNCTD Amendment Acts, and LG vs elected government tussles); Legislative Assembly powers and administrative status of UT of Jammu & Kashmir under the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019.



---

### 3. Separation of Powers, Parliament & Comparative Models

* **Separation of Powers in Practice:** Checks and balances in the Indian hybrid parliamentary model vs rigid functional separation in the US Presidential model; actual authority and executive powers of the Indian Prime Minister vs the US President.


* **Presiding Officers & House Decorum:** Office of the Lok Sabha Speaker under the Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule); structural vulnerability to partisan interests and required institutional safeguards (Kihoto Hollohan, UK "once a speaker, always a speaker" model); Vice-President’s role as ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.


* **Legislative Scrutiny & Executive Dominance:** Growth of the Cabinet system leading to marginalization of parliamentary supremacy; decline in individual MP autonomy and private member bills; role and declining referrals to Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees (DRSCs); role of Financial Committees (PAC, Estimates Committee).


* **Constitutional Comparisons:**
* *Executive Clemeny:* Presidential pardoning power in India (Article 72) vs USA (Article II), judicial review thresholds, and doctrine of "preemptive pardons".


* *Judicial Appointments:* Indian Collegium system vs US Senate confirmation hearings; Indian judicial convergence/divergence with the UK Supreme Court.


* *Secularism:* Indian model (principled distance/equal respect) vs French strict laïcité vs US non-establishment wall of separation.


* *Legislative Sovereignty:* Parliamentary sovereignty in the UK vs constitutional supremacy and judicial review in India.

* **Corrupt Practices & Electoral Integrity:** Grounds under Section 123 of RPA 1951; disproportionate increase in assets of legislators/associates as "undue influence" and a corrupt practice; simplification of disqualification procedures.

* **Malnutrition & Food Sovereignty:** Child and maternal undernutrition as an institutional failure of social equity and governance (NFHS findings, Poshan 2.0); divergence between poverty reduction figures and persistent hunger metrics.

* **India’s Neighbourhood Policy:** India-Sri Lanka economic bailout diplomacy and Tamil issue sensitivities; India-Maldives strategic security, Muizzu administration reset, and global maritime energy shipping lanes; political turbulence in Bangladesh and cross-border security ramifications; transboundary water governance (Indus Water Treaty renegotiation demands).

# GS-III: Economy, Agriculture, S&T, Environment, DM & Internal Security

* **Growth, Employment & Labor Formats:** "Jobless growth" syndrome; structural vs cyclical unemployment; PLFS computing methodology critique; gig and platform work formalization.


* **Financial Inclusion & Banking:** Inclusive growth under market economies; PMJDY and JAM Trinity; RBI’s Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index) composition and utility.


* **Digital Monetary Architecture:** Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC / Digital Rupee)—working architecture, wholesale (e₹-W) vs retail (e₹-R), and macroeconomic impacts on commercial bank deposits.


* **Monetary Policy Dynamics:** Inflation targeting framework; persistent food and fuel inflation limiting the effectiveness of RBI's repo rate transmissions.


* **Fiscal Policy & Budgeting:** Components of Capital Budget vs Revenue Budget; Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) targets vs fiscal consolidation post-pandemic; state government fiscal sustainability and Fiscal Health Index (FHI).


* **Industrial Policy & Manufacturing Missions:** Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes—outcomes, value-addition deficits, and assembly vs component manufacturing; India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)—challenges in fab fabrication, clean water/power supply, and global supply chain integration; Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) credit access and delayed payments.


* **Startup Ecosystem:** Startups as drivers of employment, patent creation, and innovation; challenges of capital winter, angel taxes, corporate governance lapses, and global market slowdowns.


* **Trade Policy & Global Deglobalization:** Rise of modern industrial protectionism, non-tariff barriers, carbon border taxes (CBAM), and currency manipulations affecting Indian exports.


* **Labour Market Reforms:** Rationalization of 29 central laws into the Four Labour Codes (Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, Occupational Safety); states' implementation delays.

### 2. Agriculture, Food Management & Supply Chains

* **Farm Subsidies & WTO Compliance:** Direct farm supports (PM-KISAN) vs indirect input subsidies (fertilizer, power, canal water); WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)—Amber Box caps, Peace Clause, and permanent solution for public stockholding.


* **PDS & Buffer Stock Architecture:** Public Distribution System leakages, One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC); Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS) and carrying-cost inefficiencies of Food Corporation of India (FCI) buffer stocks.


* **Agro-Biotechnology & Perishable Post-Harvest Management:** Application of biotechnology in delaying senescence, genetic modification for rot resistance, and enzyme applications in processing perishable fruits and vegetables.


* **Micro-Irrigation:** Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY); drip and sprinkler irrigation improving physical and economic water-use efficiency.

* **Infrastructure vs Ecological Fragility:** Managing the structural contradiction between accelerated border/mountain infrastructure development (Char Dham, rail tunnels) and disaster risk reduction in sensitive zones.


* **Community-Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR):** Local communities as first responders; training models (Aapda Mitra), indigenous disaster resilience, and community early-warning dissemination.


* **Hydro-Meteorological Disasters:** Urban flooding (drainage encroachment, concretization, sponge-city concepts); Cloudburst dynamics and predictive localized forecasting in the Himalayas; Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs) mitigation.


* **Geophysical Disasters:** Seismic vulnerability micro-zonation and building code compliance; National Landslide Risk Management Strategy; Dam safety, aging reservoir management, and Dam Safety Act 2021.


* **International Frameworks:** Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015–2030) targets, four priority areas, and its operational shift from Hyogo Framework.

### 7. Internal Security, Border Management & Defense

* **Left-Wing Extremism (LWE):** The 2026 eradication operational deadline; security forces operations (Operation Prahar) coupled with SAMADHAN doctrine and civic action programs; lingering developmental roots of Naxalism.


* **North-East Security Architecture:** Naga peace talks deadlock; tripartite peace accords (Bodo, Karbi, Bru-Reang resettlement); scrapping of the Free Movement Regime (FMR) and fencing the 1,643 km Indo-Myanmar border.


* **Jammu & Kashmir Internal Security:** Post-Article 370 and 35A abrogation security scenario; counter-infiltration dynamics on LoC vs rise in targeted terror in Jammu region; "Winning Hearts and Minds" (Operation Sadbhavana).


* **Border Area Infrastructure & Demographics:** Border Area Development Programme (BADP), Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP), and Border Infrastructure and Management (BIM) schemes; strategic vulnerabilities in Eastern Ladakh and Arunachal Pradesh along the LAC.


* **Terror Financing & Transnational Organized Crime:** Terror financing conduits (Counterfeit currency, Hawala routes, Darknet and crypto assets); Financial Action Task Force (FATF) 40+9 recommendations, mutual evaluations, and compliance obligations; Narco-terrorism linkages with the Golden Crescent and Golden Triangle.


* **Cyber Security & Cognitive Warfare:** Disinformation campaigns, fake news, and deepfakes threatening social stability; Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021 amendments; Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; cross-border state-sponsored cyber warfare targeting critical infrastructure.


* **Security Agencies & Anti-Terror Laws:** Unlawful Activities Prevention Act (UAPA) individual designation provisions vs civil liberties; National Investigation Agency (NIA) extraterritorial jurisdiction; Armed Forces Special Powers Act (AFSPA) phased withdrawal debates.

# GS-IV: Ethics, Integrity & Aptitude

### 1. Ethics, Human Values & Philosophical Foundations

* **Normative Ethical Theories:** Deontology (Kant's categorical imperative, duty for duty’s sake, treating humans as ends never means) vs Teleology/Consequentialism (Utilitarianism, greatest good for the greatest number); Virtue Ethics (Aristotelian golden mean).


* **Applied Bioethics & Medical Ethics:** Ethical tensions between paternalism (doctor deciding in perceived best interest) and beneficence vs patient autonomy and informed consent; human clinical trial ethics on vulnerable or isolated indigenous communities.


* **Applied Technology & AI Ethics:** Ethical dilemmas in outsourcing academic evaluation and intellectual appraisal to AI tools (academic integrity, accountability, intellectual honesty); biases, automated profiling, and predictive policing using facial recognition in underprivileged areas; ethics of using digital biometrics as gatekeepers for constitutional welfare delivery (Article 21 vs exclusion errors).


* **Applied Environmental Ethics:** Anthropocentrism vs Ecocentrism; ethical dilemmas in clearing biodiversity-rich forest tracts for homeless housing projects; developmental infrastructure in border areas vs ecological conservation.


* **Duty vs Charity:** Ethical distinction between supererogatory acts (charity/philanthropy) and moral duties/obligations towards severely disadvantaged populations (Peter Singer's rescue principle).


* **Just War & State Coercion:** "The arms are fair when the intent of bearing them is just" (Henry IV)—ethical deployment of state police power and coercive force in administration.


* **Constitutional Dissent vs Order:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's warning against civil disobedience and the "grammar of anarchy" in a constitutional democracy vs ethical resistance against unjust state policies.


* **Philosophical Teachings Applied to Public Life:**
* *Mahatma Gandhi:* Trusteeship doctrine for public administrators; Seven Social Sins; Talisman for decision-making.


* *Swami Vivekananda:* Strength, character, selfless service, and universal acceptance.


* *Thiruvalluvar (Tirukkural):* Fortitude in crisis, truthfulness yielding common unblemished good.


* *Sardar Patel:* Faith and administrative strength in governance.


* *Kautilya:* Systemic roots of corruption and internal state surveillance.





---

### 2. Civil Service Values, Attitude & Administrative Ethics

* **Foundational Values of Public Service:** Integrity, Impartiality, Non-Partisanship, Objectivity, Dedication to Public Service, Empathy, and Compassion towards weaker sections (Nolan Committee principles).


* **Empathy vs Rule Compliance:** Striking a balance between the letter of the law and the spirit of justice when strict compliance harms vulnerable citizens; resolving discretionary administrative conflicts (compassionate transfers vs service rules).


* **Efficiency vs Effectiveness:** Doing things right (process compliance) versus doing the right thing (outcomes of public welfare).


* **Emotional Intelligence in Administration:** Self-awareness, empathy, and emotional regulation in defusing mob protests, managing stress, handling toxic office environments, and resolving a crisis of conscience.


* **Attitude & Bureaucratic Mindset:** Overcoming rigid "bureaucratic attitude" (inflexibility, procedural obsession) to build a "democratic/facilitator attitude" focused on citizen-centric problem-solving.



---

### 3. Probity in Governance, Dilemmas & Anti-Corruption

* **Conflict of Interest:** Actual vs potential conflicts of interest; familial/commercial nexus in public procurement, awarding infrastructure projects, and land-use change approvals.


* **Dismantling Administrative Corruption Networks:** Ethical leadership in dismantling tout-official syndicates without paralyzing legitimate service delivery; protecting whistleblowers against organizational backlash.


* **Politicization of Bureaucracy:** Preserving constitutional non-partisanship in the face of political executive pressure, unlawful oral directives, and retaliatory transfer threats.


* **Code of Conduct vs Code of Ethics:** Enforceable negative restrictions (Conduct Rules 1964) vs aspirational, value-driven standards (2nd ARC recommendations).


* **Ethics in International Affairs & Arms Trade:** Ethical considerations when marketing domestic lethal weapons to foreign powers (balancing economic/strategic ties against proliferation, regional stability, and human rights violations).


* **Public Order & Communal Neutrality:** Ethical obligations of district magistrates and police chiefs during inter-community ethnic riots; preserving non-partisan trust, protecting minorities, and resisting local political pressures.
