# Tribes and Related Issues

## 1. Intro

* Scheduled Tribes (STs) are the indigenous populations of India, constituting approximately 8.6% of the national population (over 10.4 crore per Census 2011). Under Article 342, the President notifies specific communities as STs based on primitive traits, geographical isolation, distinct culture, and extreme socio-economic backwardness.
* Historically marginalized and pushed into forested/hilly terrains, tribal communities today sit at the epicenter of India’s development paradox: they reside on lands rich in minerals and biodiversity, making them the primary victims of resource extraction, displacement, and rapid state-led industrialization.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs):** A sub-category of 75 extremely vulnerable tribes (e.g., Shompen, Sentinelese, Sahariya) characterized by declining populations, pre-agricultural technology, and zero literacy.
* **De-notified, Nomadic, and Semi-Nomadic Tribes (DNTs):** Communities previously branded as "born criminals" under the colonial Criminal Tribes Act (1871). Though denotified, they still face extreme police harassment and social stigma.
* **Constitutional Asymmetry (5th vs. 6th Schedule):** The 5th Schedule provides administrative safeguards for STs in 10 mainland states via Tribal Advisory Councils (TACs). The 6th Schedule grants high political autonomy via Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) specifically in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram (AMTM).
* **Land Alienation:** The illegal or coerced transfer of tribal land to non-tribals, corporations, or the state, despite strict constitutional prohibitions (like the Samatha Judgment).
* **PESA (1996) and FRA (2006):** The Panchayat (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act empowers Gram Sabhas to govern their resources, while the Forest Rights Act recognizes Individual and Community Forest Rights (CFR), attempting to correct "historic injustices."
* **The Development-Displacement Nexus:** Tribals constitute 8.6% of the population but make up over 40% of all people displaced by dams, mines, and sanctuaries in post-independence India.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* **Pre-1947 (Colonial Exploitation):** The British enacted the Indian Forest Act (1865/1927), which criminalized traditional tribal access to forests, categorizing their homelands as "Excluded Areas" for unhindered timber extraction.
* **1950 (Constitutional Recognition):** Adoption of the 5th and 6th Schedules to protect tribal autonomy, alongside reservation in legislatures and public employment (Articles 330, 332, 16(4)).
* **1996 (PESA Act Enacted):** Following the Bhuria Committee, PESA recognized the absolute sovereignty of the tribal *Gram Sabha* over local resources and minor forest produce in 5th Schedule areas.
* **2006 (Forest Rights Act):** A watershed law explicitly acknowledging the historical injustice done to forest dwellers, granting them legal titles to the land they had cultivated for generations.
* **2023 (PM-JANMAN Launch):** The ₹24,104 crore Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan was launched exclusively to saturate the 75 PVTGs with housing, health, and road infrastructure.
* **August 2024 (SC Verdict on Sub-categorization):** A 7-judge Constitution Bench ruled that states can sub-classify SCs and STs to ensure reservation benefits reach the most marginalized micro-tribes rather than being cornered by dominant ST groups.
* **October 2024 (DAJGUA Launch):** The Prime Minister launched the massive ₹79,156 crore *Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan*, targeting saturation of basic services in over 63,000 tribal-majority villages nationwide.

## 5. Committee or reports [latest is best or some report in the past with very large impact]

* **Xaxa Committee Report (2014):** The most impactful modern report on tribal status. It concluded that tribal integration in India has largely been "integration by subjugation." It exposed the massive failure of the state in preventing land alienation and urged strict enforcement of PESA and FRA.
* **Bhuria Committee (1995):** The foundational committee whose recommendations led directly to the enactment of the PESA Act, 1996, bridging the gap between the 73rd Amendment and 5th Schedule areas.
* **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) Annual Reports:** The NCST has continually red-flagged the routine bypassing of the Forest Rights Act during land acquisition for mining and infrastructure projects, asserting that Gram Sabha consent is often forged or coerced.

## 6. Indices [better which shows India's ranking]

* **National Multidimensional Poverty Index (NITI Aayog):** Disaggregated data reveals a stark reality: Scheduled Tribes remain the poorest demographic in India, with multidimensional poverty among STs being drastically higher than the national average, primarily due to lack of maternal health and nutrition.
* **NCRB (Crime in India Report 2023/24):** Continues to highlight the rising trend of atrocities against STs (under the SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act), particularly linked to land disputes and sexual violence against tribal women in central Indian states (like Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan).
* **All India Survey on Higher Education (AISHE):** While overall tribal enrollment in higher education has improved marginally, the Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) for STs lags significantly behind the national average, with massive dropout rates post-secondary education due to language barriers.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United States (Native American Reservations):** The US operates on a "Reservation" system where Native American tribes are recognized as domestic dependent nations with their own tribal courts and police. India’s 6th Schedule offers similar autonomy, but the 5th Schedule is highly integrated into state administration.
* **Australia (The "Apology" & Integration):** Australia officially apologized for the "Stolen Generations" (forced assimilation of Indigenous children). Today, they focus on closing the socio-economic gap, though indigenous Australians still suffer from massive health and incarceration disparities.
* **Brazil (Amazon Indigenous Reserves):** Similar to India's PVTGs, uncontacted tribes in the Amazon face extreme existential threats from illegal logging and corporate mining. Brazil's constitution protects their land, but executive enforcement fluctuates wildly (e.g., under the Bolsonaro vs. Lula regimes).
* **India (The Middle Path - Integration without Assimilation):** India explicitly rejects the total isolation (National Park approach) and forced assimilation models, striving instead for "Integration"—bringing development to tribes while legally protecting their distinct cultural and land rights.

## 9. Flow chart

**The Forest Rights Act (FRA 2006) Title Claim Process**

1. **Initiation:** Tribal individual/community files a claim with evidence (pre-2005 occupancy).
2. **Gram Sabha Level:** The Forest Rights Committee (FRC) verifies the claim on the ground; Gram Sabha passes a resolution approving it.
3. **Sub-Divisional Level Committee (SDLC):** Examines the Gram Sabha resolution and forwards it to the district.
4. **District Level Committee (DLC):** The final legal authority (headed by District Collector).
5. **Outcome:**
* *Approved:* Title deed (Patta) is officially granted to the tribal family or community.
* *Rejected/Pending:* A major implementation gap, where bureaucratic apathy stalls millions of legitimate claims.



## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan (DAJGUA - Oct 2024):** The launch of this ₹79,000+ crore mega-scheme sparked debate on execution. While praised for its saturation approach (covering 63,000 villages), critics argue that infusing funds without first strictly enforcing the FRA and PESA will lead to infrastructure being built over stolen tribal lands.
* **The Great Nicobar Project & Shompen PVTG (2024-2026):** A fierce ongoing battle where an ₹81,000 crore mega-transshipment port is being built in the Andaman & Nicobar islands. Environmentalists and anthropologists are debating the catastrophic impact on the uncontacted Shompen PVTG, alleging severe violations of their habitat rights.
* **Ladakh’s Demand for 6th Schedule (2024-2026):** Climate activist Sonam Wangchuk led massive indefinite fasts demanding that Ladakh (which has a 97% tribal population) be included in the 6th Schedule to protect its fragile ecology and tribal land from external corporate exploitation post-Article 370 abrogation.
* **Manipur Ethnic Conflict (2023-2026):** The devastating Meitei vs. Kuki-Zo conflict was fundamentally triggered by a High Court order (later stayed) directing the state to consider granting ST status to the dominant Meitei community, which the Kuki-Zo tribes vehemently opposed fearing loss of hill-land protections.

## 11. Advantage/Merits/Role

* **Ecological Guardianship:** Tribal communities are the original conservationists. Their sacred groves, sustainable gathering practices, and deep reverence for nature are exactly what modern society needs to combat climate change.
* **Rich Traditional Knowledge:** They possess vast, uncodified knowledge regarding ethnobotany, medicinal plants, and drought-resistant seed varieties, which hold immense potential for modern medicine and sustainable agriculture.
* **Cultural and Linguistic Pluralism:** India's true diversity is anchored in its tribal populations, who preserve hundreds of distinct languages, art forms (like Gond and Warli art), and egalitarian social structures (where women often have higher status than in mainstream caste society).
* **Strategic Border Demography:** Many tribal communities live in sensitive border regions (North-East, Himalayas). Their integration and socio-economic satisfaction are critical for national security and intelligence-gathering against insurgents.

## 12. Disadvantage/De-Merits

*(Vulnerabilities and Issues faced)*

* **Massive Land Alienation:** The continuous circumvention of the 5th Schedule by state governments allowing private mining corporations to dispossess tribals of their ancestral lands without their Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC).
* **Health and Nutritional Crisis:** Extreme prevalence of Sickle Cell Anemia, severe acute malnutrition among children, and high maternal mortality rates due to the absence of last-mile healthcare infrastructure in dense forests.
* **Educational Alienation:** The imposition of mainstream state languages in primary schools alienates tribal children, leading to massive dropout rates. The curriculum rarely reflects their history or values.
* **Extremism and Exploitation:** The vacuum of governance and development in tribal belts (the Red Corridor) made them vulnerable to Left Wing Extremism (Naxalism), catching innocent tribals in the crossfire between Maoists and state security forces.

## 13. Challenges

* **Poor Implementation of PESA and FRA:** Even decades after enactment, many states have either not framed PESA rules or actively diluted Gram Sabha powers. Millions of FRA claims remain pending or arbitrarily rejected by forest departments.
* **Bureaucratic Apathy:** The administrative machinery in remote tribal areas is often understaffed, corrupt, or culturally insensitive. Welfare funds intended for Tribal Sub-Plans (TSP) are frequently diverted to general infrastructure projects.
* **Identity and ST Status Friction:** The increasing demand by dominant, politically powerful communities (like Gujjars, Meiteis, or Marathas) to be included in the ST list threatens to crowd out genuinely marginalized tribes from reservation benefits.
* **Impact of Climate Change:** Forest fires, changing rainfall patterns, and the depletion of Non-Timber Forest Produce (NTFP) directly destroy the subsistence economy of forest-dwelling tribes, turning them into climate refugees.

## 14. Way forward / solution / positive outlook

* **Saturation through Convergence (DAJGUA Model):** The government must strictly monitor the newly launched *Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan* to ensure that 17 different ministries genuinely converge to provide housing, roads, and optical fiber to 63,000 tribal villages within the 5-year deadline.
* **Strict Enforcement of FPIC:** Following the Xaxa Committee, the Free, Prior, and Informed Consent of the Gram Sabha must be made absolutely legally binding before any land acquisition for mining or dams, treating tribal land rights as inviolable.
* **Mother-Tongue Education (NEP 2020 Integration):** States must aggressively recruit local tribal teachers and develop primary school textbooks in tribal dialects (Ol Chiki, Gondi) to ensure foundational literacy without cultural alienation.
* **Empowering the NCST:** The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes must be granted the teeth of a civil court in practice, not just in theory, allowing it to prosecute officials who illegally reject FRA claims or facilitate land grabbing.
* **Targeted Health Interventions:** Scale up the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (launched in 2023) using mobile medical units and localized genetic counseling tailored specifically to tribal genetic vulnerabilities.

## 15. Best practices

* **India (The Niyamgiri Victory):** A global best practice in tribal rights. The Dongria Kondh PVTG in Odisha successfully used the Gram Sabha provisions of the FRA to universally vote against and block a massive bauxite mining project by Vedanta, asserting their absolute right over their sacred mountain.
* **India (Odisha's PVTG Empowerment Model):** Odisha has established dedicated Micro Project Agencies (MPAs) and utilizes translated tribal primers in schools, successfully stabilizing the populations of highly vulnerable tribes like the Bonda and Juang.
* **New Zealand (Maori Representation):** The Treaty of Waitangi framework provides the indigenous Maori people with guaranteed political representation and a formal truth-and-reconciliation process for stolen lands, a legal template for indigenous dignity.
* **Canada (First Nations Autonomy):** Canada has shifted towards granting its Indigenous 'First Nations' massive self-governing powers, allowing them to independently manage their healthcare, education, and taxation on their reserves.

## 16. Conclusion

* Tribal communities are not relics of the past to be "civilized," but equal citizens whose traditional knowledge and ecological stewardship are critical to India's sustainable future.
* True tribal empowerment requires moving beyond mere welfare handouts. By implementing massive convergence schemes like DAJGUA in tandem with the strict constitutional protection of their land and autonomy under PESA and FRA, India can finally bridge the trust deficit and ensure that the *Adivasi* shares equitably in the fruits of the *Amrit Kaal*.
