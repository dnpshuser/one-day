1. Intro
	- Cellulose is the world's most abundant natural polymer, converting atmospheric carbon into the structural framework of plants and a major potential source of renewable materials.
2. What are the natural processes this cellulose undergoes before yielding carbon dioxide, water and other end products ?
	1. **Formation:** Plants use photosynthesis to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose; glucose units join to form cellulose, which strengthens cell walls.
	2. **Ingestion:** Humans cannot digest cellulose because they lack the enzyme cellulase; ruminants and termites depend on symbiotic microbes to break it down.
	3. **Hydrolysis:** Fungal and bacterial cellulases break cellulose into cellobiose and glucose; in industrial systems, acids or enzymes may assist this step.
	4. **Aerobic decomposition:** In the presence of oxygen, microbes metabolise glucose through glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and the electron-transport chain, producing carbon dioxide, water and energy.
	5. **Anaerobic decomposition:** In oxygen-poor soil, wetlands, landfills or the rumen, microbes produce organic acids, hydrogen and eventually methane and carbon dioxide; methanogens complete the process.
	6. **Soil pathway:** Partly decomposed cellulose becomes humus and microbial biomass, while mineralisation returns carbon to the atmosphere and nutrients to soil.
	7. **Human-use pathway:** Burning releases mainly carbon dioxide and water; fermentation of hydrolysed cellulose can produce bioethanol, while pyrolysis can yield biochar, bio-oil and gases.
	8. The rate depends on temperature, moisture, oxygen, lignin content, microbial activity and the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio.
3. Conclusion
	- Understanding cellulose's carbon cycle can support composting, biofuels and biorefineries while keeping carbon locked in soils and reducing open burning and methane leakage.
