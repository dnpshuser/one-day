1. Intro: A vaccine is a biological preparation that stimulates the body's immune system to provide active acquired immunity against a specific infectious disease.

2. process of development
   1. Pre-clinical: Lab research and animal testing for safety and immunogenicity.
   2. Clinical Trials: Phase I (safety), Phase II (efficacy), Phase III (large-scale confirmation).
   3. Regulatory Approval: Review of trial data by authorities like CDSCO.
   4. Post-Market Surveillance (Phase IV): Ongoing monitoring after public rollout.
3. process of working
   1. Introduces a harmless antigen (e.g., viral protein) into the body.
   2. The immune system produces antibodies and memory cells (B and T cells) against this antigen.
   3. Upon future infection, memory cells trigger a rapid, strong response, preventing or mitigating the disease.
   4.  How Covid-19 vaccine produced
   5.  mRNA (Pfizer, Moderna): mRNA instructs cells to make the viral spike protein, triggering an immune response.
   6.  Viral Vector (Covishield): A harmless virus delivers the genetic code for the spike protein.
   7.  Inactivated Virus (Covaxin): Uses a killed version of the SARS-CoV-2 virus.
5. Advantages
   1. High Efficacy: Highly effective in preventing severe disease and death.
   2. Cost-Effective: A cornerstone of public health with high returns on investment.
   3. Rapid Platforms: New technologies like mRNA enable faster development during pandemics.
6. Disadvantages
   1. Side Effects: Can cause mild, temporary reactions; rare severe reactions are possible.
   2. Logistical Hurdles: Some vaccines require a stringent cold chain (e.g., mRNA vaccines). 
7. Benefits
   1. Individual Protection: Shields individuals from disease.
   2. Herd Immunity: Protects vulnerable, unvaccinated populations by reducing transmission.
   3. Disease Eradication: Has successfully eradicated Smallpox and nearly eradicated Polio.
    
8. Challenges
   1. Vaccine Hesitancy: Fueled by misinformation, it undermines vaccination efforts.
   2. Equitable Access: "Vaccine nationalism" and supply chain issues create global disparities.
   3. Viral Evolution: Pathogen mutations can reduce vaccine effectiveness, requiring updates.
9. Examples of some recent developed vaccines having global impact and process involved.
   1.  Malaria (Mosquirix, R21): Recombinant protein vaccines that target the malaria parasite, a major breakthrough for child health.
   2.  HPV (Gardasil 9): A Virus-Like Particle (VLP) vaccine that prevents HPV infection, the primary cause of cervical cancer.
     
10. Conclusion
    1.  Vaccines are a triumph of medicine, crucial for controlling infectious diseases and global health security. Addressing challenges like hesitancy and inequity, while fostering innovation in vaccine platforms, is essential for preparing for future health crises.
