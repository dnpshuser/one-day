1. Intro: 3D printing, scientifically known as Additive Manufacturing (AM), is a revolutionary process of creating three-dimensional solid objects from a digital file. It builds an object layer by layer, depositing material only where needed unlike traditional subtractive manufacturing.

2. working: 
   1. Design(CAD file: Computer Aided Design): 
      - 3D scan representing exact geometry.
   2. Slicing: 
      - Digital model -> printer understandable instructions: using slicing software
      - slicer: 3D model -> 100 or 1000 2D layer(slices)
      - toolpath generation: precise movmt printer needs to make
      - Calculates amt of material req
      - outputs: G-code file
   3. Printing(Layer Deposits)
      - Loads G-code file to 3D Printer
      - additive processing begins
      - layer by layer material is deposited
      - gradually building obj from ground to up
   4. Post-processing
      - curing, painting or machining is done to achieve desired results

3. Advantages
   1. Design complexity: creation of intricate geometries.
   2. Customization and Personalization
   3. Reduced material waste
   4. Speed (Rapid Prototyping): Design -> print -> test -> iterate
   5. Supply chain simplification: on demand printing reducing need for large inventories.

4. Disadvantages
   1. Limited production: slow in mass production
   2. High initial cost for industrial equipment: 3D printers for metals or high performance polymers are extremely expensive ($100000+)
   3. Material limitation
   4. Post processing support required

5. Role in Industry(current status)
   1. Aerospace and Defense: 
      - critical engine components (like fuel nozzles with complex internal channels)
   2. Healthcare & Medical: 
      - custom prosthetics and orthotics, detailed anatomical models for surgical planning, and biocompatible implants (like titanium jaw) tailored to the patient’s geometry.
   3. Automotive: 
      - rapid prototyping, specialized manufacturing tools and jigs, and producing high-performance or obsolete spare parts for classic vehicles.
   4. Consumer goods:
      - Customised eyewhere, personalised footwear, jewelery and artistic items
   5. Tooling and manufacturing: 
      - customs jigs, fixtures and rapid tooling 

6. Future outlook
   1. Bioprinting
      - living cell as ink -> complex tissues and functional organs for transplant
   2. Multi-material
      - metals, polymers and conductive inks
   3. Hybrid printing
      - Additive + subtractive capabilities
   4. advanced and sustainable materials: 
      - material palette is rapidly expanding to include stronger, heat resistant and high performance engineering plastics.
   5. Increased speed and industrial scale
   6. On demand manufacturing
   7. Automation and integration with robotics and AI for processing optimization

7. Conclusion
   - 3D printing represents a fundamental shift in how we conceive, design, and manufacture objects. Its design freedom, material efficiency, and extreme customization—enable innovations that are impossible with traditional methods.
