### Cricket: Decision Review System

3. components

1. Intro
	- Cricket's Decision Review System applies calibrated technology to reduce avoidable umpiring errors while retaining human judgement at the centre of the game.
2. What is this system
	1. DRS allows a team or the on-field umpire to refer a decision for review under the playing conditions.
	2. The third umpire examines available evidence and communicates the final decision; the on-field decision stands when technology cannot conclusively overturn it.
	3. Reviews are limited and time-bound to prevent strategic delay.
3. components
	1. **Hawk-Eye:** Multiple high-speed cameras track the ball and model its trajectory, especially for lbw decisions.
	2. **UltraEdge/Snickometer:** Synchronized audio and video identify a spike when the ball or bat passes the edge.
	3. **Hot Spot:** Infrared imaging can show heat marks caused by contact, subject to equipment and interpretation limits.
	4. **Broadcast replays and stump cameras:** Provide frame-by-frame evidence of catches, no-balls, run-outs and boundary decisions.
	5. **Third umpire and protocols:** Combine calibrated feeds, communication systems and the ICC playing conditions.
4. how silicon tape on the edge of the bat may fool the system ?
	1. Silicon tape or protective material can absorb or alter the impact vibration and reduce the audible signal detected by UltraEdge.
	2. It may also change the infrared signature or create ambiguity, but it cannot guarantee that contact will be hidden because video, ball deviation, Hot Spot and other evidence may still reveal an edge.
	3. Therefore, DRS is evidence-based rather than infallible; calibration, equipment checks, transparent protocols and the “benefit of doubt” principle remain essential.
5. Conclusion
	- Future cricket technology should pursue greater sensor accuracy and transparency while preserving the umpire's authority, sporting fairness and the game's human character.
