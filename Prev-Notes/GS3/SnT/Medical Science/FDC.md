### Fixed Dose Combinations
1. Intro
2. What is it ?
3. Merits
4. Demerits
5. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
   - A Fixed-Dose Combination (FDC) is a formulation containing two or more active pharmaceutical ingredients combined in a single dosage form, such as a single tablet or capsule.

2. What is it ?
   - It is a "2-in-1" or "3-in-1" medicine designed to treat conditions that require multiple drugs. For an FDC to be rational, the drugs must have a sound therapeutic justification for being combined, and the dosage of each component must be appropriate for a significant portion of the population.

3. Merits
   - **Improved Patient Compliance:** Reduces the number of pills a patient has to take ("pill burden"), making it easier to stick to the treatment regimen. This is crucial for chronic diseases like HIV, tuberculosis, and hypertension.
   - **Synergistic Effect:** The combined drugs may work better together than they would individually.
   - **Reduced Cost:** Often cheaper than buying the individual medicines separately.
   - **Prevents Monotherapy Resistance:** In diseases like TB and HIV, combining drugs helps prevent the development of drug resistance.

4. Demerits
   - **Irrational Combinations:** Many FDCs on the market are "irrational," combining drugs without scientific evidence of benefit, often leading to increased risk of adverse effects and contributing to antibiotic resistance (e.g., combining an antibiotic with a painkiller).
   - **Inflexible Dosing:** The fixed ratio of drugs makes it impossible to adjust the dose of one ingredient without altering the dose of the others.
   - **Increased Risk of Adverse Drug Reactions:** Combining multiple drugs increases the potential for side effects and drug interactions.
   - **Masking of Diagnosis:** An FDC might mask the symptoms of an underlying disease, delaying a correct diagnosis.

5. Conclusion
   - While rational FDCs are invaluable tools in managing complex diseases like TB and HIV by improving patient compliance, 
   - the proliferation of irrational combinations poses a significant public health threat in India. Stricter regulatory oversight, as recommended by expert committees, is essential to weed out unsafe FDCs and ensure that only those with proven therapeutic value are available to patients.
