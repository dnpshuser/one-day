1. Intro
   - Antibiotics are powerful medicines that fight bacterial infections by either killing the bacteria (bactericidal) or inhibiting their growth and reproduction (bacteriostatic). They are ineffective against viral infections like the common cold or flu.

2. How prepared ?
   - **Natural Fermentation:** Many antibiotics are naturally produced by microorganisms like fungi (e.g., Penicillin from *Penicillium* fungus) and bacteria (e.g., Streptomycin from *Streptomyces* bacteria). They are grown in large fermenters, and the antibiotic compound is then extracted and purified.
   - **Semi-synthetic:** Natural antibiotics are chemically modified in a lab to improve their effectiveness, broaden their spectrum, or reduce side effects.
   - **Fully Synthetic:** Some antibiotics are created entirely through chemical synthesis in a laboratory.

3. working
   - Antibiotics target structures or processes unique to bacteria, which is why they don't harm human cells. Common mechanisms include:
     - **Inhibiting Cell Wall Synthesis:** (e.g., Penicillin) Weakens the bacterial cell wall, causing it to burst.
     - **Inhibiting Protein Synthesis:** (e.g., Tetracycline) Prevents bacteria from producing essential proteins.
     - **Inhibiting DNA/RNA Synthesis:** (e.g., Ciprofloxacin) Interferes with the bacteria's genetic machinery.

4. Benefits
   - **Life-Saving:** Revolutionized medicine by making once-lethal bacterial infections (like pneumonia, tuberculosis) treatable.
   - **Enables Modern Medicine:** Crucial for preventing infections in surgery, chemotherapy, and organ transplants.

5. Demerits
   - **Side Effects:** Can cause digestive issues (by killing good gut bacteria), allergic reactions, and other adverse effects.
   - **Antibiotic Resistance:** Overuse and misuse lead to the emergence of bacteria that are no longer affected by the antibiotic.

6. Emergence of drug-resistant disease
   1. **Cause**
      1. **Overuse without Doctor's prescription:** Taking antibiotics for viral infections or not completing the prescribed course allows resistant bacteria to survive and multiply.
      2. **Other causes:** Widespread use of antibiotics in livestock and agriculture; poor infection control in hospitals; insufficient development of new antibiotics.
   2. **How to monitor ?**
      - **Surveillance Programs:** National networks like the Indian Council of Medical Research's (ICMR) AMR Surveillance Network track resistance patterns in hospitals and communities.
      - **Genomic Sequencing:** Analyzing the DNA of bacteria to identify resistance genes and track their spread.
   3. **How to Control ?**
      - **Antibiotic Stewardship:** Promoting the responsible use of antibiotics by doctors and patients.
      - **Infection Prevention:** Improving hygiene, sanitation, and vaccination to reduce the incidence of infections.
      - **Research & Development:** Incentivizing the development of new antibiotics and alternative therapies (e.g., phage therapy).
      - **One Health Approach:** Recognizing the link between human, animal, and environmental health in tackling resistance.

7. Conclusion
   - While antibiotics are a cornerstone of modern healthcare, their effectiveness is under threat from the growing crisis of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). A concerted "One Health" global effort focusing on stewardship, surveillance, and innovation is urgently needed to preserve these life-saving drugs for future generations.
