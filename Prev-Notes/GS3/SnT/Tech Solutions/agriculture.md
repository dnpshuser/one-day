### Tech soln: agricultural
1. Intro
2. How tech reshaped agriculture
3. Merits
4. Demerits
5. Conclusion

1. Intro
	- Technology is converting Indian agriculture from a weather-dependent activity into a data-enabled, resource-efficient and market-linked enterprise.
2. How tech reshaped agriculture
	1. **Precision farming:** Soil-health cards, GIS, drones, sensors and AI guide seed, fertiliser, pesticide and irrigation use.
	2. **Water management:** Drip, sprinkler, automated irrigation, solar pumps and remote sensing improve water productivity.
	3. **Climate resilience:** Weather advisories, stress-tolerant seeds, protected cultivation and crop-insurance platforms reduce climate risk.
	4. **Mechanisation:** Tractors, harvesters, custom-hiring centres and robotics reduce labour drudgery and post-harvest losses.
	5. **Markets and finance:** e-NAM, digital payments, warehouse receipts, Kisan Credit Cards and online farm advisory improve market access.
	6. **Post-harvest value:** Cold chains, pack houses, grading, blockchain traceability and food-processing technology raise farmer realisation.
3. Merits
	1. Higher productivity, input efficiency and farm incomes.
	2. Better forecasting, early pest detection and reduced crop losses.
	3. Inclusion of small farmers through shared machinery, FPOs and digital platforms.
	4. Lower water, chemical and energy use, supporting sustainable agriculture.
4. Demerits
	1. Digital divide, poor connectivity and low digital literacy exclude small and marginal farmers.
	2. High capital cost, fragmented holdings and dependence on proprietary platforms.
	3. Biased or poor-quality data can produce unsuitable advisories; privacy and cyber risks also exist.
	4. Mechanisation may displace labour without alternative rural employment, while e-waste and battery waste create ecological concerns.
5. Conclusion
	- A farmer-centric blend of affordable devices, public digital infrastructure, local extension and responsible AI can make Indian agriculture productive, climate-resilient and inclusive.
