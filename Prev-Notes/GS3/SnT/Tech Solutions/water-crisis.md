1. Intro
   - The water crisis demands a shift from endlessly finding new sources to producing, using, reusing and governing every drop more intelligently.
2. Various tech solution
   1. **Desalination:** Reverse osmosis removes salts from seawater; useful for coastal cities but energy-intensive and produces brine.
   2. **Wastewater treatment and reuse:** Tertiary treatment, membrane bioreactors and ultraviolet disinfection make water fit for industry, gardening or indirect potable use.
   3. **Water harvesting:** Rooftop harvesting, recharge wells, check dams and managed aquifer recharge store seasonal rain.
   4. **Smart distribution:** IoT meters, pressure sensors, GIS mapping and leak-detection systems reduce non-revenue water.
   5. **Agricultural efficiency:** Drip, sprinkler, soil-moisture sensors, precision irrigation and treated wastewater reduce demand.
   6. **Nature-based solutions:** Wetland restoration, permeable surfaces, watershed treatment and constructed wetlands improve recharge and quality.
      1. merits
         1. Increases supply reliability, reduces extraction and supports drought resilience.
         2. Saves energy and money when demand management and reuse replace distant water transfers.
         3. Creates decentralised solutions for villages, farms and industries.
      2. demerits
         1. High capital, energy and maintenance costs can exclude poorer regions.
         2. Poorly treated water, brine disposal and recharge can create health or ecological risks.
         3. Technology cannot replace conservation, regulation and equitable allocation.
3. What solutions adopted till date
   1. Jal Jeevan Mission expands rural household tap connections and source sustainability. **Source: Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation.**
   2. Atal Bhujal Yojana promotes community-led groundwater management. **Source: Ministry of Jal Shakti.**
   3. AMRUT 2.0 supports urban water supply, sewerage, reuse and water-body rejuvenation.
   4. National Mission for Clean Ganga uses sewage-treatment and river-rejuvenation measures.
   5. PMKSY promotes irrigation efficiency through “Per Drop More Crop”.
4. Conclusion
   - Combining digital monitoring, decentralised harvesting, wastewater reuse and community water budgeting can make water security affordable, circular and climate-resilient.
