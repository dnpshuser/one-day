### Plate Tectonic

1. Introduction
   - Plate Tectonic theory proposes that the lithosphere is broken into several major and minor rigid plates that move relative to each other over the semi-molten asthenosphere, causing major geological phenomena like fold mountain creation.

2. concepts
   - **Lithospheric Plates:** The Earth's rigid outer layer (crust + upper mantle) is divided into about 7 major plates (e.g., Pacific, Eurasian) and numerous minor plates (e.g., Nazca, Indian).
   - **Driving Mechanism:** The primary driver is **mantle convection**, where heat from the Earth's core creates convection cells in the mantle that drag the plates. **Slab pull** (gravity pulling a subducting plate down) and **ridge push** (gravity causing plates to slide away from elevated mid-ocean ridges) are also significant forces.
   - **Plate Boundaries:** The interactions at plate margins are responsible for most tectonic activity.
     1. **Convergent (Destructive):** Plates collide.
        - **Ocean-Continent:** Denser oceanic plate subducts, forming ocean trenches, continental volcanic arcs, and earthquakes (e.g., Andes Mountains).
        - **Ocean-Ocean:** One oceanic plate subducts under another, forming volcanic island arcs and deep trenches (e.g., Mariana Islands).
        - **Continent-Continent:** Plates buckle and fold, creating massive mountain ranges and intense earthquakes (e.g., Himalayas).
     2. **Divergent (Constructive):** Plates move apart. Magma rises to form new crust at mid-oceanic ridges (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge) or continental rift valleys (e.g., East African Rift).
     3. **Transform (Conservative):** Plates slide horizontally past each other, causing powerful earthquakes but no volcanic activity (e.g., San Andreas Fault).

3. impact
   - **Distribution of Landforms:** Explains the location of major landforms like fold mountains, rift valleys, ocean trenches, and volcanic island arcs.
   - **Geophysical Hazards:** The theory is fundamental to understanding the global distribution of:
     - **Earthquakes:** Concentrated along all three types of plate boundaries.
     - **Volcanoes:** Primarily found at convergent (subduction) and divergent boundaries (The "Ring of Fire" is a prime example).
     - **Tsunamis:** Largely caused by undersea earthquakes at convergent boundaries.
   - **Resource Formation:** Plate tectonic processes are responsible for the concentration of valuable mineral deposits (e.g., copper, gold) and the formation of sedimentary basins that host hydrocarbon reserves.

4. Conclusion
   - Plate Tectonics is the cornerstone of modern geology, providing a comprehensive framework that connects various geological processes, from the formation of mountains to the occurrence of earthquakes.
