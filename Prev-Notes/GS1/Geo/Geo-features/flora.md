1. Intro
   1. India is one of the world’s richest biodiversity hotspots with 3 out of 8 hottest of hotspot found in Indian soil.
2. Causes of diversity in India
   - The country spans tropical, subtropical, temperate, and alpine zones due to latitude, altitude, and monsoon patterns.
   - Wide variations in rainfall, temperature, and soil types produce different vegetation belts.
   - Ancient geological history, plateau systems, and the presence of the Himalaya and coastal belts support species diversity.

3. Forest Resource
  1. Current status
     - India has substantial forest and tree cover, though the quality and distribution remain uneven.
     - Dense forests are concentrated in the Himalaya, northeast, Western Ghats, and central India.
     - Forest loss, fragmentation, and degradation continue due to urbanisation, mining, fire, and land conversion.
  2. impact on climate change
     - Forests absorb carbon dioxide, regulate local climate, and reduce soil erosion.
     - Deforestation and degradation release carbon and worsen warming, drought, and biodiversity loss.
     - Restoration and community forest management are critical for achieving climate and ecological goals.

4. Conclusion: India’s flora is a living ecological resource and a national asset; its conservation is essential for climate resilience, biodiversity, and sustainable development.
