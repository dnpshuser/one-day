1. Intro 
Covering under one percent of ocean floors, coral reefs sustain a quarter of all marine life, acting as critical marine biodiversity hotspots.

2. Factors of formation
   1. shallow water of 45-55 m
   2. abundent sunlight
   3. clean water
   4. saline water: not total fresh, nor highly saline
   5. no pollution: highly sensitive towards pollution
   6. Abundant phytoplankton
- Coral polyps depend on symbiotic algae called zooxanthellae for energy through photosynthesis.

3. impact of global warming
   - Rising SST cause coral bleaching: where corals expel their symbiotic algae and lose their colour and primary food source.
   - Ocean acidification reduces carbonate availability, slowing coral calcification.
   - Increased storm intensity and pollution further weaken reef resilience.

4. Current status
   - CR declining globally due to climate change, overfishing, runoff, and destructive fishing practices.
   - The Great Barrier Reef and many tropical reefs have experienced repeated bleaching events in recent years.
   - Reefs are critical for coastal resilience but are increasingly vulnerable.
  

5. Causes of coral bleaching
   1. Global watming: 
   2. sea water pollution
   3. ocean acidification
   4. cyclones, tsunamis
   5. rising sea levels
   6. overfishing and destructive fishing
6. Conservation measures
   - Establish marine protected areas and no-take zones.
   - Reduce land-based pollution and improve coastal management.
   - Promote reef restoration, coral nurseries, and climate-resilient reef management.
   - Source: UNEP, IUCN, national marine agencies.
7. conclusion
Coral reefs are ecological treasure houses that require urgent global action as their loss threatens biodiversity, fisheries, and coastal protection.
