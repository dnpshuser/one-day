1. Intro
   1. The cryosphere (Greek - kryos: cold or frost) is the portion of the Earth’s climate system where water is in solid form, including snow, ice, and frozen ground. It exists across polar zones, high-altitude mountain ranges, and cold ocean waters, acting as a sub-component of the hydrosphere.
   2. The cryosphere acts as Earth's critical thermodynamic buffer, but accelerating polar and Third-Pole melting threatens global sea levels and freshwater security.

2. Current status
   - Arctic sea ice extent has decreased significantly in recent decades 
   - many glaciers are losing mass.
   - Glacier melting caused Nepal floods 2026
   - Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets are contributing more to sea-level rise than before.

3. Arctic/Antarctic ice melting
   1. Causes
      - Rising greenhouse gas concentrations warm the atmosphere and oceans.
      - Positive feedbacks such as reduced albedo accelerate melt.
      - Changes in ocean circulation and atmospheric patterns intensify regional warming.
      - tourism
      - excessive pollution
      - loss of regional flora and fauna that balances ecosystem.
   2. Effects and impacts
      1. Weather pattern
         - Altered jet streams and 
         - brown clouds disperse insolation disrupting amt circulation
         - Increased melting can disrupt the monsoon and regional climate patterns.
      2. Human activities
         - Coastal communities face flood and erosion risk.
         - Fisheries, 
         - shipping routes, and 
         - indigenous livelihoods are affected by changing ice conditions.
   3. Mitigation measures
         - Reduce GHG emissions 
         - transition to clean energy.
         - Strengthen climate adaptation, 
         - early warning,  
         - ecosystem protection.
         - control tourism
         - Monitor cryosphere

4. Conclusion: Sustaining cryospheric stability demands transnational scientific monitoring, robust GLOF early-warning mechanisms, and aggressive global emissions mitigation under the Paris Agreement.
