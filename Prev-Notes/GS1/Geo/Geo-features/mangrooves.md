### Mangrooves
1. Intro and concept
2. Significance
    1. significance in maintaining coastal ecology
    2. other significance
3. Threat or Causes of depletion
4. Current status
5. Protection measures

---

1. Intro: Mangrove ecosystems are coastal forests found in intertidal zones of tropical and subtropical regions. They are highly productive and act as a bridge between marine and terrestrial environments.

2. Significance
    1. significance in maintaining coastal ecology
       - protect shorelines erosion
       - nursery grounds for fish and crustaceans and sustain biodiversity.
       - Their root systems filter pollutants and trap sediments, improving coastal water quality.
    2. other significance
       - support local livelihoods: fishing, honey collection, and eco-tourism.
       - important carbon sinks: help mitigate climate change through blue carbon storage.

3. Threat or Causes of depletion
   - Coastal reclamation, 
   - urban expansion, 
   - aquaculture,  
   - port development
   - Pollution, 
   - sedimentation,  
   - upstream changes in freshwater flow weaken mangrove ecosystems.
   - Climate change, 
   - sea-level rise,  
   - extreme weather events intensify stress.

4. Current status
   - India has significant mangrove cover along the east coast, Sundarbans, Gujarat, and Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
   - areas continue to experience degradation and fragmentation.
     - Ex: The Great Nicobar Project 2026 - felling 700K-1 million pristine rainforest trees
5. Protection measures
   - Restore degraded mangrove forests through community participation and coastal zone management.
   - Restrict destructive coastal development and promote sustainable aquaculture practices.
   - Integrate mangrove conservation into climate adaptation and disaster resilience planning.

6. Conclusion: Mangroves are nature’s coastal shield and climate buffer; their conservation is essential for resilient coasts, fisheries, and biodiversity.
