1. Intro: Tornadoes are violently rotating columns of air descending from supercell thunderstorms, generating extreme localized pressure drops and catastrophic kinetic wind destruction.
2. Types
   1. Supercell Tornadoes: Most intense; develop from rotating thunderstorms.
   2. Landspouts: Form from surface-level convergence; weaker.
   3. Waterspouts: Tornadoes over water bodies; common in tropical regions.
   4. Non-supercell Tornadoes: Associated with squall lines or sea breezes.

3. process of formation
   - A thunderstorm develops strong wind shear and rotation.
   - Updrafts tilt the rotating air vertically.
   - Under favourable conditions, a narrow funnel cloud forms and extends downward.
   - When it reaches the ground, it becomes a tornado.

4. Causes
   - Strong atmospheric instability, wind shear, and moisture supply are primary triggers.
   - Severe convective systems in regions with contrasting air masses can produce tornadoes.

5. Distribution
   - US/UK

6. Recent occurrance
   - US/UK

7. Impact
   - Extremely high wind speeds can destroy buildings, uproot trees, and damage infrastructure.
   - Tornadoes also cause injuries and deaths, often with little lead time.

8. Mitigation Measures
   - Early warning systems, 
   - safe shelters, and 
   - public preparedness are crucial.
   - Community awareness, 
   - home strengthening, and 
   - storm-proofing reduce vulnerability.

9.  Conclusion: Mitigating tornado impacts requires high-resolution dual-polarization Doppler radar networks, reinforced safe-room building standards, and rapid community-level warning dissemination systems.
