1. Intro
   - Driven by sudden tectonic slip along fault lines, earthquakes trigger violent ground motion, posing severe structural threats to over 58% of India's landmass.
   - no warning

2. process of occurrence
   - Stress builds up in rocks due to tectonic movements.
   - When the stress exceeds rock strength, the crust fractures and slips along a fault.
   - This release of energy propagates as seismic waves, causing shaking.

3. Causes
   - Plate tectonic movements, 
   - faulting, 
   - volcanic activity, 
   - crustal deformation are major causes.
   - Human activities such as 
     - reservoir-induced seismicity and 
     - mining can sometimes trigger earthquakes.

4. Distribution
   1. India
      - Himalaya, Northeast, and Gujarat are seismically active.
   2. Global
      - Earthquakes are concentrated along plate boundaries such as the Pacific Ring of Fire and the Alpine-Himalayan belt.
   3. Recent occurrance
      - Himalaya, Japan, Turkey, and other tectonically active zones.

5. Impact
   - Ground shaking damages buildings, roads, bridges, and dams.
   - Secondary hazards - landslides, liquefaction, fires, and tsunamis.
   - Loss of life and economic disruption can be severe, especially in dense urban areas.

6. Mitigation Steps
   - Enforce earthquake-resistant building codes and retrofitting of vulnerable structures.
   - Improve seismic zoning, preparedness, and public awareness.
   - Use early-warning systems and emergency response planning in high-risk regions.

7. Conclusion
Earthquake risk cannot be eliminated, but it can be reduced through engineering, planning, and preparedness.
