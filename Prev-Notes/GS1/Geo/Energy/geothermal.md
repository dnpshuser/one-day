1. Intro
	- Geothermal energy is the heat stored within the Earth, a reliable renewable resource that can provide electricity and direct heating with a small land footprint.
2. How geothermal energy converted into electricity ?
	1. **Exploration:** Geological, geochemical and seismic surveys identify hot rocks, reservoirs and fractures.
	2. **Drilling:** 
   	1. Production wells bring hot water or steam to the surface, 
   	2. while injection wells return cooled water underground.
	3. **Power generation:** 
   	1. Steam directly drives a turbine in a dry-steam plant; 
   	2. hot water is flashed into steam in a flash plant; 
   	3. moderate-temperature water heats a secondary fluid in a binary-cycle plant.
	4. **Reinjection and monitoring:** Water is reinjected to maintain reservoir pressure and reduce surface pollution and subsidence.

5. Ongoing plans-targets
	- Priorities are systematic mapping, 
	- pilot binary-cycle plants, 
	- district heating, 
	- geothermal heat pumps, 
	- hybridisation with solar power in remote areas.
	- Public-private exploration, 
	- risk-sharing for unsuccessful drilling and integration with the National Geothermal Energy Policy framework can improve commercial viability.
6. Benefits
	1. Provides firm, round-the-clock renewable power unlike intermittent solar and wind.
	2. Has low operational emissions and a small land footprint.
	3. Supports local heating, greenhouse farming, tourism and livelihood diversification.
	4. Reduces diesel dependence in remote and cold regions such as Ladakh.
7. Challenges
	1. High upfront cost 
	2. site-specific resource.
	3. Difficult terrain
	4. weak grid connectivity at promising locations.
	5. emissions of dissolved gases can affect plant life.
	6. Reinjection must be carefully managed to prevent induced seismicity and reservoir depletion.
8. Key govt initiatives
	- Geological Survey of India resource assessment; 
	- Ministry of New and Renewable Energy support for renewable-energy research and pilots; 
	- and state-level exploration by agencies such as ONGC and state power corporations.
9.  Conclusion
	- With scientific exploration, risk-sharing finance and direct-use applications, geothermal energy can become a cornerstone of India's 500 GW Non fossil capacity and 50% Renewable Energy Panchamrit Targets by 2030.
