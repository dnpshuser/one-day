1. Intro
  - Global oil reserves serves as the lifeblood of modern geopolitics and industrial value chains remaining the ultimate strategic asset—governing national energy sovereignty and macroeconomic stability.
2. On-shore
  1. Geographical distribution - India
    1. Assam-Arakan Basin: Digboi, Naharkatiya and Moran.
    2. Gujarat-Cambay Basin: Ankleshwar, Kalol and Mehsana.
    3. Rajasthan's Barmer basin is an important onshore addition.
    4. Implication: 
       1. Accessible
       2. support regional industry, 
       3. limitation: many are small or geographically concentrated.
  2. Geographical distribution - World
    1. Middle East, 
    2. West Siberia, 
    3. Volga-Urals, 
    4. Caspian region, 
    5. North America and 
    6. Venezuela.
    7. Implication: 
       1. Concentration in politically sensitive regions 
       2. makes oil prices and supply vulnerable to conflict, 
       3. sanctions and transport chokepoints.
3. Off-shore
  1. Geographical distribution - India
    1. Western offshore: 
       1. Mumbai High and 
       2. neighbouring fields in the Arabian Sea remain India's leading offshore oil province.
    2. Eastern offshore: Krishna-Godavari and Cauvery basins have oil and gas prospects in the Bay of Bengal.
    3. Implication: 
       1. can reduce import dependence 
       2. limitation: require expensive technology, deep-water capability and strong environmental safeguards.
  2. Geographical distribution - World
    1. Persian Gulf, 
    2. Gulf of Mexico, 
    3. North Sea, 
    4. offshore Brazil, 
    5. West Africa 
    6. South China Sea.
    7. Implication: 
       1. diversify supply 
       2. concerns: 
          1. expose countries to hurricanes, 
          2. spills, 
          3. maritime disputes and 
          4. costly infrastructure.
4. Arctic Sea oil discovery
  1. Significance
    1. The Arctic is estimated to contain a substantial share of undiscovered global oil and gas resources, though estimates vary.
    2. Melting sea ice may improve seasonal access and intensify interest in the Northern Sea Route.
    3. Production could diversify supplies and generate strategic influence for Arctic states.
  2. Impact on environment
    1. Exploration disturbs marine ecosystem.
    2. Fragile ecosystems recover slowly from oil spills because of 
       1. cold, 
       2. darkness and 
       3. limited response infrastructure.
    3. New fossil-fuel extraction can lock in emissions, while declining ice cover creates a feedback loop that accelerates warming.
    4. Therefore, resource potential must be weighed against climate commitments, ecological risk and indigenous rights.
5. Conclusion
  - Global oil reserves must serve as a financial bridge, but not a permanent anchor to transition to green hydrogen, biofuels with the global decarbonization imperative of SDG 13 (Climate Action)."
