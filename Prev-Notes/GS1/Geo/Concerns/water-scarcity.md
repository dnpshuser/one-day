## Global fresh water scarcity / Water Stress
1. Intro
2. Causes of crisis
3. Effects and impact
4. Data/Facts
5. Mitigation measures
6. Conclusion

---
1. Intro
Home to 18% of the global population but only 4% of freshwater resources, India faces severe structural water stress.

2. Causes of crisis
- Uneven rainfall distribution 
- climate-induced droughts reduce usable freshwater availability.
- Over-extraction of groundwater faster than recharge
- Industrial pollution and untreated sewage reduce water quality and usable supply.
- Water-intensive crops, inefficient irrigation, and high conveyance losses worsen stress.

1. Effects and impact
- Crop failure, lower farm incomes, and food insecurity in water-stressed regions.
- Drinking water shortages, especially in peri-urban and rural communities.
- Ecological damage to rivers, lakes, wetlands, and biodiversity.
- Social stress, migration, and conflicts over water access.

1. Data/Facts
- About 2 billion people worldwide live in water-stressed areas, and nearly half of the world’s population may face severe water scarcity by 2030.
- India is highly groundwater-dependent, with large parts of the country facing over-exploited or critically stressed aquifers.

1. Mitigation measures
   1. Need for effective management
   - Water budgeting, watershed planning, and demand management are vital for reducing stress.
   - Recycling wastewater, desalination in coastal regions, and rainwater harvesting can augment supply.
   - Micro-irrigation and crop diversification reduce agricultural demand.
   1. Govt/Global initiatives
   - Jal Shakti Abhiyan, Atal Bhujal Yojana, and river rejuvenation efforts aim to improve water security.
   - Global frameworks such as SDG 6 emphasise universal access to clean water and sustainable water management.

2. Conclusion
Achieving long-term water security requires micro-irrigation, circular wastewater recycling, and watershed management aligned with the Jal Jeevan Mission framework.
