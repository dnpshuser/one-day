### Groundwater depletion
1. Intro
2. Factors/Causes
3. Effects/Impacts
4. Mitigation measures
5. Government initiatives
6. Conclusion


---
1. Intro
India extracts over a quarter of the world's groundwater, turning an invisible resource into an imminent ecological and food-security crisis.

2. Factors/Causes
- Excessive pumping for irrigation, especially in states like Punjab, Haryana, and western Uttar Pradesh.
- Subsidised power and free electricity for agriculture encourage over-extraction.
- Urban expansion, industrial demand, and poor recharge due to concretisation reduce natural replenishment.
- Pollution from nitrates, fluoride, and industrial effluents lowers usable groundwater quality.

3. Effects/Impacts
  1. Food security issues
  - Declining groundwater affects irrigation reliability, reducing crop productivity and threatening farm incomes.
  - Water stress in semi-arid regions can intensify vulnerability to drought and crop failure.
  2. other impacts
  - Falling water tables increase pumping costs and energy consumption.
  - Aquifer depletion can lead to land subsidence, saltwater intrusion in coastal areas, and social conflicts over water access.
  - Source: CGWB, NITI Aayog, and groundwater assessments.

5. Mitigation measures
  1. Water harvesting
      - Rainwater harvesting, recharge pits, and percolation tanks can improve aquifer recharge if designed at watershed scale.
      - Community participation and maintenance are essential for long-term effectiveness.
  2. Other measures
      - Promote micro-irrigation such as drip and sprinkler systems to reduce water demand.
      - Shift crop patterns toward less water-intensive crops in overexploited areas.
      - Regulate abstraction, fix extraction limits, and improve groundwater governance.

6. Government initiatives
- Atal Bhujal Yojana
- Jal Shakti Abhiyan and PMKSY 
  - promote recharge, 
  - rainwater harvesting 
  - water conservation.
- Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) monitors aquifer status and provides groundwater data.

1. Conclusion
Shifting to demand-side water management and localized aquifer recharge is critical to secure India's water future and meet SDG 6(Clean Water and Sanitation).
