### Coal mining
1. Intro
2. Challenges
3. Environmental concerns
5. Importance in development
6. Way forward
7. Conclusion
 
---

1. Intro
	- Powering heavy industry and baseload electricity, coal remains the historic bedrock of India's energy security and industrial modernization.
2. Challenges
	1. Land acquisition, 
	2. displacement, 
	3. illegal mining, 
	4. safety risks and difficult last-mile logistics.
	5. Declining productivity in mature mines, 
	6. grade mismatch, 
	7. transport bottlenecks and 
	8. imported coking-coal dependence.
3. Environmental concerns
	1. Open-cast mining causes deforestation, 
	2. habitat fragmentation, 
	3. soil erosion and landscape change.
	4. Mine drainage, 
	5. dust, 
	6. methane, 
	7. air-water contamination: ash and thermal pollution
	8. Coal combustion is a major source of carbon dioxide and particulate pollution.
4. Importance in development
	1. Coal supports baseload electricity, 
	2. steel, 
	3. cement, 
	4. rail freight, 
	5. jobs and 
	6. industrialisation.
	7. It strengthens energy access and reduces vulnerability to imported energy prices during India's transition.
	8. India is among the world's largest coal producers and consumers. **Source: Energy Institute, Statistical Review of World Energy 2024.**
5. Way forward
	1. Improve mine safety, 
	2. scientific reclamation, 
	3. mine-water reuse and 
	4. concurrent afforestation.
	5. Expand renewables, 
	6. storage, 
	7. energy efficiency and 
	8. clean technologies 
	9. protect coal-dependent workers through reskilling and regional diversification.
	10. Use coal gasification and emission controls selectively, with transparent environmental monitoring.
6. Conclusion
	- A just, technology-enabled coal transition can secure near-term energy access while advancing Article 21, SDG 7 and India's net-zero 2070 vision.
