### Inland water Transport
1. Intro 
2. Evolution
3. Current status
4. Challenges
5. Benefits
6. Major govt programs
7. Conclusion
 
---

1. Intro
	- Inland water transport offers India a low-cost, low-emission logistics alternative capable of connecting hinterlands with markets while easing road and rail pressure.
	- As the most fuel-efficient and low-carbon logistics mode, water transport underpins cost-effective freight mobility and balanced multimodal connectivity.
2. Evolution
	1. Rivers, canals and backwaters historically supported trade before railways and highways became dominant.
	2. Modern policy revived waterways through dredging, terminals, navigation aids, digital river information and multimodal logistics.
3. Current status
	1. India has 111 declared National Waterways; the operational network is expanding along the Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hooghly, Brahmaputra and coastal routes.
	2. **Source:** Inland Waterways Authority of India, National Waterways overview and annual reports.
	3. Jal Marg Vikas Project develops the NW-1 corridor between Varanasi and Haldia with multimodal terminals.
4. Challenges
	1. Seasonal depth variation, siltation, changing river courses, floods and inadequate last-mile connectivity.
	2. Terminal, vessel, repair, storage and navigation infrastructure remains limited.
	3. Ecological flow, dredging impacts, inter-state coordination and low cargo awareness require attention.
5. Benefits
	1. Lower fuel use, emissions and freight cost for bulk and containerised cargo.
	2. Decongests roads and railways, supports port-led development and creates regional employment.
	3. Connects landlocked production zones and strengthens multimodal logistics.
6. Major govt programs
	1. National Waterways Act, 2016; 
	2. Sagarmala; 
	3. Jal Marg Vikas Project;
	4. Arth Ganga;  
	5. subsidies for cargo and vessel development.
	6. Regional cooperation supports Indo-Bangladesh Protocol routes and North-Eastern connectivity.
7. Conclusion
	- Operationalizing National Waterways and modernizing multi-modal river terminals under Sagarmala remain pivotal to anchoring sustainable, low-carbon freight corridors
