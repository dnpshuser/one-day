### Petrolium Refinaries - not located near crude oil production
1. Intro
2. How 
3. global distribution
5. Factors
6. Implications
7. Conclusion
 
---

1. Intro
	- Refineries follow a complex geography where crude supply, markets, ports, pipelines and product logistics often outweigh proximity to oilfields.
2. How
	1. Crude is transported by pipelines, tankers, rail or road to refineries.
	2. Distillation separates crude into fractions; cracking, reforming, treating and blending convert them into fuels and petrochemical feedstocks.
	3. Products move through pipelines, terminals, rail and road networks to consumers.
3. global distribution
	1. Major clusters occur around the US Gulf Coast, Rotterdam-Antwerp, the Persian Gulf, Singapore, China, India and South Korea.
	2. Coastal mega-refineries serve both domestic markets and export-oriented trade.
5. Factors
	1. Import dependence and economies of scale favour coastal sites with deep-water ports.
	2. Product demand, pipeline networks, electricity, water, land, labour and environmental regulation shape location.
	3. Complex refineries can process diverse crude grades and maximise high-value products.
6. Implications
	1. Coastal siting lowers transport costs and supports petrochemical clusters and exports.
	2. It increases vulnerability to cyclones, sea-level rise, maritime disruption, spills and coastal pollution.
	3. Concentration can create jobs and infrastructure but also lock in fossil-fuel dependence.
7. Conclusion
	- Integrating petrochemical complexes, deploying carbon capture, and expanding green hydrogen-blended refining are critical to anchoring India's transition toward low-carbon energy resilience and net zero targets.
