### Q. NO DELTA IN WESTERN GHATS - WHY?
### A.
For a river to build a delta, it needs two fundamental things:
1. A large load of sediments (silt and sand) brought down from its course.
2. A calm, shallow marine environment where those sediments can settle and accumulate faster than waves and tides can carry them away.

Most westward-flowing rivers in India (like the short streams originating in the Western Ghats—such as the Mandovi, Zuari, Sharavati, and Periyar—as well as rift-valley rivers like the Narmada and Tapi) fail on both conditions. Instead of deltas, they form estuaries.

1. Rivers characteristics
   1. Short Course & High Gradient: The Western Ghats act as a water divide situated very close to the Arabian Sea.
   2. hard rock Terrain: river carries very little silt/sediment load.
   3. Short distance - high speed - no deposition time
2. Coastline & Maritime Dynamics
   1. Narrow & Deep Continental Shelf:
   2. Submerged Coastline creates deep funnel shaped estuaries.
   3. High Wave Energy & Strong Tidal Currents: Any sediment that the river does discharge is immediately washed out to deep sea by tidal flushing.
