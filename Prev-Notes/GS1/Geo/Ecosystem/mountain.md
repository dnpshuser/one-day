3. Impact of tourism
	1. **Positive:** 
   	1. Generates employment, 
   	2. local revenue, 
   	3. handicraft markets and 
   	4. incentives for heritage and 
   	5. biodiversity conservation.
	2. **Negative:** 
   	1. Unplanned hotels and roads cause deforestation, 
   	2. slope destabilisation, 
   	3. groundwater stress, 
   	4. waste accumulation and 
   	5. sewage pollution.
   	6. Traffic, noise and visitor pressure disturb wildlife; 
   	7. pilgrimage peaks intensify resource consumption and disaster exposure.
	3. **Way forward:** 
   	1. Regulate visitor numbers, 
   	2. promote homestays, 
   	3. enforce waste take-back, 
   	4. improve public transport and 
   	5. make tourism infrastructure disaster-resilient.
4. current challenges
	1. Climate change is accelerating glacier retreat, 
	2. altering snowfall and increasing flash floods, 
	3. landslides and glacial lake outburst flood risks.
	4. Deforestation, hydropower, mining, road construction, 
	5. urbanisation and unplanned tourism fragment habitats.
	6. Springs are drying, while soil erosion, 
	7. invasive species, 
	8. forest fires and 
	9. human-wildlife conflict are increasing.
	10. Fragile geology, seismicity and inadequate enforcement magnify the impact of infrastructure and extreme rainfall.
5. government initiatives
	1. **National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem:** Research, monitoring, capacity building and climate adaptation.
	2. **SECURE Himalaya:** Conservation of high-altitude biodiversity and sustainable livelihood support. **Source: MoEFCC and UNDP.**
	3. **National Mission on Himalayan Studies:** Research and community-based conservation.
	4. **NABARD Spring-shed development and Jal Jeevan Mission:** Support water-source sustainability and local water security.
	5. **Disaster Management Act, NDMA guidelines and IMD/ISRO monitoring:** Improve early warning, risk mapping and response.
	6. Environmental impact assessment, eco-sensitive zones, protected areas and the National Green Tribunal provide regulatory safeguards.
6. conclusion
	- Restoring mountain ecosystems through science, community stewardship and climate-resilient infrastructure can advance SDG 6, 13 and 15 while protecting future generations.
