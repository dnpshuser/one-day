---

The jurisprudence surrounding the Preamble to the Constitution of India has primarily revolved around three critical questions:

1. **Is the Preamble an integral part of the Constitution?**
2. **Can the Preamble be amended under Article 368?**
3. **Is the Preamble a substantive source of power or a restriction on legislative authority?**

---

### Core Landmark Judgments

| Landmark Case | Year & Bench Strength | Core Question Addressed | Judicial Verdict & Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **In Re: Berubari Union** | 1960  *(8-Judge Bench)* | Is the Preamble an integral part of the Constitution? | • Held that the Preamble is a **"key to open the mind of the makers"**, but **not a part of the Constitution**. • Ruled that the Preamble is **non-enforceable** and confers no substantive or limiting powers on the legislature. |
| **Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan** | 1965 *(5-Judge Bench)* | Reconsidering the Berubari interpretation. | • In their concurring opinions, **Justice Mudholkar and Justice Hidayatullah** expressed doubts about the *Berubari* stance. • Noted that the Constituent Assembly voted on the Preamble with the specific motion: *"The Preamble stands part of the Constitution."* |
| **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala** | 1973 *(13-Judge Bench)* | Can the Preamble be amended under Article 368? Is it part of the Constitution? | • **Expressly overruled *Berubari Union*** on this point. • Held that the **Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution**. • Held that the Preamble **can be amended under Article 368**, provided the amendment does not damage or destroy the **Basic Structure**. • Clarified that the Preamble itself reflects the foundational pillars of the Basic Structure (sovereignty, liberty, equality, secularism). |
| **Minerva Mills v. Union of India** | 1980 *(5-Judge Bench)* | Interplay between the Preamble and Basic Structure post-42nd Amendment. | • Reaffirmed that the Preamble sets the **constitutional goals** (social, economic, and political justice), while Parts III and IV are the means to achieve them. • Held that the harmony and balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles is an unalterable basic feature, rooted in the Preamble’s promises. |
| **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India** | 1994 *(9-Judge Bench)* | Legal enforceability and weight of terms in the Preamble (specifically "Secularism"). | • Held that **Secularism**, as declared in the Preamble, is an inviolable basic feature of the Constitution. • Any state government acting contrary to secularism can face action under Article 356 (President's Rule). |
| **LIC of India v. Consumer Education & Research Centre** | 1995 *(3-Judge Bench)* | Practical enforceability and socio-economic justice. | • Reaffirmed that the Preamble is an **integral part of the Constitution**. • Emphasized that the term **"Socialism"** in the Preamble envisions socio-economic justice, enabling the Court to interpret Article 21 broadly to cover the right to health and social security. |

---

### Judicial Standing Today

* **Part of the Constitution:** Yes, conclusively settled by *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* and reaffirmed in *LIC of India (1995)*.
* **Amendable under Article 368:** Yes. It has been amended once by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, which added the terms **"Socialist"**, **"Secular"**, and **"Integrity"**.
* **Subject to Basic Structure:** Any future amendment cannot delete or abrogate the core principles inscribed in it (such as democracy, republican character, equality, or secularism).
* **Non-Justiciable / Non-Enforceable:** The Preamble is **neither a direct source of power** for the legislature **nor a source of prohibition/limitation** upon legislative authority. It cannot be enforced independently in a court of law to invalidate an otherwise valid statute, but it serves as the ultimate **interpretative aid** when provisions of the Constitution or ordinary statutes are ambiguous.

---
1. Intro
The Preamble is the constitutional soul, reflecting the political philosophy, moral commitments, and democratic aspirations of modern India. 

2. Keywords - def and importance
   - Sovereign: India is politically independent and not subordinate to external authority.
   - Socialist: India adopts a welfare orientation, ensuring social and economic justice, not a purely laissez-faire model.
   - Secular: the State treats all religions equally and does not promote one faith over others.
   - Democratic: governance derives authority from the people through elections and representation.
   - Republic: the Head of State is elected, not hereditary.

3. Values and constitutional meaning
   - Liberty, Equality, Justice, Fraternity are the moral foundations of the Constitution.
   - The Preamble helps in interpreting vague constitutional language in light of constitutional values.
   - It legitimises the Constitution as a social contract between the State and citizens.
   - Supreme Court has treated it as a guiding principle, though not a source of enforceable rights in itself.
   - Source: Constitution of India; Kesavananda Bharati (1973); S.R. Bommai (1994).

4. Defendable or not?
   - It is not legally enforceable like Article 21, but it is persuasive in judicial reasoning.
   - The Preamble can be amended, but its essential moral vision cannot be altered.
   - It strengthens constitutional morality and public legitimacy in governance.
   - It is especially useful when deciding between competing interpretations of rights and duties.

5. Conclusion
The Preamble remains the ethical compass of the Constitution, sustaining democratic accountability, justice, and inclusion in line with SDG 16 and India’s constitutional commitments.
