### India vs France
1. Intro
India and France both value democracy and constitutional order, yet differ in political structure, secular approach, and executive legitimacy. 

2. Constitutional design
   - France has a semi-presidential system where the President is directly elected and shares power with the Prime Minister.
   - India follows a parliamentary system with a ceremonial President and a responsible Council of Ministers.
   - This makes France more presidential in practice, while India emphasises parliamentary accountability.
   - Both systems aim to combine stability with popular legitimacy.

3. Election of President
   - In India, the President is indirectly elected through an electoral college.
   - In France, the President is directly elected by citizens.
   - The French model gives stronger direct legitimacy to the executive.
   - India’s model protects federal balance and prevents over-concentration of power.

4. Secularism
   - India adopts secularism by equal treatment of religions and constitutional neutrality.
   - France follows laïcité, a stricter separation between state and religion in public institutions.
   - India’s model is more accommodating of pluralism and community diversity.
   - France’s model is sharper in state neutrality, especially in public administration and education.

5. Conclusion
The comparison shows that democratic legitimacy can be shaped differently within constitutional frameworks, strengthening institutional learning and democratic resilience.
