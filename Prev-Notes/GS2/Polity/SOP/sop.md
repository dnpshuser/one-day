### Separation of powers
1. Intro
Separation of powers is not a rigid wall but a constitutional design that prevents concentration of authority while enabling coordinated governance. 

2. checks and balances over strict SOP
- The Indian Constitution blends separation with overlap, creating checks and balances across institutions.
- This prevents authoritarian concentration while preserving governance efficiency.

3. Articles
- Articles 50, 122-123, 141-142, 361, 356, and 368 illustrate institutional balance and tension.

4. Relevant Case Laws
- Kesavananda Bharati, L. Chandra Kumar, S.R. Bommai, and the NJAC judgment show how judicial review maintains institutional equilibrium.

5. Judiciary
   1. importance of independence in democracy
   - Judicial independence preserves constitutional supremacy and protects rights.
   2. Judicial Activism: Judicial Legislation and Judicial guidelines to Executives
      1. Justification
      - It becomes necessary where institutions fail to uphold rights or constitutional norms.
      2. +ve and -ve
      - Positive when it fills rights gaps; negative when it substitutes democratic choices.

6. Executive vs Legislature
   1. Cabinet
      - The Cabinet is an executive instrument under Parliament, but also a political driver of legislative agenda.
      2. Governor vs SLA
      - Conflicts often test federal balance and constitutional interpretation.
   3. parliament ensures accountability of executives
      - Through Question Hour, debates, committees, and censure motions.
   4. Ordinances
      - Ordinances are justified as emergency tools but must not become substitutes for ordinary legislation.

7. Dispute redressal
   - Supreme Court judgments on NJAC and Governor-legislature conflicts show the role of judicial review in maintaining constitutional balance.

8. Conclusion
A workable separation of powers ensures accountability, liberty, and good governance—essential for democratic stability and SDG 16.
