### Election Disputes
1. Electoral accountability and fair process
2. Corrupt practices and legal standards
3. Filing and resolving petitions
4. Grounds for voiding elections
5. Reform and procedural simplification
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
Election disputes are a vital part of democratic accountability, ensuring that free and fair elections remain the foundation of political legitimacy. 

2. Corrupt practices
   1. clauses and conditions
   - Bribery, undue influence, misuse of government machinery, and false statements are recognised as corrupt practices.
   - Special reference: unexplained increase in assets may invite scrutiny under election laws.

3. resolution procedure
- Election petitions are filed in the High Court and then subject to a quasi-judicial process.
- The process is lengthy and often burdensome, creating a need for efficiency.

4. Grounds for declaring election of candidate - void
   1. remedy available to the aggrieved party
   - The aggrieved party can seek declaration of the election void and challenge the result through legal process.
   2. case laws
   - Judicial precedents clarify the standard of proof and the limits of election law.
   3. Need for simplification of procedure
   - A faster, clearer process would improve democratic legitimacy and reduce delay.

5. conclusion
A fair electoral system strengthens public trust and democratic accountability, which is essential for SDG 16 and constitutional governance.
