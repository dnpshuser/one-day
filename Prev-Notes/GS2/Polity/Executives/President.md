### President
1. Constitutional head and executive balance
2. Constitutional role and powers
3. Pardoning power and limits
4. Legislative and ceremonial functions
5. Constitutional conventions and accountability
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The President is the constitutional head of India, symbolising unity while exercising limited but important executive and ceremonial functions. 

2. Constitutional role
- The President is part of the Union executive under Articles 52-78.
- The President appoints the Prime Minister, governors, judges, and constitutional authorities.
- The President’s powers are largely formal, while real executive authority is exercised by the Council of Ministers.
- This reflects the parliamentary nature of the Indian system.

3. Pardoning power
- Article 72 gives the President power to grant pardon, reprieve, remission, respite, or commutation.
- This is a mercy jurisdiction meant to humanise the law in exceptional circumstances.
- However, the power must not be used arbitrarily or as a substitute for judicial review.
- The Supreme Court has emphasised that the President’s pardon cannot override judicial conviction or annul constitutional constraints.
- Some argue that time limits and greater transparency could improve accountability.

4. Legislative and ceremonial functions
- The President summons, prorogues, and dissolves Parliament as per constitutional convention.
- The President addresses Parliament and gives assent to bills.
- The office symbolizes the state and provides political neutrality in a democratic system.
- Yet the office can become controversial when constitutional conventions are ignored.

5. Conclusion
The President’s office must remain dignified, restrained, and constitutional, sustaining democratic legitimacy, federal trust, and public faith in the State.
