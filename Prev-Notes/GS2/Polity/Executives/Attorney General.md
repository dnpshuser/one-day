### Attorney General
1. Constitutional legal adviser and institutional role
2. Position and functions
3. Rights and privileges
4. Independence and limitations
5. Accountability and constitutional value
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The Attorney General is the government’s chief legal adviser, helping the State navigate constitutional and legal complexities while upholding the rule of law. 

2. Constitutional position and functions
- Article 76 provides for the office of the Attorney General.
- The Attorney General advises the Union government on legal matters.
- The office also represents the Union before the Supreme Court and High Courts.
- The role combines legal expertise with constitutional sensitivity.

3. Rights and privileges
- The Attorney General has the right to audience in all courts in India.
- The person may appear in parliamentary proceedings and advise on legal issues.
- The office enjoys legal privileges to discharge constitutional functions effectively.
- However, these privileges do not make the office politically dominant.

4. Limitations and criticisms
- The office is not independent in the same way as the judiciary.
- Some critics argue it may become a spokesperson of the executive rather than an impartial legal conscience.
- The office suffers from a lack of institutional independence compared with the judiciary.
- It must be careful not to merge legal advice with partisan political strategy.

5. Conclusion
The Attorney General is essential for legal clarity and governmental accountability, but its credibility depends on professional independence and constitutional restraint.
