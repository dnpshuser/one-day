   1. 9th Schedule introduced in 1st CAA, 1951
   2. Barred from Judicial Review in A-31(b,c)
2. Shankari Prasad(1951):
   - Patna HC struck down Land Reform Act, 1950 as unconstitutional agains A-31.
   - Parliament passed 1st Amendment, 1951 to validate the Act.
   - 1st Amendment, 1951: Added A-31(A) and A-31(B) and 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws from judicial review.
   - Shankari Prasad challenged the amendment in SC.
   - SC upheld the constitutionality of the amendment, stating that Parliament has power to amend the Constitution under A-368.
   - Now any law under the 9th Schedule cannot be challenged in court for violating fundamental rights.
3. Sajjan Singh(1965):
   - Parliament passed 17th Amendment, 1964 to validate land reform laws and added 44 laws to the 9th Schedule schielding them from judicial review.
   - Sajjan Singh from Rajasthan challenged the 17th Amendment in SC with
     - 17th CAA affects HC's power to review laws under A-13 and A-31 and thus should be ratified by at least half of the states.
     - Parliament cannot amend fundamental rights under A-368.
   - SC held that fundamental rights can be amended by Parliament with 3:2 majority under A-368 and that the 17th Amendment was valid.
   - concerns: 
     - Justice Hydayatullah: Parliament can amend fundamental rights and thus they are no more fundamental.
     - Justice Madholkar: Parliament can amend fundamental rights but cannot take away their essence.
4. Golaknath(1967): 
   - Golaknath challenged the 17th Amendment in SC with
     - Parliament cannot amend FR under A-368.
   - 6:5 judgement: SC held that Parliament cannot amend fundamental rights and thus the 17th Amendment was invalid.
5. Scenario:

6. Kesavananda Bharati(1973): 
   1. Petition of violation of A-26.
   2. Keral govt did for welfare - Keshavananda Bharti lost the case - lost thousands of acres of land
   3. SC verdict: (7:6)
      1. Can change FR
      2. CAA are not laws
      3. But can't change the Basic Structure of the Constitution
   4. Nana Palkivalah: 
     - Does Parliament possess unlimited, absolute power to rewrite the Indian Constitution, or are there implied, inherent limitations to its amending power?
     - ***The power to amend is not the power to destroy. Parliament is a creature of the Constitution; it cannot use its powers to kill its own creator.***

8. Minerva Mills Case 1980
   1. petition of violation of A-14, A-19(f: R.T.Business) on nationalization of Textile mill
   2. Govt: Nationalization of Textile Mill is immune to Judicial Review as it is a part of 42nd CAA
   3. Q: Can Parliament sought to grant itself sovereigh unreviewable power by adding: Sec 55 of 42nd CAA
      1. A-368(4): No question on CAA on any court on any ground
      2. A-368(5): No limitation on Constituent power of the Parliament - "for the removal of doubts, it is hereby declared that there shall be no limitation whatever on the constituent power of Parliament to amend by way of addition, variation or repeal the provisions of this Constitution."
   4. This CAA - dismantled the implied Basic Str limits imposed by the Kesavananda Bharati rulings
   5. Sec 4 of CAA
      1. Any DPSP can violate FR
   6. Core Q:
      1. Unlimited Amending power ?
      2. can Parliament oust Judicial Review ?
      3. FR vs DPSP ?
   7. SC Verdict: 
      1. Invalidated A-368(4,5): Parliament cannot use its limited amending power under Article 368 to convert that power into an absolute, unlimited one.
      2. Ousting judicial review via Article 368(4) damages the Basic Structure, as an independent judiciary is an essential constitutional check.
      3. The Court invalidated the broad amendment to Article 31C, restoring it to its pre-1976 status (protecting only laws implementing Articles 39(b) and 39(c)).
      4. > "The Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Parts III and IV. To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution. Part III and Part IV together constitute the core of commitment to social revolution; they are like the two wheels of a chariot, neither superior to the other."
      5. Minerva Mills won the case
      6. Judicial Review became a part of Basic Structure
      7. Constitution is supreme not the organs of Govt(Legislature, Executive, Judiciary)

      1. If law can violate FR -> they are violating Basic Str

   2. SC validated A-31(A) & A-31(C) to violate A-14 and A-19 as their goal was agrarian reform

      2. 9th Schedule is valid so unless the law completely collapses Basic str, courts can't review it
      3. Parliament treated 9th schedule as a blanket immunity vault even for non-agrarian laws
   3. Ambiguity 3

12. IR Coelho 2007 - resolved ambiguity
   1. 'Essence of Rights' test established
   2. 2 tier checks for acts of 9th schedule
   3. 9th Schedule Act -> 
      1. No violation of FR: Act is valid
      2. Violation of FR - "Essence of Rights" Test
         1. Doesn't Destroys ESSENCE, CORE VALUE, or FOUNDATIONAL IDENTITY of that Fundamental Right? -> A-31(B saves it)
         2. Destroes core identity(14,19,21) -> Act is struck down
