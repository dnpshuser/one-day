### EXPLAINATION

1. Environment problems related cases and judgments given by the Supreme Court:
   1. Cooverjee B. Bharucha vs. Excise Commissioner(1954): The Supreme Court, while deciding the matter relating to carrying on the trade of liquor in Cooverjee B. Bharucha Vs Excise commissioner, Ajmer (1954, SC 220) observed that, if there is a clash between environmental protection and right to freedom of trade and occupation, the courts have to balance environmental interests with the fundamental rights to carry on any occupations.
   2. Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra vs. State of Uttar Pradesh (1987): The right to live in a healthy environment as part of Article 21 of the Constitution was first recognized. The Supreme Court had received a writ petition from Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra regarding the unauthorized and illegal operation of limestone quarries in the Mussoorie Hill range, India. It was argued that the quarries caused a hazard to the healthy environment and affected the perennial water springs. 
   3. M.C. Mehta vs. Union of India (1987): The right to live in a pollution-free environment was recognised as a fundamental right under Article 21 of the Constitution. The case lays down the principle of absolute liability and deep pockets. 
   4. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum vs. Union of India (1996): Remediation of the damaged environment is part of the process of Sustainable Development and as such the polluter is liable to pay the cost to the individual sufferers as well as the cost of reversing the damaged ecology. The Precautionary Principle and the Polluter Pays Principle have been accepted as part of the law of the land. Article 21 of the Constitution of India guarantees the protection of life and personal liberty.
   5. Subash Kumar vs. State of Bihar (1991): The Supreme Court ruled that Article 32 of the Indian Constitution is intended to protect a citizen’s fundamental rights; it is an exceptional process to protect a citizen’s rights. The right to life is a basic right protected by Article 21 of the Constitution which gives a person the right to live in clean water and air.
   6. Residents Welfare Association vs. Union Territory of Chandigarh (2023): The Court appealed to the Legislature, the Executive and the Policy Makers at the Centre as well as at the State levels to make necessary provisions for carrying out Environment Impact Assessment (EIA) studies before permitting urban development. The warning flagged, for example, Bengaluru city.
