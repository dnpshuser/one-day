### EXPLAINATION
1. Constitutional Provisions
   1. Preamble: 
      1. Emphasises justice (social, economic, political), equality, and dignity of the individual.- guiding lights
   2. FR:
      1. Article 14: Equality before law and equal protection of law to everyone.
      2. Article 15(1): Outlaws discrimination on grounds of sex, caste, religion, race, or place of birth.
      3. Article 15(2): No disability on the basis of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth or any of them.
      4. Article 15(3): Enables special provisions for women and children, balancing equality with affirmative action. 
      5. Article 16: Ensures equality in public employment opportunities.
      6. Article 21: Guarantees right to life with dignity, privacy, and reproductive autonomy. 
      7. Article 23: Prohibits trafficking, begar, and forced labour, which disproportionately exploit women and children.
   3. DPSPs: 
      1. Article 39(a) & (d): Equal access to livelihood and equal pay for equal work.
      2. Article 39(e): Protection of health and strength of women and children.
      3. Article 42: Provides for maternity relief and humane work conditions.
      4. Articles 45 & 47: Focus on nutrition, healthcare, and education, vital for women’s empowerment.
   4. Fundamental Duties:
      1. Article 51A(e): Duty of citizens to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
   5. Political Representation:
      1. Articles 243D & 243T: Reservation of one-third seats for women in panchayats and municipalities.
      2. Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023): Provides 33% reservation in Parliament and State legislatures.
      3. Articles 325 & 326: Establish universal adult franchise, guaranteeing women equal political voice.
2. Statutory Provisions:
   1. Marriage & Family Laws: Hindu Marriage Act(1955), Special Marriage Act (1954), Prohibition of Child Marriage Act (2006) protect autonomy and prevent exploitation.
   2. Labour & Employment Laws: Equal Remuneration Act (1976), Maternity Benefit Act (1961, amended 2017 to 26 weeks leave), Code on Wages (2019) strengthen workplace equality.
   3. Violence & Safety Laws: Dowry Prohibition Act(1961), Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (2005), POCSO Act (2012), Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act (2013) create safe spaces.
   4. Property & Inheritance: Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act (2005) gave daughters equal coparcenary rights in property.
   5. Other Safeguards: 
      1. Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act (1956), 
      2. Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act (2019) broaden justice to marginalised gender identities.
3. Judicial Interventions
   1. Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997): Formulated guidelines on workplace sexual harassment, later codified by law.
   2. Mary Roy v. State of Kerala (1986): Ensured inheritance rights for Christian women.
   3. Githa Hariharan v. RBI (1999): Recognised mothers as natural guardians, breaking patriarchal bias.
   4. Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017): Invalidated Triple Talaq as unconstitutional, protecting Muslim women’s rights.
   5. Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018): Decriminalised homosexuality, expanding gender and sexual rights.
   6. Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018): Struck down adultery law as discriminatory and patriarchal.
   7. Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala (2018 – Sabarimala): Opened temples to women of all ages, reinforcing religious equality.
   8. Babita Puniya v. Union of India (2020): Granted permanent commission to women in the Army.
   9.  Supreme Court in 2024 allowed women to sit for NDA and Sainik School entrance exams, breaking defence barriers.
4. Comparision
   1. Iceland: Enforces equal pay certification, penalising companies with gender pay gaps.
   2. Rwanda: Over 60% women in Parliament, leading the world in women’s political representation.
   3. France: Gender parity law mandates equal candidate lists in elections.
   4. Nordic Countries: Provide robust parental leave, childcare systems, and rank top in gender equality indices.
   5. USA: The reversal of Roe v. Wade (2022) highlights fragility of reproductive rights and judicial shifts.
5. Way forward
   1. Strengthen enforcement of laws through independent monitoring and fast-track gender justice courts.
   2. Expand gender-responsive budgeting to ensure every policy addresses women’s needs.
   3. Mainstream gender sensitivity in school curricula, workplaces, and public institutions.
   4. Enhance institutional strength of the National Commission for Women with constitutional backing.
   5. Address intersectionality — caste, class, disability, and region must be factored in policies.
   6. Promote female participation in science, technology, judiciary, corporate boards, and defence.
