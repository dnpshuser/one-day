### Local Self Government
1. Decentralisation and democratic governance
2. Role in good governance
3. Merger and structural challenges
4. Devolution of powers and funds
5. Women’s participation and accountability
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
Local self-government is the foundation of democratic decentralisation, bringing the state closer to citizens through grassroots governance and participation. 

2. Role in good governance
- It improves public service delivery, planning, and accountability.
- Local institutions can better respond to sanitation, water, health, education, and welfare needs.

3. +ve and -ve in merging rural and urban local bodies
- Merger may improve planning and resource efficiency, but it can dilute local identity and rural priorities.
- It requires careful institutional design to preserve accountability and participation.

4. Challenges
   1. Power devolution upto local level
   - Limited functional and financial autonomy weakens local capacity.
   2. State's and Center's reluctancy
   - Many states remain reluctant to devolve real powers and resources.
   3. Functional and funds problems
   - Inadequate tax base, delayed transfers, and weak administrative capacity reduce effectiveness.

5. Limited impact
   1. in effective governanec
   - Local bodies often remain dependent on state-level discretion.
   2. women reservation in government/local government
   - Reservation has improved women’s representation, but effective participation still needs institutional support.

7. Panchayat
   1. Importance
   - Panchayats are the closest democratic institutions for rural governance and welfare delivery.
   2. Sources of funding
   - Own tax revenue, grants from the State and Centre, and central schemes such as PMAY/NRLM.

8. Conclusion
Local self-government is essential for participatory democracy, inclusive development, and SDG 11, provided fiscal and functional devolution is strengthened.
