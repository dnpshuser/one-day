### Centralised Decision Making
1. Intro
Centralised decision-making can improve coordination and speed, but it often weakens local knowledge, accountability, and public participation in complex governance systems.

2. Problems
- Information asymmetry: the centre may not capture local realities, conditions, and needs.
- Execution delays and accountability deficit: long chains of administrative approval can slow implementation.
- Alienation of stakeholders: local communities and institutions may feel excluded from planning and action.
- Policy contradictions: centrally designed schemes may conflict with regional priorities or ecological realities.
- Weak environmental protection: top-down decisions may neglect local ecological vulnerabilities and sustainability concerns.

3. Importance and necessity
- Centralisation is useful in crisis management, national standards, and strategic planning.
- It helps ensure uniformity in public policy and resource allocation across regions.
- National schemes often require a centre-led framework for scale, coordination, and fiscal support.

4. Conclusion
Centralisation improves coordination but cannot replace local participation; effective governance requires a balance between national coherence and sub-national responsiveness.
