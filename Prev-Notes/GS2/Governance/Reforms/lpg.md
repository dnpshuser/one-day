### LPG Reforms
1. Intro
The Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation reforms of 1991 marked a structural shift in India’s development strategy, responding to fiscal stress and global economic integration.

2. Scenario of 1991 and how it was solved
- India faced a severe balance-of-payments crisis, inflation, and low investor confidence.
- The reforms opened the economy, reduced excessive licensing, and improved macroeconomic stability.
- They signalled a strategic move from protectionism toward market-oriented growth and integration.

3. Positive adaptations
- Improved competition, efficiency, and investment flows in industry and services.
- Expanded consumer choice, technology transfer, and export-oriented growth.
- Strengthened capital markets and modernised public-sector management in some areas.
- Enabled India’s integration into the global economy and digital services growth.

4. Shortcomings
- Growth benefits were uneven, with regional and social inequalities persisting.
- Public welfare systems and agricultural support weakened in some areas.
- Job quality, labour protection, and rural distress remained major concerns.
- The gains were not evenly distributed across caste, gender, and class groups.

5. Measures for further improvement
- Combine market reforms with social protection, skill development, and labour reform.
- Strengthen public investment in health, education, and agriculture.
- Ensure inclusive growth through regional and social equity policies.
- Use regulation to protect consumers and workers in a globalised market environment.

6. Conclusion
LPG reforms were necessary for economic modernisation, but their long-term success depends on balancing market efficiency with social justice, welfare, and democratic accountability.
