### Neo-Liberal Paradigm
1. Intro
The neo-liberal paradigm prioritises markets, efficiency, and private participation in governance, reshaping the role of the state in economic and social policy.

2. Principles
- Free market supremacy: markets are viewed as the main engine of growth and allocation.
- Privatisation: public services and assets are shifted to private actors where feasible.
- Deregulation: state intervention is reduced to encourage competition and efficiency.
- Fiscal discipline and minimal state intervention: reducing subsidies and public spending is treated as a growth strategy.
- Free trade and globalisation: openness to global markets is seen as essential for productivity and investment.
- Individual responsibility and choice: welfare is increasingly framed through market-based incentives.

3. Challenges
- Socio-economic inequality: market-oriented reforms can deepen disparities in income, access, and opportunity.
- Erosion of welfare support: vulnerable groups may lose public protection in health, education, and labour security.
- Environmental strain: growth-first strategies can intensify resource depletion and ecological degradation.
- Institutional exclusion: market logic may ignore social and regional inequalities in policy design.

4. Conclusion
Neo-liberalism can improve efficiency and investment, but its legitimacy depends on inclusion, social protection, and constitutional concern for equality and justice.
