### Constitutionalization of Commission
1. Intro
Constitutionalisation of a commission strengthens institutional authority, independence, and legitimacy, aligning it more closely with the constitutional commitment to justice and accountability.

2. Importance of constitutionalization
- It gives a body stronger legal recognition and constitutional sanctity.
- It helps protect the institution from arbitrary executive control and political dilution.
- It signals the state’s commitment to a specific rights-based objective.
- It strengthens policy influence and public trust in governance institutions.

3. Benefits
- Greater autonomy in investigation, advice, and monitoring.
- Better ability to influence laws, schemes, and public policy.
- Enhanced status may improve resource allocation and public visibility.
- Fosters accountability and institutional continuity across governments.

4. Steps to constitutionalize
- Amend the Constitution or create enabling legal provisions with a clear mandate.
- Define functions, independence, reporting mechanisms, and removal safeguards.
- Create adequate funding, staff, and technical expertise.
- Ensure coordination with courts, executive, and state institutions.
- Provide transparency in selection and performance review.

5. Conclusion
Constitutionalisation can strengthen rights-based governance, but it must be paired with independence, accountability, and meaningful enforcement to serve democratic justice.
