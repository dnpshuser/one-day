### Competition Commission of India
1. Intro
The Competition Commission of India is the statutory regulator that promotes competition, prevents anti-competitive practices, and protects consumer welfare in a liberalised market economy.

2. Evolution
- The CCI was established under the Competition Act, 2002, replacing earlier fragmented competition oversight.
- It reflects the shift from regulation of monopoly to market governance and consumer protection.
- The law evolved to address modern issues such as digital markets, cartels, and abuse of dominant position.

3. Role
- Investigates anti-competitive agreements and abuse of dominant position by firms.
- Regulates mergers and acquisitions to prevent harmful concentration of market power.
- Protects consumer interests by ensuring fair prices, quality, and choice.
- Encourages market efficiency, innovation, and competition-led growth.

4. Challenges
- Enforcement against digital platforms and large conglomerates is complex due to network effects and innovation dynamics.
- Limited manpower and procedural complexity can slow case disposition.
- Coordination with sectoral regulators remains essential but can create overlap.
- Balancing consumer welfare with ease of doing business is a constant governance challenge.

5. Abuse of dominant positions by MNCs
- Dominant firms may use predatory pricing, exclusivity clauses, or deep-pocket advantage to foreclose competition.
- Large global firms require careful scrutiny, especially in digital markets and essential services.
- Competition law must ensure that scale efficiency does not become market abuse.

6. Recent decisions
- The CCI has intervened in mergers, digital platform cases, and antitrust investigations, reflecting growing market complexity.
- Its decisions reflect a broader understanding of market power in technology-driven sectors.
- Source: Competition Act, 2002; CCI annual reports; major orders and judgments.

7. Conclusion
The CCI is central to market democracy and consumer protection, ensuring that economic freedom does not come at the cost of fairness, competition, and constitutional governance.
