### Human Rights Commission
1. Intro
The National Human Rights Commission is a statutory body designed to protect and promote human rights, ensuring accountability and dignity in public governance.

2. Evolution
- It was established in 1993 under the Protection of Human Rights Act.
- The commission was created to strengthen rights-based governance and constitutional accountability.
- It reflects the growing emphasis on dignity, equality, and access to justice.

3. Contribution
- Examines complaints of rights violations and recommends remedial action.
- Promotes awareness of rights and accountability mechanisms for vulnerable communities.
- Supports civil liberties, legal aid, and public grievance mechanisms.
- Works to build a human-rights culture within democratic institutions.

4. Role
- Investigates violations by public authorities and recommends corrective measures.
- Advises government on policies affecting rights and social justice.
- Collaborates with state commissions and civil society to strengthen monitoring and redress.
- Encourages legal and institutional reforms for human rights protection.

5. Recent achievements
- Increased awareness and intervention in custodial violence, trafficking, and vulnerable community protection.
- Broader engagement with rights concerns in digital governance, migration, and public service delivery.

6. Challenges
- Limited enforcement power and dependence on government action weaken effectiveness.
- Large backlog of complaints affects timely redress.
- Lack of uniformity across state commissions and overlapping mandates reduce coherence.
- Scope of intervention often remains constrained by low institutional capacity.

7. Solutions
- Strengthen enforcement, resource capacity, and inter-agency coordination.
- Improve digital complaint systems and speedy case tracking.
- Enhance public awareness and collaboration with civil society and judiciary.

8. Committee reports
- Reform reports have suggested better capacity, autonomy, and coordination among human rights institutions.
- They highlight the need for stronger protection of rights in a modern democratic state.

9. Multiplicity of commissions
  1. Challenges
  - Fragmented mandates can lead to overlap, inefficiency, and poor accountability.
  2. Merger: would it help?
  - A more streamlined structure may improve coordination but risks diluting specialised rights mandates.

10. Conclusion
The NHRC is indispensable for rights accountability, but its effectiveness depends on autonomy, transparency, and stronger institutional capacity in a rights-based democracy.
