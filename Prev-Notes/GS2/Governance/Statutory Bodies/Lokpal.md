### Lokpal
1. Intro
The Lokpal is intended to be India’s anti-corruption ombudsman, strengthening institutional accountability and citizen trust in public governance.

2. Immorality and institutional limits
- Corruption is not only a legal problem; it is also deeply shaped by ethics, weak governance culture, and political incentives.
- Legal institutions alone cannot eliminate systemic immorality or moral failures in public administration.
- Policies required to address this include ethical training, transparency, digital governance, and public scrutiny.
- A stronger integrity ecosystem is essential alongside anti-corruption laws.

3. Legal powers
- Investigates complaints against public functionaries and officials under the Lokpal framework.
- Exercises inquiry and prosecution powers within the limits of the relevant legal structure.
- Strengthens accountability by bringing high-level corruption cases under scrutiny.
- Works within a constitutional and legal rule framework to uphold fairness and due process.

4. Challenges
- Delays, weak enforcement, and limited institutional capacity obstruct effective action.
- Political resistance and procedural complexity may reduce its impact in practice.
- Ethical governance requires complementary measures, not only legal powers.

5. Conclusion
The Lokpal is an essential instrument of accountability, but real anti-corruption outcomes require constitutional integrity, ethical governance, and transparency across institutions.
