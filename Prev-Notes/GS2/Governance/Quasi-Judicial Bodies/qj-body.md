### Quasi-Judicial Body
1. Intro
Quasi-judicial bodies are institutions that decide disputes with judicial characteristics but are not full courts, blending adjudication with administrative expertise.

2. Concept
- They exercise delegated adjudicatory powers under statutes, often in specialised domains like taxation, labour, telecom, and regulation.
- Their decisions are guided by law, fairness, and procedural principles but remain within the executive-administrative sphere.
- They help reduce burden on ordinary courts while ensuring technical expertise in complex matters.
- This reflects the constitutional need for administrative efficiency along with due process.

3. Functions
- Resolve disputes arising under specific laws and regulatory frameworks.
- Interpret technical and sectoral issues beyond ordinary judicial competence.
- Ensure procedural fairness, evidence-based decision-making, and administrative consistency.
- Provide expert adjudication in matters such as service disputes, competition law, and corporate regulation.
- Maintain a balance between government regulation and individual rights.

4. Challenges
- Risk of executive influence and lack of independence can undermine impartiality.
- Decisions may sometimes lack transparency or adequate appeal mechanisms.
- The distinction between quasi-judicial and administrative action remains legally nuanced.
- Delays and backlog can affect timely justice and public trust.

5. Importance
- They are essential in complex modern governance where specialised expertise is necessary.
- They support rule-based governance and reduce judicial overload.
- Their legitimacy depends on independence, procedural fairness, and effective review systems.

6. Conclusion
Quasi-judicial bodies are necessary for expert and efficient adjudication, but constitutional safeguards and accountability are vital to preserve justice, fairness, and democratic legitimacy.
