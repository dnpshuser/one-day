### Finance Commission
1. Intro
The Finance Commission is a constitutional body that redistributes fiscal resources between the Union and states, ensuring cooperative federalism and equitable development.

2. Constitutional process
- It is constituted under Article 280 of the Constitution.
- The President appoints the commission every five years, with recommendations on tax sharing and grants.
- It analyses fiscal capacity, needs, and expenditure responsibilities of states.
- The commission’s recommendations guide the distribution of central tax revenues and grants-in-aid.
- It strengthens the fiscal architecture of Indian federalism within a constitutional framework.

3. Terms of reference
- Review the distribution of Union tax revenues between the Centre and states.
- Recommend grants-in-aid to states and measures to improve financial discipline.
- Advise on fiscal federalism, local body financing, and public expenditure standards.
- Examine resource requirements and revenue mobilisation in a changing economic environment.
- Recommend policy measures to enhance transparency and efficiency in fiscal management.

4. Recommendations to improve fiscal relations
- Increase vertical and horizontal devolution to reduce inter-state disparities.
- Strengthen states’ fiscal autonomy while preserving national developmental standards.
- Align grants with performance, capacity, and need-based parameters.
- Improve local body financing and increase local government accountability.
- Support cooperative federalism through transparent, rule-based transfers.
- Source: Finance Commission reports; Constitution of India, Article 280.

5. Conclusion
The Finance Commission is central to balancing national unity and regional equity, enabling efficient public finance and inclusive, federal governance across India.
