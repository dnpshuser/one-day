### RTI
1. Intro
The Right to Information is a democratic instrument that empowers citizens to demand transparency, accountability, and ethical public administration in a constitutional order.

2. Evolution
- RTI emerged from the constitutional spirit of Articles 19 and 21, ensuring informed citizenship and democratic oversight.
- The Act was enacted in 2005 after sustained civil society pressure for participatory governance.
- It institutionalized the right to access public information in the interest of accountability and good governance.
- The movement for RTI reflects constitutional morality and citizen participation in public decision-making.
- It created a framework for proactive disclosure and proactive public scrutiny.

3. Amendments
- The RTI Act has undergone amendments to improve efficiency, strengthen exemptions, and address implementation issues.
- Exemptions under Section 8 are narrowly interpreted to protect national security, privacy, and legitimate confidentiality.
- Reforms have sought to improve transparency without compromising sensitive governance interests.
- Judicial interpretation has ensured that exemptions cannot become a blanket shield against accountability.
- Source: RTI Act, 2005; CIC decisions; Government of India notifications.

4. Benefits
- Enables citizens to monitor expenditure, procurement, and public service delivery.
- Strengthens anti-corruption measures and reduces opacity in governance.
- Promotes participatory democracy by letting communities question public authorities.
- Improves administrative efficiency through better record management and public scrutiny.
- Serves as a tool for social audits and policy accountability in welfare schemes.

5. Challenges and limitations
- Delays, backlog, and poor record management weaken effective implementation.
- Fear of retaliation against applicants creates chilling effects on public vigilance.
- Excessive exemptions and procedural complexity limit the law’s utility in practice.
- Weak public awareness and low digital literacy restrict access by marginalised citizens.
- Capacity constraints in Public Information Officers affect timely and accurate responses.

6. Solutions
- Strengthen digital filing, proactive disclosure, and training of public information officers.
- Improve grievance redress and protection for whistleblowers and applicants.
- Narrow exemptions and review them through parliamentary and judicial oversight.
- Expand awareness campaigns for rural and marginal communities to make access meaningful.
- Encourage citizen-led monitoring and social audit of public spending.

7. Committee reports
- Administrative Reforms Commission and parliamentary committees have underlined RTI’s role in public accountability.
- Recommendations focus on strengthening implementation, reducing delays, and improving citizen awareness.
- RTI is seen as a cornerstone of democratic transparency and institutional trust.

8. Conclusion
RTI is a constitutional anchor of accountable governance, enabling citizens to check power, curb corruption, and build a transparent, rights-based democracy aligned with SDG 16.
