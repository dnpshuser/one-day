# Ethics and Human Interface

**Ethics** - Set of standards which the society places on itself and which helps guide behavior, choices and actions.

* Systematic study of human action from the point of view of their rightfulness or wrongfulness.
* Aim: Achieve Summum Bonum (Highest Good)
* Origin: Greek word "ethos" - customs/habits

Not following Ethics may lead to social disapproval or sanctions.

**Essence of Ethics (Key Features of Ethics)**

* Normative Discipline: Defines what ought to be done.
* Universal but Contextual: Ethics may be universal (truth, justice) but their application varies by society.
* Moral Compass: Guides individual and societal actions.
* May Change: Eg, Sati
* Depends on Space and Time: India vs Europe
* Subjective

**Quotes & Jumla**

* "Ethics is knowing the difference between what you have a right to do and what is right to do".

**Indian Context & Examples**

* Bhagavad Gita: Krishna's advice to Arjuna on acting as per Dharma rather than emotions.
* Ashoka's Dhamma: Moral Universalism-compassion, tolerance, and respect for all.

## What if Individual decides?

Then Osama, Hitler, Duryodhana or evil corporations would consider themselves as ethical.
This is what we call as Morality, where Individuals own principles decide between right and wrong.

**Morality**
Definition - Individual's (group, individuals etc) own principles concerning the distinction between right and wrong or good or bad behaviour.
Morality is about individual standards and ethics is about established moral standards.

* All moral standards can't be ethical standards.

**Key Features of Morality**

* Subjective: Varies from person to person based on personal conscience.
* Internal Compass: Dictates personal choices and judgments.
* Emotion-driven: Influenced by guilt, empathy, and conscience.

**Example:**

* Harishchandra's Truthfulness: Despite immense suffering, he did not abandon the truth-his personal morality.
* Bhishma's Loyalty in Mahabharata: His moral duty was to his vow, even though it led to unethical consequences.

**Can Morality and Ethics Conflict?**
Yes! A moral belief may contradict ethical duty, leading to ethical dilemmas.

* Example 1: Satyam Scam (Corporate Ethics vs Personal Morality): Founder Ramalinga Raju confessed to financial fraud due to personal guilt (morality). However, his ethical responsibility was to ensure corporate transparency.
* Example 2: Doctor operating on a murderer.
* Example 3: Arjuna's Dilemma in Bhagavad Gita: Moral Conflict - Killing his own kin troubled him. Ethical Duty (Dharma) - Krishna advised him to uphold justice and duty as a warrior.

## Case Study: Euthanasia - Moral vs Ethical Dilemma

**Scenario:** A terminally ill patient in India requests euthanasia to end their suffering.

* Moral Perspective: A doctor may personally believe that life is sacred and euthanasia is immoral.
* Ethical Perspective: The medical code of ethics (and Supreme Court judgment in the Aruna Shanbaug case) allows passive euthanasia under strict guidelines.

## Case Study: The Judiciary's Dilemma - Ethics vs Personal Morality

**Scenario:** A judge has to convict a poor man who stole food for his starving family.

* Moral Perspective: The judge may sympathize and feel the law is harsh.
* Ethical Obligation: As per constitutional morality, justice must be impartial.

## Values

* Standards according to which one is supposed to conduct himself. 'Standards of human conduct'.
* Standards on basis of which we evaluate something or make judgments about how desirable or undesirable a thing is to us.
* Values are the basis for moral and ethical standards.
* Our values influence what we determine as ethical. Hence, they are called "language of ethics", Guiding lights.

**Examples**

* Truth (Satya) in Indian Philosophy: Emphasized in Gandhian ethics.
* Seva (Service) in Sikhism: Selfless service is a core value.
* Scientific Temper (Constitutional Value): Promoted by Jawaharlal Nehru for rational thinking.
* Ahimsa Parmo Dharma, Shanti Ekta Sadbhavana.

## Determinants of Ethics in Human Actions (Determining what is ethical / right or wrong)

**Key Determinants:**

1. Object Nature (e.g., widow marriage, abortion, euthanasia)
2. Circumstances (e.g., sleeping on a footpath)
3. Intent/Purpose (e.g., Ashwathama's actions)
4. Consequence (e.g., Vibhishana, Refugees)

**Ethical Frameworks:**

* **Moral Universalism → Universal Ethical Determinants:** Ethics is absolute and applies to all, regardless of culture. Determinants: Human rights, moral duties, justice, honesty. Example: Abolition of slavery—globally condemned as unethical.
* **Ethical Relativism → Culturally Influenced Ethics:** Ethics depends on society, traditions, or historical context. Determinants: Religion, customs, cultural traditions, social norms. Example: Polygamy in different societies—accepted in some cultures but banned in others.
* **Moral Nihilism → No Objective Ethics:** Ethics is a human construct and lacks any universal truth. Determinants: Individual desires, existential beliefs, rejection of morality. Example: Nietzsche's "God is dead"—rejection of moral absolutes led to existentialist perspectives.
* **Deontology (Kantian Ethics) → Duty-Based Ethical Determinants:** Ethics is about following moral duties irrespective of consequences. Determinants: Moral laws, conscience, rational thinking, duty. Example: Bhishma's vow in Mahabharata—he remained loyal to Hastinapur despite moral dilemmas.
* **Utilitarianism → Consequence-Based Ethical Determinants:** Ethics is determined by what produces the greatest good for the greatest number. Determinants: Outcome, cost-benefit analysis, public welfare. Example: Justifying war for long-term peace—Hiroshima & Nagasaki bombings.
* **Virtue Ethics (Aristotle) → Character-Based Determinants:** Ethics is about developing good character traits. Determinants: Role models, personal virtues, ethical leadership. Example: APJ Abdul Kalam's life principles—humility, integrity, and dedication to science.
* **Social Contract Theory → Ethics in Governance & Laws:** Ethics is a contract between individuals and the state to maintain social order. Determinants: Constitution, legal system, governance structures. Example: Indian Constitution as a moral guide—secularism, equality, justice.

**Factors Shaping Ethical Behavior:**

* **Individual Factors**
* Values & Conscience: Inner moral compass (e.g., Raja Harishchandra's truthfulness).
* Emotions & Desires: Ethical dilemmas arise when emotions overpower rationality (e.g., Karna's loyalty to Duryodhana despite knowing the truth).
* Education & Upbringing: Moral teachings shape ethical thinking (e.g., Guru Dronacharya's teachings on duty and loyalty).


* **Social & Cultural Factors**
* Family & Society: Family values instill moral integrity (e.g., Ramayana's emphasis on familial duty and truthfulness).
* Religious Teachings: Dharma and Karma guide moral actions (e.g., Jainism's Anekantavada—multiple perspectives in ethical decision-making).
* Historical Legacy: National movements uphold ethical values (e.g., Gandhian ethics of truth and non-violence).


* **Legal & Institutional Factors**
* Constitution & Laws: Fundamental rights and duties provide ethical guidelines (e.g., Article 51A - Fundamental Duties).
* Code of Conduct: Ethical frameworks for governance (e.g., Civil Service Conduct Rules).
* Judiciary & Institutions: Ethical governance through legal enforcement (e.g., Lokpal for anti-corruption).

## Consequences of Ethics in Human Actions

Ethical or unethical human actions shape individuals, societies, institutions, and global governance. Their consequences can be positive or negative, influencing trust, justice, economy, environment, and overall well-being.

1. **Trust & Social Cohesion:** Ethical behavior fosters trust among individuals, institutions, and governance. Example: Mahatma Gandhi's adherence to truth and non-violence helped unite India's freedom movement.
2. **Justice & Fairness:** Ensures equal treatment, protection of rights, and rule of law. Example: Supreme Court's verdict on LGBTQ+ rights (Section 377 case) ensured justice and equality.
3. **Economic Growth & Ethical Business:** Ethical businesses build credibility, long-term success, and consumer trust. Example: Infosys' transparent corporate governance established it as a global IT leader.
4. **Good Governance & Accountability:** Ethical leadership ensures transparency, accountability, and corruption-free administration. Fast and fair decision making. Example: T.N. Seshan's electoral reforms enhanced democracy's integrity.
5. **Social Welfare & Inclusion:** Ethical policies empower marginalized communities and ensure social justice. Example: B.R. Ambedkar's advocacy for Dalit rights led to reservation policies.
6. **Environmental Sustainability:** Ethical environmental policies protect future generations from ecological destruction. Example: Chipko Movement (1973) resisted deforestation and promoted forest conservation.
7. **International Reputation & Diplomacy:** Ethical foreign policies enhance a nation's global standing. Example: India's commitment to climate goals (Paris Agreement) improved its diplomatic credibility.

* **Positive Consequences of Ethical Actions:** Empowerment, Trust among Individuals, Institutional Transparency, Corruption-free administration, Equity, Future generation, Equal treatment, Protection of rights, Credibility, Long term success, Global standing, Diplomatic credibility.

---

## Consequence of Loss of Ethics

1. **Corruption & Inequality:** Ethical failures widen wealth gaps, weaken institutions, and fuel injustice. Example: Commonwealth Games Scam (2010) led to financial loss and public distrust.
2. **Loss of Public Trust & Credibility:** Unethical actions erode faith in leadership, businesses, and public institutions. Example: Satyam Scam (2009) shattered investor confidence in India's IT sector.
3. **Governance Failure & Social Unrest:** Corrupt governance leads to administrative failure, protests, and loss of legitimacy. Example: Venezuela's crisis due to corruption and mismanagement triggered economic collapse.
4. **Environmental Degradation:** Unethical corporate practices harm natural ecosystems and human health. Example: Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984) caused massive environmental and human losses due to corporate negligence.
5. **Violation of Human Rights:** Unethical governance suppresses freedoms and leads to discrimination. Example: Apartheid in South Africa institutionalized racial discrimination.
6. **Erosion of Democratic Values:** Unethical politics, electoral fraud, and misinformation weaken democratic institutions. Example: Watergate Scandal (1972, USA) led to President Nixon's resignation.
7. **Rise in Extremism & Violence:** Distorted ethical beliefs fuel terrorism, radicalism, and communal violence. Example: ISIS and radical extremism justify violence under false moral claims.

## Ethics in Private & Public Relationships

Ethics plays a vital role in both private (personal) relationships and public (professional, social) relationships. Ethical conduct in these domains ensures trust, integrity, and harmony in society.

**1. Ethics in Private Relationships**
Private relationships include family, friends, and personal interactions. Ethical behavior in personal life helps in building strong, meaningful connections.

**Key Ethical Principles in Private Life:**

* Honesty & Trustworthiness → Lying erodes relationships.
* Compassion & Empathy → Caring for family members, friends, and neighbors.
* Respect & Fairness → Treating people with dignity, irrespective of differences.
* Responsibility & Commitment → Fulfilling personal obligations (e.g., parental responsibility).

**Examples of Ethics in Private Relationships:**

* Shravan Kumar (Ramayana) - A son's ethical duty to care for his aging parents.
* Mahatma Gandhi's truthfulness - Practiced honesty in both personal and public life.
* Swami Vivekananda's principle of service - Advocated for selfless service in personal relationships.

**2. Ethics in Public Relationships**
Public relationships include interactions with society, government, and institutions. Ethical conduct in public life ensures justice, accountability, and good governance.

**Key Ethical Principles in Public Life:**

* Integrity & Transparency → Avoiding corruption and favoritism.
* Accountability & Responsibility → Answerable for public decisions.
* Justice & Fair Treatment → Ensuring fairness in governance and policy-making.
* Service Orientation → Putting the public's welfare first (NOLAN committee - SAHILOO principles).
  * selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, and leadership

**Examples of Ethics in Public Relationships:**

* Lal Bahadur Shastri's honesty - Paid for his family's train ticket despite being the Prime Minister.
* T.N. Seshan's electoral reforms - Strengthened India's democracy by enforcing ethical election practices.
* Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam's leadership - Maintained transparency and ethics in governance.

**3. Conflict Between Ethics in Private & Public Life**
Sometimes, private and public ethics come into conflict, creating moral dilemmas.

**Example 1: A Judge Sentencing a Relative**

* Private Ethics: Personal emotions may favor leniency.
* Public Ethics: A judge must remain impartial and follow the law.
* Resolution: Ethical integrity demands unbiased justice.

**Example 2: A Bureaucrat Facing Nepotism Pressure**

* Private Ethics: Family pressure to help a relative get a job.
* Public Ethics: Hiring must be based on merit.
* Resolution: Ethical governance prioritizes fairness and meritocracy.
