### Part 1: The Explanation (What, How & Why)

#### 1. What Is It? (Glacier Mass Balance & Movement Physics)

* **Glacier Mass Balance:** A glacier is an open dynamic system driven by the balance between two zones separated by the **Equilibrium Line Altitude (ELA)**:
* **Zone of Accumulation:** Upper reaches where annual snowfall exceeds summer melting.
* **Zone of Ablation:** Lower reaches where melting, sublimation, and calving exceed annual accumulation.
* *Mass Budget:* If Accumulation > Ablation, glacier advances; if Ablation > Accumulation, glacier terminus retreats.


* **The Movement Engine (How Ice Moves):**
* **1. Internal Deformation (Glen's Flow Law / Plastic Creep):**
* The upper $\sim 50\text{ meters}$ of a glacier experiences low confining pressure; it behaves as a **brittle solid**, fracturing under tension into deep chasms called **Crevasses**.
* Below $50\text{ meters}$, extreme lithostatic overburden pressure forces ice to behave as a **ductile plastic**. Individual ice crystals deform, rotate, and slip along internal molecular cleavage planes without breaking.


* **2. Basal Sliding & Regelation:**
* In "warm" (temperate) glaciers, pressure at the glacier bed lowers the melting point of ice.
* Meltwater acts as a hydraulic lubricant, allowing the entire ice mass to slide bodily over bedrock.
* *The Regelation Process:* When basal ice encounters a bedrock obstacle, pressure surges on the up-glacier side, melting the ice. The water flows around the obstacle to the low-pressure down-glacier side, where it **instantly refreezes**, expanding into rock joints and plucking blocks away.





```
                 45-SECOND EXAM SCHEMATIC: BASAL REGELATION & PLUCKING
  
         GLACIER ICE MOTION ════════════════════════════════════════════►
         ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
         HIGH PRESSURE SIDE                    LOW PRESSURE SIDE
         • Overburden pressure surges          • Pressure drops
         • Ice MELTS at lower melting point    • Water REFREEZES (Regelation)
         • Meltwater lubricates sliding        • Expands in joints & plucks rock
                      ╲                              ╱

                                  ╰────────╯
```

#### 2. Alpine Glacial Erosional Spectrum (The Carving Engine)

Erosion operates through two complementary mechanical processes:

* **Plucking (Quarrying):** Meltwater penetrates jointed bedrock, freezes, expands, and tears shattered blocks into the moving glacier base.
* **Abrasion:** Rock fragments embedded in the glacier's sole scrape against the bedrock like coarse sandpaper, gouging linear **Striations** and pulverizing rock into microscopic **Rock Flour**.

```
                           ALPINE EROSIONAL LANDFORMS
                                       │
     ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
[HEADWALL BASINS]               [RESIDUAL SHARP PEAKS]          [TROUGHS & VALLEYS]
• Cirque / Corrie / Cwm         • Arête (Serrated knife-edge)   • U-Shaped Glacial Trough
• Bergschrund Crevasse          • Horn (Pyramidal Peak)         • Hanging Valley (Waterfalls)
• Tarn Lake (Rock basin)        • Col (Pass / Gap)              • Truncated Spurs

```

* **1. Cirque (Corrie / Cwm):**
* A steep-sided, amphitheater- or armchair-shaped depression carved into high mountain heads.
* *Formation:* Snow accumulates in a pre-existing hollow. Rotational slip and freeze-thaw plucking at the **Bergschrund** (the permanent deep crevasse opening between the moving glacier ice and the static rock headwall) quarry the back wall nearly vertical.
* When ice melts, a circular rock-basin lake remains: a **Tarn Lake**.


* **2. Arêtes, Horns & Cols:**
* **Arête:** A knife-edged, razor-sharp rocky ridge formed when two opposing cirques erode headward toward one another from opposite sides of a divide.
* **Horn (Pyramidal Peak):** A sharp, steep-sided, angular mountain peak created when **three or more cirques** erode back-to-back toward a central point (e.g., the **Matterhorn** in the Alps; **Mount Shivling** in the Garhwal Himalayas).
* **Col:** A low saddle or mountain pass formed when opposing cirques breach and notch an arête.


* **3. U-Shaped Glacial Troughs & Truncated Spurs:**
* Pre-existing river valleys have a narrow V-shape. When a thick valley glacier fills the gorge, its tremendous mass widens, deepens, and straightens the valley into a broad, flat-bottomed **U-Shaped Trough** bounded by near-vertical rock walls.
* **Truncated Spurs:** Triangular cliff faces formed when the advancing, rigid glacier shears cleanly through the interlocking ridge spurs of the original river valley.


* **4. Hanging Valleys:**
* Tributary glaciers contain far less ice volume than the trunk glacier, eroding their beds much less deeply.
* *Result:* When the ice recedes, the tributary valley floor is left suspended high above the floor of the main glacial trough. Streams exiting these valleys plunge as dramatic **waterfalls** (e.g., Bridalveil Fall in Yosemite Valley, USA; Rohtang Pass cascades, India).


* **5. Roche Moutonnée vs. Crag-and-Tail:**
* **Roche Moutonnée:** An asymmetrical bedrock bump shaped by moving ice:
* *Stoss Side (Up-ice):* Gently sloping, polished, and striated by smooth **abrasion**.
* *Lee Side (Down-ice):* Steep, rough, and jagged, carved by freeze-thaw **regelation plucking**.


* **Crag-and-Tail:** A resistant plug of hard rock (Crag) protects a tapering ridge of softer rock or till (Tail) on its sheltered, down-ice lee side (e.g., Edinburgh Castle rock).

#### 3. Depositional Landforms: Glacial Till vs. Glaciofluvial Outwash

* **Glacial Till (Moraine Debris):** Directly dropped by melting ice; completely **unsorted, unstratified**, containing grain sizes ranging from microscopic clay to car-sized boulders (**Glacial Erratics**).
* **Glaciofluvial Outwash:** Deposited by flowing meltwater streams; **sorted and stratified** into distinct layers by particle size.

```
                  45-SECOND EXAM SCHEMATIC: DEPOSITIONAL TOPOGRAPHY
  
                               ICE FLOW DIRECTION ════►
    TERMINAL MORAINE                      DRUMLIN                   ESKER
    (Till ridge at maximum reach)         (Compacted Till mound)    (Meltwater river gravels)
           ┌───────┐                          Blunt      Tapering        ╭───╮
          /         \                         Stoss       Lee           /  ~  \ (Sinuous
    ─────┴───────────┴───────────────────────╭───────────╮─────────────┴───────┴──  Ridge)
    OUTWASH PLAIN (Sandur)                   │  UP-ICE   │  DOWN-ICE
    Braided meltwater channels               ╰───────────╯

```

1. **Moraines (The Till Ridges):**
* *Lateral Moraine:* Ridges of rock debris deposited along the lateral margins of a glacier valley.
* *Medial Moraine:* Formed when two tributary glaciers merge; their adjacent lateral moraines combine into a central dark stripe of debris running down the trunk glacier.
* *Terminal / End Moraine:* A crescent-shaped ridge marking the furthest down-valley point the glacier terminus reached before retreating.
* *Recessional Moraine:* Successive ridges left behind as a glacier pauses during retreat.


2. **Drumlins ("Basket-of-Eggs" Topography):**
* Smooth, elongated, teardrop-shaped hills composed of compacted glacial till.
* *Orientation:* Aligned parallel to ice flow.
* *Asymmetry (Direct inverse of Roche Moutonnée):* The **blunt, steep end points up-ice (stoss)**, while the **gently tapering, streamlined tail points down-ice (lee)**.


3. **Eskers:**
* Long, narrow, sinuous, snake-like ridges ($5–50\text{ km}$ long) consisting of sorted glaciofluvial gravels and sand.
* *Mechanism:* Deposited in subglacial tunnels by high-pressure meltwater rivers flowing underneath stagnant, melting ice sheets.


4. **Kames & Kettle Holes:**
* **Kame:** A steep-sided, conical mound of stratified sand and gravel deposited in a crevasse or meltwater delta on the glacier surface, subsequently dropped to the ground as ice melted.
* **Kettle Hole:** A depression formed when a detached, stagnant block of ice is buried in sediment. When the ice block melts, the overlying gravel collapses, leaving a water-filled pit (**Kettle Lake**).

### Part 2: Real-World Impacts & Footprints (Fjords, Permafrost & Hazards)

#### 1. Fjords: Mechanics & The Fishery Anomaly `[Direct UPSC 2023 Anchor]`

A **Fjord** is a deep, narrow, steep-sided coastal inlet formed by the drowning of a glacially carved U-shaped valley by post-glacial eustatic sea-level rise.

```
                 45-SECOND EXAM SCHEMATIC: CROSS-SECTION OF A FJORD
  
       LANDWARD INNER BASIN                             SEAWARD MOUTH
      (Extremely Deep: >1,000 m)                       (Shallow Threshold)
  
       Glacial Precipices                              OCEAN
       │                                                 │
       ▼   Fjord Surface Water (Brackish / Oxygenated)   ▼
    ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
    STAGNANT DEEP WATER                           THE TERMINAL SILL
    • Heavy, saline, isolated                     • Shallow bedrock lip
    • Extreme HYPOXIA / ANOXIA                    • Blocks oceanic deep exchange
    • Decomposing organic matter                  • Prevents nutrient upwelling
    ─────────────────────────────────\           ┌─────────┐
    DEEP ANOXIC BASIN FLOOR           \          │ BEDROCK │
    (No rich pelagic fisheries!)       \─────────┴─────────┴───────────────

```

* **The Formation Cycle:**
1. *Glacial Deepening:* During the Pleistocene ice ages, massive coastal valley glaciers carved deep troughs below sea level (ice is dense enough to erode rock down to $\sim 90\%$ of its floating thickness).
2. *Post-Glacial Drowning:* As ice sheets melted ($\sim 18,000\text{ BP}$), global eustatic sea level surged by over $120\text{ meters}$, flooding the steep-walled troughs.


* **The Structural Barrier: The Terminal Sill (Threshold):**
* At the mouth where the fjord meets the open ocean, the glacier lost its erosive power due to buoyancy/melting, depositing its terminal moraine on a shallow rock lip: the **Sill**.
* Fjord inner basins are extraordinarily deep ($>1,000\text{ meters}$, e.g., Sognefjord in Norway), but their seaward sills are often only **$50–100\text{ meters}$ deep**.


* **Why Do Fjords NOT Constitute Rich Fishing Grounds? `[The Explanatory Key]**`
1. **Bathymetric Stagnation (The Sill Barrier):** The shallow sill acts as an underwater wall, blocking dense, oxygen-rich open-ocean deep currents from penetrating into the deep inner basin.
2. **Severe Water Stratification:** Meltwater from surrounding alpine peaks caps the fjord with a light, buoyant layer of fresh/brackish water, preventing wind-driven vertical overturning of the water column.
3. **Deep Basin Hypoxia / Anoxia:** Organic matter falls to the floor and decomposes, stripping dissolved oxygen. Deep fjord waters become oxygen-starved (**hypoxic or anoxic**) and accumulate toxic hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$), making the depths biologically sterile for major pelagic marine life.
4. **Absence of Broad Continental Shelves:** Fjords have near-vertical walls plunging directly into deep water; they lack the wide, sunlit, shallow continental shelf zones (like the Grand Banks or Dogger Bank) essential for phytoplankton proliferation and commercial bottom trawling. *(Note: Commercial aquaculture/salmon farming thrives in protected surface waters, but natural pelagic marine fisheries are absent).*

#### 2. Periglacial Geomorphology & Permafrost Degradation

Periglacial regions are cold-climate areas subject to intense **frost action and permafrost**, without being permanently covered by glacial ice.

```
                      PERMAFROST VERTICAL PROFILE
  
    GROUND SURFACE
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    ACTIVE LAYER (0.5–2 m): Thaws in summer, freezes in winter.
    ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ◄── PERMAFROST TABLE
    CONTINUOUS PERMAFROST
    (Soil/Rock remaining at or below 0°C for >= 2 consecutive years)
    
    [ TALIK ]: Unfrozen pockets of ground insulated within permafrost.
  
    ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ◄── GEOTHERMAL GRADIENT BASE
    UNFROZEN GROUND (Warmed by Earth's internal heat)

```

* **1. Cryoturbation & Patterned Ground:**
* Repeated freeze-thaw cycles in the active layer cause **frost heaving** (vertical lifting) and **frost thrusting** (lateral pushing).
* Coarse stones are pushed outward toward fissure cracks, sorting sediment into striking geometric forms: **Stone Circles, Polygons, and Nets** on flat ground, and **Stone Stripes** on slopes $>5^\circ$.


* **2. Pingos (Ice-Cored Mounds):**
* Large, conical, ice-cored earth hills ($up\text{ to }70\text{ m}$ high).
* **Closed-System (Mackenzie type):** Hydrostatic pressure develops when a lake in a permafrost basin drains. The exposed saturated talik freezes inward; expanding ice forces the overlying ground upward into a dome.
* **Open-System (East Greenland type):** Hydraulic artesian groundwater flows upward through permafrost breaks from higher ground, freezing near the surface into an expanding ice core.


* **3. Thermokarst & The Runaway Methane Feedback `[GS-I / GS-III Interface]`:**
* **Thermokarst:** Irregular, hummocky subsidence landscapes created when permafrost melts, causing ground collapse, thaw slumps, and retrogressive mudslides.
* **"Drunken Forests":** Soil liquefaction in melting active layers destabilizes root anchors, tilting trees in random directions.
* **The Planetary Tipping Point:** Permafrost holds an estimated **$1,400–1,600\text{ billion metric tons}$ of organic carbon** (double the carbon currently in Earth's atmosphere). Microbial decay of thawed organic matter in anoxic meltwater lakes releases potent **Methane ($\text{CH}_4$)**, creating an irreversible, amplifying climate feedback.

#### 3. Indian Subcontinental Anchors: Himalayan GLOF Cascades

The Third Pole (Himalayan-Tibetan cryosphere) is experiencing accelerated deglaciation, creating hazardous pro-glacial and moraine-dammed lakes.

```
                      THE HIMALAYAN GLOF DISASTER CASCADE
  
    [ Climate Warming ] ──► [ Accelerated Glacial Retreat ]
                                        │
                                        ▼ Meltwater collects behind weak debris
                            [ Unstable Moraine-Dammed Lake ]
                                        │
                                        ▼ Rock/Ice Avalanche into lake OR Intense Rain
                            [ Displacement Surge Wave Overtopping Dam ]
                                        │
                                        ▼ Mechanical scouring breaches moraine
                            [ CATASTROPHIC GLOF DISASTER CASCADING DOWNSTREAM ]

```

* **1. The GLOF Mechanism:**
* As glaciers retreat up steep valleys, meltwater accumulates behind loose, unconsolidated terminal moraines, forming high-altitude lakes.
* Moraine dams lack cementation; they consist of loose boulders and ice cores.
* An ice avalanche, slope failure, or heavy rainfall creates a catastrophic displacement wave that overtops the moraine. The overflowing water cuts a trench through the dam, triggering a runaway collapse that releases millions of cubic meters of water in minutes.


* **2. The South Lhonak Lake GLOF (Sikkim, October 2023):**
* *The Setting:* South Lhonak Lake (elevation $\sim 5,200\text{ m}$) had grown dramatically over decades due to the retreat of the South Lhonak Glacier.
* *The Disaster:* Heavy rainfall and an ice/rock detachment triggered a moraine breach. Over $60\%$ of the lake's water drained within hours.
* *The Cascade:* The flash surge rushed down the Teesta River, destroying the **Chungthang Hydroelectric Dam (Teesta III, 1,200 MW)**, washing away roads, and causing extensive downstream casualties.


* **3. The Black Carbon Feedback:**
* Biomass burning, brick kilns, and diesel transport in the Indo-Gangetic Plains transport **black carbon (soot)** up the southern slopes of the Himalayas.
* Soot deposits on clean snow, lowering its **albedo** (reflectivity) from $0.85$ to $<0.50$. The darkened snow absorbs solar radiation, accelerating melt rates independent of ambient air temperatures.
