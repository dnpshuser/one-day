### Corals
1. Intro 
Coral reefs are among the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth, formed by coral polyps that secrete calcium carbonate skeletons. They support fisheries, coastal protection, and marine biodiversity, especially in tropical seas.

2. Factors of formation
- Warm, shallow, clear, and sunlit tropical waters are ideal for coral growth.
- Coral polyps depend on symbiotic algae called zooxanthellae for energy through photosynthesis.
- Stable salinity and low turbidity are essential for reef development.

3. impact of global warming
- Rising sea temperatures cause coral bleaching, where corals expel their symbiotic algae and lose their colour and primary food source.
- Ocean acidification reduces carbonate availability, slowing coral calcification.
- Increased storm intensity and pollution further weaken reef resilience.
- Source: IPCC, UNEP, IUCN.

4. Current status
- Coral reefs are declining globally due to climate change, overfishing, runoff, and destructive fishing practices.
- The Great Barrier Reef and many tropical reefs have experienced repeated bleaching events in recent years.
- Reefs are critical for coastal resilience but are increasingly vulnerable.

5. Conservation measures
- Establish marine protected areas and no-take zones.
- Reduce land-based pollution and improve coastal management.
- Promote reef restoration, coral nurseries, and climate-resilient reef management.
- Source: UNEP, IUCN, national marine agencies.

6. conclusion
Coral reefs are ecological treasure houses that require urgent global action, because their loss threatens biodiversity, fisheries, and coastal protection. Source: IUCN, IPCC.
