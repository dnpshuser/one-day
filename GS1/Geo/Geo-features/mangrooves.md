### Mangrooves
1. Intro
Mangrove ecosystems are coastal forests found in intertidal zones of tropical and subtropical regions. They are highly productive and act as a bridge between marine and terrestrial environments.

2. Significance
    1. significance in maintaining coastal ecology
    - Mangroves protect shorelines from erosion and storm surges.
    - They provide nursery grounds for fish and crustaceans and sustain biodiversity.
    - Their root systems filter pollutants and trap sediments, improving coastal water quality.
    2. other significance
    - They support local livelihoods through fishing, honey collection, and eco-tourism.
    - Mangroves are important carbon sinks and help mitigate climate change through blue carbon storage.

3. Threat or Causes of depletion
- Coastal reclamation, urban expansion, aquaculture, and port development destroy mangrove areas.
- Pollution, sedimentation, and upstream changes in freshwater flow weaken mangrove ecosystems.
- Climate change, sea-level rise, and extreme weather events intensify stress.

4. Current status
- India has significant mangrove cover along the east coast, Sundarbans, Gujarat, and Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
- However, many areas continue to experience degradation and fragmentation.
- Source: MoEFCC, Forest Survey of India.

5. Protection measures
- Restore degraded mangrove forests through community participation and coastal zone management.
- Restrict destructive coastal development and promote sustainable aquaculture practices.
- Integrate mangrove conservation into climate adaptation and disaster resilience planning.

6. Conclusion
Mangroves are nature’s coastal shield and climate buffer; their conservation is essential for resilient coasts, fisheries, and biodiversity. Source: MoEFCC, FSI.
