### Cryosphere
1. Intro
The cryosphere includes frozen components of the Earth system such as glaciers, sea ice, snow cover, permafrost, and ice sheets. It plays a major role in regulating climate, sea level, and regional hydrology.

2. Concepts
- Glaciers and ice sheets store a large share of the Earth’s freshwater.
- Snow and ice reflect solar radiation, helping regulate global temperatures.
- Melting of the cryosphere contributes to rising sea levels and alters ocean circulation.

3. Current status
- Arctic sea ice extent has decreased significantly in recent decades, and many glaciers are losing mass.
- Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets are contributing more to sea-level rise than before.
- Source: IPCC AR6, WMO, NASA.

4. Arctic/Antarctic ice melting
   1. Causes
   - Rising greenhouse gas concentrations warm the atmosphere and oceans.
   - Positive feedbacks such as reduced albedo accelerate melt.
   - Changes in ocean circulation and atmospheric patterns intensify regional warming.
   2. Effects and impacts
      1. Weather pattern
      - Altered jet streams and atmospheric circulation can influence mid-latitude weather.
      - Increased melting can disrupt the monsoon and regional climate patterns.
      2. Human activities
      - Coastal communities face flood and erosion risk.
      - Fisheries, shipping routes, and indigenous livelihoods are affected by changing ice conditions.
   3. Mitigation measures
   - Reduce greenhouse gas emissions and transition to clean energy.
   - Strengthen climate adaptation, early warning, and ecosystem protection.
   - Monitor cryosphere change using satellites and field observation systems.
