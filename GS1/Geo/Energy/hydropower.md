1. Intro
	- Hydropower converts the potential and kinetic energy of flowing or stored water into electricity and remains India's largest source of dispatchable renewable power.
2. India's progress till date: data
	- India has about 47 GW of large hydropower capacity and about 5 GW of small hydropower capacity - National Power Portal
	- Hydropower contributes roughly 9.43% of installed electricity capacity, 
	- generation share varies with monsoon, reservoir levels and competing water demands.
3. Major plant distribution in India
	1. **Himalayan region:** HP, Uk, J&K, Sikkim
	2. **North-East:** ArP
	3. **Western Ghats:** Mh, Kar, Kerala
	4. **Central and southern India:** Reservoir-based and multipurpose projects occur on the Narmada, Godavari, Krishna and Mahanadi systems.
4. Ongoing plans-targets
	- pumped-storage plants for balancing solar and wind, 
	- modernising old stations,
	- strengthening inter-state transmission.
	- Pumped storage acts like a water battery by storing surplus electricity and releasing it during peak demand.
1. Benefits
	1. Renewable, flexible and capable of rapid peaking support.
	2. Supports irrigation, drinking water, flood moderation, inland fisheries and regional development.
	3. Low operating emissions 
	4. long plant life after construction.
	5. fossil fuels dependence.
2. Challenges
	1. disruption of river ecology.
	2. Submergence, displacement, forest loss
	3. Sedimentation reduces reservoir capacity and turbine life.
	4. Landslides, glacial lake outburst floods and earthquakes increase Himalayan risks.
	5. Long gestation, cost overruns, inter-state disputes and downstream flow concerns.
	6. Climate change makes river flows and project economics more uncertain.
3. Key govt initiatives
	- National Hydroelectric Power Corporation and state utilities; 
	- National Mission for Clean Ganga; 
	- revised dam-safety framework under the Dam Safety Act, 2021; 
	- policy support for pumped-storage projects and hydropower in the renewable-energy portfolio.
4. Conclusion
	- Hydroelectricity remains critical for grid decarbonization, provided its expansion ensures fragile riverine ecological safeguards to help achieve 
  	- SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy),
  	- India's 500 GW Non fossil capacity and
  	- 50% Renewable Energy Panchamrit Targets by 2030.
