1. Intro
  - Shale gas is natural gas trapped in fine-grained shale rocks, recoverable through horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing, and has reshaped global energy geography.
2. India
  1. Distribution
    1. Prospective basins include Cambay, Assam-Arakan, Krishna-Godavari, Cauvery, Damodar Valley, Pranhita-Godavari and Vindhyan basins.
    2. Exploration is concentrated where shale thickness, organic content, maturity and existing oil-gas infrastructure are favourable.
  2. Potential
    - Shale gas can supplement conventional gas, reduce LNG imports, support fertiliser and city-gas sectors, and provide a transition fuel if methane leakage is controlled.
  3. Challenges
    1. Limited geological data and high exploration risk.
    2. Water-intensive fracturing, difficult land acquisition and inadequate pipelines.
    3. Low domestic service capacity, complex clearances and uncertain commercial viability.
    4. Competition from imported LNG and renewable energy.
  4. Environmental impact
    1. Groundwater contamination risk from poor well integrity and waste-fluid management.
    2. High water consumption and possible induced seismicity.
    3. Methane leakage can offset climate benefits; land fragmentation, noise and air pollution affect nearby communities.
3. World
  1. Distribution
    1. United States: Appalachian, Permian, Haynesville and Bakken regions.
    2. Canada: Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin; China: Sichuan and other basins; Argentina: Vaca Muerta; Australia and parts of Europe also have prospects.
  2. Potential
    - The United States demonstrated that shale technology can rapidly increase gas supply, lower prices and support a gas-based industrial transition.
  3. Challenges
    1. Declining output from individual wells requires continuous drilling.
    2. Price volatility, infrastructure needs, water stress and social opposition affect projects.
    3. Methane leakage and long-term dependence on gas conflict with deep decarbonisation.
  4. Environmental Impact
    1. Contamination from surface spills or faulty casing, fugitive methane, flaring and induced earthquakes are major concerns.
    2. Strong well standards, baseline groundwater data, chemical disclosure, wastewater treatment and independent monitoring are essential.
4. Conclusion
  - Shale gas may serve as a carefully regulated transition resource, but India's durable energy security lies in efficiency, renewables, storage and low-carbon gases rather than unrestricted fracking.
