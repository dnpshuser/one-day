1. Intro
  - Petroleum reserves are unevenly distributed geological stores of a finite fuel that have shaped industrial growth, geopolitics and India's continuing import vulnerability.
2. On-shore
  1. Geographical distribution - India
    1. Assam-Arakan Basin: Digboi, Naharkatiya and Moran.
    2. Gujarat-Cambay Basin: Ankleshwar, Kalol and Mehsana.
    3. Mumbai High is offshore, while Rajasthan's Barmer basin is an important onshore addition.
    4. Implication: Onshore fields support regional industry and are relatively accessible, but many are mature, small or geographically concentrated.
  2. Geographical distribution - World
    1. Major onshore provinces include the Middle East, West Siberia, Volga-Urals, Caspian region, North America and Venezuela.
    2. Implication: Concentration in politically sensitive regions makes oil prices and supply vulnerable to conflict, sanctions and transport chokepoints.
3. Off-shore
  1. Geographical distribution - India
    1. Western offshore: Mumbai High and neighbouring fields in the Arabian Sea remain India's leading offshore oil province.
    2. Eastern offshore: Krishna-Godavari and Cauvery basins have oil and gas prospects in the Bay of Bengal.
    3. Implication: Offshore resources can reduce import dependence but require expensive technology, deep-water capability and strong environmental safeguards.
  2. Geographical distribution - World
    1. Major offshore provinces include the Persian Gulf, Gulf of Mexico, North Sea, offshore Brazil, West Africa and the South China Sea.
    2. Implication: Offshore fields diversify supply but expose countries to hurricanes, spills, maritime disputes and costly infrastructure.
4. Arctic Sea oil discovery
  1. Significance
    1. The Arctic is estimated to contain a substantial share of undiscovered global oil and gas resources, though estimates vary.
    2. Melting sea ice may improve seasonal access and intensify interest in the Northern Sea Route.
    3. Production could diversify supplies and generate strategic influence for Arctic states.
  2. Impact on environment
    1. Fragile ecosystems recover slowly from oil spills because of cold, darkness and limited response infrastructure.
    2. Exploration disturbs marine mammals and indigenous livelihoods.
    3. New fossil-fuel extraction can lock in emissions, while declining ice cover creates a feedback loop that accelerates warming.
    4. Therefore, resource potential must be weighed against climate commitments, ecological risk and indigenous rights.
5. Conclusion
  - India's prudent path is to use domestic reserves as a transition bridge while accelerating efficiency, strategic reserves, clean transport and renewable alternatives.
