1. Intro
	- Nuclear energy stands as the ultimate baseload bridge to reconcile rapid industrial growth with global Net Zero commitments and SDG 7 (Clean Energy).
2. Evolution (step by step point wise): [ref: plot]
3. India's progress till date: data - current capacity
	- 8780 MW capacity - npp.gov.in 
	- 24 reactors - 7 states
4. India's target
	- Short term target 22.5 GW by 2031-32
	- long-term goal of 100 GW by 2047, 
	- net-zero emissions by 2070.
5. Major plant distribution
	1. **Tarapur, Maharashtra:** India's first commercial nuclear power station.
	2. **Rawatbhata, Rajasthan** 
	3. Kakrapar, Gujarat; 
	4. Narora, Uttar Pradesh; 
	5. Kaiga, Karnataka: 
	6. **Kudankulam, Tamil Nadu:** VVER-based reactors developed with Russian cooperation.
	7. **Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu:** Fast breeder and advanced nuclear research centre.
	8. *Proposed*: **Jaitapur, Maharashtra and Kovvada, Andhra Pradesh:**
6. Benefits
	1. High energy density, reliable baseload power with low carbon footprint.
	2. Small land footprint compared with equivalent fossil or renewable generation.
	3. Supports industrialisation, 
	4. medical isotopes, 
	5. food irradiation, 
	6. agriculture and strategic autonomy.
	7. Reduces: coal use and air pollution
7. Availability of raw materials
	- Uranium 
  	- Jharkhand, 
  	- Andhra Pradesh, 
  	- Telangana, 
  	- Rajasthan 
  	- Meghalaya; 
  	- mining and processing are undertaken through UCIL(Uranium Corporation of India).
	- Large thorium resources in *monazite*-bearing beach sands of 
  	- Kerala, 
  	- Tamil Nadu, 
  	- Andhra Pradesh and 
  	- Odisha, 
  	- but commercial thorium utilisation requires the successful completion of the three-stage programme.
8. Challenges
	1. High capital cost, 
	2. long construction periods 
	3. project delays.
	4. Safety, 
	5. radioactive-waste management
	6. emergency preparedness concerns.
	7. Land acquisition, 
	8. public acceptance
	9. coastal vulnerability at some sites.
	10. Fuel constraints, 
	11. technology access and 
	12. liability concerns
	13. Not In My Backyard problem
	14. Nuclear plants require substantial cooling water and careful thermal-discharge management.
9. Key govt initiatives
	- Department of Atomic Energy and NPCIL reactor programme; 
	- Bharatiya Nabhikiya Vidyut Nigam for fast breeder technology; 
	- the Atomic Energy Act, 1962; 
	- liability framework under the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010; 
	- policy for greater private participation and small modular reactor research.
10. Conclusion
	- nuclear energy is pivotal for realizing 
  	- SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy), 
  	- driving SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), and 
  	- advancing decarbonization mandate of SDG 13 (Climate Action) 
    - providing pathway to reliable net-zero emissions by 2070.
