1. Intro
	- Converting inexhaustible atmospheric kinetics into power, wind energy serves as a parallel pillar for low-carbon baseloads and global energy sovereignty.
2. India's progress till date: data
	- 58.13 GW wind energy potential
3. India's targets and commitments(if any)
	1. Wind contributes to the national goal of 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030.
	2. The government is promoting offshore wind, with an assessed potential of about 70 GW along the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coasts.
	3. Net-zero emissions by 2070 requires faster wind deployment alongside storage, transmission and flexible demand.
4. Distribution of wind energy plants across India
	1. **Tamil Nadu:** High capacity due to the Palghat Gap and coastal winds; major sites include Muppandal.
	2. **Gujarat:** Strong coastal and Kutch potential, with scope for wind-solar hybrid and offshore projects.
	3. **Karnataka:** Deccan plateau, Western Ghats corridors and industrial demands support large installations.
	4. **Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh:** 
	5. Offshore potential is concentrated in the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coastal zones because of favourable wind speeds and shallow continental-shelf conditions.
5. Benefits
	1. Renewable, 
	2. fuel-free electricity
	3. low operational emissions and water use.
	4. Complements solar, as wind generation may be stronger at night or during monsoon months.
	5. Creates manufacturing, construction, maintenance and rural lease-income opportunities.
	6. Wind-solar hybrids improve land and transmission utilisation.
6. Challenges
	1. wind dependent
	2. Fetal strikes to birds often looking for their preys fail to notice wind blades. eg: golden eagle, white tailed eagle
	3. storage and transmission infractures needs to be strenghtened
	4. Land acquisition
	5. and right-of-way disputes, bird and bat mortality, noise and visual impacts require careful siting.
	6. Delays in land, grid and transmission approvals increase project costs.
	7. Offshore projects face high capital costs, cyclones, corrosion, port limitations and difficult maintenance.
7. Solutions: Domestic manufacturing must manage dependence on rare-earth materials and improve turbine recycling.
8. Key govt initiatives
	- National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy, 2018; 
	- National Offshore Wind Energy Policy, 2015; 
	- Green Energy Corridors; 
	- competitive bidding through SECI; 
	- repowering policy; 
	- transmission planning for renewable-energy zones.
9. Conclusion
	- Scaling offshore potential and repowering legacy turbines remain pivotal to anchoring wind energy within India's hybrid renewable Net-Zero roadmap."
