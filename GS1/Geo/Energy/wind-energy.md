1. Intro
	- Wind energy harnesses the kinetic energy of moving air and offers India a mature, low-carbon source that complements solar generation, especially during different seasons and hours.
2. India's progress till date: data
	- India has about 51 GW of installed onshore wind capacity and ranks among the world's leading wind-energy markets.
	- The strongest growth has occurred in utility-scale projects, while repowering old low-capacity turbines offers additional potential without proportionate land use.
3. India's targets and commitments(if any)
	1. Wind contributes to the national goal of 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030.
	2. The government is promoting offshore wind, with an assessed potential of about 70 GW along the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coasts.
	3. Net-zero emissions by 2070 requires faster wind deployment alongside storage, transmission and flexible demand.
4. Distribution of wind energy plants across India
	1. **Tamil Nadu:** High capacity due to the Palghat Gap, coastal winds and long experience; major sites include Muppandal.
	2. **Gujarat:** Strong coastal and Kutch potential, with scope for wind-solar hybrid and offshore projects.
	3. **Karnataka:** Deccan plateau and Western Ghats corridors support large installations.
	4. **Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh:** Important onshore regions with expanding projects.
	5. Offshore potential is concentrated in the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coastal zones because of favourable wind speeds and shallow continental-shelf conditions.
5. Benefits
	1. Renewable, fuel-free electricity with low operational emissions and water use.
	2. Complements solar, as wind generation may be stronger at night or during monsoon months.
	3. Creates manufacturing, construction, maintenance and rural lease-income opportunities.
	4. Wind-solar hybrids improve land and transmission utilisation.
6. Challenges
	1. Variable generation requires forecasting, storage, balancing power and stronger transmission.
	2. Land and right-of-way disputes, bird and bat mortality, noise and visual impacts require careful siting.
	3. Delays in land, grid and transmission approvals increase project costs.
	4. Offshore projects face high capital costs, cyclones, corrosion, port limitations and difficult maintenance.
	5. Domestic manufacturing must manage dependence on rare-earth materials and improve turbine recycling.
7. Key govt initiatives
	- National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy, 2018; National Offshore Wind Energy Policy, 2015; Green Energy Corridors; competitive bidding through SECI; repowering policy; and transmission planning for renewable-energy zones.
8. Conclusion
	- Repowering, wind-solar storage hybrids and ecologically sensitive offshore development can make wind power a dependable partner in India's clean and secure energy system.
