1. Intro
India’s National Perspective Plan for river linking seeks to correct regional hydrological asymmetry by transferring water from surplus to deficit basins.

2. Concept
- excess/surplus -> deficit/drought
- Himalayan + Peninsular region
- aim: to reduce regional disparity in water access + improving drought preparedness.

3. Benefits and Significance
- Could improve irrigation and support agriculture in drought-prone regions.
- May help water-scarce areas of western and peninsular India through additional supply.
- Flood moderation in high-flow basins and improved inland navigation are possible co-benefits.
- It can strengthen regional water security and reduce dependence on erratic monsoon rainfall.

4. Challenges
- Huge costs, 
- long gestation periods,
- implementation difficult.
- Ecological impacts on riverine ecosystems, fish migration, and biodiversity are serious concerns.
- Displacement of communities, resettlement issues, and inter-state disputes complicate execution.
- Hydrological uncertainties, climate change, and sedimentation reduce expected benefits.

1. Govt steps
- The National Perspective Plan for inter-basin transfer was prepared by the Ministry of Jal Shakti.
- The Ken-Betwa link is the most prominent project currently under active consideration.
- Regulatory and environmental clearances remain major bottlenecks.

1. Projects
  1. Planned
  - Himalayan and Peninsular component links are part of the long-term plan.
  1. Ongoing
  - Ken-Betwa river linking is the flagship project under development.
  1. Completed
  - Several small-scale interlinking and diversion works exist, but large national-scale projects remain largely uncompleted.
  - Source: Ministry of Jal Shakti, NITI Aayog, CWC.

2. Conclusion
- River linking can be useful only if it is planned with ecological caution, transparent cost-benefit assessment, and inter-state cooperation. 
- Balancing macro-engineering like the Ken-Betwa link with rigorous environmental impact assessments and decentralized conservation ensures sustainable water federalism without ecological fragmentation.
