### Micro Watershed Development Projects
1. Intro
Micro watershed development projects are a decentralised approach to land and water management that integrates soil conservation, water harvesting, afforestation, and livelihood improvement. They are especially important in rain-fed and drought-prone regions where resource efficiency is critical.

2. Concepts
- A micro watershed is a small drainage unit that can be managed through local planning and community participation.
- Interventions include contour bunding, check dams, gully plugs, farm ponds, vegetation restoration, and recharge structures.
- The objective is to conserve soil moisture, reduce runoff, improve groundwater recharge, and sustain rural livelihoods.

3. examples and cities
- In states such as Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan, watershed programmes have been implemented in drought-prone districts and rain-fed tracts.
- Many tribal and rural regions have benefited from village-level watershed planning linked with MGNREGA and agricultural support.
- Source: Ministry of Rural Development, NRSC, and state watershed departments.

4. Effects and impacts
- Improved groundwater recharge and better soil moisture support agriculture and horticulture.
- Reduced soil erosion, siltation, and flash runoff during heavy rainfall.
- Increased resilience against drought and climate variability, especially for small and marginal farmers.
- Development of common property resources and local ecological benefits.

5. Government initiatives/projects
- Integrated Watershed Management Programme (IWMP) and ongoing watershed components under rural development schemes.
- Convergence with MGNREGA, PMKSY, and state-level water conservation programmes.
- Community-based governance and participatory planning are central to success.

6. Conclusion
Micro watershed development is a practical and scalable model for climate-resilient rural development, combining ecological restoration with livelihood security. Source: Ministry of Rural Development, NRSC.
