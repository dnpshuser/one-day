### Juno Mission of NASA
1. Intro
The Juno mission, launched by NASA in 2011, is a landmark exploration programme designed to study Jupiter, the largest planet in the Solar System. It has transformed our understanding of planetary formation, magnetism, and the early history of giant planets.

2. mission
- Juno entered Jupiter’s orbit in 2016 and is studying the planet’s atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity field, and internal structure.
- It is designed to answer questions about how giant planets formed and whether they have a solid core.
- The spacecraft is also analysing Jupiter’s auroras, radiation belts, and atmospheric dynamics.

3. significance/key discoveries
- Juno has revealed that Jupiter’s atmosphere is more complex than previously thought, with powerful storms and deep atmospheric structures.
- It has helped confirm that Jupiter has a non-uniform interior and that its magnetosphere is highly dynamic.
- The mission has improved understanding of how giant planets may have formed from the primitive solar nebula.
- It has also shown how intense radiation and magnetic fields create extraordinary auroral activity.
- Source: NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, JPL mission updates.

4. conclusion
Juno is a high-value planetary science mission because it connects solar system evolution, atmospheric physics, and magnetospheric science in one comprehensive exploration programme. Source: NASA/JPL.
