### El Nino

   2. concept
   3. process of occurring
   4. recent occurrance
   5. effect and impact

   7. Conclusion


---

1. Intro: El Niño is the warm phase of El Niño Southern Oscillation characterised by the abnormal warming of sea surface temperature in the central and equitorial pacific ocean.

2. Process
   1. Normal Phase: 
      1. upwelling at Peru, 
      2. normal walker cell.
      3. warm water near Indonesia
      4. Low pressure over Australia-Indonesia region
      5. Thermocline deepens in the east
   2. El Nino Phase:
      1. Trade winds weakens
      2. Warm water moves towards central and eastern pacific
      3. more precipitation over central and eastern pacific
      4. Sustained high SST over Nino region (El Niño Threshold: ≥ +0.5°C)[170-120°W, 5°S - 5°N]

3. Impacts
   1. Global Climate ->
      1.  Warmer global temperatures, shift in rainfall patterns, more extreme events.
      2.  Example: 2023–24 was the warmest year on record.
   2. On Indian Monsoon 
      1. weakens SW monsoon due to subsidence (over Indian region) and reduced moisture transport.
      2. Example: 2023 monsoon rainfall was 94% of LPA (Below Normal)
   3. Agriculture -> Reduced kharif output, drought risk increases.
      1. Example: Lower paddy, pulses, and oilseeds production in El Niño years.
      2. 45-55% of Net sown area depends on monsoon - Economic survey 2024-25.
      3. Example: Stress on drinking water & irrigation. Irrigated area withdraws groundwater which is replinished by monsoon.
   4. Water Resources
      1. Lower reservoir levels, reduced groundwater recharge.
   5. Fisheries:
      1.  Warm SST reduces nutrient upwelling -> affects marine productivity & fish catch.
      2.  Example: Peru anchovy fishery collapse during strong El Niño.
   6. Economy:
      1. Higher inflation (food), energy demand rise, trade & growth affected.
      2. Example: 1997–98 El Niño caused ~1 fall in India's GDP growth.
   7. Extreme Events: 
      1. More floods in some regions (E. Pacific), droughts in others, stronger cyclones in Pacific.
      2. Example: Increased Atlantic hurricane activity during El Niño.

4. OTHER SIMILAR PHENOMENA GLOBALLY
   1. La Niña (Cold Phase of ENSO)Opposite of El Niño (cooler SST in central/eastern Pacific)Strengthens trade winds and Indian monsoon.
      1. Example: 2010–11 La Niña $\rightarrow$ good monsoon.
   2. Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)Difference in SST between western (Arabian Sea) and eastern (Bay of Bengal) Indian Ocean.
      1. Positive IOD -> good monsoon; 
      2. Negative IOD -> weak monsoon.
      3. Example: 2019 positive IOD offset weak El Niño.
   3. Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO)Eastward movement of clouds & rainfall around equator every 30–60 days.Affects onset, active & break spells of monsoon.
   4. Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO)Long-term SST pattern in Pacific ~20-30 year
      1. .Influences ENSO frequency & intensity. 

5. Recent Occurrance: [ONI: Oceanic Nino Index]
   1. 2015-16: ONI peak: 2.6
   2. 2023-34: ONI peak: 2.1
6. Conclusion: El Nino is a powerfull ocean atmospheric phenomena with far reaching impacts on climate, food, water, energy and economies making their tracking indispensable for climate resilient planning.
