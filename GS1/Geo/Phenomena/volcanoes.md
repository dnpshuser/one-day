### Volcanoes
1. Intro: 
   1. Driven by endogenic forces and plate tectonics, volcanic eruptions vent magma, gases, and pyroclasts, driving landform evolution while altering global atmospheric radiation balances.

2. Concept
   - Magma originates in the mantle and rises when crustal conditions allow.
   - When pressure is released, volcanic eruptions may occur, ranging from gentle lava flows to explosive eruptions.
   - Volcanoes may be active, dormant, or extinct depending on eruptive history.

3. Causes
   - Plate tectonics create weak zones where magma can ascend.
   - Subduction zones, 
   - mid-ocean ridges, and 
   - hotspots are major volcanic settings.
   - Stored magma pressure and gas content influence eruption type.

4. Global Distribution
      - Most volcanoes lie along the Pacific Ring of Fire, along with volcanic chains in Indonesia, Japan, Central America, and the Andes.
      - Hotspots such as Hawaii also produce volcanic activity away from plate boundaries.
   1. Recent occurrance
      - Recent eruptions in Indonesia, Iceland, and other regions show continuing volcanic risk.

5. Impact
   1. Environmental impact
      - Ash clouds can disrupt aviation, climate, and agriculture.
      - Lava flows and pyroclastic material can devastate ecosystems and settlements.
   2. Other impact
      - Volcanic gases such as sulfur dioxide can affect air quality and local climate.
      - Tourism, farming, and transport may be disrupted after major eruptions.
      - Source of Geothermal energy: California 60% energy

6. Mitigation Measures
   - Monitor volcanic activity using seismic and thermal sensors.
   - Establish exclusion zones and emergency evacuation plans for vulnerable communities.
   - Improve volcanic hazard mapping and public education.

7. Conclusion: Volcanoes are both hazardous and geologically valuable for modern energy infrastructure; understanding their behaviour is essential for risk reduction and sustainable land use in vulnerable regions.
