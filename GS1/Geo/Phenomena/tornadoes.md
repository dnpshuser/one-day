### Tornado/Twister
1. Intro
A tornado is a rapidly rotating column of air extending from a thunderstorm to the ground. It is one of the most violent weather phenomena, capable of causing severe destruction in a very short time.

2. process of formation
- A thunderstorm develops strong wind shear and rotation.
- Updrafts tilt the rotating air vertically.
- Under favourable conditions, a narrow funnel cloud forms and extends downward.
- When it reaches the ground, it becomes a tornado.

3. Causes
- Strong atmospheric instability, wind shear, and moisture supply are primary triggers.
- Severe convective systems in regions with contrasting air masses can produce tornadoes.

4. Distribution
- Tornadoes are most common in North America, especially the central United States.
- They also occur in parts of Europe, South America, Australia, and occasionally in South Asia.

5. Recent occurrance
- Several intense tornado events have been documented in the United States and other regions in recent years.
- Source: NOAA, WMO.

6. Impact
- Extremely high wind speeds can destroy buildings, uproot trees, and damage infrastructure.
- Tornadoes also cause injuries and deaths, often with little lead time.

7. Mitigation Measures
- Early warning systems, safe shelters, and public preparedness are crucial.
- Community awareness, home strengthening, and storm-proofing reduce vulnerability.

8. Conclusion
Tornadoes are short-lived but highly destructive; improved forecasting and local preparedness remain essential to reduce human loss. Source: NOAA, WMO.
