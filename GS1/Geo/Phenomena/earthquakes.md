### Earthqukes
1. Intro
Earthquakes are sudden vibrations of the Earth’s crust caused by the release of accumulated stress along faults. They are one of the most destructive natural hazards because they strike without warning and affect large areas.

2. process of occurrence
- Stress builds up in rocks due to tectonic movements.
- When the stress exceeds rock strength, the crust fractures and slips along a fault.
- This release of energy propagates as seismic waves, causing shaking.

3. Causes
- Plate tectonic movements, faulting, volcanic activity, and crustal deformation are major causes.
- Human activities such as reservoir-induced seismicity and mining can sometimes trigger earthquakes.

4. Distribution
   1. India
   - India is highly vulnerable due to its location near the Himalayan collision zone and active fault systems.
   - Regions such as the Himalaya, Northeast, and Gujarat are seismically active.
   2. Global
   - Earthquakes are concentrated along plate boundaries such as the Pacific Ring of Fire and the Alpine-Himalayan belt.
   3. Recent occurrance
   - Frequent seismic events continue in the Himalaya, Japan, Turkey, and other tectonically active zones.
   - Source: USGS, IMD, geological surveys.

5. Impact
- Ground shaking damages buildings, roads, bridges, and dams.
- Secondary hazards include landslides, liquefaction, fires, and tsunamis.
- Loss of life and economic disruption can be severe, especially in dense urban areas.

6. Mitigation Steps
- Enforce earthquake-resistant building codes and retrofitting of vulnerable structures.
- Improve seismic zoning, preparedness, and public awareness.
- Use early-warning systems and emergency response planning in high-risk regions.

7. Conclusion
Earthquake risk cannot be eliminated, but it can be reduced through engineering, planning, and preparedness. Source: USGS, NDMA, BIS.
