---

1. Intro: Auroras are luminous natural displays seen in polar skies, caused by interactions between the solar wind and the Earth’s magnetosphere. They are among the most spectacular examples of space-weather phenomena visible from high-latitude regions.

2. Process of formation
   1. Aurora Australis
      - Seen in the Southern Hemisphere around the Antarctic region.
   2. Aurora Borealis
      - Seen in the Northern Hemisphere near the Arctic circle.
   - Charged particles from the Sun collide with gases in the upper atmosphere, exciting them and producing glowing light.

3. Causes
   - Solar flares and coronal mass ejections release streams of charged particles toward Earth.
   - The Earth’s magnetic field channels these particles toward polar regions, where they interact with atmospheric gases.

4. Colors
   - Oxygen
     - Pale Yellow-Green: 100-250km
     - Deep Red: above 250km
   - N2+ ion
     - Blue: Below 100km
   - N2
     - Purple/Magenta/Violet: Below 100km
   - O2 + N2 mix
     - pink: 90-100km

5. Recent occurrance
   - Strong solar activity in recent years has increased the frequency and intensity of auroral displays in higher latitudes.
   - Such events can sometimes be seen farther from the poles than usual.

6. Effect/Impact - Tourist income
   - Auroras enhance eco-tourism and adventure tourism in Arctic and Antarctic regions.
   - They contribute to local income through guided expeditions, photography tours, and hospitality services.

7. conclusion
Auroras are beautiful examples of solar-terrestrial interaction, linking solar activity with atmospheric physics and regional tourism. Source: NASA, NOAA Space Weather.
