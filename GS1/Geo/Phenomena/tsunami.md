### Tsunami
1. Intro: A tsunami is a series of ocean waves generated by a sudden displacement of seawater, usually caused by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides.

2. process of formation
   - A large underwater disturbance lifts or drops the sea floor.
   - This generates a massive wave train that radiates outward.
   - In deep ocean, tsunami waves move fast but are low in height; near shores, they rise sharply due to wave shoaling.

3. Causes
   - Undersea earthquakes along subduction zones are the most common trigger.
   - Volcanic eruptions, coastal landslides
   - Underwater activities like mining for metal extraction

4. Distribution
   1. India
      - Eastern coast + Andaman and Nicobar Islands
   2. Global
      - Pacific subduction zones
   3. Recent occurrance
      - The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami remains the most severe recent example, affecting multiple countries.

5. Impact
   - Tsunamis can inundate coastal settlements, destroy infrastructure, and cause large-scale loss of life.
   - They can also damage ecosystems, ports, fisheries, and tourism infrastructure.

6. Mitigation Steps
   - Install tsunami early-warning systems and 
   - maintain real-time seismic monitoring.
   - zonation: Use coastal hazard mapping, 
   - evacuation routes, and 
   - public awareness campaigns.
   - Build resilient coastal planning and education for vulnerable communities.
7. Conclusion: Mitigating tsunami risks requires robust deep-ocean bottom pressure recorders, intertidal mangrove bio-shields, and standardized community evacuation protocols under the Sendai Framework.
