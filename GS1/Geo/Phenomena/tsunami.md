### Tsunami
1. Intro
A tsunami is a series of ocean waves generated by a sudden displacement of seawater, usually caused by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides. These waves can travel across entire ocean basins at high speeds and become devastating when they strike coasts.

2. process of formation
- A large underwater disturbance lifts or drops the sea floor.
- This generates a massive wave train that radiates outward.
- In deep ocean, tsunami waves move fast but are low in height; near shores, they rise sharply due to wave shoaling.

3. Causes
- Undersea earthquakes along subduction zones are the most common trigger.
- Volcanic eruptions, coastal landslides, and rare meteor impacts can also generate tsunamis.

4. Distribution
   1. India
   - India’s coastal regions, especially the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and the eastern coast, are exposed to tsunami risk.
   2. Global
   - Tsunami-prone regions are concentrated around Pacific subduction zones and other active tectonic boundaries.
   3. Recent occurrance
   - The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami remains the most severe recent example, affecting multiple countries.
   - Source: UNESCO IOC, USGS, IMD.

5. Impact
- Tsunamis can inundate coastal settlements, destroy infrastructure, and cause large-scale loss of life.
- They can also damage ecosystems, ports, fisheries, and tourism infrastructure.

6. Mitigation Steps
- Install tsunami early-warning systems and maintain real-time seismic monitoring.
- Use coastal hazard mapping, evacuation routes, and public awareness campaigns.
- Build resilient coastal planning and education for vulnerable communities.
