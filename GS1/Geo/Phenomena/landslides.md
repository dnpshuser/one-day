1. Intro: Triggered by intense precipitation, seismic shaking, and unplanned slope cutting, landslides cause catastrophic disasters across geologically fragile Himalayan and Western Ghats terrains.

2. process of occurrence
   - Slope instability develops when shear strength of the slope becomes less than the driving force such as gravity or heavy precipitation.
   - Heavy rain, earthquakes, or human activity can trigger rapid downslope movement.
   - Debris, mud, and rock move downhill, sometimes with great force and speed.

3. Causes
   1. Himalayan reason causes
      - Steep slopes, 
      - fragile geology, 
      - intense rainfall, and 
      - tectonic activity increase landslide risk.
   2. Western Ghat Causes
      - Heavy monsoon rainfall, 
      - weathering, and cut slopes along roads and hill settlements trigger landslides.
   3. Common Causes
      - Deforestation, 
      - slope cutting, 
      - drainage failure, 
      - construction,
      - blasting can weaken slopes.

4. Distribution
   1. India
      - Landslide-prone regions include the Himalaya, northeastern hills, and Western Ghats.
   2. Global
      - Mountain belts and coastal cliffs globally experience recurring slope failures.
   3. Recent occurrance
      - Frequent events in the Himalaya and in monsoon-hit hill regions highlight increasing exposure.

5. Impact
   - Loss of life, damage to homes, roads, bridges, and hydropower infrastructure.
   - Sedimentation of rivers and reservoirs, 
   - disruption of transport and connectivity.

6. Mitigation Measures
   - Land-use zoning, 
   - slope stabilization, 
   - afforestation, and 
   - drainage control.
   - Early warning systems and 
   - community preparedness in vulnerable districts.
   - Restrict construction in high-risk slopes and 
   - improve road engineering.

7. Conclusion: Mitigating landslide vulnerability demands granular slope-susceptibility zonation, bio-engineering stabilization, and community-centric early warning networks aligned with NDMA guidelines and the Sendai Framework.
