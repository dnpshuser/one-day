
### Landslides
1. Intro
Landslides are downslope movements of rock, soil, and debris triggered by gravity and environmental instability. They are especially common in mountainous and hilly regions and can be sudden and highly destructive.

2. process of occurrence
- Slope instability develops when shear strength of the slope becomes less than the driving force.
- Heavy rain, earthquakes, or human activity can trigger rapid downslope movement.
- Debris, mud, and rock move downhill, sometimes with great force and speed.

3. Causes
   1. Himalayan reason causes
   - Steep slopes, fragile geology, intense rainfall, and tectonic activity increase landslide risk.
   2. Western Ghat Causes
   - Heavy monsoon rainfall, weathering, and cut slopes along roads and hill settlements trigger landslides.
   3. Common Causes
   - Deforestation, slope cutting, drainage failure, construction, and blasting can weaken slopes.

4. Distribution
   1. India
   - Landslide-prone regions include the Himalaya, northeastern hills, and Western Ghats.
   2. Global
   - Mountain belts and coastal cliffs globally experience recurring slope failures.
   3. Recent occurrance
   - Frequent events in the Himalaya and in monsoon-hit hill regions highlight increasing exposure.
   - Source: Geological Survey of India (GSI), NDMA, IMD.

5. Impact
- Loss of life, damage to homes, roads, bridges, and hydropower infrastructure.
- Sedimentation of rivers and reservoirs, and disruption of transport and connectivity.

6. Mitigation Measures
- Land-use zoning, slope stabilization, afforestation, and drainage control.
- Early warning systems and community preparedness in vulnerable districts.
- Restrict construction in high-risk slopes and improve road engineering.

7. Conclusion
Landslides are a challenge of both geology and governance; sustainable hill-area development and early warning are critical. Source: GSI, NDMA, IMD.
