
### Cyclones
1. Intro
Cyclones are intense low-pressure systems that spiral around a calm centre, bringing heavy rain, strong winds, and storm surges. They are among the most destructive weather systems in tropical regions.

2. Process of formation(steps)
- Warm ocean water provides moisture and heat energy.
- The rising warm, moist air creates low pressure and condensation.
- Coriolis force causes the system to rotate.
- With sustained energy from the sea, the cyclone intensifies and moves with the prevailing winds.

3. Causes
   1. Sea Surface Temperature Rise
      - Higher sea surface temperatures provide more latent heat and moisture for cyclone intensification.
      - This increases the potential for stronger storms and higher rainfall rates.
   2. Other Causes
      - Ocean-atmosphere coupling, low-level wind convergence, and moisture availability are critical triggers.
      - Climate change is likely to increase the intensity of some tropical cyclones.

4. Distribution
   1. India
   - The Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea are major cyclone-prone regions for India.
   2. Global
   - Tropical cyclones are common in the western Pacific, North Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and South Pacific.
      1. Reasons for confinement upto South China Sea, Bay of Bengal and Gulf of Mexico
      - Warm waters, favourable atmospheric conditions, and a suitable Coriolis effect make these basins highly conducive to cyclone formation.
   3. Recent occurrance
   - Recent years have seen highly damaging cyclones in the Indian Ocean and Atlantic basins, including in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.
   - Source: IMD, WMO, NOAA.

5. Impact
- Strong winds damage housing, power lines, transport, and crops.
- Storm surges can inundate coastal areas and cause massive loss of life and property.
- Heavy rainfall causes inland flooding and landslides.

6. Naming convension
- Cyclones are named by regional meteorological bodies to improve public communication and early warnings.
- The naming system is often alphabetical and follows agreed regional lists.

7. Mitigation Steps
   1. Color Coded Weather Warning System
   - IMD uses colour-coded warnings to communicate cyclone risk and guide evacuation and preparedness.
- Coastal shelters, evacuation plans, resilient housing, and mangrove protection reduce vulnerability.

8. Conclusion
Cyclones are a major climate and disaster risk for coastal communities; better forecasting, resilient infrastructure, and preparedness remain essential. Source: IMD, WMO, NOAA.
