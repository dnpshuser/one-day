### Climate Change
1. Intro
Climate change is the most serious geophysical and socio-economic challenge of the 21st century, driven largely by anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions. It affects weather, ecosystems, agriculture, water, and human security across the globe.

2. Factors/Causes
- Burning fossil fuels for energy, transport, and industry releases carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases.
- Deforestation and land-use change reduce carbon sinks and intensify warming.
- Agriculture, especially methane from livestock and rice cultivation, and industrial processes add to GHG concentration.
- Urbanisation and unsustainable consumption patterns exacerbate emissions.

3. Effects and Impacts
   1. Existence threat of island nations
      - Rising sea levels, coastal erosion, saline intrusion, and extreme weather threaten small island states and low-lying coasts.
      - Source: IPCC AR6; UNFCCC and small-island-state assessments.
   2. Food security
      - Heat stress, irregular rainfall, and water stress reduce crop yields, especially in tropical regions and rain-fed agriculture.
      - Climate variability also affects fishery and livestock productivity, increasing food insecurity.
   3. Desertification
      - Extended drought, loss of vegetation cover, and land degradation convert productive land into desert-like conditions.
      - It is especially evident in the Sahel, parts of South Asia, and arid regions of Asia and Africa.

4. Mitigation efforts
- Paris Agreement aims to keep global warming well below 2°C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.
- Renewable energy, energy efficiency, electric mobility, and low-carbon industrial processes are crucial.
- Carbon pricing, green finance, and climate-smart agriculture are gaining importance.
- Source: UNFCCC, IPCC AR6.

5. Forther steps
- Scale up adaptation with resilient infrastructure, early-warning systems, and climate-proof agriculture.
- Strengthen loss and damage financing for vulnerable countries.
- Focus on nature-based solutions such as afforestation, wetland restoration, and mangrove revival.
- Combine equitable climate justice with technology transfer and capacity building.

6. Conclusion
Climate action is not optional but essential for securing livelihoods, biodiversity, and intergenerational justice in a warming world. Source: IPCC AR6, UNEP Emissions Gap Report.
