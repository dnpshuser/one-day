### Climate Change
1. Intro
2. Factors/Causes
3. Effects and Impacts
4. Mitigation efforts
5. Further steps
6. Conclusion


---

1. Intro
Climate change is the most serious geophysical and socio-economic challenge of the 21st century, driven largely by anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions. It affects weather, ecosystems, agriculture, water, and human security across the globe.

2. Factors/Causes
   1. Burning fossil fuels for Energy and Transport
   2. industry: CO2 + GHG
   3. Deforestation and land-use change reduce carbon sinks and intensify warming.
   4. CH4 from livestock and rice cultivation
   5. Heat islands - Urbanisation
   6. unsustainable consumption patterns.

3. Effects and Impacts
   1. Existence threat of island nations
      - Rising sea levels, 
      - coastal erosion, 
      - saline intrusion,
      - extreme weather threaten small island states and low-lying coasts. 
   2. Food security
      - Heat stress
      - crop losses, 
      - water stress reduce crop yields
      - fishery and livestock productivity, 
      - increasing food insecurity.
   3. Desertification
      - Extended drought, 
      - loss of vegetation cover 
      - productive land -> desert-like.
      - evident - Sahel
   4. Biodiversity loss
      - Habitat shifts, 
      - coral bleaching, 
      - species extinction, 
      - altered migration patterns threaten ecosystems.
      - glaciers melting threatening: Polar and alpine species.
   5. Health impacts
      - Heatwaves, 
      - vector-borne diseases, 
      - respiratory illnesses, 
      - malnutrition, 
      - mental health stress increase morbidity and mortality.
   6. Weather extremes
      - More frequent and intense cyclones, 
      - floods, 
      - droughts, 
      - wildfires, 
      - storms disrupt communities and economies. 
      - erratic rainfall, 


2. Mitigation efforts
   - Paris Agreement: 2 deg C, better 1.5 deg C
   - Renewable energy, energy efficiency, electric mobility, and low-carbon industrial processes are crucial.
   - Carbon pricing, green finance, and climate-smart agriculture are gaining importance.
   - Source: UNFCCC, IPCC AR6.

3. Further steps
   - resilient infrastructure, 
   - early-warning systems
   - climate-proof agriculture.
   - Strengthen loss and damage financing for vulnerable countries.
   - nature-based sol 
     - afforestation, 
     - wetland restoration,
     - mangrove revival.
   - Combine equitable climate justice with technology transfer and capacity building.

4. Conclusion
- Climate action is not optional but essential for securing livelihoods, biodiversity, and intergenerational justice in a warming world.
- Achieving the 1.5°C threshold demands translating COP pacts into aggressive decarbonization, climate finance mobilization, and resilient adaptation frameworks aligned with LiFE.
