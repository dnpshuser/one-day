### Flood 
1. Intro
Floods are among the most recurring and damaging natural hazards in India, affecting agriculture, livelihoods, urban infrastructure, and public safety. They are increasingly intensified by climate variability, land-use change, and unplanned development.

2. Causes
- Heavy rainfall from monsoon depressions and cloudbursts causes river and flash flooding.
- Poor drainage, encroachment on floodplains, and wetland loss increase vulnerability.
- Deforestation in upstream catchments and siltation of rivers reduce water carrying capacity.
- Cyclonic storms, glacial lake outburst floods, and urban concretisation worsen flood risk.

3. how to convert to
  1. sustainable source of irrigation
  - Floodwater can be stored in reservoirs, check dams, and recharge structures for use in lean seasons.
  - Community-managed ponds and floodplain irrigation can improve crop resilience and groundwater recharge.
  - Source: CWC and Ministry of Jal Shakti on flood management and irrigation planning.
  2. all-weather inland navigation
  - Seasonal rivers can be stabilised through dredging, channel deepening, and lock-gate systems.
  - Inland navigation on rivers like Ganga and Brahmaputra can reduce transport costs and ease logistics.
  - This requires environmental safeguards to avoid ecological damage.

4. Mitigation measures
- Floodplain zoning and restrictions on settlement in high-risk areas.
- Upstream watershed management, afforestation, and wetland restoration.
- Improved forecasting, early warning, and emergency response.
- Embankments, spillways, and reservoir regulation must be complemented with ecological and community measures.

5. Conclusion
Floods should be treated not only as disasters but also as an underutilised resource for irrigation and navigation if managed scientifically and equitably. Source: Central Water Commission (CWC), National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA).
