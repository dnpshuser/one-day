### Sea level rise
1. Intro
Sea-level rise is one of the clearest indicators of climate change, driven by thermal expansion of warming oceans and melting of land-based ice. It threatens coastal communities, infrastructure, and ecologically sensitive regions.

2. Causes
- Global warming increases ocean temperature, causing water to expand and sea levels to rise.
- Melting glaciers and ice sheets in Greenland and Antarctica add freshwater to the ocean.
- Land subsidence, groundwater extraction, and coastal erosion worsen relative sea-level rise in many regions.

3. Effects/impacts
- Coastal flooding and saltwater intrusion damage agriculture, drinking water, and urban infrastructure.
- Low-lying island nations face existential risks from loss of land and livelihoods.
- Erosion of beaches and mangroves reduces natural coastal protection and biodiversity.
- Source: IPCC AR6, NASA, NOAA.

4. mitigation steps
- Reduce greenhouse gas emissions through clean energy and low-carbon development.
- Build coastal buffers such as mangroves, dunes, and seawalls in vulnerable areas.
- Integrate climate-resilient planning into coastal zoning and urban design.
- Improve early warning, relocation planning, and adaptation financing for coastal communities.

5. Conclusion
Sea-level rise is a slow but accelerating risk that demands both global climate mitigation and local adaptation, especially for vulnerable coastal populations. Source: IPCC AR6, NOAA.
