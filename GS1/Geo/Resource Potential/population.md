1. Intro
	- India's population is not merely a demographic pressure; with the right capabilities, its large working-age cohort can become the country's strongest productive resource.
2. Gangetic population distribution
	1. Fertile alluvial plains, perennial rivers, level terrain and long agricultural history support dense settlement from Punjab and Haryana through Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and West Bengal.
	2. Irrigation, transport corridors and cities reinforce density, while floods, land fragmentation and rural underemployment create pressure.
	3. **Source:** Census of India 2011; the next Census is awaited.
3. Total fertility rate
	1. India's total fertility rate is 2.0 children per woman, below the replacement level of 2.1. **Source: NFHS-5, 2019-21, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.**
	2. The transition is uneven: southern states face ageing concerns, while parts of central and eastern India need stronger reproductive healthcare, education and employment.
4. population projection
	1. Population growth is slowing while ageing will increase. **Source: UN DESA, World Population Prospects 2024; projections are estimates, not Census counts.**
	2. India must create productive jobs for youth while preparing social security and healthcare for an ageing population.
5. how to reap demographic dividend ?
	1. Ensure quality school education, foundational literacy, digital access and employable skills.
	2. Raise female labour-force participation through childcare, safe transport, flexible work and equal wages.
	3. Shift from low-productivity agriculture to manufacturing, care economy, tourism, logistics and modern services.
	4. Improve nutrition, primary healthcare, reproductive choice and migration-friendly urban planning.
6. on going schemes to enhance population employability
	1. Skill India Mission, Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana and National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme.
	2. National Education Policy 2020: multidisciplinary, vocational and outcome-based learning.
	3. Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana and MGNREGA support rural skills and livelihood security.
	4. MUDRA, Startup India, Stand-Up India and Digital India support entrepreneurship and formalisation.
7. Conclusion
	- Investing in health, learning, decent work and gender equality can convert India's demographic transition into inclusive growth before the window gradually narrows.
