1. Intro
	- "अद्भिः सर्वाणि भूतानि जीवन्ति प्रभवन्ति च।" — महाभारत (शान्ति पर्व)
	- (From water all living beings emerge, and by water they are sustained.)
2. current water need of India
	1. Agriculture - largest user of freshwater, followed by domestic and industrial demand.
	2. Demand rises with population, urbanisation, irrigation, energy, industry and changing consumption.
3. current water availability
	1. India receives about 4,000 Billion Cubic Meters(BCM) of annual precipitation, but only a limited portion is utilizable because of seasonality, floods, evaporation and storage constraints.
	2. Per-capita availability has declined with population growth. 
4. Factors for water stress in India
	1. Monsoon concentration and regional variability.
	2. Groundwater over-extraction,
	3. inefficient flood irrigation and 
	4. water-intensive crops.
	5. Pollution from sewage, industry, agriculture and solid waste.
	6. Wetland loss, urban concretisation, climate extremes and fragmented governance.
5. Water resource distribution in India
	1. Himalayan rivers are relatively perennial; 
	2. peninsular rivers are more seasonal and monsoon-dependent.
	3. Brahmaputra, Ganga and Godavari basins have substantial resources, 
	4. western Rajasthan, Deccan interiors and parts of Tamil Nadu face higher stress.
	5. Groundwater is important in alluvial plains but discontinuous in hard-rock peninsular terrain.
6. Measures to improve water efficiency
	1. Microirrigation: Drip and sprinkler irrigation, 
	2. crop diversification and millets in stressed regions.
	3. Aquifer mapping, groundwater budgeting, community monitoring and energy-pricing reform.
	4. Reuse treated wastewater for industry, landscaping and non-potable urban uses.
	5. Restore wetlands, lakes, floodplains and catchments; expand rainwater harvesting and watershed development.
	6. Strengthen river-basin institutions, pollution enforcement and climate-resilient infrastructure.
	7. Jal Jeevan Mission, Atal Bhujal Yojana, National Water Mission, AMRUT and PMKSY provide policy support.
7. Conclusion
	- Securing long-term water resilience requires decentralized watershed management, micro-irrigation, and wastewater circularity, anchoring India's transition to SDG 6 (Clean Water).
