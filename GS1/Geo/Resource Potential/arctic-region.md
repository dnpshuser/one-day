### India's interest in Arctic region
1. Intro
2. India's interest: What all resources Arctic region can provide
3. Current status of India in arctic activity
4. Global competition
5. Way forward
6. Conclusion
 
1. Intro
	- The Arctic is a climate-sensitive frontier whose resources, shipping routes and scientific signals increasingly shape India's energy security, climate policy and Indo-Pacific interests.
2. India's interest: What all resources Arctic region can provide
	1. **Energy and minerals:** Oil, gas, nickel, cobalt, copper, rare earths and graphite, though extraction faces severe ecological and cost constraints.
	2. **Fisheries and freshwater:** Changing sea ice may alter fisheries and ocean circulation.
	3. **Shipping:** The Northern Sea Route may shorten Europe-Asia sailing seasonally, but depends on ice, insurance, ports and geopolitics.
	4. **Climate and science:** Arctic warming affects monsoon behaviour, sea-level rise and extreme weather; polar research improves Indian climate modelling.
3. Current status of India in arctic activity
	1. India has held Observer status in the Arctic Council since 2013 and released its Arctic Policy in 2022. **Source: Ministry of External Affairs, India's Arctic Policy, 2022.**
	2. India operates Himadri research station at Ny-Alesund, Svalbard, and the IndARC observatory in Kongsfjorden.
	3. The National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research coordinates India's polar research and expeditions.
4. Global competition
	1. Russia controls much of the Northern Sea Route and has major Arctic energy and infrastructure interests.
	2. The United States, Canada, Norway and Denmark pursue security and resource interests; China describes itself as a near-Arctic state and invests in research and connectivity.
	3. Militarisation, sanctions, indigenous rights and weakening cooperation increase uncertainty.
5. Way forward
	1. Prioritise science, climate diplomacy and data sharing over extraction-first approaches.
	2. Expand polar research, satellite monitoring, indigenous knowledge partnerships and Arctic-Monsoon studies.
	3. Develop responsible shipping standards, emergency response and green fuels before route dependence.
	4. Secure critical minerals through transparent, environmentally responsible and diversified supply chains.
	5. Uphold UNCLOS, precaution and indigenous rights.
6. Conclusion
	- India should pursue science-led, rules-based Arctic engagement that protects the polar commons while preparing for its consequences on India's climate and economy.
