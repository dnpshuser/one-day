### Mountain Ecosystem
1. Intro
2. How to restore
3. Impact of tourism
4. current challenges
5. government initiatives
6. conclusion

---

1. Intro
	- Mountain ecosystems are India's natural water towers and biodiversity refuges, sustaining rivers, livelihoods and climate resilience far beyond their slopes.
2. How to restore
	1. **Landscape-based planning:** Map watersheds, springs, wetlands, forests, wildlife corridors and hazard zones before approving infrastructure.
	2. **Ecosystem restoration:** Restore native forests, alpine meadows and degraded slopes through assisted natural regeneration, mixed plantations and invasive-species control.
	3. **Water security:** Revive springs, recharge aquifers, maintain environmental flows and use traditional systems such as naulas, dharas and zings.
	4. **Soil and slope stability:** Apply contour trenches, terracing, check dams, bio-engineering and slope drainage instead of excessive concrete structures.
	5. **Community stewardship:** Empower gram sabhas, Van Panchayats, tribal communities and women through benefit-sharing, ecotourism and payments for ecosystem services.
	6. **Climate adaptation:** Use early-warning systems, climate-resilient crops, glacial-lake monitoring and carrying-capacity assessments.
3. Impact of tourism
	1. **Positive:** Generates employment, local revenue, handicraft markets and incentives for heritage and biodiversity conservation.
	2. **Negative:** Unplanned hotels and roads cause deforestation, slope destabilisation, groundwater stress, waste accumulation and sewage pollution.
	3. Traffic, noise and visitor pressure disturb wildlife; pilgrimage peaks intensify resource consumption and disaster exposure.
	4. **Way forward:** Regulate visitor numbers, promote homestays, enforce waste take-back, improve public transport and make tourism infrastructure disaster-resilient.
4. current challenges
	1. Climate change is accelerating glacier retreat, altering snowfall and increasing flash floods, landslides and glacial lake outburst flood risks.
	2. Deforestation, hydropower, mining, road construction, urbanisation and unplanned tourism fragment habitats.
	3. Springs are drying, while soil erosion, invasive species, forest fires and human-wildlife conflict are increasing.
	4. Fragile geology, seismicity and inadequate enforcement magnify the impact of infrastructure and extreme rainfall.
	5. The Himalaya contains major biodiversity and water resources, but its exact ecological condition varies by region. **Source: NITI Aayog, Indian Himalayan Region reports; IPCC, Sixth Assessment Report.**
5. government initiatives
	1. **National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem:** Research, monitoring, capacity building and climate adaptation.
	2. **SECURE Himalaya:** Conservation of high-altitude biodiversity and sustainable livelihood support. **Source: MoEFCC and UNDP.**
	3. **National Mission on Himalayan Studies:** Research and community-based conservation.
	4. **NABARD Spring-shed development and Jal Jeevan Mission:** Support water-source sustainability and local water security.
	5. **Disaster Management Act, NDMA guidelines and IMD/ISRO monitoring:** Improve early warning, risk mapping and response.
	6. Environmental impact assessment, eco-sensitive zones, protected areas and the National Green Tribunal provide regulatory safeguards.
6. conclusion
	- Restoring mountain ecosystems through science, community stewardship and climate-resilient infrastructure can advance SDG 6, 13 and 15 while protecting future generations.
