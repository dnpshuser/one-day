### Rubber production
1. Intro
2. Climate requirements
3. India's position in prodction and consumption
4. Global distribution of production
5. Environmental concerns
6. Mitigation measures
7. Conclusion

1. Intro
	- Natural rubber links tropical ecology with tyres, healthcare and industry, making sustainable plantation management essential for both livelihoods and supply security.
2. Climate requirements
	1. Hot, humid conditions; generally 25-35°C temperature and about 200 cm or more well-distributed rainfall.
	2. Deep, well-drained acidic loamy soils, gentle slopes and a short dry spell support latex tapping.
3. India's position in prodction and consumption
	1. India is a major natural-rubber producer and one of the world's largest consumers, with demand led by tyres and transport.
	2. Kerala dominates production, followed by Tripura, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Assam. **Source: Rubber Board, Indian Rubber Statistics.**
4. Global distribution of production
	1. Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, India, China and Malaysia are major producers; Southeast Asia dominates due to humid tropical conditions.
	2. Production is concentrated in smallholder plantations and linked to global tyre supply chains. **Source: Association of Natural Rubber Producing Countries.**
5. Environmental concerns
	1. Monoculture reduces biodiversity, 
	2. while clearing forests and poorly planned expansion can fragment habitats.
	3. Soil erosion, 
	4. agrochemical runoff, 
	5. water stress and 
	6. climate-driven disease threaten plantations.
6. Mitigation measures
	1. Agroforestry, 
	2. mixed planting, 
	3. contour conservation, 
	4. integrated pest management and 
	5. rainwater harvesting.
	6. Improve smallholder productivity, traceability, recycling of rubber products and sustainable certification.
7. Conclusion
	- Climate-resilient agroforestry and responsible value chains can secure rubber supplies while advancing SDG 12, farmer welfare and biodiversity conservation.
