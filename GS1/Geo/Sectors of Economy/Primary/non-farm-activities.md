### Non-Farm Primary Activities

3. relation with physiographic features
4. Impact
5. Enironmental concerns
6. Mitigation measures
7. Govt initiatives
8. Conclusion
 
1. Intro
	- Encompassing allied sectors like forestry, fisheries, and pastoralism, non-farm primary activities form the vital safety net for rural livelihoods and ecological diversification.
2. enumerate all non-farm primary activities
	1. Forestry, collection of non-timber forest produce, logging and social forestry.
	2. Fishing, aquaculture, beekeeping, sericulture and livestock rearing.
	3. Quarrying, mining, salt production and extraction of medicinal plants.
	4. Ecotourism, nature-based recreation and watershed-related activities.
3. relation with physiographic features
	1. Forests and hills support, livestock, forestry and ecotourism.
	2. Coasts, estuaries and rivers support fisheries, aquaculture, salt pans and ports.
	3. Drylands favour pastoralism, 
	4. mining and solar-linked land uses; 
	5. plateaus support quarrying and mineral extraction.
4. Impact
	1. Generate employment, 
	2. supplementary income, 
	3. food security, 
	4. exports and women-led livelihoods.
	5. Reduce excessive dependence on rainfed farming and encourage local value addition.
5. Enironmental concerns
	1. Overfishing, 
	2. deforestation, 
	3. illegal mining, 
	4. habitat loss, 
	5. soil erosion and 
	6. groundwater depletion.
	7. Pollution, 
	8. invasive species, 
	9. human-wildlife conflict and 
	10. climate vulnerability.
6. Mitigation measures
	1. Community-based resource governance, 
	2. sustainable harvest limits, 
	3. certification and scientific extraction.
	4. Value addition at source, 
	5. circular production, 
	6. restoration funds, 
	7. insurance and alternative livelihoods.
7. Govt initiatives
	1. MGNREGA, 
	2. Van Dhan Vikas Kendras, 
	3. National Livelihood Mission, 
	4. PMMSY and 
	5. National Bamboo Mission.
	6. Joint Forest Management and 
	7. District Mineral Foundation support participation and local development. **Source: concerned Union ministries.**
8. Conclusion
	- Resource-sensitive diversification can advance SDG 8 and constitutional livelihood justice by making rural economies resilient without exhausting ecological capital.
