1. Intro
	- Pulses are India's affordable protein bridge, enriching soil while strengthening nutrition, farmer resilience and the sustainability of water-stressed agriculture.
2. Importance
	1. Provide plant protein, 
	2. fibre, 
	3. iron and 
	4. folate; 
	5. essential for nutrition security.
	6. Legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria, 
	7. reducing fertiliser demand and improving soil fertility.
3. Advantages in cultivation
	1. Need relatively less water and fit rainfed, 
	2. dryland and intercropping systems.
	3. Short-duration varieties enable crop rotation, 
	4. residue benefits and climate-risk management.
4. Production distribution in India
	1. Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh are major producers.
	2. Gram dominates semi-arid rabi regions; tur, urad and moong are important kharif pulses.
5. India's position in global production and consumption
	1. India is the world's largest producer and consumer of pulses but periodically imports to bridge supply gaps.
6. Challenges
	1. Low and unstable yields, 
	2. pests, 
	3. drought, 
	4. fragmented holdings and 
	5. weak seed systems.
	6. Price volatility, 
	7. inadequate procurement, 
	8. storage and processing discourage farmers.
7. Solutions
	1. Promote improved climate-resilient seed, 
	2. assured procurement, 
	3. FPOs, 
	4. storage and processing.
	5. Expand pulses in rice fallows, 
	6. intercropping, 
	7. micro-irrigation and 
	8. region-specific crop planning.
8. Govt schemes
	1. National Food Security Mission-Pulses, 
	2. PM-AASHA, 
	3. minimum support price procurement and seed minikits.
	4. Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses supports domestic production.
9. Conclusion
	- Achieving pulse self-sufficiency demands high-yielding seed varieties, assured procurement under PM-AASHA, and climate-resilient farming, anchoring long-term nutritional security.
