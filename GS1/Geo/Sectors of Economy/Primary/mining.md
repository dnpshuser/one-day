1. Intro
	- Supplying critical raw materials for industrial metabolism, mining serves as the foundational catalyst for infrastructure, manufacturing, and national strategic autonomy.
2. India's distribution
	1. Coal: eastern and central belts of Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal and Madhya Pradesh.
	2. Iron ore: Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Jharkhand and Goa.
	3. Bauxite: Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh; 
	4. non-ferrous minerals occur across Rajasthan, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh.
3. global distribution
	1. Iron ore: Australia, Brazil, China, India and Russia; 
	2. copper: Chile, Peru, China and the Democratic Republic of Congo.
	3. Lithium: Australia, Chile, Argentina and China; 
	4. cobalt: mainly the Democratic Republic of Congo.
4. India's position in the world
	- India is among leading producers of coal, iron ore, mica and bauxite, but remains import-dependent for several critical minerals and coking coal. 
5. Factors for little growth in India
	1. Deep or low-grade deposits, 
	2. land and forest clearances, 
	3. regulatory delays and inadequate exploration.
	4. Weak mineral processing, 
	5. logistics, 
	6. technology, 
	7. finance and local acceptance.
6. How to improve growth further ?
	1. Expand geological exploration, 
	2. auction transparent blocks and 
	3. promote district mineral planning.
	4. Build domestic processing and recycling for critical minerals; 
	5. Enforce mine safety, 
	6. benefit-sharing and 
	7. community participation.
7. Govt schemes
	1. National Mineral Exploration Trust, 
	2. District Mineral Foundation and 
	3. National Critical Mineral Mission.
	4. Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) framework  
	5. Sustainable Development Framework for mining.
8. Conclusion
	- Transitioning toward sustainable mining demands integrating green extraction technologies, circular mineral recycling, and robust District Mineral Foundation (DMF) community welfare.
