### Tea cultivation
1. Intro
2. Regions of cultivation
3. Favourable climate - enumerate
4. India's position in global production and consumption
5. Why couldn't extend beyond Darjeeling
6. Conclusion
 
1. Intro
	- As a labor-intensive agro-industrial plantation crop, tea drives regional hill employment, vital foreign exchange earnings, and organized rural livelihoods.
2. Regions of cultivation
	1. Assam valley and Cachar: warm, humid lowlands and Brahmaputra-Barak basin conditions.
	2. Darjeeling and Dooars: Himalayan foothills; high-quality orthodox and specialty teas.
	3. Nilgiris, Kerala and Karnataka: highland plantations with year-round harvesting possibilities.
3. Favourable climate - enumerate
	1. Temperature of roughly 20-30°C, high humidity and 150-300 cm well-distributed rainfall.
	2. Slightly acidic, deep, porous, well-drained soils rich in organic matter.
	3. Slopes aid drainage; mist and cloud improve leaf quality, while shade regulates heat.
	4. Abundant skilled and seasonal labour is necessary for careful plucking.
4. India's position in global production and consumption
	1. India is among the world's largest tea producers and consumers, with Assam and West Bengal major production centres.
	2. Tea is an important export, but domestic demand absorbs much output. **Source: Tea Board India; FAOSTAT.**
5. Why couldn't extend beyond Darjeeling
	1. Darjeeling's distinctive quality depends on altitude, slope, cool mist, drainage, soil and terroir, which cannot be replicated everywhere.
	2. Expansion is constrained by fragile terrain, small holdings, high labour costs, land scarcity and climate variability.
	3. Quality also depends on specialised cultivars, traditional processing and geographical indication-based identity.
6. Conclusion
	- Premiumisation, climate-smart plantations and GI-led value addition can secure tea livelihoods while advancing SDG 8, sustainable production and rural prosperity.
