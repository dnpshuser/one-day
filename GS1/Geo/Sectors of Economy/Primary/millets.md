1. Intro
	- "Millets are not coarse grains; they are climate-smart nutri-cereals that hold the key to our nutritional and ecological security." — Dr. M.S. Swaminathan(father of green revolution)
2. Importance
	1. Rich in fibre, minerals and micronutrients; 
	2. useful against malnutrition and lifestyle diseases.
	3. Support food security, 
	4. livestock feed, 
	5. local cuisines, 
	6. women-led processing and 
	7. dryland livelihoods.
3. Advantages in cultivation
	1. Require less water and 
	2. shorter growing periods than rice and wheat.
	3. Tolerate heat, drought and poor soils; 
	4. suitable for rainfed and marginal lands.
	5. Diverse root systems improve soil cover and reduce climate risk.
4. Production distribution in India
	1. Rajasthan, 
	2. Karnataka, 
	3. Maharashtra, 
	4. Uttar Pradesh, 
	5. Haryana, 
	6. Gujarat and 
	7. Madhya Pradesh are major producers.
	8. Bajra dominates arid-western regions; 
	9. jowar is important in the Deccan; 
	10. ragi is prominent in Karnataka and adjoining areas.
5. India's position in global production and consumption
	1. India is the world's largest millet producer. 
	2. Domestic consumption is rising through public procurement, nutrition programmes, urban demand and value-added products.
6. Challenges
	1. Low productivity, 
	2. weak processing and storage, 
	3. fragmented markets and consumer preference for polished rice and wheat.
	4. Limited assured procurement, 
	5. quality seed supply, 
	6. branding and organised value chains.
7. Solutions
	1. Invest in improved seed, extension, mechanisation, processing clusters, 
	2. FPOs and local procurement.
	3. Include millets in school meals, PDS where locally suitable, hospitals and public institutions.
8. Govt schemes
	1. National Mission on Nutri-Cereals, 
	2. NFSM, 
	3. PM POSHAN and PDS support production and consumption.
	4. India led the declaration of 2023 as the International Year of Millets. 
9. Conclusion
	- Mainstreaming millets through nutrition-sensitive procurement and innovation can advance SDG 2(Zero Hunger), farmer resilience and India's climate commitments.
