### Green Revolution
1. Intro
2. Evolution: from food importer to food exporter
3. Regions of production
4. Why bypassed eastern regions
5. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
	- The Green Revolution changed India from a food-deficit nation into a major producer, but its next phase must deliver nutrition without ecological exhaustion.
2. Evolution: from food importer to food exporter
	1. The 1960s crisis prompted high-yielding varieties, assured irrigation, chemical inputs, extension and procurement.
	2. Wheat transformed Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh; 
	3. rice expanded through irrigated regions.
	4. Public research, fertiliser support, minimum support prices and the Food Corporation of India enabled adoption.
	5. India is a leading producer of rice, wheat, milk, pulses and several horticultural commodities. **Source: FAOSTAT; Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Agricultural Statistics at a Glance.**
3. Regions of production
	1. Wheat: Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan.
	2. Rice: eastern and southern deltas, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.
	3. High-input concentration reflects irrigation, procurement, roads, electricity and market access.
4. Why bypassed eastern regions
	1. Small fragmented holdings, 
	2. flood and waterlogging risks, 
	3. weak irrigation and 
	4. limited mechanisation.
	5. Poor storage, roads, extension, credit and procurement reduced incentives despite fertile soils and abundant water.
	6. Social and institutional conditions slowed technology diffusion.
5. Conclusion
	- A climate-smart, eastern-region-focused Green Revolution can advance SDG 2, conserve resources and fulfil constitutional commitments to equitable development.
