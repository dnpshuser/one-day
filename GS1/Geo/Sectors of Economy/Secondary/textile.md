### Cotton textile industry
1. Intro
2. Evolution
3. India's position now
4. High productive regions
5. Factors for highly decentralised industry
6. govt initiatives
7. Conclusion
 
---

1. Intro
	- As India's second-largest employment generator, the textile sector underpins labor-intensive industrialization, female workforce participation, and global export competitiveness.
2. Evolution
	1. Handlooms and cotton centres grew around traditional skills, raw cotton, ports and trading towns.
	2. Colonial mills, post-independence public-sector expansion and later liberalisation reshaped the industry.
	3. It now spans cotton, synthetics, garments, technical textiles, handlooms and fashion value chains.
3. India's position now
	1. India is among leading producers of cotton, yarn and textiles; the sector is a major employer after agriculture.
	2. Textiles and apparel support exports, women’s employment and MSMEs. **Source: Ministry of Textiles, Annual Report 2023-24.**
4. High productive regions
	1. Gujarat and Maharashtra: cotton, spinning, weaving and ports.
	2. Tamil Nadu: integrated spinning, knitting and garment cluster.
	3. Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh: varied textile and handloom activities.
5. Factors for highly decentralised industry
	1. Raw-material diversity, dispersed markets, skilled labour, power, transport and entrepreneurial capital.
	2. Small production units, handloom traditions and flexible garment supply chains.
	3. Synthetic fibres reduce dependence on cotton geography.
6. govt initiatives
	1. PM MITRA Parks, 
	2. Production Linked Incentive scheme, 
	3. National Technical Textiles Mission and 
	4. Samarth skilling programme.
	5. Amended Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme and 
	6. National Handloom Development Programme. **Source: Ministry of Textiles.**
7. Conclusion
	- Scaling PM MITRA mega-parks and advancing high-value technical textiles are pivotal to anchoring India's 'Farm to Fashion' (5F) global market leadership.
