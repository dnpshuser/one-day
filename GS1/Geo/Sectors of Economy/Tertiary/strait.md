### Strait
1. Intro
2. Significance in international trade
3. Major busy straits of the world
4. Current Challenges
5. Mitigation measures
6. Conclusion
 
1. Intro
	- A strait is a naturally narrow water passage linking larger water bodies, making it a geographic chokepoint where commerce, security and diplomacy converge.
2. Significance in international trade
	1. Straits shorten sea routes and connect production centres, ports and consumer markets.
	2. Their control influences energy security, naval mobility, insurance costs and global supply chains.
	3. Malacca, Hormuz and Bab-el-Mandeb carry vital energy and container traffic.
3. Major busy straits of the world
	1. Strait of Malacca: Indian and Pacific Ocean link; major Asia-Europe route.
	2. Strait of Hormuz: Persian Gulf outlet and critical oil-export chokepoint.
	3. Bab-el-Mandeb: Red Sea-Gulf of Aden gateway.
	4. Bosporus-Dardanelles, Gibraltar, Dover and Sunda straits support regional and global trade.
4. Current Challenges
	1. Piracy, 
	2. terrorism, 
	3. accidents, 
	4. oil spills, 
	5. congestion and 
	6. geopolitical conflict.
	7. Climate change, extreme weather, naval rivalry and disruption of shipping raise vulnerability.
	8. Blockages can create inflation, 
	9. rerouting costs and 
	10. supply-chain shocks.
5. Mitigation measures
	1. Maritime domain awareness, 
	2. convoy or naval patrols, 
	3. search-and-rescue cooperation and 
	4. navigational safety.
	5. Diversify energy routes, 
	6. build strategic reserves, 
	7. improve port capacity and 
	8. maintain international freedom of navigation under UNCLOS.
6. Conclusion
	- Cooperative and resilient management of straits can safeguard SDG 9 trade, India's energy security and a stable rules-based maritime order.
