### East Africa Rift Valley
1. Intro
2. Factors driving East Africa rift valley
3. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
	- The East African Rift Valley is an active continental divergent boundary where Africa is gradually splitting into the Nubian and Somali plates.
2. Factors driving East Africa rift valley
	1. **Mantle upwelling:** Heat rising from the mantle creates doming, weakens and thins the overlying lithosphere.
	2. **Tensional forces:** The Nubian and Somali plates move away from each other, producing stretching and normal faulting.
	3. **Crustal thinning:** The crust fractures into fault blocks; the down-dropped central block forms a graben or rift valley.
	4. **Magma intrusion and volcanism:** Cracks allow basaltic magma to rise, causing volcanic activity and further weakening of the crust.
	5. **Triple-junction setting:** The rift system connects the eastern and western branches around the Afar Triangle, where the African, Arabian and Somali plates diverge.
	6. **Erosion and deposition:** Rivers and lakes occupy the subsiding troughs, while sediments accumulate in the rift basins.
	7. **Evidence:** The Ethiopian and Kenyan rifts contain active volcanoes, frequent earthquakes, elongated deep lakes and high geothermal potential, all indicating continuing crustal extension.
	8. **Future evolution:** If divergence continues, continental rupture may lead to seafloor spreading and the formation of a new ocean, as occurred in the Red Sea.
3. Conclusion
	- The East African Rift is a living laboratory of plate tectonics, whose geothermal, mineral and tourism potential must be developed through earthquake-aware and environmentally sensitive planning.
