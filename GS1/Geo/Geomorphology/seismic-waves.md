1. Introduction
   - Seismic waves are waves of energy that travel through the Earth's layers, resulting from earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or large man-made explosions.
   - They are the primary tool used by seismologists to study the Earth's interior structure, composition, and physical state.

2. P-Waves (Primary Waves)
   - **Nature:** Longitudinal or compressional waves where particles oscillate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.
   - **Speed:** Fastest of all seismic waves (~6-8 km/s in the crust), arriving first at seismograph stations.
   - **Medium:** Can travel through solids, liquids, and gases. Their speed changes as they pass through different materials.
   - **Analogy:** Similar to a sound wave or the compression-expansion motion of a slinky spring.

3. S-Waves (Secondary Waves)
   - **Nature:** Transverse or shear waves where particles move perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
   - **Speed:** Slower than P-waves (~3-4 km/s in the crust), arriving second at seismograph stations.
   - **Medium:** Can only travel through solids. They cannot propagate through liquids or gases (like the outer core), as fluids cannot support shear stress.
   - **Analogy:** Similar to the motion of a wave on a rope shaken from one end.

4. Surface Waves
   - **Formation:** Generated when P and S waves reach the Earth's surface. They are confined to the near-surface layers.
   - **Characteristics:** They are the slowest but have the largest amplitude, causing the most ground shaking and destruction during an earthquake.
   - **Types:**
     - **Love Waves:** Move the ground in a side-to-side, horizontal motion.
     - **Rayleigh Waves:** Create a rolling motion, moving the ground both up-and-down and side-to-side, similar to waves on water.

5. Shadow zones
   - **Definition:** Specific areas on the Earth's surface where seismographs do not detect direct seismic waves from an earthquake. Their existence provides crucial evidence about the Earth's interior.
   1. **P-Wave Shadow Zone:** A ring-like zone between **105° and 145°** from the epicenter. This occurs because P-waves are sharply refracted (bent) when they enter the liquid outer core.
   2. **S-Wave Shadow Zone:** A vast area beyond **105°** from the epicenter. This is because S-waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core, creating a large "shadow" on the opposite side of the Earth. This is the definitive proof of a liquid outer core.

6. Effects & Impacts
   - **Primary Impact:** Intense ground shaking leading to the collapse of buildings, bridges, and infrastructure.
   - **Secondary Impacts:**
     - **Liquefaction:** Saturated soil loses strength and behaves like a liquid, causing structures to sink or tilt.
     - **Landslides & Avalanches:** Triggered in hilly and mountainous terrain.
     - **Tsunamis:** Generated by large undersea earthquakes that displace the seafloor.
     - **Fires:** Caused by ruptured gas lines and downed power lines.

7. Mitigation measures
   - **Structural Measures:**
     - **Earthquake-Resistant Design:** Enforcing building codes (e.g., National Building Code of India) for new constructions.
     - **Retrofitting:** Strengthening existing vulnerable buildings and infrastructure.
     - **Base Isolation:** Using flexible bearings to separate a structure from the ground's motion.
   - **Non-Structural Measures:**
     - **Hazard Zonation Mapping:** Identifying and regulating land use in high-risk zones.
     - **Early Warning Systems:** Providing seconds to minutes of warning before strong shaking arrives.
     - **Public Awareness & Drills:** Educating communities on safety protocols (e.g., "Drop, Cover, and Hold On").

8. Conclusion
   - Seismic waves are not only destructive forces of nature but also invaluable scientific tools that have unveiled the layered structure of our planet. Understanding their behavior is fundamental for both advancing geoscience and developing effective disaster mitigation strategies to build resilient communities and save lives.
