3. Major Mountain ranges in the world - distribution
   - **Asia:** Himalayas (India, Nepal, China), Kunlun, Tian Shan, Zagros (Iran).
   - **Europe:** Alps (Central Europe), Pyrenees (France, Spain), Caucasus (Europe/Asia border).
   - **North America:** Rocky Mountains (USA, Canada), Appalachian Mountains (USA).
   - **South America:** Andes (runs along the western coast).
   - **Africa:** Atlas Mountains (North Africa), Drakensberg (South Africa).
   - **Australia:** Great Dividing Range (Eastern Australia).

4. Impact on local weather pattern
   - **Orographic Rainfall:** Force moist air to rise, cool, and condense, causing heavy rainfall on the windward side (e.g., Western Ghats).
   - **Rain Shadow Effect:** Create arid or semi-arid conditions on the leeward side (e.g., Deccan Plateau).
   - **Blocking Cold/Hot Winds:** Act as barriers, protecting regions from extreme air masses (e.g., Himalayas block cold Siberian winds from entering India).
   - **Anabatic & Katabatic Winds:** 
     - Anabatic winds are warm upslope breezes driven by daytime solar heating, 
     - katabatic winds are cold downslope flows caused by nighttime surface cooling and gravity
   - **Local Winds**: 
     - Chinook - snow eater on leeward side of Rockeys
     - Foehn - Alps mts(EU)

5. Fold mountains
   1. **Located along the plate margins - why?**
      - Formed at convergent plate boundaries where two tectonic plates collide. The immense compressional stress causes the crustal rocks to buckle, fold, and uplift, creating long, linear mountain ranges.
      - **Example:** The Himalayas were formed by the collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate.
   2. **Global distribution:**
      - **Circum-Pacific Belt ("Ring of Fire"):** Includes the Andes and the Rockies.
      - **Alpine-Himalayan Belt:** Extends from the Alps in Europe through the Middle East to the Himalayas in Asia.
   3. **Relation with Earthquakes and Volcanoes:**
      - **High Seismicity:** The ongoing stress and movement at convergent boundaries make these zones highly prone to earthquakes (e.g., Nepal earthquake 2015).
      - **Volcanic Activity:** In ocean-continent convergence, subduction of the oceanic plate leads to melting and magma formation, resulting in active volcanoes (e.g., Andes). Continent-continent collisions (like Himalayas) have less volcanism but intense seismic activity.

6. Conclusion
   - From triggering local Foehn winds to dictating macro-precipitation divides, mountain topography remains the fundamental architect of terrestrial micro-climates.
