### Water mass
1. Intro
2. Role in marine life
3. Role in coastal environment
4. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
   - A water mass is a large, identifiable body of ocean water with a distinct and uniform range of temperature and salinity, acting as tracers for ocean circulation.

2. Role in marine life
   - **Nutrient Distribution:** Deep water masses, like the Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW), are rich in nutrients (silicates, phosphates). As they slowly move and mix, they supply these essential nutrients to the upper ocean, fueling primary productivity.
   - **Oxygen Supply:** Cold, dense water masses formed at the poles (e.g., AABW, North Atlantic Deep Water) carry dissolved oxygen from the surface to the deep sea, making life possible in the ocean's depths.
   - **Habitat Formation:** The boundaries between different water masses (fronts) are often zones of high biological activity, as they concentrate plankton and attract larger predators.

3. Role in coastal environment
   - **Upwelling:** The movement of deep, cold, and nutrient-rich water masses towards the coast (coastal upwelling) creates highly productive ecosystems and supports major fisheries (e.g., the coast of Peru).
   - **Climate Moderation:** The properties of the water mass adjacent to a coast significantly influence its climate. A warm water mass will lead to a milder, wetter climate, while a cold water mass will result in a cooler, drier climate.
   - **Influence on Weather:** The temperature of coastal water masses can affect local weather phenomena, such as the formation of sea breezes and coastal fog.

4. Conclusion
   - Water masses are the fundamental components of oceanography, acting as massive reservoirs and transporters of heat, salt, oxygen, and nutrients.
