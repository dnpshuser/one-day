### Fjords
1. Intro
2. How do they form ?
3. Why are they so picturesque ?
4. examples
5. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
   - A fjord is a long, deep, narrow inlet of the sea with steep sides or cliffs, created by a glacier.
   - They are characteristic U-shaped valleys that have been inundated by the sea after the retreat of the glacier.

2. How do they form ?
   - **Glacial Scouring:** During an ice age, massive glaciers flow down pre-existing river valleys towards the sea.
   - **Deepening and Widening:** The immense weight and erosive power of the glacier carve the V-shaped river valley into a deep, steep-sided U-shaped trough, often extending far below sea level.
   - **Formation of a Sill:** The glacier deposits a pile of rock and debris (a terminal moraine) at its snout. This often forms a shallow underwater ridge, known as a sill or threshold, at the fjord's mouth.
   - **Inundation by Sea:** As the ice age ends, the glacier melts and retreats, and the global sea level rises, flooding the deeply carved valley to form the fjord.

3. Why are they so picturesque ?
   - **Dramatic Relief:** The stark contrast between the towering, near-vertical cliffs and the deep, calm waters of the inlet creates breathtaking scenery.
   - **Waterfalls:** Smaller tributary glaciers create "hanging valleys" high up on the fjord walls. Rivers flowing through these now create spectacular waterfalls that plunge directly into the sea (e.g., The Seven Sisters waterfall in Geirangerfjord).
   - **Reflective Waters:** The calm, sheltered waters often create mirror-like reflections of the surrounding snow-capped mountains and sky.
   - **Pristine Wilderness:** Fjords are typically found in remote, high-latitude regions, offering a sense of untouched, pristine natural beauty.

4. examples
   - **Norway:** Sognefjord (longest in Norway), Geirangerfjord (UNESCO World Heritage Site).
   - **New Zealand:** Milford Sound and Doubtful Sound in Fiordland National Park.
   - **Chile:** The fjords of the Patagonian coast.
   - **Greenland:** Scoresby Sund (the world's largest and longest fjord system).
   - **Canada:** Fjords along the coast of British Columbia and in Gros Morne National Park, Newfoundland.

5. Conclusion
   - Fjords are dramatic testaments to the immense erosional power of glaciers during past ice ages. Beyond their scenic value which drives significant tourism, they are important marine ecosystems. As unique geographical features, their preservation is vital in the face of climate change, which threatens the glaciers that feed them and the delicate balance of their ecosystems.
