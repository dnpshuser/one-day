### GOI: 1935

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the lengthiest and most detailed statute enacted by the British Parliament for colonial India. Derived from the Simon Commission Report (1930), the deliberations of the Three Round Table Conferences (1930–32), and the British White Paper of 1933, it served as the foundational administrative and structural blueprint for the Constitution of India (1950).

---

### Key Features and Clauses

| Category | Constitutional Provision & Mechanism | Practical Impact & Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All-India Federation** | Proposed a federation comprising **British Indian Provinces** and **Princely States** as constituent units. Accession for Princely States was voluntary via an *Instrument of Accession*. | **Never materialized.** Princely States refused to surrender their sovereign privileges, so the federal tier never came into operation; the Centre continued under the transitional provisions of the 1919 Act. |
| **Three Legislative Lists** | Divided legislative powers into three distinct tiers: • **Federal List:** 59 items (Defence, Foreign Affairs, Currency). • **Provincial List:** 54 items (Police, Education, Public Health). • **Concurrent List:** 36 items (Civil/Criminal Law, Marriage). | Formed the direct structural precedent for the **Seventh Schedule** of the current Indian Constitution. |
| **Residuary Powers** | Unlike modern federations, residuary legislative powers were vested neither in the Federal Legislature nor the Provinces, but placed solely in the hands of the **Governor-General** in his discretion. | Ensured final executive dominance of the British Crown over unanticipated or emerging legislative domains. |
| **Provincial Autonomy** | **Abolished Dyarchy at the provincial level** (introduced by the 1919 Act). Provinces were granted separate legal identities and freed from direct central superintendence in devolved subjects. Responsible government was introduced—ministers were accountable to elected provincial legislatures. | Led to the **1937 Provincial Elections**, resulting in Indian National Congress ministries forming governments in the majority of provinces. |
| **Dyarchy at the Centre** | Introduced dyarchy in the proposed Federal Executive by bifurcating federal subjects into: • **Reserved Subjects:** Defence, External Affairs, Ecclesiastical Affairs, Tribal Areas (administered solely by the Governor-General with appointed counselors). • **Transferred Subjects:** All other federal subjects (administered by a Council of Ministers responsible to the legislature). | Because the All-India Federation never took effect, this central dyarchical structure was never implemented. |
| **Bicameralism in Provinces** | Introduced bicameral legislatures (Legislative Council and Legislative Assembly) in **6 out of 11 provinces**: Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, and the United Provinces (UP). | Expanded legislative capacity and accommodation of landed and commercial elites in provincial upper chambers. |
| **Expansion of Communal Electorates** | Retained and expanded separate communal electorates to include **Depressed Classes (Scheduled Castes)**, **Women**, and **Labour (Trade Unions)**, in addition to existing provisions for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, and Anglo-Indians. | Intensified political fragmentation along sectional and communal lines, institutionalizing demographic divisions. |
| **Franchise Expansion** | Extended voting rights based on property, tax, and educational qualifications. | Expanded the electorate to approximately **10% to 14% of the total adult population** (around 30 million people). |
| **Federal Court** | Provided for the establishment of a **Federal Court** (set up in Delhi in 1937) consisting of a Chief Justice and up to six judges, to resolve inter-provincial disputes and interpret the 1935 Act. | Precursor to the modern **Supreme Court of India**; however, appeals still lay to the **Privy Council in London**. |
| **Financial & Institutional Machinery** | Mandated the establishment of: • **Reserve Bank of India (RBI):** To control currency, monetary policy, and public credit (established via the RBI Act, 1934 in April 1935). • **Federal & Provincial Public Service Commissions:** For administrative recruitment (modern UPSC and State PSCs). | Established the bureaucratic, administrative, and central banking infrastructure of the modern Indian state. |
| **Territorial Reorganisation** | • **Burma (Myanmar)** was formally separated from British India. • Two new provinces were carved out: **Sindh** (separated from the Bombay Presidency) and **Orissa** (separated from Bihar and Orissa). • **Aden** was detached from India and made a separate British Crown Colony. | Finalized external eastern frontiers and altered internal provincial boundaries. |
| **Abolition of the Council of India** | Abolished the 15-member **Council of the Secretary of State for India** established under the Government of India Act, 1858, replacing it with a small team of corporate advisers. | Reduced direct operational overhead in Whitehall while retaining overarching parliamentary control via the Secretary of State. |

### Executive Safeguards: The "Governor's Discretion"

Despite introducing responsible government at the provincial level, the Act retained sweeping emergency and discretionary powers for the British executive:

* The **Governor-General** and **Provincial Governors** held overriding "special responsibilities" (prevention of grave menace to peace, protection of minority interests, safeguarding the financial stability of the federation).
* Governors could withhold assent to bills, promulgate emergency ordinances, and dismiss elected provincial ministries under **Section 93** (the direct ancestor of **Article 356** / President's Rule in the current Constitution).
