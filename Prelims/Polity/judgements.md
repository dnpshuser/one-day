| Landmark Case | Year | Key Doctrine / Legal Ratio | Constitutional Articles / Laws Involved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras** | 1950 | Took a narrow, literal interpretation of Article 21; held that **"procedure established by law"** does not require the law to be just, fair, or reasonable (distinguished from American "due process of law"); viewed Fundamental Rights as mutually exclusive silos. *(Later overruled in Maneka Gandhi).* | Article 19, Article 21, Preventive Detention Act, 1950 |
| **State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan** | 1951 | Struck down communal reservations in medical and engineering colleges; held that **Fundamental Rights prevail over Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs)** in case of conflict. Led directly to the **1st Constitutional Amendment Act (Art 15(4))**. | Article 14, Article 15, Article 29(2), Article 46 |
| **Shankari Prasad v. Union of India** | 1951 | Upheld the 1st Amendment; ruled that Parliament's constituent power under **Article 368 includes the power to amend Fundamental Rights**; held that the word "law" in Article 13(2) refers only to ordinary legislative law, not constitutional amendments. | Article 13, Article 31A, Article 31B, Article 368 |
| **Berubari Union Case (In re)** | 1960 | Presidential Reference (Art 143); held that the **Preamble is not a part of the Constitution**; held that Parliament cannot cede Indian territory to a foreign state through an ordinary Article 3 law and requires a constitutional amendment under Article 368. Led to the **9th Amendment**. | Article 1(3)(c), Article 3, Article 368, Preamble |
| **K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra** | 1961 | Addressed grave and sudden provocation under Section 300 IPC; exposed vulnerability of juries to media sensationalism, leading directly to the **abolition of the jury trial system** in India via the CrPC. | Sections 300 & 302 IPC, Indian Penal Code / CrPC |
| **Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan** | 1965 | Upheld the validity of the 17th Amendment; reaffirmed *Shankari Prasad*; notable for the dissenting opinions of Justices J.R. Mudholkar and M. Hidayatullah, which first seeded the concept of the Constitution having "basic features." | Article 13, Article 31A, Article 368 |
| **I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab** | 1967 | An 11-judge bench ruled (6:5) that constitutional amendments under Article 368 fall within the scope of "law" under Article 13(2); held that **Parliament cannot amend or curtail Fundamental Rights**; applied the doctrine of prospective overruling. Led to the **24th Amendment**. | Article 13, Part III, Article 368 |
| **R.C. Cooper v. Union of India** *(Bank Nationalisation Case)* | 1970 | Struck down the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1969; rejected the "silo" theory of *A.K. Gopalan*; established the **"effect doctrine"**—the validity of a law is judged by its direct impact on fundamental rights rather than its formal legislative object. | Article 14, Article 19(1)(f), Article 31 |
| **Madhav Rao Scindia v. Union of India** *(Privy Purses Case)* | 1970 | Struck down the Presidential Order derecognizing the rulers of former princely states; held that privy purses and privileges were constitutional guarantees that could not be revoked by executive fiat. Led to the **26th Amendment**. | Article 291, Article 362, Article 366(22) |
| **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala** | 1973 | A 13-judge bench established the **Basic Structure Doctrine** (7:6); held that Parliament's amending power under Article 368 is vast but not unlimited—it cannot destroy or alter the foundational identity or basic structure of the Constitution; recognized the **Preamble as an integral part** of the Constitution. | Article 13, Article 31C, Article 368, Preamble |
| **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain** | 1975 | Struck down Article 329A(4) (introduced by the 39th Amendment), which insulated the election of the PM and Speaker from judicial review; **first application of the Basic Structure Doctrine**; affirmed that judicial review and free and fair elections are basic features. | Article 329A, Article 368, Basic Structure |
| **ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla** *(Habeas Corpus Case)* | 1976 | Held (4:1) that during a National Emergency under Article 352, the right to approach courts for enforcement of Article 21 is suspended. Celebrated for **Justice H.R. Khanna's lone dissent**, which asserted the rule of law. *(Overruled by 44th Amendment and formally in Puttaswamy).* | Article 21, Article 352, Article 359 |
| **Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India** | 1978 | Transformed Article 21: imported the American standard of **"substantive due process"**; held that procedure established by law must be **just, fair, and reasonable**, not arbitrary, fanciful, or oppressive; established the **"Golden Triangle"** (interconnectedness of Articles 14, 19, and 21). | Article 14, Article 19, Article 21, Passports Act, 1967 |
| **Sunil Batra v. Delhi Administration** | 1978 & 1980 | Expanded prisoners' rights; held that solitary confinement without judicial approval violates Articles 14, 19, and 21; affirmed that prisoners do not shed their fundamental rights at the prison gates; broadened locus standi for public interest interventions. | Article 14, Article 19, Article 21, Prisons Act |
| **Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar** | 1979 | Recognized the **Right to Speedy Trial** as an inalienable fundamental right implicit in Article 21; ordered the immediate release of over 40,000 undertrial prisoners languishing in Bihar jails; marked the institutional dawn of **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)** in India. | Article 21, Article 39A |
| **Minerva Mills v. Union of India** | 1980 | Struck down Clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368 (introduced by the 42nd Amendment) that gave unlimited amending power to Parliament and ousted judicial review; famously held that the Constitution is built on the **delicate balance between Part III (FRs) and Part IV (DPSPs)**. | Article 31C, Article 368, Basic Structure |
| **Waman Rao v. Union of India** | 1981 | Clarified application of the Basic Structure doctrine to the **Ninth Schedule**: laid down that all laws included in the Ninth Schedule **before April 24, 1973 (date of the Kesavananda verdict)** are immune, but laws added on or after that date are open to judicial review on basic structure grounds. | Article 31B, Article 368, Ninth Schedule |
| **S.P. Gupta v. Union of India** *(First Judges Case)* | 1981 | Held that in the appointment of judges under Articles 124 and 217, the word **"consultation" does not mean "concurrence"**; gave primacy to the Executive over the Chief Justice of India (CJI) in judicial appointments. | Article 124(2), Article 217(1) |
| **Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab** | 1980 | Upheld the constitutional validity of capital punishment under Section 302 IPC; formulated the **"Rarest of Rare Cases"** doctrine; mandated balancing aggravating and mitigating circumstances before awarding death sentences. | Article 14, Article 19, Article 21, Section 302 IPC |
| **Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation** | 1985 | Extended Article 21 to include the **Right to Livelihood**; held that evicting pavement and slum dwellers without notice or reasonable opportunity to be heard violates procedural due process, though municipalities retain power to remove encroachments. | Article 21, Article 19(1)(e), Bombay Municipal Corporation Act |
| **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India** *(Oleum Gas Leak Case)* | 1987 | Evolved the **Doctrine of Absolute Liability** (replacing the limited 1868 English rule of strict liability in *Rylands v. Fletcher*); held that hazardous enterprises carry an unconditional, non-delegable duty of care with no exceptions for act of God or third-party interference. | Article 21, Article 32 |
| **Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum** | 1985 | Ruled that Section 125 of the CrPC applies to all citizens regardless of religion; held that a divorced Muslim woman is entitled to maintenance beyond the *iddat* period if she cannot maintain herself. Led to the enactment of the *Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986*. | Section 125 CrPC, Article 44 |
| **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India** *(Mandal Case)* | 1992 | Upheld 27% reservation for Other Backward Classes (OBCs); capped **total reservations at 50%**; introduced the **Creamy Layer** exclusion; ruled that reservations cannot apply to promotions. Led to the **77th, 81st, and 85th Amendments**. | Article 16(1), Article 16(4), Article 340 |
| **Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association (SCARA) v. UOI** *(Second Judges Case)* | 1993 | Overruled *S.P. Gupta*; interpreted "consultation" as **"concurrence"**; vested primacy in the CJI and two senior-most judges of the Supreme Court in judicial appointments, formalizing the **Collegium System**. | Article 124(2), Article 217(1) |
| **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India** | 1994 | Curtailed the misuse of **Article 356 (President's Rule)**; held that the presidential proclamation is subject to judicial review; ruled that floor tests in the assembly are the only legitimate test of majority; declared **Secularism and Federalism as basic features**. | Article 356, Article 74(2), Basic Structure |
| **D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal** | 1997 | Formulated mandatory **11-point guidelines on arrest and detention** to prevent custodial violence and torture; mandated memo of arrest, right to notify a relative, and mandatory medical examination every 48 hours. | Article 21, Article 22, CrPC |
| **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan** | 1997 | Addressed workplace sexual harassment; invoked international conventions (CEDAW via Art 51) to frame the **Vishaka Guidelines** as binding law under Article 141 in the absence of enacted legislation; precursor to the **POSH Act, 2013**. | Article 14, Article 19(1)(g), Article 21, Article 141 |
| **Special Reference No. 1 of 1998** *(Third Judges Case)* | 1998 | Presidential Reference under Art 143; expanded the SC Collegium to include the **CJI and 4 senior-most puisne judges** for Supreme Court appointments, and CJI plus 2 senior-most judges for High Court appointments/transfers. | Article 124(2), Article 217(1), Article 143 |
| **T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka** | 2002 | An 11-judge bench recognized the right to establish and administer private educational institutions (both minority and non-minority) as a fundamental freedom under Article 19(1)(g); held that the state cannot impose excessive fee/admission controls on unaided private institutions. | Article 19(1)(g), Article 29, Article 30 |
| **P.A. Inamdar v. State of Maharashtra** | 2005 | Held that the State cannot enforce its reservation policy on private unaided minority or non-minority educational institutions. Directly prompted the **93rd Constitutional Amendment Act (Art 15(5))**. | Article 15, Article 19(1)(g), Article 30 |
| **M. Nagaraj v. Union of India** | 2006 | Upheld the validity of Articles 16(4A) and 16(4B) (reservation in promotions with consequential seniority); conditioned promotion quotas on three quantifiable criteria: backwardness, inadequacy of representation, and administrative efficiency (Art 335). | Article 16(4A), Article 16(4B), Article 335 |
| **I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu** | 2007 | A 9-judge bench unanimously held that any law inserted into the **Ninth Schedule after April 24, 1973** that violates the basic structure of the Constitution via Part III (specifically Arts 14, 19, 21) can be invalidated by courts. | Article 31B, Article 368, Ninth Schedule |
| **Selvi v. State of Karnataka** | 2010 | Held that non-consensual administration of **narco-analysis, polygraph (lie detector), and brain mapping tests** violates the right against self-incrimination under Article 20(3) and the right to privacy under Article 21. | Article 20(3), Article 21 |
| **Aruna Ramchandra Shanbaug v. Union of India** | 2011 | Permitted **passive euthanasia** (withdrawal of life support from patients in a permanent vegetative state) under strict High Court supervision; distinguished active euthanasia (illegal) from passive euthanasia. | Article 21 |
| **Lily Thomas v. Union of India** | 2013 | Struck down Section 8(4) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951; ruled that convicted MPs/MLAs sentenced to imprisonment of 2 or more years stand **immediately disqualified** from legislative bodies without the 3-month protection window. | Section 8(4) RPA 1951, Article 102(1)(e), Article 191(1)(e) |
| **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) v. UOI** | 2014 | Recognized **transgender persons as the "Third Gender"**; affirmed gender identity as an aspect of personal autonomy under Articles 14, 19, and 21; directed Central and State governments to treat them as socially and educationally backward classes. | Article 14, Article 15, Article 19(1)(a), Article 21 |
| **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India** | 2015 | Struck down **Section 66A of the Information Technology Act, 2000** for being vague, overbroad, and having a chilling effect on speech; distinguished between "discussion," "advocacy," and "incitement." | Article 19(1)(a), Article 19(2), Section 66A IT Act |
| **Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. UOI** *(NJAC Case / Fourth Judges Case)* | 2015 | Struck down the **99th Constitutional Amendment and the NJAC Act, 2014**; held that the inclusion of the Union Law Minister and two "eminent persons" undermined judicial independence, which is an inviolable basic feature. | Article 124A, Article 124B, Article 124C, Basic Structure |
| **Shayara Bano v. Union of India** | 2017 | Declared the practice of **Talaq-e-Biddat (instant triple talaq)** unconstitutional by a 3:2 majority; held it to be manifestly arbitrary and violative of Article 14, and not an essential religious practice under Article 25. | Article 14, Article 21, Article 25 |
| **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India** | 2017 | A 9-judge bench unanimously declared the **Right to Privacy** as an intrinsic fundamental right protected under Article 21; formally overruled the majority judgments in *A.K. Gopalan*, *M.P. Sharma*, and *ADM Jabalpur*. | Article 14, Article 19, Article 21 |
| **Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India** | 2018 | Decriminalized consensual homosexual sex among adults; read down **Section 377 IPC** to exclude consensual sexual acts between consenting adults; affirmed constitutional morality over majoritarian social morality. | Section 377 IPC, Article 14, Article 15, Article 19, Article 21 |
| **Joseph Shine v. Union of India** | 2018 | Struck down **Section 497 of the IPC (Adultery)** and Section 198(2) CrPC; held that treating women as the property of husbands infringes Articles 14 and 21; adultery remains a ground for civil divorce, not a criminal offence. | Section 497 IPC, Section 198(2) CrPC, Articles 14 & 21 |
| **Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala** *(Sabarimala Case)* | 2018 | Struck down Rule 3(b) of the Kerala Hindu Places of Public Worship Rules; held that barring women aged 10–50 from entering Sabarimala Temple violates Articles 14, 15, and 25; held that physiological traits like menstruation cannot be bases for discrimination. | Articles 14, 15, 21, 25, 26 |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta** | 2018 | Clarified *M. Nagaraj*; held that the state is not required to collect quantifiable data on backwardness for SC/ST promotions, but **creamy layer exclusion applies to SCs and STs** for promotional reservations as well. | Article 16(4A), Article 335 |
| **Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India** | 2020 | Ruled that freedom of speech and expression and freedom to practice trade/profession over the **internet are constitutionally protected under Article 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g)**; held indefinite internet suspensions illegal; mandated proportionality test and periodic review. | Article 19(1)(a), Article 19(1)(g), Telecom Suspension Rules |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (Review)** | 2022 | Refused to lay down any yardstick for determining adequacy of representation for SCs/STs in promotions; held that the assessment of under-representation must be based on the cadre as a whole rather than the total service strength. | Article 16(4A), Article 335 |
| **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India** *(EWS Quota Case)* | 2022 | Upheld the validity of the **103rd Constitutional Amendment** (3:2 majority); ruled that economic criteria alone can form the basis of classification; held that the 50% reservation ceiling is not immutable and applies strictly to caste-based quotas under Articles 15(4) and 16(4). | Articles 15(6), 16(6), Basic Structure |
| **Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India** | 2023 | Directed that Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and Election Commissioners be appointed by the President on the advice of a committee comprising the **Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition, and the Chief Justice of India** until Parliament enacted a law under Article 324(2). *(Parliament subsequently passed the CEC and other ECs Act, 2023, substituting CJI with a nominated Union Cabinet Minister).* | Article 324(2) |
| **Supriyo @ Supriya Chakraborty v. UOI** *(Same-Sex Marriage Case)* | 2023 | A 5-judge bench unanimously held that there is no unqualified fundamental right to marry; refused to read gender-neutral terms into the Special Marriage Act, 1954; left the decision to recognize same-sex marriage to Parliament and state legislatures. | Articles 14, 19, 21, Special Marriage Act, 1954 |
| **In re Article 370 of the Constitution** | 2023 | A 5-judge Constitution bench unanimously upheld the presidential notifications aborogating the special status of **Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370**; confirmed that Art 370 was an interim, transitional arrangement; upheld the reorganisation of Ladakh as a Union Territory while directing the restoration of statehood to J&K. | Article 370, Article 356, Article 3 |
| **Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) v. UOI** *(Electoral Bonds Case)* | 2024 | A 5-judge bench unanimously struck down the **Electoral Bond Scheme, 2018** and corresponding amendments to the Representation of the People Act, Companies Act, and Income Tax Act; held that unlimited, anonymous political donations violate the voters' **Right to Information under Article 19(1)(a)**. | Article 19(1)(a), Section 29C RPA 1951, Companies Act |
| **State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor of Punjab** | 2023 | Clarified the scope of **Article 200**; held that a Governor cannot sit on bills passed by the State Legislature indefinitely or withhold assent without immediately sending the bill back to the House for reconsideration; underscored that a Governor is a titular head and cannot throttle lawmaking. | Article 200, Article 163 |
| **State of Uttar Pradesh v. Jai Bir Singh** | 2024 | Upheld the expansive definition of "industry" under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 laid down in the 1978 *Bangalore Water Supply* case; confirmed that institutions like hospitals, universities, and statutory bodies fall within industrial dispute resolution mechanisms. | Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 |
| **Mineral Area Development Authority (MADA) v. Steel Authority of India** | 2024 | An 8:1 majority held that **States have the legislative power to tax mineral rights** under Entry 50 of the State List; held that royalty paid under Section 9 of the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957 is not a tax; empowered state governments to recover past mineral tax dues subject to specified caps. | Entry 50 List II, Entry 54 List I, MMDR Act 1957 |
| **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh** | 2024 | A 7-judge bench (6:1) held that States have the constitutional competence to **sub-classify Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes** to extend affirmative action benefits to the most disadvantaged groups within those categories (overruling *E.V. Chinnaiah*); clarified that sub-classification must be based on empirical data and cannot 100% exclude any group. | Article 14, Article 16(4), Article 341 |
