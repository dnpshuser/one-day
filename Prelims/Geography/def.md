1. A gyre is a large system of rotating ocean currents connected to global wind patterns and Earth's rotation.
   - five major subtropical gyres: the North Atlantic, South Atlantic, Indian, North Pacific, and South Pacific. 
2. A hanging glacier is a mass of ice that clings to a steep mountain slope or cliff edge, ending abruptly before reaching the valley floor
3. Primordial heat is the leftover thermal energy accumulated in a planet during its first few million years of formation and evolution.
4. Rheological: how matter flows with physical characteristics like 
      1. Viscosity
      2. Elasticity
      3. Viscoelasticity
5. The neritic zone is the shallow part of the ocean that extends from the low-tide mark down to about 200 meters (660 feet) over the continental shelf.
6. A geostrophic flow or wind is a theoretical movement of air or water where the horizontal pressure gradient force is balanced perfectly by the Coriolis effect due to Earth's rotation.
7. Rossby waves, also known as planetary waves, are giant meanders in high-altitude winds and ocean currents caused by the rotation of the Earth
8. Jet streams do not maintain a uniform speed along their path; they feature localized pockets of maximum wind velocity known as jet streaks.
9. Coastal reclamation is the process of turning shallow water, tidal marshes, or seabeds into dry, usable land
10. Hydrothermal vents are deep-sea hot springs that form rocky chimneys and rich deposits of polymetallic sulphides when superheated, mineral-laced water meets cold ocean water
11. Pyroclastic material is a collective term for any volcanic rock fragment that is blasted into the air or carried by explosive volcanic eruptions.
