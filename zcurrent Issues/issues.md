## CEC: Disinfranchisement
1. Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar is facing demands for a criminal investigation following a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) filed in the Supreme Court of India. [1] 
The legal and political controversy stems from a series of severe allegations regarding internal irregularities and unilateral decision-making at the [Election Commission of India (ECI)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/plea-in-supreme-court-says-cec-gyanesh-kumar-can-be-probed-and-tried-like-an-ordinary-accused/article71507762.ece): [2, 3] 

* 
* Internal Dissent and Secret Decisions: An investigative report published by The Indian Express in September 2026 revealed that the other two Election Commissioners, Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, formally recorded objections at least 14 times over 10 months. They alleged that major constitutional decisions were being made by CEC Gyanesh Kumar without their knowledge or concurrence. [3, 4] 
* Voter Deletion Controversy (SIR): The primary point of contention involves the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. Critics and opposition parties point out that over 13 crore names have been removed from draft voter lists, raising massive concerns over voter exclusion and citizen disenfranchisement. [5, 6] 
* Unilateral Form 6 Alterations: The [Indian National Congress](https://www.facebook.com/Timesnow/posts/congress-has-accused-chief-election-commissioner-gyanesh-kumar-of-knowingly-viol/1609165561254700/) has accused Kumar of knowingly violating legal and constitutional protocols by unilaterally changing Form 6 (the application form used for new voter registration). [7] 
* Supreme Court Petition: A petition filed on September 25, 2026, requests the Supreme Court to form an independent, high-powered Judicial Inquiry Commission or a Special Investigation Team (SIT) to criminally prosecute Kumar under the Representation of the People Act. The plea argues that his statutory protection does not cover acts that subvert democratic processes. [1, 2] 
* 

The Official Response:
The [Election Commission of India](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cqkgw68yjg29o) has denied allegations of an internal rift. The ECI issued a statement maintaining that all final decisions were taken unanimously, framing the internal friction as standard democratic debate and healthy, diverse input-sharing during the decision-making process. [1, 8]
