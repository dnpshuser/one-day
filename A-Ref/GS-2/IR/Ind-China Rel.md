# Conflict regions: Doklam, Ladakh and Galwan

Doklam, [Galwan](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11jnxckklq), and Ladakh represent key flashpoints in the strategic military standoffs and border tensions between India and China along the Himalayas. [1, 2] 

## Overview of Key Regions

* Ladakh (Eastern Ladakh / Line of Actual Control): The broader high-altitude region where major, prolonged military standoffs occurred starting in May 2020, driven by infrastructure disputes like the Darbuk-Shyok-DBO road and differing perceptions of the Line of Actual Control (LAC). [3, 4, 5] 
* [Galwan Valley](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11jnxckklq): A specific site within eastern Ladakh where a brutal, hand-to-hand clash took place on June 15, 2020, resulting in the deaths of 20 Indian soldiers and several Chinese casualties in a fight involving primitive non-firearm weapons. [5, 6, 7, 8, 9] 
* Doklam: A strategic plateau located at the tri-junction of India, Bhutan, and China, which was the site of a tense 73-day military standoff in 2017 after Chinese troops attempted to build a road in territory disputed between Bhutan and China. [7, 10] 

## Strategic Significance and Evolution

* Rules of Engagement: The [2017 Doklam Standoff](https://www.defencexp.com/india-china-border-doklam-galwan-analysis/) marked a robust physical intervention by the Indian Army on behalf of Bhutan, whereas the 2020 Galwan clash permanently altered India's tactical approach to border management, infrastructure building, and strategic preparedness along the LAC. [6, 7] 
* Disengagement: Following intense military and diplomatic dialogues spanning multiple rounds of Corps Commander talks, progressive disengagement processes have de-escalated active friction points in Eastern Ladakh, though complete restoration to pre-2020 norms remains partial. [3, 9]
