# Women’s Political Representation: 73rd/74th to 106th CAA

## 1. Intro

* **Evolving** from grassroots governance to parliamentary parity, the trajectory of women’s representation redefines the democratic destiny of the nation.
* **Anchoring** this shift requires moving from tokenistic mandates to structural metamorphosis, ensuring legislative empathy outshines patriarchal stagnation.

## 2. Features/Qualities

* **Grassroots** mandate under the 73rd and 74th CAA structurally institutionalized 33% reservation in PRI and ULBs.

* **National** elevation via the 106th (CAA) secures 33% reserved seats in the LS and SLA.

* **Intersectional** one-third horizontal sub-quota exclusively for SC and ST women within already reserved constituencies.

* **Conditional** implementation suspends actual enforcement until the completion of the next decadal Census and subsequent Delimitation commission exercise.

* **Sunset** capped lifespan at 15 years, requiring explicit parliamentary re-authorization for any continuation.

* **Rotational** mandates ensure reserved constituencies periodically shift across the map, preventing long-term electoral stagnation in specific districts.

## 3. Sub components of the topic [most imp]

* **Article 330A: LS** legally mandates the reservation of one-third of total Lok Sabha seats filled by direct election for women.

* **Article 332A: SLA** similar provisions to SLA.

* **Article 334A** linked the law's operationalization strictly to the post-census delimitation exercise.

* **Article 239AA** extends this 33% women’s reservation to the LA of NCTD.

* **Omission** of the Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Councils leaves the Upper Houses entirely immune to mandatory gender parity.

## 4. Evolution timeline

* 1974: CSWI Report
The Committee on the Status of Women in India publishes "Towards Equality," sparking the demand for grassroots panchayat reservations.


* 1992: 73rd & 74th Amendments
33% reservation in village PRI and ULB


* 1996: First Legislative Attempt
The 81st CAB is introduced but lapses due to the dissolution of the Lok Sabha.


* 2010: Rajya Sabha Passage
The Upper House passes the Women's Reservation Bill, but fierce coalition pressure over OBC sub-quotas prevents Lok Sabha tabling.


* 2023: 106th Constitutional Amendment
The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam is passed in a Special Session, legally securing 33% reservation subject to the delimitation freeze.


* 2026: Failed Delimitation Bill
The 131st CAB attempts to decouple reservation from the census by increasing Lok Sabha seats to 850, but fails to secure a special majority and is withdrawn.

# QC-2

### 1. Context & Contemporary Triggers

The debate on women’s political representation has shifted from grassroots experimentation to parliamentary constitutional design. While the **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992)** created a critical mass of over 1.4 million elected women representatives at the local level, women’s presence in the Union Parliament has hovered between 14% and 15% (Lok Sabha) and roughly 13% (Rajya Sabha), ranking India in the bottom half globally.

The contemporary flashpoints driving this theme include:

* **Enactment of the 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*):** Inserting **Articles 330A, 332A, and 334A**, it reserves one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies, and the Legislative Assembly of the NCT of Delhi.
* **The "Sunrise Conditionality" of Article 334A:** The amendment contains a deferral mechanism: the quota will become operational only **after** an exercise of delimitation is undertaken based on the relevant figures of the first Census conducted after the commencement of the Act. With the decennial Census delayed, operationalization is deferred toward the late 2020s.
* **The Delimitation & Federal Friction:** Southern states that successfully managed population growth fear that a post-Census delimitation will penalize them in parliamentary seat distribution vis-à-vis higher-fertility northern states. Tying women’s reservation to delimitation has entangled gender equity with Centre-State federal discord.
* **UPSC CSE Mains 2026 Core Demand (GS-II, Q2):** *"Women as a class neither belong to a minority group nor are they regarded as forming a backward class." In the light of this statement, discuss the evolution of women's reservation from local bodies to Parliament.*

#### A. The Constitutional Progression

* **Local Self-Government (Articles 243D & 243T):** Mandated not less than **one-third** reservation for women in seats directly elected, as well as in the offices of Chairpersons. Over 20 states (e.g., Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala) raised this threshold to **50%**.
* **Union & State Legislatures (106th Amendment Act, 2023):**
* **Article 330A:** Reserves one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha, including a horizontal sub-quota for women belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes within their reserved seats.
* **Article 332A:** Extends the one-third reservation to all State Legislative Assemblies.
* **Article 239AA(2)(ba):** Extends the reservation to the Legislative Assembly of the NCT of Delhi.
* **Article 334A:** Imposes a dual prerequisite: reservation takes effect only after the first Census conducted post-commencement is published and the subsequent delimitation exercise is completed. It provides a **15-year sunset clause**, subject to parliamentary extension, with seat rotation after each delimitation.



#### B. Theoretical Anchor: Descriptive vs. Substantive Representation

* **Descriptive Representation (Hanna Pitkin):** The composition of a legislature should physically mirror the demographic characteristics of the electorate. Women constitute ~48.5% of the population; their presence is a demand of democratic legitimacy.
* **Substantive Representation:** The degree to which elected representatives champion policies advancing women's rights, bodily autonomy, and welfare.
* **The "Non-Minority / Non-Backward Class" Distinction:** As highlighted in Constituent Assembly debates and reaffirmed in the 2026 paper, women are not an isolated demographic minority or a distinct socio-economic class living in separated enclaves. They cut across all castes, religions, and classes. Consequently, women's quotas function as a **horizontal mechanism of democratic inclusion** rather than standard backward-class compensatory affirmative action.

### 3. Core Dimensions & Applied Frictions

#### A. The PRI Experience: Substantive Gains vs. "Sarpanch Pati" Syndrome

Over three decades of local governance provide concrete empirical evidence of how quotas operate:

* **The Transformative Gains (The Esther Duflo & Raghabendra Chattopadhyay Study):** Empirical research across West Bengal and Rajasthan demonstrated that women leaders in Panchayats invest significantly more in public goods directly aligned with women’s priorities—notably potable drinking water, primary sanitation, maternal healthcare, and primary school attendance.
* **The "Proxy Politics" Bottleneck:** In many patriarchal rural pockets, elected women face the **"Sarpanch Pati" (or "Pradhan Pati") phenomenon**, where husbands or male relatives perform official functions, sign records, and chair meetings, reducing women to rubber-stamping figureheads.
* **Institutional Maturation:** Longitudinal data shows that by their second or third term, women sarpanches consistently break free from proxy control, negotiate with local bureaucracies, and challenge entrenched caste hierarchies.

#### B. The Delimitation Linkage: The "Sunset before Sunrise" Dilemma

Under Article 334A, women’s reservation is held hostage to two distinct administrative events:

$$\text{Notification of 106th CAA} \longrightarrow \text{Conduct of Census} \longrightarrow \text{Delimitation Commission} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement of Quotas}$$

* **The Stagnation Hazard:** If the Census and delimitation are delayed, the implementation of the reservation risks being postponed past multiple general elections.
* **Federal Apportionment Flashpoint:** The 84th Amendment (2001) froze the total number of Lok Sabha seats based on the 1971 Census until after the first Census taken after 2026. If delimitation is carried out strictly on updated population figures, northern states will gain seats while southern states (which effectively implemented population control) stand to lose relative representation. Tying women’s reservation to this redistricting ties gender equity to federal friction.

#### C. The Sub-Quota Debate & The OBC Exclusion

* **The SC/ST Horizontal Reservation:** The 106th Amendment reserves one-third of the seats already earmarked for SCs and STs under Articles 330 and 332 for SC/ST women.
* **The OBC Exclusion:** Unlike the recommendations of the **Geeta Mukherjee Committee (1996)**, the 106th Amendment does not include a specific sub-quota for women belonging to Other Backward Classes. Opponents argue that without an OBC quota, unreserved seats will disproportionately benefit socio-economically privileged and urban-educated women, undermining intersectional equity. Proponents note that the Constitution contains no vertical reservation for OBCs in the Lok Sabha or State Assemblies to subdivide in the first place.

#### D. The Rotation Disincentive

Under Article 334A, seats reserved for women will be rotated after each subsequent delimitation exercise.

* **Erosion of Accountability:** If sitting legislators know their constituency will be rotated out and reserved (or de-reserved) in the next cycle, their incentive to nurture their local electorate and complete long-gestation developmental projects is diminished, fostering a **"lame-duck" phenomenon**.

### 4. Structural Bottlenecks & Counter-Perspectives

| Bottleneck / Critique | Institutional Friction | Systemic Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Omission of Upper Chambers** | The 106th Amendment excludes the **Rajya Sabha** and **State Legislative Councils**. | The Council of States—designed as an elder, deliberative body—remains sheltered from gender equity norms. |
| **Inner-Party Gatekeeping** | Indian political parties operate as centralized, high-command-driven machines. | Parties rarely field women in unreserved constituencies (~9–10% of total candidates fielded in recent general elections), treating 33% as a ceiling rather than a floor. |
| **Elite & Dynastic Capture** | Absence of institutionalized inner-party primaries or transparent candidate selection. | Risk that tickets in reserved constituencies will go to wives, daughters, or relatives of established male political leaders ("Dynastic Proxies"). |
| **Alternative Model: Party-Based Quotas vs. Seat Quotas** | Organizations like the *Forum for Democratic Reforms* (led by Jayaprakash Narayan) argued for statutory party quotas. | Instead of reserving constituencies, amend the **Representation of the People Act, 1951** to mandate that parties give 33%–50% of their tickets to women, preserving voter choice while forcing parties to build female leadership cadres. |

### 5. Reforms & Forward-Looking Roadmap

#### A. Decoupling Reservation from Delimitation

* **Immediate Implementation:** Parliament could pass an amendment to remove the linkage in Article 334A that predicates enforcement on the post-Census delimitation.
* The 33% quota could be rolled out using the existing, frozen 2008 delimitation boundaries via a transparent lottery system drawn by the Election Commission of India.

#### B. Reforming Political Party Nominations

* **Mandatory Ticket Distribution (The French *Parité* Model):** Amend **Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951** to mandate that political parties nominate a minimum of 33% women candidates across state and national elections, backed by loss of tax-exemption status or withdrawal of party election symbols for non-compliance.

#### C. Addressing Proxy Governance at Grassroots Levels

* **Statutory Disqualification of Proxies:** States should amend Panchayati Raj Acts to define proxy interference (*Sarpanch Pati*) as an offense leading to disqualification, mirroring provisions adopted by states like Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan.
* **Capacity and Budgetary Empowerment:** Establish institutionalized leadership academies for first-time elected women representatives, paired with direct control over untied development funds.

#### D. Inclusion of the Upper Chambers

* Introduce a constitutional amendment to provide a quota or an indirect proportional representation framework for women in the Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Councils.
