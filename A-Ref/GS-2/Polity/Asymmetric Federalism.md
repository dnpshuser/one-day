# Asymmetric Federalism: Article 371, 5th/6th Schedules, and SCS

## 1. Intro

* **Asymmetric Federalism** proves that  Indian Constitution is not a rigid monolith, but a highly flexible ecosystem designed to accommodate extreme sub-national diversity.
* **Constitutional Architecture** operationalizes this asymmetry through A-371 and  5th/6th Schedules, *brilliantly balancing* national integration with fierce regional autonomy.

## 2. Features/Qualities

* **Fifth Schedule (Article 244(1))** grants  Governor extraordinary discretionary powers to override parliamentary laws to protect 
  * tribal land, 
  * culture, and 
  * administrative autonomy across 10 designated states.

* **Sixth Schedule (Article 244(2))** creates highly 
  * autonomous District Councils (ADCs) 
  * with independent legislative, judicial, and financial powers exclusively in 
  * four norastern states (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram).

* **Article 371 Series** highly specific tailored constitutional solutions ranging from regional development boards in Maharashtra to religious protections in Nagaland.

* **Special Category Status (SCS)** provides asymmetric fiscal federalism by granting favorable 90:10 funding ratios for centrally sponsored schemes to structurally disadvantaged hilly and border states.

* **Symmetrical Foundation** despite asymmetric privileges,  core constitutional rights and  basic parliamentary federal structure remain uniformly applicable to all citizens.

* **Governor’s Discretionary Sweep** primary constitutional vehicle for enforcing asymmetric provisions bypassing  elected state cabinet in specific scheduled areas.

## 3. Sub components of  topic

* **Article 371A (Nagaland)** prohibits parliamentary laws from interfering with Naga customary law, social practices, and  absolute ownership/transfer of indigenous land.

* **Article 371J (Karnataka)** separate statutory development board and explicit employment/education quotas for backward Kalyana Karnataka region.

* **Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)** under 6th Schedule function as 'mini-states' with  constitutional power to collect 
  * land revenue, 
  * manage forests 
  * constitute customary village courts.

* **Tribes Advisory Councils (TACs)** 
  * 5th Schedule mandate that tribal MLAs advise Governor, 
  * legally ensuring representative tribal input in state governance and welfare programs.

* **PESA Act 1996 Interface** 
  * extends 73rd Amendment to Fifth Schedule areas, 
  * empowering local Gram Sabhas with absolute veto power over minor minerals and corporate land acquisition.

## 4. Evolution timeline

* 1950: Constitutional Adoption
  * 5th and 6th Schedules are formally incorporated into  Constitution, 
  * heavily based on  integration blueprint of  Gopinath Bardoloi Committee.


* 1962: 13th Constitutional Amendment
  * Article 371A is inserted to guarantee absolute cultural and territorial autonomy to Nagaland, 
  * successfully defusing violent secessionist insurgencies.


* 1969: Gadgil Formula & SCS 
  * National Development Council introduces Special Category Status (SCS) to provide asymmetric, highly subsidized central assistance to disadvantaged border states.


* 1996: Enactment of PESA 
  * Panchayat (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act is passed, 
  * granting Gram Sabhas in Fifth Schedule areas 
  * asymmetric power to govern their own natural resources.


* 2012: 98th Constitutional Amendment
  * Article 371J is inserted to create asymmetric employment and educational reservations specifically for  Hyderabad-Karnataka (Kalyana Karnataka) region.


* 2015: 14th Finance Commission Verdict
  * abolishes Special Category Status for general states, 
  * asymmetric grants => symmetrically untied tax devolution of 
  * 32 => 42%.


## 5. Economic perspective

* **Untied Fiscal Devolution** (currently 41% under 15th FC) shifted  asymmetric funding model from discretionary political grants => formalized, statutory tax transfers.

* **Resource Curse Mitigation** 

* **Market Fragmentation Risks** A-371 provisions (like absolute land ownership restrictions in Himachal Pradesh or Nagaland) prevent pan-India capital investment and corporate industrialization.

* **Fiscal Pacification Strategies** 
  * tailored financial packages instead of granting permanent Special Category Status to stabilize politically volatile regions (like Andhra Pradesh and Bihar in 2024)

* **Budgetary Starvation** frequently paralyzes Sixth Schedule ADCs because hostile state governments deliberately delay transferring  mandated central funds to  autonomous councils.

## 11. Flow chart

**The Governor's Fifth Schedule Override Mechanism**

1. **Legislation:** Parliament or State Legislature officially passes an Act.
2. **Assessment:** Governor assesses the Act's impact on indigenous populations in Tribal Scheduled Areas.
3. **Consultation:** Governor initiates mandatory consultation with the state's Tribes Advisory Council (TAC).
4. **Discretionary Action:** Governor explicitly utilizes Article 244(1) extraordinary discretionary power.
5. **Notification:** Governor issues a public notification completely blocking or heavily modifying the Act's application in the Scheduled Area.

## 14. Advantage/Merits/Role

* **Secessionist Defusion** successfully integrated highly volatile, armed insurgencies in the Northeast (e.g., the Mizo Accord 1986) into the constitutional framework by guaranteeing absolute cultural autonomy.

* **Ecological Preservation** empowers ADCs and Gram Sabhas to legally block reckless corporate deforestation and mining in environmentally hyper-sensitive Himalayan and tribal belts.

* **Customary Law Protection** ensures that indigenous dispute resolution systems are constitutionally insulated, saving marginalized tribals from the slow, expensive, and alien formal judiciary.

* **Targeted Economic Re-engineering** via Article 371J forces SG to legally allocate budgets and employment exclusively to historically neglected sub-regions, curing internal state inequalities.

* **Federal Flexibility** proves that the Constitution is not a rigid colonial legacy, but a highly adaptable social contract capable of micro-managing hyper-local ethnic diversity.

## 15. Disadvantage/De-Merits

* **State Within a State Friction creating constant clashes btw SG and ADC** 

* **Economic Balkanization(A-371) blocking outer capital investment keeping permanent dependency on Central** 

* **Ethnic Compartmentalization deepens dangerous "Us vs Them" mentalities. eg: Manipur ethinic clashes**

* **Governance Deficits** rampant corruption within ADCs, lacking bureaucratic capacity, auditing mechanisms, and transparency.

* **Gender Discrimination** protected under the guise of "customary law" 
  * e.g., denying women ancestral inheritance rights, 
  * placing asymmetric cultural rights in direct conflict with Article 14 (Equality).

## 16. Challenges

* **Overlapping Jurisdictions btw SG and ADC paralyzing effective law enforcement**

* **The Funding Squeeze cripples ADCs to depend on State's mercy without a mandated Fin Comm**

* **Erosion of PESA** SG bypassing mandatory GS consent through executive loopholes for rapid corporate land acquisition remains a chronic crisis.

* **The Domino Effect** granting 6th Schedule status to Ladakh or SCS to Bihar triggers copycat agitations in regions like Gorkhaland, Bundelkhand, and Odisha threatening federal stability

## 17. Way forward/solution/positive outlook

* **Direct Funding Mechanisms** Consider amending the 6th Schedule - allowing central funds to reach ADCs directly to bypass politically hostile state executives.

* **Codification of Customary Law** 
  * to ensuring asymmetric federalism harmonizes perfectly with basic fundamental rights weeding out patriarchal, anti-women practices

* **Empowering Governor's Discretion** through strict Presidential guidelines (as recommended by the Punchhi Commission) 
  * to ensure Governors 5th Schedule veto protects marginalized tribals rather than playing partisan politics.

* **Replacing SCS with the Rajan Formula** will permanently depoliticize asymmetric funding, 
  * utilizing a transparent 'Multi-Dimensional Backwardness Index' to allocate central grants based on empirical need, not *coalition blackmail*.

## 18. Best practices

* **Canadian Comprehensive Land Claims Agreements** (e.g. the *Nunavut territory*) offer a brilliant global blueprint for granting indigenous populations absolute territorial and administrative sovereignty over natural resources.

* **Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC)** massive legislative powers over 40 state subjects to successfully quell violent ethnic separatism.

* **Spanish Asymmetric Revenue Model** (*The Basque Quota*) - autonomous region collect its own taxes and pay a fixed quota to the centre - eliminate fiscal friction.

## 19. Other dimensions relevant to the topic but not covered in the previous pointers

* **Inter-State Council (Article 263)** remains criminally underutilized; 
  * it must be resurrected as the primary constitutional theater for resolving the intense socio-political frictions generated by asymmetric federalism.

* **Climate Change Vulnerability** leading to higher demands for specialized disaster-relief fund to handle catastrophic glacial bursts and extreme floods.

* **Urbanization of Tribal Belts** creates unprecedented legal chaos, 
  * as the Sixth Schedule was designed for rural agrarian tribes, completely failing to address the complexities of modern, cosmopolitan tribal cities like Shillong or Aizawl.

## 20. Conclusion

* **Embracing** asymmetric federalism is not a constitutional compromise, but a political masterstroke to bind regional diversity into national unity.

* **Balancing** fierce regional autonomy with unyielding national integration ensures that India's diversity remains its greatest strength, delivering a **flexible, formidable, federation.**

#### A. oretical Anchor: Charles Tarlton's Model of Asymmetry

* **Symmetrical Federalism:** All constituent sub-units possess identical constitutional status, territorial integrity, and powers vis-à-vis  central government (e.g., US Senate equal representation).
* **Asymmetrical Federalism:** Sub-units possess unequal constitutional powers, protections, and institutional relationships with  Centre.
* **De Jure (Constitutional) Asymmetry:** Explicit constitutional provisions granting special powers to specific territories (Articles 371–371J, Fifth and Sixth Schedules).


* **De Facto (Political/Fiscal) Asymmetry:** Disparities arising from economic size, geographical isolation, population volume, and fiscal bargaining power (e.g., Special Category Status, discretionary Article 282 transfers).

| Constitutional Article | Target Territory / State | Nature of Asymmetry & Protective Safeguards |

### 3. Core Dimensions & Applied Frictions

#### A. Fifth Schedule vs. Sixth Schedule (Article 244)

Article 244 establishes two distinct models of sub-state self-governance for tribal areas:

```
                  FIFTH SCHEDULE vs. SIXTH SCHEDULE
                   (Sub-State Tribal Governance)

        FIFTH SCHEDULE [Art. 244(1)]         SIXTH SCHEDULE [Art. 244(2)]
     ┌───────────────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────────────┐
     │ • Applies to 10 States        │   │ • Applies to 4 States (AMTM:  │
     │   (Central/Peninsular India)  │   │   Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura,  │
     │ • Integrationist Approach     │   │   Mizoram)                    │
     │ • Executive Dominance:        │   │ • Autonomy / Self-Rule Model  │
     │   Governor acts via advisory  │   │ • Institutionalized Autonomy: │
     │   Tribes Advisory Council     │   │   Autonomous District         │
     │   (TAC)                       │   │   Councils (ADCs)             │
     │ • Law-filtering power:        │   │ • Devolved Powers:            │
     │   Governor can bar/modify     │   │   Make laws on land, forests, │
     │   Acts of Parliament/State    │   │   customary courts, and levy  │
     │   Legislatures.               │   │   local taxes.                │
     └───────────────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────────────┘

```

1. ** Fifth Schedule Dysfunction:**
* **Tribes Advisory Councils (TAC):** Envisaged to guide  Governor, TACs meet infrequently and are often dominated by  ruling political party of  State.
* ** Governor's Inactive Discretion:** Although  Governor has  power under Para 5(1) to declare that any central or state law shall not apply to a Scheduled Area, this power is rarely exercised independently of  state cabinet. This inaction has contributed to issues such as displacement and  spread of Left-Wing Extremism (LWE).


* **PESA Dilution:**  Panchayat (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 was enacted to extend grassroots democracy to Fifth Schedule areas. However, several states delayed framing rules or created parallel administrative bodies that dilute  mandatory powers of  Gram Sabha regarding land acquisition, minor forest produce, and mining leases.




2. ** Sixth Schedule Mechanics & Contemporary Friction:**
* **Autonomous District Councils (ADCs):** Sixth Schedule areas feature legislative, administrative, and judicial bodies (ADCs and Regional Councils) with up to 30 members.
* ** Legislative Filtering Power:** Under Paragraph 12/12A/12AA, laws made by  State Legislature or Parliament do not automatically apply to an ADC area if y conflict with council laws on devolved subjects, unless approved by  ADC or modified by  Governor.
* **State Executive Conflict:** State governments frequently withhold financial grants, delay  conduct of ADC elections, and resist devolving executive departments to  councils.



#### B.  Ladakh Standoff: Sixth Schedule vs. UT Bureaucracy

* ** Strategic and Demographic Context:** Following  Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, Ladakh became a Union Territory without a legislature. Over 97% of its population is tribal (Balti, Beda, Bot, Brokpa, Changpa, Garra, Mon, Purigpa).


* ** Legal Impasse:**  Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has resisted granting Sixth Schedule status, pointing out that Article 244(2) was historically crafted exclusively for  "tribal areas in  States of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram" (AMTM states).  Centre has offered high-powered committees and job/land reservations under administrative rules instead.


* ** Counter-Argument:** Protesters point to Bodoland (Assam) and  NCST's 2019 recommendation, which formally advised  Union Government to bring Ladakh under  Sixth Schedule to safeguard its cultural identity and land tenure.

### 5. Reforms & Forward-Looking Roadmap

#### A. Passage of  Constitution (125th Amendment) Bill

* **Direct Fiscal Devolution:** Amend  Sixth Schedule to mandate that  **Central Finance Commission recommend financial allocations directly to ADCs**, bypassing state finance ministries.
* **Strengning Grassroots Democracy:** Establish elected Village Councils within ADCs to prevent power from being concentrated solely among elite tribal council leaders.
* **Anti-Defection Application:** Extend Tenth Schedule provisions to ADCs to stabilize local governance.



#### B. Punchhi Commission on Centre-State Relations (2010)

* **Reactivating  Governor's Discretion in Schedule V:**  Governor's power under  Fifth Schedule should be treated as an explicit, independent constitutional duty exercised in consultation with  TAC, free from binding state ministerial advice.
* **Consultation on Article 371 Amendments:** Any proposal to alter or dilute special provisions under Article 371 must require mandatory prior consultation with  affected State Legislative Assemblies.

#### C. Moving Beyond SCS: Rule-Based Fiscal Equalization

* Rar than reviving  politicized "Special Category Status,"  **Sixteenth Finance Commission** should institutionalize targeted equalization grants:
* **Targeted Capital Deficit Grants:** Providing direct capital expenditure support to states with low infrastructure indices.
* **Climate & Disaster Risk-Weighted Devolution:** Factoring recurring vulnerability to floods and sea-level rise directly into  horizontal tax devolution formula.
