
## Intro

* Governors sitting on bills indefinitely (unwritten "pocket veto") has created a severe choke point in Centre-State relations, paralyzing the legislative mandate of democratically elected state governments escalated constitutional crisis.

* * Article 213 empowers the Governor to promulgate Ordinances during the recess of the State Legislature, when "circumstances exist which render it necessary" to take immediate action.

* Ordinance promulgating power(A-213) has been routinely abused through the practice of "re-promulgation" which the Supreme Court has heavily condemned as a "fraud on the Constitution."

* Under Article 200 of the Constitution, a Bill passed by a State Legislature must be presented to the Governor, who can assent, withhold assent, return it (if not a Money Bill), or reserve it for the President.

* The Governor is the constitutional head of the State executive (Part VI, Articles 153-167) serving as a vital link btw C&S Gov.


## Sub-components of the topic
* **Appointment and Tenure (Articles 155 & 156):** Appointed by President;  Holds office *during the pleasure of the President*

* **Executive Powers:** 
  * All executive actions of the state are taken in their name. 
  * They appoint the CM, other ministers, and the Advocate General (Article 164 & 165).
* **Legislative Powers (Article 200 & 201):** 
  * The power to 
    * grant assent, 
    * withhold assent, 
    * return a bill (if not a Money Bill),
    * reserve it for the consideration of the President; 
      * Reserving a bill that endangers the position of the State High Court is mandatory.
* * **The "As Soon As Possible" Clause(A-200):** 
  * the Governor "may, as soon as possible... return the Bill" if it is not a Money Bill. crux of the legal battle over indefinite delays. 
  * No prescribed time limit(30, 60 or 90 days) - weaponised silence
* * **The Mandatory Assent (Repassage):** Gov returns a bill and the SL passes it again (with or without amendments), the Governor *shall not* withhold assent.
* * **Article 201 (Presidential Consideration):** Gov reserves the bill: The President can 
  * assent, 
  * withhold, or 
  * return the bill. 
  * Unlike the Governor, if the state repasses the bill, the President is *still* not bound to give assent.
* Unelected, Centrally nominated stalling the legislative agenda of elected Government
* * **The Invalid "Withhold then Reserve" Route:**

* **Ordinance Making Power (A-213):** promulgates ordinances when the SL is not in session;
* Gov to act on the advice of CoM(A-163)
* 6 weeks since reassembly
* Can't promulgate ord, if similar bill req Presidential assent(HC pos)
* **The Abuse - "Re-promulgation":** creating a parallel sys of permanent law making.

* **Pardoning Power (Article 161):** 
  * Can grant 
    * pardons, 
    * reprieves, or 
    * remissions for offenses against state laws. 
    * Unlike the President, they cannot pardon a death sentence or court-martial.
* **Discretionary Powers (Article 163):** 
  * reserving bills, 
  * seeking information from the CM(A-167), or 
  * recommending President's Rule under Art 356. 
  * The validity of their discretion cannot be questioned in court.
* **Special Responsibilities (Article 371 Series):** Specific Governors have special responsibilities for regional development (e.g., Vidarbha in Maharashtra, tribal areas in Assam), where they act independently of the state cabinet.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* 1935: Government of India Act: created the office of the Provincial Governor with massive discretionary powers to act as an agent of the imperial center


* 1949: Constituent Assembly Debates: Elected vs Appointed Gov(avoids parallel centres' of power)

* 1979: Hargovind Pant vs. Dr. Raghukul Tilak: Governor is an independent constitutional office, not an "employee" or subordinate of the Central Government, despite being appointed by them.

* 1988: Sarkaria Commission: 
  * Governor should not act contrary to the advice of the CoM 
  * should dispose of bills within a reasonable timeframe.

* 1994: S.R. Bommai Judgment: The SC curtailed the misuse of the Governor's report to dismiss state governments (Article 356) - "ruling that the majority of a government must be tested on the floor of the House, not in the Raj Bhavan"

* 2010: Punchhi Commission
  * highlighted "pocket veto" misuse and recommended a strict, legally binding 6-month time limit for Governors to decide on bills.


* 2010: B.P. Singhal vs. Union of India: SC- while the President can remove a Governor at any time ("pleasure doctrine"), the removal cannot be arbitrary, capricious, or purely for political reasons (e.g., a change of government at the Centre).


* 2022-2023: The Federal Flashpoints
KL, TN, TG PB to SC: Governors for sitting on crucial bills (some pending for over 3 years) ranging from public health to university administration.


* 2023: State of Punjab vs. Principal Secretary to Governor: 
  * SC- Governor cannot "veto" a state legislature's bill by simply sitting on it indefinitely; 
  * if they withhold assent, they must return it to the assembly "as soon as possible."

* Dec 2023: The Tamil Nadu Clarification
SL re-passes the bill, the Governor cannot *then* reserve it for the President.

## Committee or Reports

* **Sarkaria Commission (1988):** 
  * eminent person from outside the state, a detached figure, 
  * not intimately connected with the local politics of the state,
  * not a recently active politician.
  * communicate reasons if reserving a bill to SL
* **Punchhi Commission (2010):** 
  * fixed tenure of 5 years; 
  * "Doctrine of Pleasure" -> impeachment process like SC/HC Judge.
  * strict 6 months time limit to act on bills
* **Venkatachaliah Commission (NCRWC, 2002):** 
  * Appointing Committee: PM, HM, Speaker, LoP and CM
  * Fixed time limit to decide on bills
    * 4 months to Gov
    * 6 months to President 
* **1st ARC, '68:** 356 sld be objective and reserve bills for President only in exceptional cases, not routinely.


## Global comparison

* **Australia:** The federal government has no role in their appointment. State Governors are appointed by the Monarch on the advice of the State Premier (Chief Minister). 
* **United States:** 
  * Governors are independent of the Federal President directly elected by people.
  * 10 day window to sign or veto a bill
  * No action => assent to the bill => becomes an Act
* **Canada:** 
  * Gov appointed by cabinet similar to Indian model.
  * Strictly Act on the advice of Provincial Premier(CM)
* **UK**: Monarch assent is a pure formality since 1707
