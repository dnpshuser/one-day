Following the 2011 Aruna Shanbaug (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F080n_ww&q=Aruna+Shanbaug) verdict, India’s passive euthanasia and end-of-life autonomy laws evolved dramatically. The legal landscape moved from a cumbersome court-driven process to recognizing the "Right to Die with Dignity" as a Fundamental Right under Article 21 of the Constitution. [1] (https://www.scobserver.in/cases/common-cause-euthanasia-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity-case-background/), [2] (https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/passive-euthanasia-in-india-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity)
The transition took place through two landmark updates in the Common Cause v. Union of India case, legalizing and streamlining Living Wills (Advance Medical Directives). [1] (https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/passive-euthanasia-in-india-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity), [2] (https://www.scobserver.in/cases/common-cause-euthanasia-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity-case-background/)

Understanding Living Wills (Advance Medical Directives)

An Advance Medical Directive is a legal document written by a person of sound mind detailing their preferences for medical treatment if they become terminally ill or lose the ability to give consent. [1] (https://www.scobserver.in/journal/supreme-court-review-2023-right-to-life/), [2] (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7450298), [3] (https://midhati.com/blog-right-to-die-living-will-amd-in-india/)

How to Execute a Living Will (As of the 2023 Guidelines)

1. Drafting: The document must clearly state under what specific conditions (e.g., permanent vegetative state, terminal illness) life-support should be withheld or withdrawn. It should also name a trusted healthcare representative (surrogate) to make decisions if the individual cannot speak. [1] (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7450298), [2] (https://lawtarazoo.com/blog/living-will-india)
2. Witnessing: It must be signed in the presence of two independent witnesses. [1] (https://lawtarazoo.com/blog/living-will-india)
3. Attestation: Instead of waiting on a court magistrate, the individual can now easily have it authenticated by a Notary Public or a Gazetted Officer. [1] (https://lawtarazoo.com/blog/living-will-india)
4. Record-keeping: The document is handed over to family members and forwarded to the local Judicial Magistrate’s office to be stored in digital records. [1] (https://lawtarazoo.com/blog/living-will-india)

How a Living Will is Activated

If an individual enters an irreversible terminal state, a strict verification process is triggered to prevent any misuse: [1] (https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/passive-euthanasia-in-india-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity)
• Primary Medical Board: Formed by the treating hospital (including the treating physician and two specialists), they review the patient's condition. [1] (https://midhati.com/blog-right-to-die-living-will-amd-in-india/)
• Secondary Medical Board: The hospital sets up a second independent board (including a doctor nominated by the district Chief Medical Officer) to review and endorse the primary board’s findings. [1] (https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/passive-euthanasia-in-india-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity), [2] (https://midhati.com/blog-right-to-die-living-will-amd-in-india/)
• Implementation: If both boards conclude recovery is impossible, the hospital informs the local Judicial Magistrate, and life support can be legally withdrawn according to the patient's wishes. [1] (https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-allows-first-passive-euthanasia-permits-withdrawal-of-life-support-for-man-in-vegetative-state-525943), [2] (https://midhati.com/blog-right-to-die-living-will-amd-in-india/)

Practical Milestone: The Harish Rana Case (2026)

The practical execution of these guidelines reached a milestone in Harish Rana v. Union of India (2026) (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fg%2F11z16xgjft&q=Harish+Rana+v.+Union+of+India+%282026%29). In this case, the Supreme Court authorized passive euthanasia for a 32-year-old man who had been in an irreversible persistent vegetative state for 13 years. The court explicitly ruled that Clinically Assisted Nutrition and Hydration (CANH) qualifies as medical treatment, and withdrawing it in hopeless cases aligns with the patient's best interest and right to die with dignity. [1] (https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-allows-first-passive-euthanasia-permits-withdrawal-of-life-support-for-man-in-vegetative-state-525943), [2] (https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-editorials/passive-euthanasia-in-india-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity)
Detailed analysis and context regarding the Common Cause cases can be found on the Supreme Court Observer. [1] (https://www.scobserver.in/cases/common-cause-euthanasia-and-the-right-to-die-with-dignity-case-background/)
