## 1. Intro

* Recent Draft UGC Regulations 2025 in context of state varsity laws have led to questions on appropriate use
of delegated legislation as well as federalism

* **Definition-Driven:** Delegated legislation represents the transfer of technical rule-making authority from the Legislature to the Executive, bounded strictly by the parent statute to prevent executive autocracy.

* **Burning Current Issue:** The escalating friction over ordinance re-promulgation and the judicial striking down of arbitrary executive notifications violently highlights the modern crisis of the executive usurping primary legislative domains.

## 2. Features/Qualities

* **Essential Legislative Function Protection** 
  * guarantees that Parliament must lay down the core policy framework, 
  * explicitly forbidding the total abdication of its law-making duty to the bureaucracy.
* **Strict Subordinate Status** 
  * legally mandates that executive rules, regulations, and bylaws can never override, expand, or violate the boundaries established by the parent parliamentary act.
* **Conditional Legislation Mechanism** 
  * allows the legislature to pass a complete law while 
  * empowering the executive merely to determine the exact geographic area or timing of its enforcement.
* **Emergency Ordinance Lifespan** 
  * ensures that executive ordinances exist purely as temporary gap-fillers, 
  * rigidly capped at a maximum of 6 months plus 6 weeks unless ratified by the legislature.
* **Absolute Judicial Reviewability** 
  * subjects all ordinances, 
  * statutory orders, and 
  * executive notifications to 
  * rigorous constitutional scrutiny and the test of substantive *ultra vires*.
* **Procedural Ultra Vires Vulnerability** means delegated legislation can be struck down by courts if the executive fails to follow mandatory pre-publication or consultation procedures outlined in the parent act.

## 3. Sub components of the topic

* **Primary Parent Legislation** 
  * represents the skeletal parliamentary law providing the foundational guiding framework, objectives, and boundaries for executive action.
* **Subordinate Rules and Regulations** 
  * comprise the detailed executive mandates (like IT Rules or Environmental Clearances) that govern daily corporate and civic compliance.
* **Statutory Orders and Notifications** 
  * are specific, highly targeted executive directions enforcing the *parent act in real-time administrative scenarios.*
* **The Henry VIII Clause** 
  * is a highly dangerous, exceptional provision allowing the executive to modify or override the parent parliamentary act itself to "remove difficulties."
* **Ordinance-Making Power (Art 123/213)** 
  * represents the emergency executive authority to enact binding primary legislation when legislative houses are prorogued or not in session.
* **The "As May Be Prescribed" Provision** is the standard statutory loophole used at the end of bills, effectively transferring sweeping, undefined punitive powers to future executive rule-makers.

## 4. Evolution timeline

* 1951: In Re Delhi Laws Act
The SC definitively establishes that the legislature cannot completely abdicate its essential legislative function to the executive.


* 1960: Hamdard Dawakhana Case
The Court strikes down sweeping executive powers to add diseases to a prohibited advertising list, ruling it a violation of the non-delegation doctrine.


* 1976: KSEB v. Indian Aluminium Co.
The SC formally establishes the strict boundaries of the ultra vires doctrine(beyond the power), ruling executive notifications cannot transcend the parent act.


* 1987: D.C. Wadhwa Verdict
A landmark ruling declares the mechanical repromulgation of ordinances by state governors without facing the legislature as a blatant "fraud on the Constitution."


* 2016: Cellular Operators Association Case
The SC applies the test of "manifest arbitrariness" to specifically strike down delegated legislation (TRAI call drop penalty regulations).


* 2017: Krishna Kumar Singh Verdict
A historic 7-judge bench outlaws ordinance repromulgation entirely, ruling that failing to table an ordinance is a constitutional abuse, and rights created by lapsed ordinances do not automatically endure.


## 5. Articles

* **Article 123 (Union Executive) / 213 (State Executive)**
  * ordinances during the recess of Parliament.
* **Article 13(3)(a)** explicitly defines the term 'law' to comprehensively include any ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, or notification, subjecting them all to Fundamental Rights scrutiny.
* **Articles 245 and 246** 
  * establish the primary legislative competence of Parliament and State Assemblies, 
  * implicitly prohibiting the unconstitutional transfer of this sovereign competence to the bureaucracy.

## 6. Courts landmark judgements

* **In Re Delhi Laws Act (1951):** 
  * Established the foundational non-delegation doctrine,
  * Parliament must lay down an "intelligible principle" and cannot delegate essential policy-making.
* **Dwarka Prasad Laxmi Narain v. State of UP (1954)**
  * Executive cannot use delegated legislation to bypass the legislature’s essential functions.
* **D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1987):** 
  * The SC ended the Bihar government's practice of ruling via 256 repromulgated ordinances, 
  * declaring the circumvention of the legislature unconstitutional.
* **Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017):** 
  * The 7-judge bench decisively ruled that placing an ordinance before the legislature is mandatory
  * actions taken under a lapsed ordinance automatically dies.
* **Cellular Operators Association of India v. TRAI (2016):** 
  * The Court expanded judicial review by allowing delegated legislation (TRAI regulations) to be struck down if found to be "manifestly arbitrary" under Article 14.
* **Madras Bar Association v. Union of India (2021):** 
  * The SC struck down executive rules governing tribunals, 
  * severely reprimanding the executive for attempting to bypass previous judicial rulings via subordinate legislation.

## 13. Flow chart

**The Mechanism of Delegated Legislation & Judicial Veto**

1. **Primary Enactment:** Parliament passes a skeletal Parent Act leaving technical details to the Executive.
2. **Executive Drafting:** The concerned Ministry drafts specific Rules/Regulations under the Act's authority.
3. **Official Notification:** The Executive publishes the rules in the Official Gazette to enforce them on citizens.
4. **Parliamentary Scrutiny:** Rules are laid before the Committee on Subordinate Legislation for review (often delayed or ignored).
5. **Judicial Challenge:** A citizen files a writ petition arguing the rule is arbitrary or exceeds the Paren t Act.
6. **Judicial Veto:** The Constitutional Court strikes down the executive notification as *ultra vires* or manifestly arbitrary.

## 16. Need/Importance/Necessity

* **Technical Expertise Imperative:** 
  * Modern governance involves highly complex scientific, financial, and digital issues (like AI regulation) that generalist MPs cannot realistically codify in rigid primary laws.
* **Emergency Responsiveness:** 
  * Unprecedented crises (pandemics, economic crashes, national security threats) demand instantaneous legal responses via ordinances or emergency notifications that slow parliamentary procedures cannot provide.
* **Administrative Flexibility:** 
  * Delegated legislation allows the state to experiment, tweak, and adjust socio-economic rules in real-time based on immediate ground feedback without requiring a full parliamentary amendment.
* **Pressure on Parliamentary Time**
  * Legislature formulates general policy and delegates details to executive.
* **Flexibility**
  * Allows timely response to changing needs without lengthy amendment processes.
* **Experimentation**
  * Executive can trial rules before full-scale implementation.
* **Implementation Efficiency** 
  * Local bodies better understand regional issues, enabling tailored governance.


## 17. Effect and Impact

* **Erosion of Parliamentary Sovereignty:** 
  * The legislative branch is systematically reduced to a mere rubber-stamp 
  * while the un-elected permanent bureaucracy becomes the actual, de facto lawmaker of the nation.
* **Democratic Deficit:** 
  * Executive rules and ordinances are drafted in secret bureaucratic silos, 
  * completely lacking the public consultation, media scrutiny, and opposition debate inherent in the parliamentary process.
* **Judicial Overburdening:** 
  * Because parliamentary scrutiny of subordinate rules is hopelessly weak, 
  * the entire burden of auditing thousands of arbitrary executive notifications falls upon an already backlogged SC.
* **Impact on Parliamentary Democracy**
  * Positive impact: Allows Parliament to focus on major policy issues; enables efficient and responsive governance.
  * Negative impact: Risk of executive overreach; dilution of parliamentary sovereignty; potential for reduced public scrutiny and debate.
  * Democratic deficit: If not properly controlled, can undermine accountability and transparency, leading to “bureaucratic legislation
* **Impact on Separation of Powers**
  * Traditional view: Separation of powers is blurred as the executive performs legislative functions.
  * Modern reality: Complete separation is impractical; delegation is a pragmatic necessity for effective governance.
  * Constitutional balance: While delegation is permitted, essential legislative functions (policy-making) must remain with the legislature. Courts ensure that delegation does not amount to abdication of legislative responsibility


## 18. Achievements

* **Rapid Pandemic Response:** 
  * Delegated legislation (via the Epidemic Diseases Act and DMA) successfully allowed the executive to rapidly enforce nationwide health mandates, saving countless lives during critical COVID-19 waves.
* **Financial Market Agility:** 
  * The dynamic, delegated rule-making power of SEBI and the RBI has successfully insulated the Indian financial markets from catastrophic global crashes by enabling overnight regulatory tightening.
* Disaster Management instant Action capability

## 19. Advantage/Merits/Role

* **Decongesting Parliament:** 
  * Massively saves thousands of hours of parliamentary time by stripping out hyper-technical administrative debates, allowing the House to focus exclusively on broad national policy.
* **Localized Application:** 
  * Executive notifications allow national laws to be tailored specifically to different geographic, cultural, or economic zones through flexible, staggered enforcement dates.
* **Crisis Management Lifeline:** 
  * Ordinances act as the ultimate constitutional safety valve, ensuring the state apparatus is never legally paralyzed when Parliament is prorogued.

## 20. Disadvantage/De-Merits

* **The Henry VIII Abuse:** 
  * Executive clauses that allow bureaucrats to alter the parent parliamentary act itself structurally destroy the foundational doctrine of separation of powers.
* **Bureaucratic Tyranny:** 
  * Empowers unaccountable joint secretaries to draft coercive, penalizing rules that dramatically affect civic liberty, free speech, and corporate survival.
* **The Fait Accompli Trap:** 
  * By the time an ordinance is actually tabled in Parliament, it has already altered facts on the ground, 
  * forcing the legislature to grudgingly approve it to prevent administrative chaos.

## 21. Challenges

* **Toothless Parliamentary Committees:** 
  * The Committee on Subordinate Legislation lacks the technical staff, time, and political teeth to review the sheer avalanche of thousands of executive notifications issued annually.
* **Post-Facto Ratification Injustice:** 
  * Executive rules often come into force immediately upon gazette notification, 
  * meaning citizens suffer the consequences for months before Parliament or courts can ever review them.
* **Vague "As May Be Prescribed" Clauses:** 
  * Parent acts increasingly use hopelessly broad phrasing, 
  * essentially writing a blank cheque to the executive to define criminal offenses and penalties arbitrarily.

## 22. Way forward/solution/positive outlook

* **Pre-Legislative Consultation Policy (PLCP):** 
  * The executive must rigidly enforce a *mandatory 30-day public consultation window* before publishing any substantive delegated rule, ensuring democratic civic input.
* **Empowering Scrutiny Committees:** 
  * The Parliamentary Committee on Subordinate Legislation must be massively upgraded with independent legal and technical experts to audit every executive notification instantly.
* **Strict "Essential Legislative Function" Audit:** 
  * The SC must routinely strike down skeleton bills that fail to outline an "intelligible principle," 
  * forcing Parliament to actually write the core law.
* **Automatic Ordinance Lapsing:** 
  * Constitutional amendments should explicitly state that any actions taken under an ordinance are immediately voided if the ordinance fails to secure legislative ratification.

## 23. Best practices

* **UK Affirmative Resolution Procedure:** 
  * Requires that highly sensitive delegated legislation cannot come into legal force until it is explicitly debated and actively approved by both Houses of Parliament.
* **US Administrative Procedure Act (APA):** 
  * Legally mandates strict "notice-and-comment" periods for all executive agency rulemaking, 
  * allowing citizens and corporations to heavily contest arbitrary draft rules before they are finalized.
* **South African Constitutional Court Scrutiny:** 
  * Has developed highly aggressive jurisprudence striking down executive regulations that attempt to bypass the deliberative process of the National Assembly.

## 24. Other dimensions relevant to the topic

* **Federal Friction via Notifications:** 
  * The Union government frequently uses obscure statutory notifications under the Concurrent List to quietly override state-level administrative domains without risking a full parliamentary showdown.
* **Media Censorship via Rules:** 
  * The executive increasingly prefers bypassing primary legislation by using delegated IT rules to enforce backdoor censorship and digital content takedowns, avoiding parliamentary free-speech debates.

## 25. Conclusion

* **Forward-Looking Vision:** Harmonizing executive administrative agility with Parliamentary legislative supremacy is the definitive path to secure a **principled, progressive, polity.**

* Its legitimacy lies not in convenience but in constitutional fidelity—to the rule of law, parliamentary oversight, and the citizen’s right to be governed by accountable laws. 

* As India’s administrative complexity deepens, the challenge is to wield delegation not as a shortcut, but as a carefully monitored tool that serves, rather than supplants, the legislature.


* **Institutional Accountability:** Eradicating the culture of skeleton legislation requires elevating parliamentary scrutiny of subordinate rules from a procedural formality into a rigorous administrative reality.

* **Constitutional Restraint:** Enforcing the strict mandates of *Krishna Kumar Singh* ensures that emergency ordinance powers never permanently subvert the deliberative democratic mandate.
