# One Nation One Election

## Intro
"One Nation, One Election" (ONOE) proposes synchronizing the electoral cycles of the Lok Sabha and all State Legislative Assemblies so they are held simultaneously.

* **Handling Premature Dissolution:** In the event of a hung parliament/assembly or a successful no-confidence motion, fresh elections will be held, but the new House will serve *only for the remainder (unexpired term)* of the original 5-year cycle, ensuring synchronization is not broken.
* **Constitutional Amendments:** Implementing ONOE requires amending 
  1. A-83 - Duration of House of Parliament, 
  2. A-85 - Session of Parliament, prorogation and dissolution, 
  3. A-172- Duration of State Legislature, 
  4. A-174- Session of State Legislature, prorogation and dissolution, 
  5. A-356- Clauses of Presidential Rule.
* **State Ratification:** Amendments regarding local bodies (Article 324A) and the single electoral roll (Article 325) will require ratification by at least 50% of the states as they alter the federal structure.

## Procedure
1. Synchronization of LS and SLA: Presidents notification of Appointed Date(1st Sitting of new LS): SLA formed after this date will have truncated tenures to expire alongside LS 5 year term.
2. Synchronization of Local body elections within 100 days of national/state elections
3. Single electoral roll under A-325
4. Handling Premature Dissolution: New House will serve only for the remainder of original 5 year cycle.
5. Constitutional Amendments and State Ratification: A-83,85,172,174 and 356

## 1. Intro

* "One Nation, One Election" (ONOE) proposes synchronizing the electoral cycles of the Lok Sabha and all State Legislative Assemblies so they are held simultaneously, either on a single day or within a specific time frame.
* Recently, the Union Cabinet (September 2024) approved the recommendations of the High-Level Committee (HLC) chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind to implement this, aiming to replace the current asynchronous, perennial election cycle that keeps the country in constant campaign mode.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **The Transition Mechanism:** The Kovind Committee proposes the President will notify an "Appointed Date" (the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha). State assemblies formed after this date will have their tenures truncated to expire alongside the Lok Sabha's 5-year term.
* **Two-Step Approach:** Step 1 involves synchronizing Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Step 2 requires synchronizing local body elections (Panchayats and Municipalities) to be held within 100 days of the national/state elections.
* **Single Electoral Roll:** Amending Article 325 to empower the Election Commission of India (ECI), in consultation with State Election Commissions, to prepare a common electoral roll and single Elector’s Photo Identity Card (EPIC) for all three tiers.
* **Handling Premature Dissolution:** In the event of a hung parliament/assembly or a successful no-confidence motion, fresh elections will be held, but the new House will serve *only for the remainder (unexpired term)* of the original 5-year cycle, ensuring synchronization is not broken.
* **Constitutional Amendments:** Implementing ONOE requires amending at least five core constitutional articles, primarily dealing with the duration and dissolution of the Houses (e.g., Articles 83, 85, 172, 174, and 356).
* **State Ratification:** Amendments regarding local bodies (Article 324A) and the single electoral roll (Article 325) will require ratification by at least 50% of the states as they alter the federal structure.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* 1951-1967: The Golden Era of ONOE
India naturally held simultaneous elections for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies in 1951-52, 1957, 1962, and 1967.


* 1968-1970: The Cycle Breaks
The cycle was broken due to the premature dissolution of some State Assemblies in 1968 and 1969, followed by the premature dissolution of the Lok Sabha in 1970 by Indira Gandhi.


* 1999: Law Commission's 170th Report
The Law Commission of India explicitly recommended reverting to simultaneous elections to curb election expenditure and administrative paralysis.


* 2015: Parliamentary Standing Committee
The Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice tabled a report heavily advocating for ONOE in two phases.


* March 2024: Kovind Committee Report Submitted
The High-Level Committee submitted an 18,000-page report outlining the exact constitutional amendments and transitional mechanisms required to implement ONOE.


* September 2024: Union Cabinet Approval
The Union Cabinet formally approved the Kovind Committee's recommendations, paving the way for the introduction of twin constitution amendment bills.


* December 2024: Bills Introduced & JPC Formed
The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha. A 39-member Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) was formed to examine the bills and interact with state CMs.

## 5. Committee or Reports

* **The Ram Nath Kovind High-Level Committee (2024):** The most definitive and actionable report on the subject. It established that asynchronous elections fuel inflation, slow down economic growth, and upset social harmony. It provided the exact legal architecture (Two-Step approach, Appointed Date, unexpired term rule) to bypass the constitutional roadblocks of premature dissolution.
* **Law Commission of India (170th Report, 1999):** Pioneered the modern push for ONOE, stating, "This cycle of elections every year, and in the out of season, should be put an end to."
* **NITI Aayog Discussion Paper (2017 - Bibek Debroy & Kishore Desai):** Argued that simultaneous elections would lead to a saving of over Rs 10,000 crores for the exchequer and massive unquantifiable savings for political parties, curbing the influence of black money.
* **Parliamentary Standing Committee (79th Report, 2015):** Highlighted the "policy paralysis" caused by the prolonged enforcement of the Model Code of Conduct (MCC), severely halting welfare schemes.

## 6. Indices

*(Note: ONOE is a domestic procedural reform, but it impacts factors measured by these global indices)*

* **Democracy Index (Economist Intelligence Unit):** India scores highly on the "Electoral Process" parameter. Critics argue ONOE could degrade this score if it is perceived to undermine federalism or artificially truncate the mandate of regional governments.
* **Ease of Doing Business / Competitiveness Indices:** Continuous elections create policy uncertainty. A stable 5-year uninterrupted governance cycle (a primary goal of ONOE) would positively impact investor confidence and these index rankings.
* **Corruption Perceptions Index (Transparency International):** By reducing the frequency of elections, ONOE aims to reduce the total volume of unaccounted campaign finance ("black money"), potentially improving India's ranking on this index.

## 7. Global comparison

* **South Africa:** Follows a highly synchronized model. National and Provincial elections are held simultaneously every five years. Municipal elections are held two years later, ensuring a predictable electoral cycle without perennial campaigning.
* **Sweden:** Holds elections to the national legislature (Riksdag), provincial councils, and local assemblies all on the exact same day (the second Sunday of September every four years).
* **United Kingdom (Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011 - repealed):** The UK attempted to mandate fixed 5-year terms to create stability, but political realities forced early elections (2017, 2019), leading to its repeal. This serves as a warning that artificially fixing terms can fail in a parliamentary system.
* **Germany:** The Bundestag has a highly stable term because of the "Constructive Vote of No Confidence"—a Chancellor can only be removed if the opposition simultaneously votes in a successor, drastically reducing premature dissolutions (a mechanism India could learn from for ONOE).

## 9. Flow chart

**The Transitional Roadmap to ONOE (Kovind Committee)**

1. **Constitutional Amendment:** Parliament passes the 129th Amendment Bill (requires 2/3rd majority).
2. **State Ratification:** At least 50% of State Legislatures ratify the provisions related to local bodies and single electoral rolls.
3. **The Appointed Date:** Post-2029 (or designated) General Election, the President notifies the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha as the "Appointed Date."
4. **State Assembly Truncation:** Any State Assembly elected *after* this Appointed Date will automatically have its term expire concurrently with the Lok Sabha.
5. **Step 1 Execution:** The next election cycle features simultaneous voting for Lok Sabha and all State Assemblies.
6. **Step 2 Execution:** Within 100 days of Step 1, unified elections for all Panchayats and Municipalities are conducted using a Single Electoral Roll.

## 10. Recent context/issues/debate

### 10.1. Should Do (Arguments in Favor)

* **Governance over Campaigning:** Ministers and Chief Ministers spend significant time campaigning in different states every few months. ONOE frees the executive to focus strictly on administration and policy implementation for a solid 5-year block.
* **Economic Savings & Halting Black Money:** Conducting segregated elections costs the ECI thousands of crores, but the unrecorded expenditure by political parties is astronomically higher. ONOE drastically limits the frequency of campaign funding requirements.
* **Mitigating Policy Paralysis:** The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) restricts the government from announcing new projects. Frequent elections mean the MCC is active somewhere in the country almost constantly, stalling national development.
* **Social Cohesion:** Elections often exacerbate caste, communal, and regional fault lines as political parties polarize voters. Limiting elections limits the frequency of this societal polarization.

### 10.2. Should Not Do (Arguments Against)

* **Undermining Federalism (Basic Structure):** Critics argue ONOE violates the federal structure by subsuming regional issues under national narratives. Voters tend to vote for the same party in simultaneous elections, unfairly benefiting well-funded national parties at the expense of regional ones.
* **Artificial Truncation is Anti-Democratic:** Forcing state assemblies that have a legitimate 5-year mandate to dissolve early simply to align with the Lok Sabha cycle is viewed as an arbitrary and undemocratic imposition by the Centre.
* **The "Accountability" Argument:** Frequent elections act as a continuous feedback loop and pressure mechanism on the government. A guaranteed 5-year window without electoral tests might make the executive arrogant and unresponsive to public grievances.
* **Logistical Nightmare:** The ECI would require an estimated 30 lakh EVMs and VVPATs, and the deployment of Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) simultaneously across the entire nation poses a massive, perhaps unmanageable, security and logistical challenge.

## 11. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Adopting the Constructive Vote of No Confidence:** Recommended by the Law Commission (170th Report). To prevent governments from falling and triggering mid-term polls, the Constitution should be amended so that a no-confidence motion is only valid if the house simultaneously elects a new leader.
* **Phased Rollout:** Rather than forcing a nationwide reset on a single day, the ECI (as suggested in NITI Aayog's working paper) could bunch elections into two phases (e.g., holding half the state elections alongside the Lok Sabha, and the other half exactly 2.5 years later).
* **Strengthening the ECI's Capacity:** The government must proactively sanction the massive budgetary requirements for the ECI to procure the necessary EVMs/VVPATs and upgrade digital infrastructure for a Single Electoral Roll well in advance of the targeted implementation year.
* **Consensus Building via Inter-State Council:** As ONOE requires ratification by half the states, the Centre must utilize the Inter-State Council to address the genuine fears of regional parties regarding the overshadowing of local issues, ensuring the reform is built on federal consensus, not just parliamentary majority.

## 12. Best practices

* **Germany (Constructive Vote of No Confidence):** As noted, Germany's constitutional mechanism ensures that parliament cannot simply bring down a government; it must take responsibility for forming a new one. This is the best global practice to ensure legislative stability and avoid premature elections.
* **South Africa (Sequential Synchronization):** South Africa avoids the administrative chaos of doing everything on one day by splitting the synchronization—National and Provincial on one day, and Local bodies on a separate schedule. The Kovind Committee adopted this "100-day gap" best practice for India's local bodies.
* **Sweden (Fixed Polling Day):** Embedding the exact election date into the constitution removes executive discretion over timing, preventing the ruling party from calling early elections for political advantage, creating absolute systemic predictability.

## 13. Conclusion

* "One Nation, One Election" is a bold, structural reform aimed at optimizing India's democratic machinery, shifting the national focus from perpetual politicking to continuous governance and developmental delivery.
* However, its success hinges not just on constitutional amendments, but on achieving a broad political consensus. It must be implemented in a manner that meticulously preserves the federal diversity of India, ensuring that the drive for administrative efficiency does not silence the democratic voice of the states.
