# Poverty and Related Issues

## 1. Intro

* Poverty is a state of severe capability deprivation, fundamentally characterized by the inability of an individual or household to fulfill basic minimum human needs (food, shelter, healthcare, education) and participate effectively in society.
* Traditionally measured in purely monetary terms (consumption expenditure or income), the paradigm has shifted toward understanding poverty as **multidimensional**. While India has achieved historic success in drastically reducing extreme destitution over the last decade, the contemporary crisis is shifting towards tackling inequality, relative poverty, and the massive vulnerability of the "near-poor" to economic and climate shocks.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Absolute vs. Relative Poverty:** Absolute poverty refers to lacking the minimum resources required for basic survival (e.g., the World Bank's $2.15/day extreme poverty line). Relative poverty measures inequality, comparing the lowest income brackets to the national average.
* **Multidimensional Poverty:** Moves beyond just income to measure concurrent deprivations across health (nutrition, child mortality), education (schooling), and standard of living (electricity, sanitation, cooking fuel).
* **Feminization of Poverty:** The phenomenon where women represent a disproportionate percentage of the world's poor due to unequal access to property rights, the burden of unpaid care work, and the gender wage gap.
* **Intergenerational Transmission:** The vicious cycle where children born to impoverished, malnourished parents suffer from stunted cognitive and physical growth, effectively locking them into low-productivity, low-wage lives.
* **Climate-Induced Poverty:** A rapidly emerging sub-field. Extreme weather events (floods, heatwaves) wipe out agricultural assets and informal livelihoods overnight, pushing vulnerable communities back below the poverty line.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* **1970s (The "Garibi Hatao" Era):** Poverty alleviation became the central political slogan. Early measurement committees (like the Alagh Committee in 1979) defined poverty lines strictly based on minimum nutritional calorie intake (2100 in urban, 2400 in rural).
* **1993 (Lakdawala Committee):** Transitioned to defining state-specific poverty lines based on consumer price indices.
* **2009 (Tendulkar Committee):** A major shift from just calories to a broader consumption basket (including health and education), moving away from calorie-linked poverty lines.
* **2014 (Rangarajan Committee):** Further revised the consumption basket and raised the daily per capita expenditure limits, concluding that nearly 29.5% of Indians were poor in 2011-12.
* **2021 (The MPI Era):** NITI Aayog released India's first National Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI), formally aligning India's poverty measurement with global UNDP standards.
* **2024-2025 (The Consumption Survey Milestone):** The release of the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) 2022-2024 proved that rural consumption grew faster than urban, drastically shrinking the rural-urban gap.
* **November 2025 (A State-Level Eradication):** Kerala became the first Indian state to be officially declared entirely free from extreme poverty, utilizing hyper-targeted family-level micro-plans.

## 5. Committee or reports [latest is best or some report in the past with very large impact]

* **Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (2025):** Published by UNDP and OPHI. For the first time, this report explicitly overlaid climate hazard data with poverty, warning that globally 309 million poor people face three or four overlapping climate hazards (heat, drought, floods) simultaneously.
* **Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES 2023-24):** A landmark statistical release showing that the average Monthly Per Capita Expenditure (MPCE) rose significantly (Rs 4,122 in rural and Rs 6,996 in urban areas). The Gini coefficient (measuring inequality) dropped notably in both rural and urban areas.
* **NITI Aayog National MPI Report:** Revealed that India’s multidimensional poverty halved over the past decade, pulling nearly 24.8 crore people out of multidimensional poverty, bringing the current national rate to an estimated 11.28%.

## 6. Indices [better which shows India's ranking]

* **Global MPI (2025):** India ranks 66th out of 109 countries. The report officially notes India's massive success in lifting 415 million people out of poverty between 2005-06 and 2019-21, reflecting a current headcount ratio of 16.4%.
* **Human Development Index (HDI 2025):** India ranks 130th out of 193 countries, placed in the "medium human development" category. HDI improvements are fundamentally dependent on poverty eradication.
* **Global Hunger Index (GHI):** India historically struggles on this index due to child stunting and wasting metrics. This highlights the paradox that while monetary poverty is dropping, nutritional poverty (hidden hunger) remains a stubborn challenge.

## 7. Global comparison

* **Sub-Saharan Africa:** The global epicenter of acute absolute poverty. Unlike India, where poverty is rapidly declining, population growth combined with political instability in African nations ensures extreme poverty continues to grow in absolute numbers.
* **China (State-Led Eradication):** In 2021, China officially declared the total eradication of extreme absolute poverty. They achieved this through massive, state-directed infrastructure building, forced relocation of remote villages, and micro-targeted digital tracking of individual poor households.
* **United States / Europe (Relative Poverty):** Absolute destitution is extremely rare due to robust social safety nets. Poverty there is measured *relatively* (e.g., earning less than 60% of the median national income) and is heavily tied to systemic racial inequality and housing crises.

## 9. Flow chart

**The Measurement Transition in India**

1. **1970s (Nutritional Paradigm):** Poverty measured strictly by Minimum Calorie Intake (Alagh Task Force).
2. **1990s (State-Specific Baskets):** Adjusting calorie costs based on varying state-level inflation (Lakdawala).
3. **2009 (Consumption Paradigm):** Dropping calorie limits, moving to a broader Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure (MPCE) including health/education (Tendulkar).
4. **2021+ (Capability Paradigm):** Adopting the NITI Aayog Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) to capture actual living standard deprivations rather than just money spent.

## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **The "Under-5% Poverty" Debate (2024-2025):** Following the release of the HCES 2023-24 data, top economists and policy bodies asserted that absolute extreme poverty in India has plummeted to below 5% (with extreme poverty at just 2.3%). However, a fierce debate erupted as critics argued that these estimates artificially inflated rural incomes by imputing the monetary value of free government rations (food grains), and emphasized that India urgently needs to define a *new, higher official poverty line* relevant to a middle-income country rather than celebrating the defeat of an obsolete destitution line.
* **The Climate Penalty on Poverty:** The 2025 Global MPI triggered a massive international debate by proving that environmental shocks are acting as a "poverty multiplier". The debate highlights that climate change adaptation (protecting the poor from floods and heatwaves) is now just as critical as direct poverty alleviation.

## 11. Advantage/Merits/Role (Factors driving recent poverty alleviation)

* **The JAM Trinity & DBT:** Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, and Mobile connectivity have eliminated middle-men. Direct Benefit Transfers (DBT) ensure that welfare subsidies reach the poorest without bureaucratic leakage.
* **Asset Creation & Infrastructure:** Schemes like PM Awas Yojana (Housing), Saubhagya (Electricity), and Jal Jeevan Mission (Piped Water) directly attack the "Standard of Living" deprivations measured in the MPI.
* **Free Food Security:** The PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (providing free rations to 80 crore people) acted as a massive economic shock-absorber during crises, freeing up household income for health and education.
* **Women-Led Development (NRLM):** The National Rural Livelihoods Mission and the "Lakhpati Didi" initiative successfully organized millions of rural women into Self-Help Groups (SHGs), turning them into micro-entrepreneurs and financially emancipating ultra-poor households.

## 12. Disadvantage/De-Merits (Impacts of Poverty)

* **Nutritional Deficit (Stunting):** Poverty causes pregnant mothers and infants to lack vital micronutrients, resulting in physical stunting and irreversible cognitive damage, permanently capping the nation's human capital.
* **Child Labor and Trafficking:** Severe economic distress forces families to pull children out of school and into hazardous informal labor or makes them vulnerable to human trafficking networks.
* **Out-of-Pocket Health Expenditure (OOPE):** Poverty and poor health are symbiotic. A single catastrophic medical emergency forces marginalized families to sell off productive assets (livestock, land), pushing them deeply into debt traps.
* **Democratic Exclusion:** The ultra-poor are entirely consumed by the struggle for daily survival, severely restricting their ability to politically organize, protest, or demand their civic rights.

## 13. Challenges

* **The "Missing Middle" / Vulnerability:** Millions of Indians have officially escaped extreme poverty but remain highly precarious. They work in the unorganized sector (gig workers, casual laborers) without social security; a single health crisis or climate disaster can plunge them back into destitution.
* **Urban Poverty & Slumification:** As rural distress drives migration, urban poverty is exploding. Cities fail to provide affordable housing, leading to massive informal squatter settlements devoid of basic sanitation.
* **Jobless Growth:** The economy is growing, but the growth is heavily concentrated in capital-intensive services and manufacturing that do not absorb the millions of low-skilled youth exiting agriculture.
* **Hidden Hunger:** Calorie intake is sufficient (due to subsidized wheat and rice), but protein and micronutrient intake remains abysmally low, creating a crisis of "hidden hunger" among the poor.

## 14. Way forward / solution / positive outlook

* **Define a New Poverty Line:** India must officially constitute a new committee to define a modern, higher poverty line that reflects the aspirational standards of a developing superpower, rather than relying on the obsolete Tendulkar methodology.
* **Urban Employment Guarantee:** To combat rising urban inequality, the government should consider launching a national urban equivalent to MGNREGA to provide an employment safety net for casual urban and migrant laborers.
* **Universalize Healthcare (PMJAY Expansion):** Aggressively scale the Ayushman Bharat scheme to completely eliminate out-of-pocket medical expenditure, which remains the primary driver of families falling back into poverty.
* **Climate-Proofing Livelihoods:** Integrate poverty alleviation with climate resilience. Invest heavily in drought-resistant seeds, micro-irrigation, and crop insurance so rural assets are not wiped out by extreme weather events.

## 15. Best practices

* **India (Kerala's Eradication Model):** In Nov 2025, Kerala became the first state to eradicate extreme poverty by identifying 64,006 ultra-poor families through physical surveys and creating hyper-localized, personalized "micro-plans" (providing land, documents, and medical care) for each specific household rather than using generic blanket schemes.
* **India (Kudumbashree, Kerala):** A globally renowned female-empowerment network. By converting poor women into neighborhood micro-credit collectives, it broke the grip of predatory local moneylenders.
* **Brazil (Bolsa Familia):** A highly successful Conditional Cash Transfer (CCT) program. Poor families receive direct cash from the state *only if* they prove their children are attending school and receiving vaccinations, simultaneously tackling current poverty and future capability.

## 16. Conclusion

* Poverty is not merely the absence of money; it is the ultimate denial of human choices, freedom, and dignity.
* India stands at a historic crossroads. Having largely defeated acute, absolute destitution, the administrative machinery must now evolve to conquer the next frontier: eradicating multidimensional deprivations, closing the vast inequalities of urban centers, and climate-proofing the vulnerable, ensuring that every citizen can genuinely partake in the prosperity of the *Amrit Kaal*.
