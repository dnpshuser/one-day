## 1. Intro

* Constitutional Morality refers to the adherence to the core principles and underlying spirit of the Constitution—such as liberty, equality, fraternity, and justice—rather than merely following its literal text.
* It acts as a bulwark against majoritarianism, ensuring that the dignity of the individual is protected against popular orthodoxies and temporary shifts in public morality.
* Cultivating this morality requires citizens and public authorities to display self-restraint, respect for democratic processes, and tolerance for dissenting views.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Rule of Law:** The foundational pillar ensuring that government actions are constrained by legal frameworks, treating all citizens equally without arbitrary discrimination.
* **Social Justice:** A commitment to rectifying historical inequalities and ensuring equitable distribution of resources, often achieved through affirmative action (Part IV - DPSP).
* **Individual Liberty vs. Public Morality:** The principle that an individual's fundamental rights (dignity, autonomy) cannot be sacrificed at the altar of societal norms or traditional majoritarian views.
* **Democratic Ethos:** The peaceful transfer of power, respect for the opposition, and the resolution of conflicts through debate rather than force.
* **Constitutionalism:** The concept of limited government, where checks and balances (Separation of Powers) prevent any single organ of the state from becoming tyrannical.
* **Secularism and Pluralism:** State neutrality towards all religions while allowing diverse cultural and religious identities to coexist peacefully.
* **Fraternity (Bandhutva):** The psychological and social integration of citizens, promoting a sense of brotherhood that transcends caste, creed, and gender.
* **Tolerance of Dissent:** Protecting the right to peacefully disagree with the state or the majority, which is the oxygen of a functioning democracy.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* 1948: Constituent Assembly Debates
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar introduced the concept of "Constitutional Morality" in the Assembly (borrowing from historian George Grote), warning that democracy in India was only a "top-dressing" on an essentially undemocratic soil.


* 1973: Kesavananda Bharati Case
The Supreme Court propounded the "Basic Structure Doctrine." Although the term Constitutional Morality wasn't heavily used, this doctrine became its highest judicial expression, protecting the Constitution's core from parliamentary amendment.


* 2010: Naz Foundation Case (Delhi HC)
The Delhi High Court explicitly differentiated between "Public Morality" and "Constitutional Morality" while decriminalizing homosexuality, stating that popular disgust cannot override constitutional rights.


* 2018: Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India
The Supreme Court decriminalized Section 377, anchoring its judgment heavily on Constitutional Morality. The Court held that the Constitution must transform society, not mirror its prejudices.


* 2018: Indian Young Lawyers Association (Sabarimala Case)
The Supreme Court struck down the ban on the entry of women of menstruating age into the Sabarimala temple, ruling that religious customs (Public Morality) must yield to the Constitutional Morality of gender equality and dignity.


* 2018: Joseph Shine v. Union of India
The Court struck down Section 497 (Adultery), stating that treating a woman as the chattel of her husband violates the Constitutional Morality of individual agency and equality.


## 4. Quotation

* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** "Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment. It has to be cultivated. We must realize that our people have yet to learn it. Democracy in India is only a top-dressing on an Indian soil, which is essentially undemocratic."
* **Mahabharata (Shanti Parva):** Focuses heavily on *Rajadharma* (the duty of the state), emphasizing that a ruler's ultimate duty is the welfare and protection of all subjects impartially, echoing the modern concept of Constitutional justice.
* **Nyaya (न्याय - Justice):**
* *Sanskrit Root:* "न्याय" शब्द संस्कृत की धातु **'नी' (Ni)** से बना है, जिसका अर्थ है 'ले जाना' या 'मार्गदर्शन करना'।
* *Explanation:* हिंदी में इसका अर्थ है "उचित मार्गदर्शन या सही निर्णय"। संवैधानिक नैतिकता (Constitutional Morality) का मूल उद्देश्य समाज को न्याय के मार्ग पर ले जाना है, जहाँ बहुसंख्यक वर्ग की धारणाओं (Public Morality) के बजाय संविधान के आदर्शों का पालन हो।


* **Samvidhan (संविधान - Constitution):**
* *Sanskrit Root:* यह **'सम्' (Sam - सम्यक रूप से) + 'वि' (Vi - विशेष) + 'धा' (Dha - धारण करना/रखना)** धातु से बना है।
* *Explanation:* इसका अर्थ है "विधानों और नियमों का वह समूह जिसे समाज ने सम्यक रूप से धारण किया हो"। यह केवल एक पुस्तक नहीं है, बल्कि एक जीवंत नैतिकता है जिसे राष्ट्र धारण करता है।



## 5. Reports

* **National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (NCRWC, 2002):** Emphasized that the mere existence of a Constitution is insufficient without a pervasive culture of Constitutional Morality among political parties, civil servants, and citizens.
* **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) - 4th Report (Ethics in Governance):** Directly linked administrative ethics to Constitutional Morality, stating that civil servants must be guided by the constitutional principles of equity and justice, not just procedural rules.
* **Law Commission of India (262nd Report - The Death Penalty):** Discussed how the evolving standards of Constitutional Morality (focusing on human dignity and Article 21) necessitate a re-evaluation of capital punishment.
* **Law Commission of India (267th Report - Hate Speech):** Explored the tension between freedom of speech and the Constitutional Morality of fraternity, concluding that speech designed to destroy the dignity of a community violates the constitutional spirit.
* **Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) Petitions:** While not traditional reports, ADR's foundational PILs have repeatedly argued that the criminalization of politics and opaque electoral funding (e.g., Electoral Bonds) are direct assaults on Constitutional Morality.

## 6. Indices

*(While Constitutional Morality is qualitative, these global indices measure its practical enforcement)*

* **Rule of Law Index (World Justice Project):** Measures constraints on government power, fundamental rights, and open government. High scores indicate a strong adherence to Constitutional Morality.
* **Democracy Index (Economist Intelligence Unit):** Evaluates electoral processes, civil liberties, and political culture. A political culture lacking Constitutional Morality (where majoritarianism dominates) severely degrades this ranking.
* **Freedom in the World Index (Freedom House):** Assesses political rights and civil liberties. The protection of minority rights against prevailing public morality is a key metric here.
* **Social Progress Index (Social Progress Imperative):** Measures inclusiveness, personal rights, and tolerance. This heavily overlaps with the constitutional goal of fraternity and social justice.
* **Varieties of Democracy (V-Dem) - Liberal Democracy Index:** Specifically tracks the protection of individual liberties against the tyranny of the state or the majority, which is the essence of Constitutional Morality.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United Kingdom:** Lacks a written constitution. Therefore, Constitutional Morality relies entirely on unwritten "Constitutional Conventions" (e.g., the Monarch always acting on Cabinet advice). Respect for these conventions *is* their constitutional morality.
* **United States:** Deeply rooted in "Constitutional Faith" and the Bill of Rights. The US Supreme Court often uses the concept of "Due Process" and "Equal Protection" to enforce a morality of liberty over state legislations (e.g., *Obergefell v. Hodges* legalizing same-sex marriage).
* **South Africa:** Operates on the principle of "Transformative Constitutionalism." Their Constitutional Morality is explicitly designed to heal the divisions of the past (Apartheid) and establish a society based on democratic values, social justice, and the indigenous philosophy of *Ubuntu* (humanity to others).
* **Germany:** Utilizes "Militant Democracy." Their Basic Law contains an "Eternity Clause" making human dignity and the democratic order unamendable. The state is constitutionally bound to aggressively defend these morals against extremist political parties.
* **India:** A hybrid model. It uses Transformative Constitutionalism (like South Africa) to eradicate social evils (untouchability) while relying heavily on the judiciary to define and enforce the boundaries between ancient Public Morality and modern Constitutional Morality.

## 8. Committee reports

* **Justice J.S. Verma Committee (2013):** Formed post the Nirbhaya case. The committee rooted its recommendations deeply in Constitutional Morality, stating that the state has a constitutional obligation to protect women's dignity and bodily autonomy, regardless of societal patriarchy.
* **Sachar Committee (2006) & Ranganath Mishra Commission (2007):** Both highlighted the socio-economic backwardness of minorities. Addressing these disparities is framed as an imperative of the Constitutional Morality of equality and fraternity.
* **N.N. Vohra Committee (1993):** Highlighted the nexus between politicians, bureaucrats, and criminals. Such a nexus is the ultimate subversion of Constitutional Morality, replacing the rule of law with the rule of muscle and money.
* **Punchhi Commission (2010):** Stressed that the Governor's office must act with the highest degree of Constitutional Morality, acting as a neutral constitutional sentinel rather than an agent of the ruling party at the Centre.
* **Kothari Commission (1964-66):** On education, it emphasized that the destiny of India is being shaped in its classrooms. Instilling democratic values in children is the primary way to "cultivate the sentiment" of Constitutional Morality that Ambedkar spoke of.

## 10. Flow chart

**Resolving the Conflict: How the Court Applies Constitutional Morality**

1. **Status Quo:** A long-standing Social Custom or a State Law exists based on traditional "Public Morality."
2. **The Challenge:** A petition is filed claiming the custom/law violates Part III (Fundamental Rights) of the Constitution.
3. **The Judicial Test:** The Court places the custom/law on the anvil of Constitutional Morality.
4. **Analysis Questions:**
* Does it violate Individual Dignity (Art 21)?
* Does it cause arbitrary discrimination (Art 14 & 15)?
* Does it destroy Fraternity?


5. **Decision Node (Yes/No):**
* *If NO:* The custom/law is upheld as it does not conflict with constitutional ethos.
* *If YES:* (Proceed to step 6).


6. **The Overriding Principle:** The Court declares that Fundamental Rights and Constitutional Morality are superior to societal disgust, tradition, or majoritarian views.
7. **Final Outcome:** The Custom/Law is struck down (e.g., Section 377 decriminalized, Sabarimala ban lifted), advancing Transformative Constitutionalism.

## 11. Recent context/issues/debate

* **Critique of Judicial Overreach:** Critics, including former Attorneys General and executive officials, argue that "Constitutional Morality" is a dangerously subjective concept not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution. They argue it acts as a "dangerous weapon" allowing unelected judges to strike down laws passed by the elected legislature based on their personal philosophies.
* **Same-Sex Marriage (Supriyo v. Union of India):** A fascinating debate where the Court recognized the Constitutional Morality of equal treatment and dignity for LGBTQ+ couples, but exercised judicial restraint, stating that changing the Special Marriage Act falls into the domain of the legislature, not the judiciary.
* **The Electoral Bonds Verdict:** The Supreme Court invoked the Constitutional Morality of democratic transparency and the voter's Right to Information, striking down a scheme that allowed anonymous corporate funding, regardless of the legislative majority that passed it.
* **Federal Friction and the Governor's Role:** Recent clashes in states like Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Punjab regarding Governors withholding assent to bills indefinitely highlight a breakdown in Constitutional Morality. The Supreme Court has repeatedly reminded Governors that they are bound by constitutional conventions, not political affiliations.
* **Hate Speech and Fraternity:** The rising incidence of hate speech and communal polarization is viewed as a direct assault on the Constitutional Morality of "Fraternity." The Supreme Court's recent *suo motu* directives to police to register FIRs against hate speech regardless of religion underscore the urgency of protecting this constitutional ethos.

## 12. Conclusion

* Constitutional Morality is the invisible soul that animates the written text of the Constitution, transforming it from a mere legal document into an instrument of social revolution and emancipation.
* While its interpretation requires judicial prudence to avoid overreach, it remains the ultimate safeguard for the marginalized, ensuring that India's democracy is governed by the rule of law and human dignity, rather than the transient whims of majoritarian public morality.
