# Parliamentary Disruptions

## 1. Intro

* Parliament is the supreme legislative body and the primary forum for debate, accountability, and the passage of laws in a vibrant democracy.
* However, frequent parliamentary disruptions have become a systemic malady in India, reducing the institution to an arena of sloganeering rather than a theater of constructive policy-making, severely compromising executive accountability.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Causes of Disruption:** Often stem from the government's lack of responsiveness, insufficient time allocated for the opposition to raise crucial issues, and parties intentionally causing disturbances for political signaling or publicity.
* **Rules for Discipline (Lok Sabha):**
* *Rule 373:* Speaker can direct a member to withdraw immediately for disorderly conduct (for the remainder of the day).
* *Rule 374:* The Speaker can name a member who disregards authority. The House may then pass a motion to suspend them for up to the remainder of the session.
* *Rule 374A (Added in 2001):* Allows for *automatic* suspension for 5 days or the remainder of the session (whichever is less) for severe charges or coming into the well of the House.


* **Rules for Discipline (Rajya Sabha):** Governed by Rules 255 (withdrawal for the day) and 256 (naming by the Chairman followed by a motion for suspension by the House). Crucially, the Rajya Sabha Chairman *cannot* automatically suspend a member like the Lok Sabha Speaker under 374A.
* **Implications of Suspension:** A suspended member cannot enter the chamber, attend committee meetings, give notices for discussion, or get replies to their questions, effectively disenfranchising their constituency for that period.
* **The Guillotine:** When time is lost to disruptions, Demands for Grants for various ministries are lumped together and passed without discussion (guillotined) to pass the budget on time, bypassing financial scrutiny.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* 1950s-1960s: The Formative Era
Parliamentary debates were highly structured and disruptions were rare. The opposition, though small, was given ample time and respect by the executive.


* 1989: First Mass Suspension
In a precursor to modern disruptions, 63 opposition MPs were suspended from the Lok Sabha in a single day during the debate on the Thakkar Commission report (Indira Gandhi assassination).


* 2001: Rule 374A Introduced
In response to increasing unruliness, Lok Sabha introduced Rule 374A to allow the Speaker to automatically suspend members for grave disorder without needing a motion.


* 2014-2019: 16th Lok Sabha
According to PRS Legislative Research, the 16th Lok Sabha lost 16% of its scheduled time purely to disruptions, marking a steep decline in legislative productivity.


* June 2019 - Feb 2024: 17th Lok Sabha
Functioned for only 88% of scheduled time. Had the fewest sittings amongst all full-term Lok Sabhas (274 sittings). Notably, it operated without a Deputy Speaker for its entire duration.


* Dec 2023: Winter Session Mass Suspension
A historic low. 146 MPs (100 from LS, 46 from RS) were suspended after protesting a major Parliament security breach. Crucial bills, including the massive overhaul of criminal laws (BNS, BNSS, BSA), were passed during their absence.

## 5. Committee or Reports

* **PRS Legislative Research (17th Lok Sabha Vital Stats):** Reported that 58% of Bills were passed within two weeks of their introduction. 35% of Bills were passed with less than an hour of discussion. Over the last decade, an average of 67% of the Budget has been guillotined without discussion.
* **Vice President/Chairman RS Statements:** Jagdeep Dhankhar and Venkaiah Naidu have repeatedly highlighted that the cost of running Parliament is roughly ₹2.5 lakh per minute, meaning disruptions cause massive financial loss to the exchequer.
* **National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (NCRWC, 2002):** Strongly recommended that Parliament should sit for a minimum of 120 days in a year for the Lok Sabha, and 100 days for the Rajya Sabha, to ensure there is no scarcity of time for debates, which is a root cause of disruptions.

## 6. Indices

*(Note: Domestic parliamentary procedures affect broader governance rankings)*

* **Democracy Index (Economist Intelligence Unit):** India's score on "Functioning of Government" is negatively impacted when the legislature fails to hold the executive accountable due to constant disruptions or when massive bills are passed without opposition presence.
* **Rule of Law Index (World Justice Project):** Passing foundational laws (like the new criminal codes) without adequate opposition debate undermines the "Open Government" and "Regulatory Enforcement" perception metrics.
* **Freedom in the World (Freedom House):** Ranks political rights. The mass suspension of 146 MPs inherently reduces the political representation of millions of voters, affecting this metric.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United Kingdom (House of Commons):** Has a highly institutionalized "Prime Minister's Questions" (PMQs) every Wednesday where the PM directly answers questions from the Leader of the Opposition. It channels aggression into structured debate. They also dedicate "Opposition Days" (20 days a session) where the opposition dictates the agenda.
* **United States (Congress):** Uses the "Filibuster" in the Senate (talking a bill to death), which is a procedural delay, but physical disruptions or rushing the well of the house are practically unheard of due to strict enforcement of decorum.
* **Germany (Bundestag):** Operates on a highly consensus-driven model with powerful parliamentary committees that do the heavy lifting of legislation before it reaches the floor, minimizing the need for floor protests.

## 9. Flow chart

**The Anatomy of a Suspension (Lok Sabha)**

1. **Trigger:** An MP intentionally disrupts proceedings, shouts slogans, or enters the Well of the House.
2. **Warning:** Speaker requests order and asks the member to return to their seat.
3. **Action 1 (Rule 373):** Speaker directs the member to withdraw immediately for the remainder of the day.
4. **Escalation:** Member refuses to leave or commits a grave disorder.
5. **Action 2 (Rule 374A - Automatic):** Speaker "names" the member. Member is automatically suspended for 5 days or the rest of the session.
6. **Action 3 (Rule 374 - Motion):** Speaker names the member. Parliamentary Affairs Minister moves a motion for suspension. The House adopts it via voice vote.
7. **Result:** MP is barred from the chamber, committees, and loses the right to submit questions.

## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **The Winter Session 2023 Purge:** The unprecedented suspension of 146 MPs across both Houses. The opposition demanded a statement from the Home Minister regarding a major security breach where intruders jumped into the Lok Sabha chamber. The government refused, leading to protests and mass suspensions.
* **Passing Landmark Bills in a Vacuum:** During this period of mass suspension, the government passed foundational bills, most notably the *Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita* (replacing the IPC) and the Telecom Bill, with almost zero opposition debate, raising severe questions about the democratic legitimacy of the laws.
* **The Missing Deputy Speaker:** For the first time in history, the 17th Lok Sabha functioned for its entire 5-year term without electing a Deputy Speaker (a post conventionally given to the opposition), further fueling the opposition's grievance of being systematically marginalized.

## 11. Challenges

* **Erosion of Democratic Values:** The suspension of a substantial number of Opposition MPs casts a shadow over democratic values, raising concerns about the fair representation of diverse voices within the legislative process.
* **Deteriorating Quality of Laws:** When bills are passed in the din or during mass suspensions, they escape rigorous parliamentary scrutiny. This leads to poorly drafted laws that inevitably face massive public protests (e.g., Farm Laws) or constitutional challenges in the Supreme Court.
* **Partisan Presiding Officers:** There is a growing perception that Speakers and Chairmen act in a partisan manner, swiftly suspending opposition members while ignoring similar unruly behavior or provocative statements by members of the ruling party.
* **The "Opposition Day" Deficit:** Unlike the UK, the Indian Parliament does not allocate specific days where the opposition dictates the agenda. This leaves them feeling that disruption is the *only* tool available to force the government to discuss uncomfortable issues (like inflation, border disputes, or security breaches).

## 12. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Institutionalize 'Opposition Days' (NCRWC):** The Parliament must amend its rules to guarantee a specific number of days in each session where the agenda is entirely decided by the Opposition, giving them a constructive outlet to raise grievances without resorting to disruption.
* **Shadow Cabinet Concept:** India should formally recognize a 'Shadow Cabinet' (as in the UK), giving specific opposition leaders the recognized authority and allocated time to counter respective ministers, structuring the debate.
* **Mandatory Minimum Sittings:** The Constitution should be amended to fix a minimum of 100-120 sitting days per year for Parliament. This prevents the government from shrinking sessions to avoid accountability and ensures ample time for all debates.
* **Pre-Legislative Scrutiny (Committee System):** Make it mandatory for all bills (except emergency ordinances) to be routed through Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs). Committee meetings are held in-camera and are generally free from political grandstanding, ensuring quality scrutiny.
* **Suspension as a Last Resort:** Presiding officers must build consensus and use suspensions only as an absolute last resort. Punishing 146 MPs for demanding a debate on a legitimate security breach reflects a failure of parliamentary management.

## 13. Best practices

* **United Kingdom (Prime Minister's Questions - PMQs):** Every Wednesday for 30 minutes, the PM faces direct, unfiltered questioning from the Leader of the Opposition and backbenchers. This is a global best practice in executive accountability that India lacks (the Indian PM rarely answers questions directly during Question Hour).
* **United Kingdom (Speaker's Neutrality):** Upon election, the Speaker of the House of Commons legally resigns from their political party to ensure absolute neutrality. In India, Speakers retain their party membership, leading to allegations of bias.
* **New Zealand (Business Committee):** The scheduling of Parliament is done by a Business Committee representing all parties proportionally, rather than being dictated solely by the ruling party's Parliamentary Affairs Minister, ensuring a fair allocation of time.

## 14. Conclusion

* Parliamentary disruptions are a symptom of a deeper malaise: a breakdown in communication and trust between the executive and the opposition.
* While enforcing discipline through rules like 374A is necessary for order, the ultimate solution lies in fostering a parliamentary culture where the government is responsive to debate, and the opposition acts responsibly, ensuring that the temple of democracy does not become a casualty of partisan warfare.
