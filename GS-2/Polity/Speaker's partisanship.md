# Speaker's Partisanship Image

## 1. Intro

* The Speaker is the constitutional and ceremonial head of the Lok Sabha (and State Legislative Assemblies), designed to act as the neutral, impartial umpire of parliamentary democracy.

* The Speaker actig in accordance with party politics has severely eroded the institutional credibility of the legislature, converting Parliament from a forum of executive accountability into a government mouthpiece.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic 

* **Tenth Schedule Adjudication:** 
  * quasi-judicial power to decide anti-defection cases.
  * delay in deciding defection petition of ruling party members
  * prompt decisions on defection petition of oppotion members.
* **Money Bill Certification:** 
  * Speakers certification of Money Bill under A-110 is final.
  * Speaker use Money Bill route to bypass RJ(Ruling party lack majority) to pass Non Money Bill provisions.
* **Disproportionate Disciplinary Action:** 
  * selective suspension of opposition members for unruly behavior
  * ignoring similar provocations from the ruling benches.
* **Muting the Opposition:**
  * deliberately switching off the microphones of the LoP
* **Committee Appointments:** 
  * The Speaker dictates the composition of Parliamentary Committees.
  * opposition being denied the chairmanship of crucial scrutiny committees (like the Public Accounts Committee or Finance Committee).
* **The Missing Deputy Speaker:** 
  * By convention, the Deputy Speaker post belongs to the opposition to balance the Chair. 
  * Vaccant Deputy Speaker post(17th and 18th LS) reveals a systematic denial of opposition space.

  * immediately resigned from the Swaraj Party to establish absolute neutrality - rarely followed std
* **1952-1956 (The Mavalankar Era):**

## 5. Committee or Reports

* **2nd ARC - Ethics in Governance:** 
  * Speaker’s political dependence on the ruling party compromises their role. 
  * Recom: disqualification powers under the 10th Schedule be transferred to the President/Governor acting on the binding advice of the ECI.
* **Dinesh Goswami Committee on Electoral Reforms (1990):** 
  * strip of the Speaker's anti-defection powers
  * disqualification should only be triggered if a member votes against the party whip during a Motion of Confidence or No-Confidence.
* **NCRWC, "02:**
  * Highlighted partisan misuse of the 10th Schedule 
  * disqualification powers should vest in the ECI  rather than the Speaker.
* **Page Committee (UK References):** 
  * Speaker officially resigns from their party
  * generally not contested by major parties in subsequent general elections to protect their neutrality.
* **Law Commission of India (255th Report):** 
  * concerns about lack of intra-party democracy and 
  * the excessive power the partisan Speaker holds in validating "mergers" and "splits," destabilizing democratic mandates.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United Kingdom (The Gold Standard of Neutrality):** 
  * "Once a Speaker, always a Speaker." 
  * The Speaker legally resigns from their political party upon election. 
  * "Speaker seeking re-election," usually unopposed by major parties.

* **United States (The Hyper-Partisan Model):** 
  * political leader of the majority party is the Speaker (e.g., Nancy Pelosi, Mike Johnson). 
  * They push ruling party's legislative agenda, making no pretense of absolute neutrality.

* **India (The Flawed Hybrid):** 
  * pretense to neutrality but structurally not.

* **Ireland (Ceann Comhairle):** 
  * Speaker is deemed elected in the next Gen Assembly if they wish to continue.
  * Absolute independence from the govt.

* **France:** The President of the National Assembly behavioural neutrality during debates without any biasness.

## 9. Flow chart

**The 10th Schedule Adjudication Loophole**

1. **Defection Occurs:** Opposition MLAs defect to the ruling party to secure ministerial berths.
2. **Petition Filed:** Opposition leader files a disqualification petition under the 10th Schedule.
3. **The Speaker's Discretion:** Speaker receives the petition. (No constitutional time limit exists for a decision).
4. **Partisan Inaction:** Speaker deliberately sits on the petition for months or years.
5. **Judicial Intervention Attempt:** The Supreme Court is petitioned, but courts are reluctant to interfere while the Speaker is "seized of the matter."
6. **Government Survival:** The defecting MLAs continue as ministers. By the time the SC sets a deadline or the Speaker is forced to act, the Assembly's 5-year term expires.
7. **Infructuous Outcome:** The 10th Schedule is defeated via strategic procedural delay.

## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **The Maharashtra Crisis (2023-2024):** 
  * The Supreme Court heavily reprimanded the Maharashtra Speaker for reducing the anti-defection law to a "charade." 
  * The Speaker controversially declared the defecting faction as the "real" political party based merely on legislative majority, bypassing the core intent of the 10th Schedule and legitimizing the defection.

* **Mass Suspensions (Winter 2023):** 
  * The presiding officers suspended 146 opposition MPs for protesting a security breach.
  * weaponization of disciplinary rules to clear the house for passing foundational criminal laws without scrutiny.

* **Microphone Muting Allegations (2026):**
  * The opposition moved rare resolutions demanding the removal of the Speaker, alleging that the 
  * microphones of the Leader of the Opposition and other critical voices were being deliberately switched off from the control room during crucial national debates.

* **The Aadhaar/Tribunal Reforms Money Bill Route:**  
  * partisan certifications of complex, non-financial policies as "Money Bills" to bypass the RS.
  * have triggered deep constitutional debates, currently pending before a Supreme Court Constitution Bench.

* **Absence of the Deputy Speaker:** 
  * Vaccant Deputy Speaker's post in the 17th and 18th Lok Sabha
  * symbol of: ruling party's refusal to grant the opposition its legitimate, balancing space in parliamentary management.

## 11. Challenges

* **Structural Conflict of Interest:** 
  * Their future political career of Speaker depends on the party's high command making true neutrality professional suicide.

* **Ambiguity in Discretionary Powers:** 
  * The Constitution gives the Speaker final say on Money Bills and 10th Schedule disqualifications without providing strict guidelines or mathematical time limits, creating massive loopholes for partisan manipulation.

* **Limits of Judicial Review:** 
  * *Kihoto Hollohan* allowed judicial review of the Speaker's disqualification decisions, 
  * courts cannot intervene *before* the Speaker makes a decision. 
  * This allows the Speaker to defeat the law simply through indefinite delay.

* **Decline of Parliamentary Decorum:** 
  * The opposition's lack of faith in Speaker's neutrality => resort to disruptions and rushing the Well of the House. 
  * The Speaker then uses these disruptions to justify mass suspensions, creating a toxic, cyclical breakdown of trust.

* **"Majority Tyranny":** 
  * absolute parliamentary majorities => ruling party feels no compulsion to build consensus, 
  * Speaker reduced to an enforcer of the executive's will rather than the custodian of the legislature's dignity.

## 12. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Independent Anti-Defection Tribunal (SC Recommendation):** 
  * SC advice in the *Keisham Meghachandra* case to transfer 10th Schedule powers from the Speaker to a Permanent Tribunal headed by a retired SC judge.

* **Statutory Time Limits:** 
  * The 10th Schedule must be amended to enforce a strict, legally binding 3-month timeline for the Speaker to decide on disqualification petitions.

* **Adopting the UK Convention:** 
  * Speaker resign from Political party
  * In return, parties agree not to oppose Speaker in next Gen Election.

* **Judicial Review of 'Money Bills':**

## 14. Conclusion

* The Speaker balances the machinery of parliamentary democracy. Their partisanship ceases to hold the executive accountable, defeating the core premise of a parliamentary procedure.

* Restoring the pristine image of this office requires urgent structural amputation—specifically stripping the Speaker of anti-defection adjudicatory powers and institutionalizing their political independence—to ensure that Parliament remains the voice of the nation, rather than the echo chamber of the government.
