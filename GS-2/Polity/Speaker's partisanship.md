# Speaker's Partisanship Image

## 1. Intro

* The Speaker is the constitutional and ceremonial head of the Lok Sabha (and State Legislative Assemblies), designed to act as the neutral, impartial umpire of parliamentary democracy.
* However, the persistent image of partisanship—where the Speaker frequently acts to further the political agenda of the ruling party—has severely eroded the institutional credibility of the legislature, converting Parliament from a forum of executive accountability into a government mouthpiece.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Tenth Schedule Adjudication:** The Speaker acts as a quasi-judicial tribunal under the anti-defection law. The partisan delay in deciding disqualification petitions of defecting MLAs/MPs to save ruling governments is the most glaring sub-component of this issue.
* **Money Bill Certification:** Under Article 110, the Speaker’s decision to certify a bill as a 'Money Bill' is final. Partisan Speakers have allegedly misused this to bypass the Rajya Sabha (where the ruling party lacked a majority) for contentious legislation.
* **Disproportionate Disciplinary Action:** The selective suspension of opposition members for unruly behavior, while ignoring similar provocations from the treasury benches, highlights structural bias.
* **Muting the Opposition:** Allegations of the Chair deliberately switching off the microphones of the Leader of the Opposition or expunging critical remarks against the executive without valid parliamentary reasons.
* **Committee Appointments:** The Speaker dictates the composition of Parliamentary Committees. Partisan bias often results in the opposition being denied the chairmanship of crucial scrutiny committees (like the Public Accounts Committee or Finance Committee).
* **The Missing Deputy Speaker:** By convention, the Deputy Speaker post belongs to the opposition to balance the Chair. The refusal to even elect a Deputy Speaker (as seen in the 17th and 18th Lok Sabha) reveals a systematic denial of opposition space.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* **1925 (The Vithalbhai Patel Precedent):** Vithalbhai Patel became the first elected President of the Central Legislative Assembly and immediately resigned from the Swaraj Party to establish absolute neutrality, a standard rarely followed post-independence.
* **1952-1956 (The Mavalankar Era):** G.V. Mavalankar, the first Lok Sabha Speaker, maintained high dignity but controversially refused to sever his ties with the Indian National Congress, setting a flawed precedent for the Indian hybrid model.
* **1985 (52nd Amendment):** The introduction of the Tenth Schedule fundamentally altered the Speaker's role, granting them vast quasi-judicial powers over defection without establishing a timeframe for their decisions.
* **1992 (*Kihoto Hollohan* Case):** The Supreme Court upheld the Speaker's adjudicatory power under the 10th Schedule but stripped them of absolute immunity, making their disqualification decisions subject to judicial review.
* **2020 (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh* Case):** The Supreme Court openly acknowledged the structural partisanship of the Speaker and strongly advised Parliament to amend the Constitution to hand over disqualification powers to an independent permanent tribunal.
* **2023-2026 (Modern Flashpoints):** The Maharashtra Speaker's highly criticized handling of the Shiv Sena and NCP splits, combined with the mass suspension of 146 MPs (2023) and subsequent opposition resolutions to remove the Speaker (2026), marked a historic low in the office's perceived neutrality.

## 5. Committee or Reports

* **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC - Ethics in Governance):** Explicitly stated that the Speaker’s political dependence on the ruling party inherently compromises their role. It recommended that disqualification powers under the 10th Schedule be transferred to the President/Governor acting on the binding advice of the Election Commission.
* **Dinesh Goswami Committee on Electoral Reforms (1990):** Recommended stripping the Speaker of broad anti-defection powers, suggesting that disqualification should only be triggered if a member votes against the party whip during a Motion of Confidence or No-Confidence.
* **National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (NCRWC, 2002):** Highlighted the partisan misuse of the 10th Schedule and recommended that the power to decide questions of disqualification should vest in the Election Commission of India rather than the Speaker.
* **Page Committee (UK References):** Often cited in Indian parliamentary reports, this outlines the strict British convention where the Speaker officially resigns from their party and is generally not contested by major parties in subsequent general elections to protect their neutrality.
* **Law Commission of India (255th Report):** While focusing on electoral reforms, it echoed concerns regarding the lack of intra-party democracy and the excessive power the partisan Speaker holds in validating "mergers" and "splits," destabilizing democratic mandates.

## 6. Indices

* **Democracy Index (Economist Intelligence Unit):** India's scores on "Functioning of Government" and "Political Culture" suffer when the Speaker acts in a partisan manner, silencing the opposition and diminishing executive accountability.
* **Rule of Law Index (World Justice Project):** A partisan Speaker negatively impacts the "Constraints on Government Powers" metric. If the legislative check on the executive is neutralized by a biased Chair, the rule of law is structurally weakened.
* **V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) Deliberative Component Index:** Measures how decisions are reached in a polity (respectful debate vs. bulldozing). The rampant use of the guillotine and muting of opposition mics drastically lowers this index for India.
* **Freedom in the World (Freedom House):** Ranks political rights. A partisan Speaker who unfairly disqualifies or suspends opposition MPs artificially shrinks political pluralism, directly depressing this ranking.
* **Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU) Institutional Metrics:** The IPU tracks the independence of global parliaments. A Chair dependent on the ruling party's mercy for their political survival violates global best practices for legislative autonomy.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United Kingdom (The Gold Standard of Neutrality):** The UK follows the convention: "Once a Speaker, always a Speaker." The Speaker legally resigns from their political party upon election. They sever all political ties and run in general elections as an independent "Speaker seeking re-election," usually unopposed by major parties.
* **United States (The Hyper-Partisan Model):** The US Speaker of the House is explicitly the political leader of the majority party (e.g., Nancy Pelosi, Mike Johnson). They actively push the ruling party's legislative agenda, making no pretense of absolute neutrality.
* **India (The Flawed Hybrid):** India has adopted the robes and procedural rhetoric of the UK model (claiming neutrality) but practically operates on the US model (hyper-partisanship), leaving the Speaker politically vulnerable without the institutional protections of the UK system.
* **Ireland (Ceann Comhairle):** The Speaker of the lower house is automatically deemed re-elected in the next general election if they wish to continue, completely removing their dependence on a party ticket and ensuring absolute independence from the government.
* **France:** The President of the National Assembly retains party affiliations and does not resign from their party, but exercises strict, deeply institutionalized neutrality during debates, acting more as an administrator than a political enforcer.

## 9. Flow chart

**The 10th Schedule Adjudication Loophole**

1. **Defection Occurs:** Opposition MLAs defect to the ruling party to secure ministerial berths.
2. **Petition Filed:** Opposition leader files a disqualification petition under the 10th Schedule.
3. **The Speaker's Discretion:** Speaker receives the petition. (No constitutional time limit exists for a decision).
4. **Partisan Inaction:** Speaker deliberately sits on the petition for months or years.
5. **Judicial Intervention Attempt:** The Supreme Court is petitioned, but courts are reluctant to interfere while the Speaker is "seized of the matter."
6. **Government Survival:** The defecting MLAs continue as ministers. By the time the SC sets a deadline or the Speaker is forced to act, the Assembly's 5-year term expires.
7. **Infructuous Outcome:** The 10th Schedule is defeated via strategic procedural delay.

## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **The Maharashtra Crisis (2023-2024):** The Supreme Court heavily reprimanded the Maharashtra Speaker for reducing the anti-defection law to a "charade." The Speaker controversially declared the defecting faction as the "real" political party based merely on legislative majority, bypassing the core intent of the 10th Schedule and legitimizing the defection.
* **Mass Suspensions (Winter 2023):** The presiding officers suspended 146 opposition MPs for protesting a security breach. This sparked a massive debate on the weaponization of disciplinary rules to clear the house for passing foundational criminal laws without scrutiny.
* **Microphone Muting Allegations (2026):** The opposition moved rare resolutions demanding the removal of the Speaker, alleging that the microphones of the Leader of the Opposition and other critical voices were being deliberately switched off from the control room during crucial national debates.
* **The Aadhaar/Tribunal Reforms Money Bill Route:** Successive partisan certifications of complex, non-financial policies as "Money Bills" to bypass the Rajya Sabha have triggered deep constitutional debates, currently pending before a Supreme Court Constitution Bench.
* **Absence of the Deputy Speaker:** The deliberate, ongoing refusal to fill the Deputy Speaker's post in the 17th and 18th Lok Sabha has been debated as the ultimate symbol of the ruling party's refusal to grant the opposition its legitimate, balancing space in parliamentary management.

## 11. Challenges

* **Structural Conflict of Interest:** A Speaker in India does not resign from their party. Their future political career (getting an election ticket or becoming a Cabinet Minister) depends entirely on the mercy of the party high command, making true neutrality professional suicide.
* **Ambiguity in Discretionary Powers:** The Constitution gives the Speaker final say on Money Bills and 10th Schedule disqualifications without providing strict guidelines or mathematical time limits, creating massive loopholes for partisan manipulation.
* **Limits of Judicial Review:** While *Kihoto Hollohan* allowed judicial review of the Speaker's disqualification decisions, courts cannot intervene *before* the Speaker makes a decision. This allows the Speaker to defeat the law simply through indefinite delay.
* **Decline of Parliamentary Decorum:** The opposition's lack of faith in the Speaker's neutrality leads them to resort to disruptions and rushing the Well of the House. The Speaker then uses these disruptions to justify mass suspensions, creating a toxic, cyclical breakdown of trust.
* **"Majority Tyranny":** In an era of absolute parliamentary majorities, the ruling party feels no compulsion to build consensus, reducing the Speaker to an enforcer of the executive's will rather than the custodian of the legislature's dignity.

## 12. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Independent Anti-Defection Tribunal (SC Recommendation):** Parliament must urgently act on the Supreme Court’s advice in the *Keisham Meghachandra* case and amend the Constitution to transfer 10th Schedule powers from the Speaker to a Permanent Tribunal headed by a retired Supreme Court judge.
* **Statutory Time Limits:** The 10th Schedule must be amended to enforce a strict, legally binding 3-month timeline for the Speaker to decide on disqualification petitions.
* **Adopting the UK Convention:** Political parties must build a consensus to adopt the British model: the Speaker must formally resign from their party upon election, and in return, major political parties must agree not to field candidates against the Speaker in the next general election.
* **Automatic Election of Deputy Speaker:** The Rules of Procedure should be amended to mandate the election of a Deputy Speaker from the principal opposition party within 30 days of the constitution of a new Lok Sabha.
* **Judicial Review of 'Money Bills':** The impending Supreme Court Constitution Bench must clearly lay down strict parameters for what constitutes a Money Bill under Article 110, limiting the Speaker's absolute, unreviewable discretion in certification.

## 13. Best practices

* **United Kingdom (Resignation and Security):** The Speaker of the House of Commons resigns from their party and is granted near-total political security by not being opposed in general elections. This severs the umbilical cord of party loyalty, which is the root cause of partisanship in India.
* **Ireland (Automatic Re-election):** The automatic re-election of the Ceann Comhairle (Speaker) removes the fear of losing the next election due to annoying the ruling party, providing immense institutional courage to be neutral.
* **India (Article 103 Mechanism):** Under Article 103, disputes regarding the disqualification of an MP (for office of profit, insolvency, etc.) are decided by the President acting on the *binding* advice of the Election Commission of India. Applying this exact best practice to the 10th Schedule would instantly cure the partisan defection crisis.
* **New Zealand (Business Committee):** The allocation of parliamentary time and the selection of debate topics are managed by a cross-party Business Committee based on proportionality, rather than being left to the partisan discretion of the Speaker and the ruling party's Parliamentary Affairs Minister.
* **India (Historical Vithalbhai Patel Model):** Reclaiming our own pre-independence legacy, where Vithalbhai Patel broke from the Swaraj Party to act impartially, proving that structural neutrality can be achieved in the subcontinent if the political will exists.

## 14. Conclusion

* The Speaker is the pivot upon which the entire machinery of parliamentary democracy balances. When the referee starts playing for the ruling team, the legislature ceases to hold the executive accountable, defeating the core premise of a parliamentary republic.
* Restoring the pristine image of this office requires urgent structural amputation—specifically stripping the Speaker of anti-defection adjudicatory powers and institutionalizing their political independence—to ensure that Parliament remains the voice of the nation, rather than the echo chamber of the government.
