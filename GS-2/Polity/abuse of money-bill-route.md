# Abuse of Money Bill Route: Ordinary Bills Passed as Money Bills

## 1. Intro

* A Money Bill (Article 110) is a special piece of legislation dealing strictly with taxation, government borrowing, and the Consolidated Fund of India. Crucially, the Rajya Sabha cannot amend or reject it; it can only recommend changes within 14 days, which the Lok Sabha can ignore.
* In recent years, governments have increasingly weaponized this constitutional exception, introducing complex ordinary bills (like the Aadhaar Act or Tribunal reforms) as Money Bills. This "route" is used to deliberately bypass the Rajya Sabha when the ruling party lacks a majority there, fundamentally undermining the bicameral structure of Parliament.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **Article 110 (The "Only" Clause):** Article 110(1) states a bill is a Money Bill if it contains *only* provisions dealing with matters enumerated in sub-clauses (a) to (g) (taxation, borrowing, CFI appropriation). The word "only" is the crux of the legal dispute, arguing that extraneous provisions disqualify it.
* **The Speaker's Absolute Certification (Article 110(3)):** If any question arises whether a Bill is a Money Bill, the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha shall be final. This grants immense power to a politically affiliated Chair.
* **Bypassing the Council of States:** The Rajya Sabha represents the federal interests of the states. Bypassing it via the Money Bill route is considered a fraud on the Constitution and a direct assault on cooperative federalism.
* **Judicial Review Immunity (Article 122 vs. Substantive Illegality):** Historically, courts refrained from questioning the Speaker's certificate under Article 122 (immunity from procedural irregularity). However, recent jurisprudence (like *Aadhaar* and *Rojer Mathew*) established that classifying an ordinary bill as a Money Bill is a *substantive illegality*, not just a procedural error, and is therefore open to judicial review.
* **The Aadhaar Precedent:** The Aadhaar Act (2016) contained massive surveillance and identity creation provisions, but was passed as a Money Bill because Section 7 linked Aadhaar to subsidies flowing from the Consolidated Fund of India.
* **PMLA and Tribunal Reforms:** Severe amendments to the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (giving ED sweeping powers) and changes to Tribunal appointments were passed by slipping them into the Finance Act (a Money Bill), avoiding rigorous upper house debate.

## 3. Evolution timeline

* **1950 (Constituent Assembly):** The framers restricted the Money Bill definition specifically to ensure the Lok Sabha (directly elected) had supremacy *only* in matters of state finance, not general legislation.
* **2016 (Aadhaar Act Passed):** The government pushed the Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act as a Money Bill to overcome strong opposition in the Rajya Sabha.
* **2017 (Finance Act Reforms):** The government used the Finance Act (a traditional Money Bill) to overhaul the appointment rules of 19 independent tribunals (like NGT, CAT), a move entirely unrelated to national taxation.
* **2018 (K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India - Aadhaar Verdict):** A 5-judge Supreme Court bench upheld (4:1) the passage of the Aadhaar Act as a Money Bill. However, Justice D.Y. Chandrachud (now CJI) issued a fierce dissent, calling it a "fraud on the Constitution."
* **2019 (Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank):** Another 5-judge bench struck down the Tribunal rules passed via the Finance Act 2017. Crucially, this bench expressed deep doubts about the correctness of the majority *Aadhaar* verdict regarding the Money Bill definition and referred the entire issue to a larger 7-judge Constitution Bench.
* **2023-2024 (The 7-Judge Bench Reference):** Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud constituted a 7-judge Constitution bench to finally settle the boundaries of Article 110 and whether amendments to the PMLA and Tribunal Rules via Money Bills were constitutional. The matter remains pending as of late 2026.

## 4. Quotation

* **Justice D.Y. Chandrachud (Dissent in Aadhaar Case, 2018):** "Bypassing the Rajya Sabha to pass an ordinary bill as a Money Bill is a subterfuge and a fraud on the Constitution... The Rajya Sabha represents the pluralist diversity of India, and bypassing it undermines the federal structure."
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Defended the Rajya Sabha's role, stating it serves as a revising chamber to delay hasty legislation and ensure that the "passions of the moment" do not dictate the permanent laws of the land.
* **Maryada (मर्यादा - Constitutional Limits):**
* *Sanskrit Root:* **'मृ' (Mri - सीमा/हद्द)** धातु से बना है, जिसका अर्थ है सीमा या अनुशासन।

## 5. Committee or Reports

* **The Supreme Court Reference (*Rojer Mathew* Judgment, 2019):** The most impactful legal document on this issue. The 5-judge bench noted that the word "only" in Article 110(1) must be given strict meaning. If extraneous, non-financial matters (like Tribunal appointments or PMLA arrest powers) are smuggled into a Finance Bill, it destroys the bicameral structure.
* **National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (NCRWC, 2002):** Highlighted the need to maintain the delicate balance between the two Houses. It warned against the Executive using procedural loopholes to evade scrutiny by the Council of States.
* **Law Commission of India:** Has historically cautioned against the omnibus use of Finance Acts to push non-tax administrative reforms, arguing it severely compromises the quality of legislative scrutiny and public debate.

## 6. Indices

*(Note: Abuse of parliamentary procedure negatively affects international democratic and governance metrics)*

* **Democracy Index (Economist Intelligence Unit):** India's scores on "Functioning of Government" and "Checks and Balances" are depressed when the primary legislative check (the Upper House) is systematically sidelined through procedural subterfuge.
* **Rule of Law Index (World Justice Project):** The "Constraints on Government Powers" parameter drops when the ruling party uses the Speaker's absolute certification power to bypass legislative opposition.
* **V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) Deliberative Component Index:** Measures the depth of legislative debate. Passing complex laws like Aadhaar or PMLA amendments as Money Bills completely shuts out the Rajya Sabha's deliberative capacity, severely lowering this index.

## 7. Global comparison

* **United Kingdom (The Parliament Act 1911):** The concept of a Money Bill originated here to ensure the unelected House of Lords couldn't block the budget of the elected House of Commons. However, the UK Speaker's certification of a Money Bill is strictly limited by convention, and it is almost *never* used to push through sweeping, non-financial public policy reforms.
* **Australia:** The Senate (Upper House) cannot amend proposed laws imposing taxation or appropriating revenue. However, the Australian Constitution strictly prevents the government from "tacking" (adding extraneous, non-financial matters to a money bill to force it through). Tacking invalidates the bill. India lacks an explicit anti-tacking provision, leading to the current crisis.
* **United States:** All revenue bills must originate in the House of Representatives, but the Senate has full power to amend or reject them. There is no concept of a "Money Bill" that entirely bypasses the upper house, ensuring robust bicameralism for all legislation.

## 9. Flow chart

**The Abuse Mechanism and Judicial Check**

1. **Executive Intent:** Government wants to pass a highly controversial administrative bill (e.g., Aadhaar / PMLA amendments).
2. **Political Reality:** Government lacks a majority in the Rajya Sabha.
3. **The Subterfuge (Tacking):** Government attaches a minor financial clause (e.g., drawing funds from the Consolidated Fund) to the massive administrative bill.
4. **Speaker's Certification:** The Lok Sabha Speaker (acting in a partisan manner) certifies it as a Money Bill under Article 110(3).
5. **Rajya Sabha Bypassed:** The RS is forced to return it within 14 days; the LS ignores all recommendations. The Bill becomes Law.
6. **The Constitutional Challenge (Post-2018):** Citizens challenge the law in the Supreme Court. The SC (via *Rojer Mathew* logic) pierces the Speaker's certificate to check if the bill dealt *only* with finance, striking it down if extraneous matters are found.

## 10. Recent occurrence - debate

* **The Pending 7-Judge Bench Resolution (2026):** The Supreme Court has formed a 7-judge Constitution Bench (headed by the CJI) to finally settle the boundaries of a Money Bill based on the *Rojer Mathew* reference. The Centre is heavily contesting this, arguing that the Speaker's certificate is completely immune from judicial review under Article 122 (procedural immunity).
* **Electoral Bonds Scheme:** The heavily debated (and recently struck down) Electoral Bonds scheme was originally introduced through the Finance Act, 2017 (a Money Bill). Critics endlessly debated that political funding transparency has nothing to do with national taxation, making its passage unconstitutional.
* **PMLA Amendments:** Sweeping powers granted to the Enforcement Directorate (ED) regarding raids, arrests, and the reversal of the burden of proof were slipped in as Money Bills via Finance Acts. The SC upheld the PMLA's draconian powers but kept the question of its passage as a Money Bill open for the 7-judge bench.

## 11. Challenges

* **Speaker's Partisanship:** The root of the problem is the Speaker's final authority under Article 110(3). Since Indian Speakers do not sever party ties, they face immense pressure to certify ordinary bills as Money Bills to help the executive.
* **Erosion of Federalism:** The Rajya Sabha represents state interests. Bypassing it on crucial subjects (like Tribunals, which affect state administration, or PMLA, which affects state policing) severely damages cooperative federalism.
* **The "Tacking" Loophole:** Almost every government action eventually involves drawing money from the Consolidated Fund. If the mere expenditure of money allows a bill to be classified as a Money Bill, then *every* bill can become a Money Bill, rendering the Rajya Sabha entirely obsolete.
* **Judicial Restraint vs. Intervention:** Courts walk a tightrope. Striking down the Speaker's certification breaches the traditional wall between the judiciary and parliamentary proceedings, but failing to do so allows the executive to shred the Constitution.

## 12. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Defining the Word "Only" (SC Clarification):** The impending 7-judge bench must provide a restrictive and iron-clad interpretation of the word "only" in Article 110(1). A bill must be overwhelmingly and substantively financial to qualify; incidental expenditure cannot justify the certification.
* **Judicial Review of the Speaker's Certificate:** The Supreme Court must decisively lay down that the Speaker's certification is not shielded by Article 122 (procedural irregularity) but is a matter of substantive constitutional illegality, making it fully subject to judicial review (reaffirming the dissenting view in the Aadhaar case).
* **Adopting the "Anti-Tacking" Rule:** Parliament should amend its Rules of Procedure (or the Constitution) to adopt the Australian model, explicitly banning "tacking." Any non-financial provisions attached to a Money Bill should automatically invalidate the bill.
* **Neutrality of the Speaker:** Long-term reform requires insulating the Speaker from political pressure (e.g., adopting the UK convention of "Once a Speaker, always a Speaker"), ensuring they evaluate Article 110 certifications objectively rather than politically.

## 13. Best practices

* **Australia (Anti-Tacking Constitutional Provision):** Section 54 of the Australian Constitution states: "The proposed law which appropriates revenue or moneys for the ordinary annual services of the Government shall deal *only* with such appropriation." If a government tries to sneak in policy changes, the High Court immediately strikes it down. This is the exact constitutional safeguard India needs.
* **United Kingdom (Strict Scrutiny of Certification):** In the UK, a Money Bill is strictly defined by the Parliament Act 1911. The Speaker consults two senior members of the Panel of Chairs before certifying a bill. This consultative process, combined with the Speaker's absolute political neutrality, ensures the Money Bill route is almost never abused for substantive policy laws.

## 14. Conclusion

* The Money Bill exception was crafted by the founders to prevent legislative gridlock over the nation's budget, not to provide the executive with a bypass lane to evade democratic scrutiny on complex public policies.
* The systematic abuse of this route poses a mortal threat to bicameralism and federalism. The upcoming 7-judge Supreme Court ruling is a watershed moment; it must unequivocally check this executive overreach to ensure the Rajya Sabha is not reduced to a constitutional spectator.
