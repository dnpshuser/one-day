# Cosntitutional Commission: NCSC, NCST: Enforcement Powers & Limitations: NCSC (Art. 338) & NCST (Art. 338A)

## 1. Intro

* **Constitutional Mandate:** The NCSC under A-338 and the NCST under A-338A are apex constitutional bodies established to safeguard the socio-economic, educational, and political rights of marginalized communities.

* **The Paradox of Power:** debate: investigatory power to summon but finding are only recommendatory - toothless in punitive actions.

## 2. Features/Qualities

* **Civil Court Powers (Art 338(8) & 338A(8)):** During investigations- sweeping powers of a civil court trying a suit including
  *  summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person, 
  *  requiring the production of any document, 
  *  receiving evidence on affidavits,
  *  requisitioning public records.
* **Mandatory Consultation (Art 338(9) & 338A(9)): vital advisory bodies in law-making.** The UG and SG are constitutionally mandated to consult the Commissions on all major policy matters affecting SCs and STs.
* **Presidential Reporting Mechanism:** 
  * Both Comms submit their reports to President. 
  * The President => Parliament (State Governor => state legislatures),  
  * Action Taken Report (ATR) explaining why any recommendations were not accepted.
* **Suo-Motu Cognizance:** *suo-motu* quality to 
  * inquiries into incidents of atrocities, 
  * deprivation of rights,
  * policy failures
* **Lack of Adjudicatory Power:** they lack
  * adjudicatory tribunal
  * criminal court. 
  * can't pronounce binding verdicts, 
  * issue injunctions, 
  * award compensation directly
  * sentence offenders.

## 3. Sub-components of the topic

* **The Investigation vs. Enforcement Gap:** can investigate but rely on police to file FIR and judiciary to convict, where cases often stall.
* **Service Safeguard Monitoring:** Recomm are ignored citing 'non-binding' on A-16(4)-Gov jobs and PSU promotions.
* **The NCST & PESA/FRA Oversight:** 
  * NCST monitors FRA"06 and PESA'96 implementation. 
  * frequently battles the MoEFCC (Environment Ministry) over the illegal diversion of tribal land without Gram Sabha consent, yet its directives to halt mining are routinely bypassed.
* **The Delay in Action Taken Reports (ATRs):** Parliament delays tabling report and ATR for years, issue looses political momentum and public scrutiny.
* **Vulnerability to Vacancies:** Vacancies in C, VC or Member(dependent on Executives) paralyze the Commissions' ability to conduct hearings or issue directives.

## 4. Evolution timeline

* 1950: The Special Officer: A-338 originally provided for only a single "Special Officer" (Commissioner for SCs and STs) to investigate all matters of constitutional safeguards.

* 1978: First Non-Statutory Commission: inadequacy of a single officer recognised; multi-member, non-statutory Commission for SCs and STs setup via an executive resolution.

* 1990: 65th CAA: non-statutory body replaced by a high-level, multi-member statutory National Commission for SCs and STs, formally amending A-338.


* 2003: 89th CAA: combined Commission was bifurcated. The NCSC retained A-338, and a separate NCST was established under the new A-338A.


* 2018: 102nd CAA: 
  * NCSC was relieved of its duty to investigate the grievances of OBCs
  * New NCBC was granted constitutional status under Article 338B.


* 2022-2023: The Great Nicobar Friction: 
  * NCST demanded halting of forest clearance for the mega Great Nicobar project citing threats to the Shompen PVTG. 
  * Union Env Minst bypassed the NCST's objections.

## 11. Flow chart

**The NCSC/NCST Grievance Redressal Paradox**

1. **Trigger:** SC/ST employee alleges denial of promotion due to caste bias.
2. **Cognizance:** Commission registers case and issues notice to the Department Head.
3. **Civil Court Powers Deployed:** Commission summons the Secretary and demands the Reservation Roster documents (Article 338(8)).
4. **Hearing & Finding:** Commission finds clear violation of Article 16(4).
5. **The Enforcement Wall:** Commission issues a "Recommendation" to promote the employee.
6. **Executive Defiance:** The Department refuses, citing Supreme Court precedent that Commission orders are advisory, not binding.
7. **Dead End:** The employee is forced to start over and file a fresh case in the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) or High Court.

## 13. Advantage/Merits/Role

* **Institutional Empathy:** provide a dedicated, culturally sensitive forum where marginalized SC/ST citizens can voice grievances.
* **Data Aggregation and Exposure:** exposes systemic administrative failures to the public by scrutinizing Executive policies.
* **Civil Court Investigation:** 
  * The power to summon a Chief Secretary or a DGP directly to New Delhi acts as a massive psychological deterrent 
  * forces high-level bureaucratic accountability that an ordinary citizen could never achieve in reality
* **Policy Consultation:** Despite being ignored often, the mandatory consultation clause (Art 338(9)) ensures that the perspective of SC/ST communities is officially placed on the constitutional record during the drafting of laws like the FRA or SC/ST Atrocities Amendment Acts.

## 14. Disadvantage/De-Merits

*(The Structural Failures)*

* **Recommendatory Nature:** Executives routinely ignore their directives without fear of contempt proceedings.
* **Politicization of Appointments:** they rarely take an aggressive stance against the very Union Government that appointed them.
* **Post-Mortem Role:** They react to atrocities *after* they occur lacking the intelligence network, field staff, or structural mandate to prevent them.
* **Dependence on State Police:** 
  * They lack their own independent investigative agency (like the CBI or ED). 
  * They rely on local state police for ground reports—the same police who are often accused of complicity or apathy in atrocity cases.
* **No Power to Punish transforming this commission to a mere toothless tiger** 

## 15. Challenges

* **Mounting Pendency:** 
  * understaffing- frequent vacancies
  * lack of technical legal expertise, 
  * Massive unresolved complaints(67K), 
  * defeating the purpose of speedy justice.
* **Delayed Action Taken Reports (ATRs):** 
  * The constitutional mechanism of accountability is broken. 
  * The UG and SG delay tabling the Commission reports and their ATRs in Parliament/Assemblies for years, 
  * ensuring the issues become politically irrelevant.
* **Jurisdictional Overlap & Friction:** 
  * The NCST frequently clashes with the Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA) and the MoEFCC, 
  * while the NCSC overlaps with the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC). 
  * fragmented investigations and bureaucratic turf wars.
* **Financial Dependence:** dependent on the nodal ministries (Social Justice and Tribal Affairs) for their budget and administrative sanctions, crippling their institutional autonomy.

## 16. Way forward / solution / positive outlook

* **Confer Binding Adjudicatory Powers (Xaxa Recommendation):** Parliament must amend Articles 338 and 338A to grant the Commissions the 
  * power to issue binding, 
  * legally enforceable directions (specifically regarding reservation rosters and FRA violations) and 
  * the power to penalize officials for non-compliance, 
  * similar to the Central Information Commission (CIC).
* **Independent Selection Collegium:** Should Executive monopoly in select committee => bipartisan collegium(PM, LoP, CJI)
* **Independent Investigative Wing:** should be provided to bypass reliance on complicit local state police during atrocity probes.
* **Statutory Deadline for ATRs:** Should ensure 3-month statutory deadline for the government to table the Commission's reports and the ATR before the legislature.
* **Mandatory Veto on Sub-Plan Diversion:** to avoid funds getting diverted to general expenditure

## 17. Best practices

* **India (The Election Commission Model):** Can use ECI model for full autonomity.
* **South Africa (The SAHRC Litigation Model):** Consider SAHRC Litigation model for implementation enhancement
* **Canada (Binding Tribunal Mechanism):** Canada separates the investigation (Human Rights Commission) from the enforcement (Human Rights Tribunal). - SC/ST investigation + SC/ST Tribunal

## 18. Conclusion

* Upgrading these Commissions from "recommendatory watchdogs" to "binding constitutional adjudicators" is the essential next step in realizing the Ambedkarite vision of true social justice.

* The National Commissions for SCs and STs embody a profound constitutional paradox: they possess the majestic procedural powers of a civil court, yet suffer from the substantive impotence of a mere advisory committee.

* In the era of *Amrit Kaal*, marginalized communities do not merely need an institutional shoulder to cry on; they need an institutional sword to enforce their rights.

### 1. Context & Contemporary Triggers

Constitutional commissions designed to protect marginalized communities are experiencing a crisis of credibility. Despite being elevated from statutory committees to constitutional status via the **65th Amendment (1990)** and bifurcated via the **89th Amendment (2003)**, both the National Commission for Scheduled Castes (**NCSC - Article 338**) and the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (**NCST - Article 338A**) face criticism for operating as institutional "paper tigers".

Key contemporary developments highlighting this operational gap include:

* **UPSC CSE Mains 2026 Core Demand (GS-II, Q5):** *"Do constitutional bodies like the National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) and the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) possess adequate enforcement powers to secure substantive social equality? Suggest measures to enhance their institutional effectiveness."*

* **Sub-Classification of SCs/STs (*State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh, August 2024*):** The 7-judge Constitution Bench permitted states to sub-classify SCs/STs for affirmative action. This highlighted **Article 338(9) / 338A(9)**, which mandates that the Union and State governments consult the Commissions on major policy decisions—a consultative process that is often reduced to an administrative afterthought.
* **The NCST vs. MoEFCC Stand-off over Forest Conservation Rules:** The NCST publicly challenged the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) for notifying revised Forest Conservation rules that eased project clearances without prior Gram Sabha consent under the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006. The dispute underscored the Commission’s inability to stop or modify executive rules that dilute tribal safeguards.
* **Atrocities & Implementation Deficit:** Despite rising cases under the PoA (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, NCSC/NCST annual reports face multi-year delays in being tabled in Parliament, depriving them of legislative backing.

#### A. Core Mandate under Clauses (5) of Articles 338 & 338A

* **Investigate & Monitor:** Oversee all matters relating to constitutional and legal safeguards for SCs/STs and evaluate their working.
* **Inquire into Deprivations:** Inquire into specific complaints regarding the deprivation of rights and safeguards.
* **Participate & Advise:** Participate in and advise on the socio-economic planning process of the Union and States.
* **Annual & Special Reports:** Present reports to the President on the implementation of safeguards.

#### B. The Legal Nature of Powers: Clause (8) vs. Clause (9)

* **Clause (8) – Civil Court Powers:** While investigating matters or inquiring into complaints, the Commissions are vested with the powers of a **Civil Court trying a suit under the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908**:
* Summoning and enforcing the attendance of witnesses under oath.
* Discovery and production of official documents.
* Receiving evidence on affidavits.
* Requisitioning public records from courts or offices.


* **Clause (9) – Mandatory Consultation Clause:** Requires the Union and State governments to **consult the Commission on all major policy matters** affecting SCs and STs.

#### C. Theoretical Anchor: Substantive Equality vs. Formal Equality

* **Formal Equality:** Treating everyone identically before the law (Article 14).
* **Substantive Equality (Sandra Fredman / Amartya Sen):** Requires redressing structural disadvantages, accommodating difference, and eliminating institutional bias. NCSC and NCST were conceptualized not merely as complaint forums, but as **Fourth-Branch Integrity Institutions** meant to turn formal guarantees into substantive equality.

### 3. Core Dimensions & Applied Frictions

#### A. The Illusion of Civil Court Powers (*The IO Bank Doctrine*)

The central legal limit on NCSC/NCST authority was established in ***All India Indian Overseas Bank SC & ST Employees' Welfare Association v. Union of India (1996)***:

* The Supreme Court held that the powers of a Civil Court granted under Clause (8) are **strictly procedural investigative tools** (summoning records, examining witnesses).
* The Commission **is NOT an adjudicatory court**.
* It **cannot grant interim injunctions, issue stay orders, or grant substantive relief** (e.g., halting a discriminatory promotion roster or stopping a land eviction).
* Its final findings are strictly **recommendatory**, not binding decrees.

#### B. The Dilution of Mandatory Policy Consultation (Clause 9)

While Articles 338(9) and 338A(9) make consultation mandatory, governments often bypass them during major policy changes:

* Policies concerning affirmative action criteria, the classification of communities, or changes to welfare schemes are routinely rolled out without prior input from the Commissions.
* In practice, courts have rarely struck down executive policies solely because the Commission was not consulted, turning a constitutional requirement into optional executive tokenism.

#### C. Tabling Backlogs and the Action Taken Memorandum (ATM)

Under Clauses (6) and (7), the President and State Governors must table the Commission's reports in Parliament and State Legislatures, accompanied by an **Action Taken Memorandum (ATM)** explaining reasons for rejecting any recommendations:

* Executive ministries often delay tabling reports for three to five years.
* As a result, reports are debated long after the violations occur, blunting parliamentary scrutiny.

#### D. Institutional Overlaps and Jurisdictional Competition

The human rights architecture faces jurisdictional duplication across multiple bodies:

* In cases involving tribal women or sanitary workers, jurisdiction overlaps across **NCSC/NCST, the National Commission for Women (NCW), the National Commission for Safai Karamcharis (NCSK), and the NHRC**.


* Disputants often bounce between forums, while regular police and Special Courts under the PoA Act retain exclusive jurisdiction over criminal trials.

### 5. Reforms & Forward-Looking Roadmap

#### A. Upgrading Procedural Powers to Quasi-Judicial Authority

* **Enforceable Summons & Contempt Sanctions:** Grant the Commissions limited contempt powers (similar to the Central Information Commission or National Green Tribunal) to fine public officials who repeatedly ignore summons, refuse to yield records, or fail to respond within 30 days.
* **Interim Protective Orders:** Amend the parent provisions to empower Commissions to issue time-bound **status-quo orders** in severe cases involving unlawful evictions or immediate physical threats to tribal habitats, pending high court review.

#### B. Reforming Appointments & Selection

* **Independent Selection Panel:** Shift away from unilateral executive selection toward a tripartite committee consisting of:
1. The Prime Minister of India.
2. The Chief Justice of India (or nominee).
3. The Leader of the Opposition (or single largest opposition party in Lok Sabha).


* **Merit-Driven Composition:** Reserve membership for retired higher-judiciary jurists, tribal-affairs specialists, and proven subaltern rights advocates to limit political patronage.

#### C. Establishing an Independent Investigative Cadre

* Create an autonomous, in-house **Special Investigation Unit (SIU)** within both Commissions, led by officers on deputation who report directly to the Commission rather than state home departments, enabling independent fact-finding at atrocity sites.

#### D. Enforcing Statutory Timelines for Reports & Consultation

* **Strict 6-Month Tabling Mandate:** Make it mandatory to table annual reports before Parliament within six months of submission, with a provision for automatic public disclosure if the deadline is missed.
* **Judicially Enforced Prior Consultation:** The Supreme Court should interpret Clause (9) as requiring **prior, substantive consultation**, making any major affirmative-action or tribal policy notified without Commission appraisal subject to judicial review.

2. ***Collector of Bilaspur v. Ajit Jogi (2011):*** Reaffirmed that Commissions cannot verify or cancel caste/tribal status certificates—this is an executive/verification committee function (*Madhuri Patil* guidelines).

5. ***Second Administrative Reforms Commission (13th Report):*** Recommended rationalizing overlapping human rights commissions and introducing strict timelines for processing their reports.
