## 1. Intro

* **BRICS** is a premier intergovernmental organization that originated as a coalition of five major emerging economies (Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa) and has now expanded to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the UAE as of January 2024.
* It serves as the geopolitical and geo-economic vanguard of the Global South, aiming to counterbalance Western hegemony, champion a multipolar world order, and reform multilateral institutions like the UN, IMF, and World Bank.

## 2. Sub-components of the topic [most imp]

* **New Development Bank (NDB):** Headquartered in Shanghai, the NDB finances sustainable development and infrastructure projects across emerging economies. Unlike the World Bank, it operates without strict political conditionality or structural adjustment mandates.
* **Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA):** A financial safety net framework designed to provide short-term liquidity support to member nations facing balance of payment pressures, reducing reliance on the IMF.
* **The 2024 Expansion (BRICS+):** The historic inclusion of Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the UAE transformed BRICS from an emerging markets club into a massive demographic and energy powerhouse, controlling nearly half of the world's population.
* **De-dollarization & Local Currency Trading:** A strategic, long-term push to bypass the US Dollar in intra-BRICS trade. Members are actively exploring cross-border transactions in local currencies and developing a "BRICS Pay" system to circumvent SWIFT.
* **Institutional Frameworks:** BRICS is highly institutionalized beyond annual summits, featuring the BRICS Business Council, Academic Forum, Parliamentary Forum, and working groups on counter-terrorism and space cooperation.
* **Energy Geopolitics:** With the induction of major Middle Eastern producers (Iran, UAE), BRICS now controls a massive share of global oil production and reserves, fundamentally altering global energy security dynamics.
* **Strategic Multi-alignment:** For countries like India and Brazil, BRICS is a platform for multi-alignment—engaging with adversaries (like China) and heavily sanctioned states (like Russia/Iran) while simultaneously maintaining robust strategic ties with the West (e.g., the Quad).

## 3. Evolution timeline

* 2001: The 'BRIC' Acronym Coined
Jim O'Neill, an economist at Goldman Sachs, coined the term "BRIC" to highlight the growth potential of Brazil, Russia, India, and China, predicting they would dominate the global economy by 2050.


* 2006: Formalization in New York
The foreign ministers of the four countries met formally for the first time on the margins of the UN General Assembly in New York, transforming an economic acronym into a geopolitical reality.


* 2009: First Summit
The first formal BRIC Summit was held in Yekaterinburg, Russia, amidst the global financial crisis, focusing on reforming global financial institutions.


* 2010-2011: Inclusion of South Africa
South Africa was invited to join, adding an African footprint to the bloc. The group officially became "BRICS" at the 2011 Sanya Summit in China.


* 2014: Fortaleza Declaration
The historic 6th Summit in Brazil led to the signing of the agreement establishing the New Development Bank (NDB) and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA).


* 2023: Johannesburg Summit & Expansion
At the 15th Summit, BRICS announced its first major expansion, inviting six new members, fundamentally altering the bloc's global weight.


* 2024: BRICS+ Becomes Operational
Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the UAE officially became full members on January 1, 2024. (Argentina withdrew following a change in government, and Saudi Arabia's status remains under domestic review).

## 5. Reports

* **Goldman Sachs BRIC Reports (2001 & 2003):** The foundational economic papers that originally projected the BRIC nations' trajectory, arguing their combined economies would eclipse the G6 (G7 minus Canada) by 2050 due to demographics and productivity growth.
* **NDB Annual Reports:** Crucial documents detailing the bank's project approvals (over $32 billion to date), with a strong emphasis on clean energy, transport infrastructure, and water sanitation across the Global South without political strings attached.
* **BRICS Business Council Annual Reports:** Highlights intra-bloc trade bottlenecks and tracks the progression of non-tariff barrier removals, advocating for smoother customs cooperation and digital economy integration.
* **Observer Research Foundation (ORF) Briefs:** Frequently analyzes India's strategic balancing within BRICS, noting how New Delhi uses the platform to limit Chinese hegemony over the Global South while maintaining an independent foreign policy.
* **UNCTAD World Investment Report:** Tracks the massive outward and inward Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) flows of BRICS nations, showing how South-South economic cooperation is gradually reducing reliance on Western capital markets.

## 6. Indices

* **Global GDP (PPP) Share:** BRICS+ has officially surpassed the G7 in terms of Purchasing Power Parity. The expanded BRICS accounts for roughly 36% of global GDP (PPP), compared to the G7's 30%.
* **Global Population Share:** The expanded bloc represents nearly 46% of the world's population, giving it an undeniable demographic dividend compared to the aging populations of the G7 (under 10%).
* **Global Oil Production Index:** Prior to expansion, BRICS had limited control over oil. With the UAE and Iran, the bloc now controls over 40% of global crude oil production and reserves, fundamentally shifting energy market indices.
* **Global Innovation Index (WIPO):** Tracks the rapid technological rise within the bloc, specifically China (ranking near the top 10 globally) and India, reflecting a shift in R&D and patent filings from West to East.
* **Human Development Index (HDI):** Exposes the internal contradictions within BRICS. While Russia and the UAE score "Very High," India and Ethiopia fall into the "Medium" or "Low" categories, showing stark developmental disparities.

## 7. Global comparison

* **BRICS vs. G7 (Nature of the Bloc):** The G7 is a homogeneous group of high-income, Western liberal democracies with aligned political and security interests. BRICS is highly heterogeneous, comprising democracies (India, Brazil), single-party states (China), and theocracies (Iran), bound by geo-economic interests rather than political ideology.
* **NDB vs. World Bank:** The World Bank assigns voting power based on capital contributions (giving the US veto power). The NDB structurally mandates equal voting rights (20% each for the original 5) and allows no single member a veto, ensuring democratic financial governance.
* **CRA vs. IMF:** The IMF provides bailouts with severe macroeconomic "structural adjustment" conditionalities (austerity). The CRA acts as a liquidity safety net for BRICS members without demanding sovereign domestic policy changes.
* **Expansion Model (BRICS vs. EU):** The EU expands regionally with incredibly strict criteria regarding human rights, democracy, and free-market economics. BRICS expands globally with minimal ideological barriers, focusing on a multipolar alignment and economic potential.
* **Security Posture:** Unlike NATO (a collective defense treaty) or the Quad (a maritime security dialogue), BRICS is explicitly non-military. It focuses on geo-economics and global governance reform, though the West views it with increasing security suspicion.

## 8. Committee reports

* **Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs (India):** Regularly evaluates the strategic utility of BRICS for India. Recent reports emphasize that India must ensure the bloc does not become a Chinese mouthpiece or an explicitly anti-American forum.
* **Johannesburg II Declaration (2023):** The defining committee document that established the guiding principles, standards, and criteria for BRICS expansion, moving the bloc from a closed group to an expanding platform for the Global South.
* **Fortaleza Declaration (2014):** The seminal agreement that laid the operational and legal foundation for the New Development Bank, setting the terms for authorized capital ($100 billion) and equal shareholding.
* **BRICS Think Tank Council (BTTC):** An academic and policy committee that produces actionable recommendations for summit leaders, heavily focusing in recent years on supply chain resilience, semiconductor manufacturing, and climate finance.
* **St. Petersburg Action Plan:** Formulated during Russia's presidency, focusing heavily on counter-terrorism cooperation, setting up working groups to curb terror financing and drug trafficking across member borders.

## 10. Flow chart

**The Process of Bypassing the Dollar (Intra-BRICS Trade Hypothesis)**

1. **Trade Agreement:** India and UAE (both BRICS+ members) agree on an oil trade deal.
2. **Invoice Generation:** Rather than invoicing in US Dollars, the invoice is generated in Indian Rupees (INR) or UAE Dirhams (AED).
3. **Local Currency Settlement (LCS) System:** The importer's central bank guarantees the exchange rate directly with the exporter's central bank.
4. **Bypassing SWIFT:** The transaction utilizes a BRICS alternative payment messaging system (under development) instead of the Western-controlled SWIFT network.
5. **Final Settlement:** Payments are cleared in local currency, saving currency conversion fees and insulating both nations from US Treasury sanctions or dollar liquidity crunches.

## 11. Recent context/issues/debate

* **The Anti-West vs. Non-West Debate:** With the induction of heavily sanctioned nations like Iran and the dominance of China/Russia, Western analysts argue BRICS is becoming an explicitly anti-American axis. India and Brazil fiercely debate internally and externally to keep the bloc strictly "non-Western" and focused on economic cooperation.
* **India-China Border Friction:** The ongoing military standoff at the Line of Actual Control (LAC) severely limits the political cohesion of BRICS. India restricts Chinese investments, contrasting sharply with the bloc's goal of deep economic integration.
* **The BRICS Currency Mirage:** While "de-dollarization" is a buzzword, creating a unified BRICS currency (like the Euro) is widely debated as economically impossible given the massive disparities in inflation, monetary policy, and export dependence among members.
* **Saudi Arabia's Ambiguity:** Saudi Arabia was invited to join BRICS in 2023 but has delayed formalizing its membership, highlighting the immense pressure Middle Eastern states face in balancing their security reliance on the US with economic ties to China.
* **UNSC Reform Stagnation:** Despite a decade of joint declarations, China and Russia (the P5 members of BRICS) have offered only lip service—but no concrete action—towards securing permanent UNSC seats for India, Brazil, or South Africa, exposing internal hypocrisy.

## 12. Way forward / Solution / Positive Outlook

* **Operationalizing BRICS Pay:** Instead of chasing a unified currency, the bloc must aggressively finalize and implement a secure digital cross-border payment system that allows for seamless local currency trading, insulating the Global South from weaponized sanctions.
* **Institutionalizing the Expansion:** BRICS must establish a formal secretariat and clear, transparent rules for future expansion and "partner country" status to prevent the group from becoming an unwieldy, unmanageable talk shop.
* **Leveraging NDB for Climate Finance:** The NDB should position itself as the premier lender for the Global South's green transition, aggressively funding solar and wind infrastructure to counter the West's failure to deliver on climate finance pledges.
* **India’s Balancing Act:** India must continue to play the role of the democratic anchor within BRICS, using its diplomatic weight to veto any explicitly anti-Western declarations pushed by Beijing or Moscow.
* **South-South Supply Chains:** Establishing resilient supply chain frameworks specifically for critical minerals and pharmaceuticals among member states to reduce dependencies on Western tech monopolies.
* **Resolving Bilateral Friction:** A positive outlook requires the realization that BRICS can only reach its true geopolitical potential if major members (India and China) establish a stable *modus vivendi* and compartmentalize border disputes away from multilateral economic goals.

## 13. Conclusion

* BRICS is the most visible manifestation of the irreversible shift in global gravity from the trans-Atlantic axis to the Global South, proving that developing nations can successfully create their own parallel institutions (like the NDB).
* While it faces profound internal contradictions and geopolitical friction, its continued expansion and economic magnetism demonstrate that a multipolar world order is no longer a distant theory, but a functioning reality.
