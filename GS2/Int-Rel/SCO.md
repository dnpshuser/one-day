1. Intro: the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) is a permanent intergovernmental organisation, created in 2001 in Shanghai, China representing 25% of the world GDP and over 40% of global population.
2. Evolution
   1. USSR collapse 1991
   2. security vulnerabilities and disputed borders issues following the collapse
   3. Shangai 5: RCKKT
   4. primary goal: 
      1. demilitarize borders
      2. resolve territorial demarcations which were left unresolved
      3. build military mutual trust
      4. implement force reduction agreement
   5. 1990-2000: New challenges
      1. Rise of Taliban - 1996
      2. The Tazikistan Civil War - 1992-1997
      3. Unrest in China's Xinjiang province
      4. Millitant and separatist's network
   6. estb: 2001, June 15
   7. 3 evils: 
      1. Terrorism
      2. Separatism
      3. Extremism
3. aims and objectives
   1. Fight against 3 evils
      1. Terrorism
      2. Separatism
      3. Extremism
   2. Preventing Unipolar Hagemony of US/NATO
   3. Limiting western footprint in central asia - following US invasion in Afg in 2001
   4. Trade corridors
4. significance for India
   1. Security & Counter-Terrorism(सुरक्षा एवं आतंकवाद-रोधी) Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS) gives India intelligence sharing and joint counter-terrorism exercises against cross-border terrorism.
   2. Connect Central Asia Policy (मध्य एशिया नीति) Direct gateway to hydrocarbon- and uranium-rich Central Asian Republics (CARs) — e.g. Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan — securing India’s long-term energy needs.
   3. Strategic Autonomy(रणनीतिक स्वायत्तता) Balances SCO membership (China, Russia) with Quad membership (United States, Japan, Australia) — multi-alignment in a multipolar world.
5. Key outcomes of 26th summit
   1. Regional peace, security and development
   2. let newer member shape the SCO's future
   3. Centre to counter security threats, boosts intelligence sharing against terrorism and organised crime
   4. Anti drug centre against drug addiction and synthetic drugs upto 2030
   5. Green Tech corridors
   6. biodiversity conservation with joint management
6. India's stance at 26th summit
   1. PM outlined Security, Connectivity and Opportunity as SCO's three future pillars
   2. called to dismantle terror financing and safe havens without double standards
   3. resolve conflicts through dialogue and diplomacy, not battlefield
   4. all connectivity proj must respect sovereignty and territorial integrity
      1. indirect critique to China's BRI(Belt and Road Initiative) and CPEC(China Pakistan Economic Corridor)
7. Challenges
   1. Works by consensus: Ind-China and Ind-Pak tension slows the process
   2. Growing China/Russia influence + Iran and Belarus addition risks 'anti-western' perception
   3. Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure(RATS) has limited capacity to enforce collective action
   4. China's larger economy dominates - 
      1. risk of trade imbalance, 
      2. Chinese dependence
      3. China's debt trap policy
   5. Differing interests: 
      1. China's BRI
      2. (INSTC): Indias International North-South Transport corridor
8. Conclusion
   1. The SCO can play a meaningful role in shaping a multipolar and just world order only if it moves beyond internal bilateral disputes, addresses terrorism through consistent standards, and focuses on practical cooperation.
   2. For India, the SCO remains a vital Eurasian platform to preserve strategic autonomy, strengthen connectivity with Central Asia, and advance its economic and security interests.
