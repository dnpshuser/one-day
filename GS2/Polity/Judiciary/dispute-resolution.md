### Alternate Dispute Resolution
1. Access to justice and efficient dispute settlement
2. Lok Adalats and conciliation
3. Arbitration and tribunals
4. NALSA and legal aid
5. CAT and administrative justice
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) reduces pendency and ensures faster, cheaper, and more accessible justice in a democracy with a heavy litigation burden. 

2. Lok Adalats
- They settle civil, family, and compoundable criminal disputes quickly.
- They are especially useful for poor litigants who cannot afford prolonged litigation.
- Their informal approach encourages compromise and social harmony.
- They reduce the backlog of ordinary courts and create a pragmatic justice culture.

3. Arbitration and tribunals
- Arbitration is common in commercial, infrastructural, and contract disputes.
- The Arbitration and Conciliation Act reforms improved institutional efficiency and party confidence.
- Sector-specific tribunals handle administrative, tax, and service-related disputes efficiently.
- These institutions aim to create expertise and reduce pressure on regular courts.

4. NALSA and legal aid
- NALSA promotes legal aid and mediation for the poor and marginalised.
- It expands access to justice beyond formal litigation.
- It is especially important in rural and vulnerable communities.
- Legal aid strengthens equality before law and constitutional justice.

5. CAT and administrative justice
- The Central Administrative Tribunal handles service disputes and administrative grievances.
- It reduces burden on ordinary courts and offers specialised adjudication.
- It is especially relevant in public administration and governance disputes.
- Recent reforms have focused on digitalisation and efficiency.

6. Conclusion
ADR is an essential pillar of access to justice, reducing burden on courts while strengthening constitutional values of speed, fairness, and equality.
