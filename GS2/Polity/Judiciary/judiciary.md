### Judiciary
1. Institutional structure and constitutional role
2. Structure and independence
3. PIL and access to justice
4. Supreme Court powers and constraints
5. Diversity and judicial activism
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The Indian judiciary is the constitutional guardian of rights, balancing power between institutions and protecting democratic values under the rule of law. 

2. Structure and independence
- The judiciary is hierarchical, with the Supreme Court at the apex and High Courts below.
- Judicial independence is protected through fixed tenure, security of service, and constitutional safeguards.
- This independence is crucial for impartial adjudication and protection against executive pressure.
- Yet, judicial legitimacy also depends on transparency, accountability, and sensitivity to social realities.

3. PIL and access to justice
- Public Interest Litigation has widened access to courts for marginalised communities.
- It allows issues of environment, labour, governance, and human rights to reach the judiciary.
- The rise of PIL reflects judicial activism and public awareness.
- However, it also raises concerns about judicial overreach and burden on the courts.

4. Supreme Court’s unmatched powers
- Articles 32, 136, 142, and the power of judicial review give the Supreme Court extraordinary reach.
- It can enforce Fundamental Rights, hear appeals, and issue necessary directions for complete justice.
- This makes it a powerful constitutional institution.
- But such capacity requires restraint, accountability, and respect for the separation of powers.

5. Women representation and diversity
- Greater representation of women judges improves equity and public trust.
- It also helps address gender bias in legal interpretation and access to justice.
- Diversity strengthens institutional legitimacy and social responsiveness.
- India still needs more gender-inclusive judicial appointments and career pathways.

6. Judicial activism and democracy
- Judicial activism has expanded rights jurisprudence on privacy, environment, and dignity.
- It can fill gaps where the executive and legislature are slow or inadequate.
- But activism must not substitute legislative wisdom or exceed constitutional boundaries.
- The ideal is dialogue and restraint, not judicial dominance.

7. Conclusion
A strong, independent, and representative judiciary is essential for justice, democratic accountability, and constitutional morality in India.
