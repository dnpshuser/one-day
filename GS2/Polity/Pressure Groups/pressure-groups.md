### Pressure groups
1. Influence and pluralism in democracy
2. Structure and functioning
3. Policy influence and public advocacy
4. Formal vs informal groups
5. Environmental and farmers’ movements
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
Pressure groups are organised interest formations that seek to influence public policy without directly contesting elections, thereby shaping democratic governance from outside the party system. 

2. How do they influence policies ?
- Through lobbying, demonstrations, public campaigns, legal intervention, and media mobilisation.
- They play an important role in making policy responsive to social and economic interests.

3. Structure
- Formal organisations include trade unions, industry associations, and NGOs.
- Informal groups include caste associations, farmer unions, and social movements.

4. Functions
- Aggregate interests, create public awareness, and influence legislation and administration.
- They often represent sections outside party structures and thus deepen pluralism.

6. Role
- Pressure groups strengthen participative democracy by giving voice to civil society.
- They sometimes act as policy entrepreneurs and watchdogs.

7. Formal PG vs Informal PG
   1. effectiveness - eg
   - Formal groups often have institutional access; informal movements may have mass mobilisation but less administrative leverage.
   2. Khap Panchayat
   - Their influence highlights how informal social regulation can conflict with constitutional morality and state law.
      1. Actions taken by 
         1. Legislative
         - Legislation seeks to curb discriminatory customary practices.
         2. Executive
         - Police and administration act on unlawful coercion and caste-based violence.
         3. Judiciary
         - Courts have invalidated regressive custom when it violates constitutional rights.

8. Environmental PG
   1. Examples - achievements
   - NGOs and movements have influenced environmental clearance, public awareness, and climate policy.
   2. role
      1. awareness
      - Environmental groups shape public opinion and policy in sustainability issues.
      2. policy influence
      - They often push for stronger regulation and participatory governance.

9. Business association - influence - how ?
- Through formal representation, policy advocacy, and participation in consultation processes.

10. Farmers' organisation 
   1. methods used to influence policy
   - Protests, legal action, negotiations, and political mobilisation.
   2. effectiveness of these methods
   - They often shape agriculture policy, subsidy design, and rural welfare strategies.

11. Conclusion
Pressure groups enrich democratic pluralism, but they must operate within constitutional morality and public accountability to strengthen inclusive governance.
