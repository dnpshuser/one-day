### India vs UK
1. Intro
India and the UK both operate under parliamentary institutions, but their constitutional foundations differ sharply in rigidity, rights protection, and institutional design. 

2. Constitutional form
- The UK has an uncodified constitution based on statutes, conventions, and judicial decisions.
- India has a written, rigid Constitution with supreme law and clear amendment procedures.
- This makes the Indian system more explicit and rights-protective.
- The UK system is more flexible but can be less clear in constitutional disputes.

3. Parliamentary sovereignty
- In the UK, Parliament is the ultimate law-making authority.
- In India, the Constitution is supreme, and judicial review limits legislative excess.
- This gives India stronger rights enforcement and greater protection against majoritarianism.
- The trade-off is that India’s Constitution is more cumbersome to amend and interpret.

4. Judiciary and rule of law
- Britain relies more on common law and conventions, with a strong but less codified constitutional tradition.
- India’s judiciary is a powerful constitutional guardian with expanded powers under Articles 32 and 136.
- Judicial review in India allows constitutional correction where institutions fail.
- This reflects a stronger emphasis on rule-of-law enforcement.

5. Conclusion
India’s written constitutionalism gives it stronger judicial accountability and rights protection, while the UK model offers parliamentary flexibility and political efficiency.
