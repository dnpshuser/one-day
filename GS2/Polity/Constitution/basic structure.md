### Basic structure
1. Core constitutional identity
2. Essential features and doctrine
3. Judicial interpretation
4. Federalism, democracy and rights
5. Major cases and limits
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The Basic Structure doctrine protects the Constitution’s core identity from destructive amendment, preserving democracy, justice, and constitutional supremacy. 

2. Essential features
- Supremacy of the Constitution and rule of law.
- Judicial review and independent judiciary.
- Federalism, secularism, and democratic form of government.
- Parliamentary democracy and separation of powers.
- Equality, liberty, dignity, and judicially enforceable constitutional values.

3. Why it matters
- It prevents Parliament from altering the Constitution’s identity through simple majority amendments.
- It preserves constitutional continuity despite political majorities.
- It acts as a check against authoritarian impulses and majoritarian excesses.
- It maintains balance between stability and constitutional reform.

4. Case laws
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973): basic structure doctrine recognised.
- Golaknath (1967): Parliament cannot amend basic features affecting rights.
- Minerva Mills (1980): balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs is essential.
- I.R. Coelho (2007): judicial review, federalism, and other features are part of the basic structure.
- Source: Supreme Court judgments.

5. Conclusion
The Basic Structure doctrine ensures constitutional resilience, protects democratic values, and strengthens the rule of law and justice for future generations.
