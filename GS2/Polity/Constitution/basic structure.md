### Basic structure
1. Intro
2. Core constitutional identity
3. Essential features and doctrine
4. Judicial interpretation
5. Major cases and limits
6. Conclusion


---

1. Intro: The Basic Structure doctrine protects the Constitution’s core identity from destructive amendment, preserving democracy, justice, and constitutional supremacy. 

1. Essential features
   - Supremacy of the Constitution and rule of law.
   - Judicial review and independent judiciary. [declared in Minerva Mills case (1980)]
   - Federalism, secularism, and democratic form of government.
   - Parliamentary democracy and separation of powers.
   - Equality, liberty, dignity, and judicially enforceable constitutional values.

2. Why it matters
   - It prevents Parliament from altering the Constitution’s identity through simple majority amendments. [eg: 42nd CAA 1976]
   - preserves constitutional continuity despite political majorities.
   - check against authoritarian impulses and majoritarian excesses.
   - balance between stability and constitutional reform.

3. Case laws
   - Kesavananda Bharati (1973): basic structure doctrine recognised.
   - Golaknath (1967): Parliament cannot amend FR.
   - Minerva Mills (1980): balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs is essential.
   - I.R. Coelho (2007): judicial review, federalism, and other features are part of the basic structure.

4. Conclusion: The Basic Structure doctrine ensures constitutional resilience, protects democratic values, and strengthens the rule of law and justice for future generations.
