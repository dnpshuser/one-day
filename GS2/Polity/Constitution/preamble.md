### Preamble
1. Constitutional philosophy and values
2. Source of interpretation
3. Key words and ideals
4. Relation with rights and duties
5. Judicial role
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The Preamble is the constitutional soul, reflecting the political philosophy, moral commitments, and democratic aspirations of modern India. 

2. Keywords - def and importance
- Sovereign: India is politically independent and not subordinate to external authority.
- Socialist: India adopts a welfare orientation, ensuring social and economic justice, not a purely laissez-faire model.
- Secular: the State treats all religions equally and does not promote one faith over others.
- Democratic: governance derives authority from the people through elections and representation.
- Republic: the Head of State is elected, not hereditary.

3. Values and constitutional meaning
- Liberty, Equality, Justice, Fraternity are the moral foundations of the Constitution.
- The Preamble helps in interpreting vague constitutional language in light of constitutional values.
- It legitimises the Constitution as a social contract between the State and citizens.
- Supreme Court has treated it as a guiding principle, though not a source of enforceable rights in itself.
- Source: Constitution of India; Kesavananda Bharati (1973); S.R. Bommai (1994).

4. Defendable or not?
- It is not legally enforceable like Article 21, but it is persuasive in judicial reasoning.
- The Preamble can be amended, but its essential moral vision cannot be altered.
- It strengthens constitutional morality and public legitimacy in governance.
- It is especially useful when deciding between competing interpretations of rights and duties.

5. Conclusion
The Preamble remains the ethical compass of the Constitution, sustaining democratic accountability, justice, and inclusion in line with SDG 16 and India’s constitutional commitments.
