### Art-21:
1. Right to life and dignity
2. Meaning and expansion
3. Privacy, autonomy and procedural fairness
4. Environmental and health dimensions
5. Judicial interpretation
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
Article 21 is the most transformative right in the Constitution, extending the idea of life and liberty to dignity, fairness, and human flourishing. 

2. Evolution and expansion
- Originally interpreted narrowly, it now includes livelihood, education, privacy, shelter, and urban living conditions.
- The Supreme Court has expanded it to cover health, environment, and personal autonomy.
- This reflects a shift from procedural to substantive due process.
- Article 21 is central to the constitutional idea of dignity under the rule of law.

3. Privacy and personal autonomy
- K.S. Puttaswamy recognised privacy as part of the right to life and personal liberty.
- It protects bodily integrity, reproductive choice, and personal autonomy.
- This strengthens individual freedom against state overreach.
- However, privacy is not absolute and may be balanced against legitimate state interests.

4. Right to environment and public health
- The judiciary has linked Article 21 with the right to a clean environment.
- Cases on pollution, noise, and firecrackers reflect how environmental quality affects life and health.
- Clean air and safe living conditions are treated as constitutional obligations.
- This connects constitutional rights with SDG 3 and SDG 13.

5. Due process and fair procedure
- Article 21 requires fair procedure before deprivation of life or liberty.
- It ensures legal safeguards, natural justice, and reasoned decision-making.
- This is crucial in criminal law, detention, and administrative action.
- The principle strengthens democratic legitimacy and accountability.

6. Conclusion
Article 21 remains the constitutional foundation of dignity, liberty, and human development, making it the most powerful rights guarantee in Indian democracy.
