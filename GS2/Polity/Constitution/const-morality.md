1. Intro: Constitutional morality is the moral architecture of the Constitution, guiding governance beyond procedural legality to ethical constitutionalism. 

1. Meaning and importance
- It requires public institutions to act in conformity with constitutional values, not narrow political interests.
- It includes dignity, justice, liberty, equality, secularism, and inclusivity.
- It is central to preserving democracy in a plural society.
- This principle has become an interpretive tool in judicial reasoning.

1. Judicial independence and accountability
- Judges must be independent from executive pressure, but not beyond scrutiny for misconduct.
- Constitutional morality requires ethics, fairness, and transparency in judicial functioning.
- Independence is essential for rights protection; accountability must be institutional and legal, not political.
- This balance ensures judicial legitimacy.

1. Case law and practical application
- K.S. Puttaswamy recognised privacy as intrinsic to dignity and autonomy.
- Navtej Singh Johar upheld dignity over majoritarian morality.
- Sabrimala and triple talaq debates show how constitutional morality clashes with social conservatism.
- Courts often use the idea to strike a balance between rights and public order.

1. Constitutional anchors
- Articles 14, 19, 21, 25, and 32 embody constitutional morality in rights-based governance.
- It also informs legislative and executive action in social policy and public administration.

1. Conclusion
Constitutional morality reinforces justice, dignity, and accountability, making Indian democracy more resilient, inclusive, and rights-based.
