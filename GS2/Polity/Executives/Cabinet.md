### Cabinet
1. Executive coordination and accountability
2. Role and significance
3. Size and efficiency
4. Coalition politics and challenges
5. Collective responsibility
6. Conclusion

---

1. Intro
The Cabinet is the nerve centre of the Union government, translating parliamentary majority into policy and administrative action. 

2. Role and significance
- It is responsible for policy formulation, legislation, and execution of government programmes.
- It coordinates the work of various departments and ministries.
- It maintains collective responsibility to Parliament.
- It gives political direction to the civil service and administrative machinery.

3. Size of the Cabinet
- A larger Cabinet can enhance representation of regions, castes, and communities.
- But excess size raises coordination problems and weakens accountability.
- A too-large Cabinet may reduce efficiency and create overlapping portfolios.
- The ideal is a lean structure with effective governance, not cosmetic representation.

4. Challenges
- Coalition politics can increase demands for ministerial representation, creating complexity.
- Frequent portfolio changes can disrupt policy continuity and implementation.
- Ministers often face conflicts between political goals and administrative realities.
- The cabinet system requires strong coordination, discipline, and transparency.

5. Conclusion
The Cabinet should remain politically responsive yet administratively efficient, because governance quality depends on accountability, coherence, and public trust.
