2. Disruption
   1. What happened
      1. Monsoon session 2026(20th July-13th Aug) - extensive disruption
      2. LS - functioned 15% of the scheduled time
      3. RS - functioned 33% ---------------------
      4. QH - lasted only 9 min with only 2 oral answers
      5. Parliament should have Discussion, Dabate, Deliberation but not Disruption - Pranav Mukharjee
   2. Causes of Disruption
      1. Perceived partisanship of the Presiding officer
      2. Absense of effective deterance
      3. Parliamentary remedies are exhausted
      4. Issue of exceptional public importance
      5. Denial of debate or adjournment motion on urgent issues was accepted
         1. Winter session of 2024 - No adjournment motion or notice under Rule 267 accepted
      6. Government - opposition deadlock
      7. Inadequate space for opposition
      8. Executive: avoidance of accountability
      9. Social Media visibility and political strategy due to virality of the disruption
      10. Rigid whip system(10th schedule)
   3. Can't be justified when
      1. routine obstruction
      2. disruption on the very issue on which debate is demanded
      3. physical intimidation, tearing papers or damaging properties
   4. Responsibility
      1. Opposition
      2. Government
      3. Presiding officer
   5. Impacts/Concerns
      1. Question Hour
         1. 17th LS: 60% efficiency -> 1% in 2026 - deadly disruptions
      2. Scrutiny bypassed
         1. bills passed without discussion: 
            1. 9 out of 11 in Monsoon 2026 in LS.
            2. Tribunal Reform Bill passed in 12min without any MP participating
         2. Even budgets are voted without discussion
            1. 17th LS(2019-2023): 80% Union Budget passed without discussion
            2. Budget-2023: entire budget passed without discussion 
      3. Small parties/Individual MPs are silent without proper chance to speak. Gov vs Oppo
      4. Executive dominance: ordinance over deliberative legislation
   6. Reforms
      1. Guarentee a min annual calendar as recommended by NCRWC
         1. 120 days for LS
         2. 100 days for RS
      2. Create guarenteed opposition time: UK model Opposition days.
      3. Protect QH
      4. Require Prelegislative consultation Policy, 2014
      5. Adopt a graded code of conduct
      6. Strenghten Presiding officer neutrality
      7. Use a Parliament Productivity Index
3. Conclusion
   - India needs to move from a politics of mutual veto to a culture of structured disagreement. The majority must govern, the minority must question, and citizens expect both to perform within the constitutional forum.
