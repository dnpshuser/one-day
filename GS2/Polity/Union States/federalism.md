---

1. Intro
Federalism in India is a dynamic bargain between unity and diversity, balancing national coherence with regional autonomy and pluralism. 

2. meaning and concepts
- Federalism implies division of powers between the Union and states under a written Constitution.
- It ensures governance diversity while preserving national integration.

3. evolving pattern of Centre-State financial relations
- Finance Commission awards, GST, and centrally sponsored schemes have transformed fiscal federalism.
- States depend increasingly on central transfers, affecting autonomy and capacity.

4. recent reforms
   1. fiscal federalism
   - GST and devolution reforms aim to strengthen tax sharing and cooperative federalism.

5. measures to build trust 
- Better consultation, transparent fiscal transfer, and state participation in policy design.

6. Centralising tendency
   1. Acts
      - Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897; Disaster Management Act, 2005; and large-scale legislation have tilted governance toward central direction in emergencies.

7. cooperation, competition and confrontation - providing new shape - eg
- Cooperative federalism has grown through NITI Aayog and intergovernmental councils, but disputes remain over revenue, resource, and policy control.

9. ‘Principle of Federal Supremacy’ and ‘Harmonious Construction’
- Constitutional interpretation seeks to preserve both Union supremacy and state autonomy within a shared federal framework.

10. cooperative federalism
    1. current structure drawbacks
    - Delay in implementation, asymmetrical capacities, and political confrontation weaken trust.
    2. how solved through - cooperative federalism
    - Institutional coordination, fiscal devolution, and policy consultation can improve outcomes.

11. Strong Centre
   1. benefits
   - National coordination, crisis management, and uniform standards.
   2. Concerns 
   - Central dominance may reduce state autonomy and policy responsiveness.
   3. Articles and Case Laws
   - Articles 256, 263, and cases such as S.R. Bommai reflect the federal balance.

12. Inter-state water dispute resolution Failure 
    1. Reasons
       - Shared river disputes often reflect governance, political, and asymmetry issues.
       1. federal structure
       - States and the Union have overlapping interests and legal claims.
       2. process inadequacy  
       - Institutional mechanisms often lag behind hydrological and political realities.

13. Smaller state in effective governance
    1. +ve and -ve
    - Smaller states can improve administrative responsiveness, but may suffer from resource and capacity constraints.

14. Conclusion
A resilient federal system is essential for balancing national unity with regional diversity, promoting inclusive development and constitutional equity.
