#### CAG
1. Intro
The Comptroller and Auditor General is the constitutional guardian of public finance, ensuring that public expenditure is lawful, prudent, and accountable to the citizenry.

2. Importance
- CAG audits government expenditure, revenue, and public-sector undertakings to maintain financial accountability.
- It reinforces parliamentary control and transparency in the use of public money.
- As an independent constitutional authority, it strengthens rule of law and fiscal discipline.
- It supports democratic accountability by auditing efficiency and compliance in public programmes.
- The office is central to the constitutional principle that public resources are held in trust for the public.

3. Duty
   1. Legality of Expense
   - Checks whether expenditure conforms to constitutional powers, laws, and authorised budgets.
   - It scrutinises compliance with rules, procedures, and approvals in public spending.
   2. Propriety of Expense
   - Assesses whether spending is prudent, economical, and aligned with public interest.
   - Evaluates whether money is used efficiently and not wasted through procedural or policy failure.

4. Appointment
   1. Method
   - Appointed by the President under Article 148 of the Constitution.
   - The process is designed to insulate the office from executive influence.
   2. Term
   - Holds office for six years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.
   - Removal procedure mirrors that of a Supreme Court judge, ensuring independence.

5. Audit of government policy implementation and CAG jurisdiction
- CAG audits policy execution and financial compliance but does not determine policy wisdom.
- Critics argue that auditing implementation may cross into policy evaluation, while supporters defend it as accountability for public money.
- The debate reflects the need to preserve independence while avoiding overreach into governance choices.
- The office must balance financial scrutiny with constitutional restraint.

6. Conclusion
The CAG is indispensable for democratic accountability, preventing fiscal impropriety and strengthening constitutional governance under the principle of public trust.
