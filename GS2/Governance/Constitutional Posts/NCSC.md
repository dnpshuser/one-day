#### NCSC
1. Intro
The National Commission for Scheduled Castes is a constitutional body designed to safeguard the rights, dignity, and inclusion of Scheduled Castes in public life and governance.

2. Evolution
- It was originally a statutory body and later became a constitutional commission under Article 338.
- The commission was established to review implementation of safeguards for marginalised communities.
- It reflects the constitutional commitment to justice, equality, and social transformation.
- Its mandate has expanded to cover discrimination, exploitation, and deprivation patterns.

3. Role and duty
- Investigates matters relating to safeguards for Scheduled Castes and reports to the President.
- Reviews implementation of constitutional and legal provisions for SC welfare.
- Advises the government on policy reform and social inclusion programmes.
- Monitors discrimination in education, employment, and public access.
- Promotes affirmative action for historically disadvantaged communities under the constitutional scheme.

4. Can they enforce reservation in religious minority institutions?
- The commission’s remit does not extend to enforcing reservation in institutions run by religious minorities.
- Constitutional protections under Articles 29 and 30 give minority institutions special autonomy.
- However, the commission can monitor broader questions of discrimination and access in public systems.
- This distinction shows the balance between social justice and minority rights in constitutional jurisprudence.

5. Conclusion
The NCSC remains a vital instrument of constitutional justice, widening access, dignity, and equal opportunity forScheduled Castes while respecting the diversity of India’s plural social order.
