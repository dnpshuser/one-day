### Multi-Level Planning
1. Intro
Multi-level planning is a governance model that integrates planning across national, state, district, and local levels to better match policy with ground realities.

2. Significance and benefits
- Brings local knowledge into development planning and resource allocation.
- Improves policy relevance by matching interventions to regional needs and capacities.
- Encourages convergence of schemes and administrative coordination across institutions.
- Enhances accountability by linking planning outcomes to local governance institutions.
- Supports inclusive development through targeted interventions for vulnerable regions and communities.

3. Limitations
- Coordination failures across levels can create duplication and delay.
- Unequal capacities among states and districts may produce uneven implementation.
- Political competition and fiscal constraints sometimes distort planning priorities.
- Weak data systems may undermine evidence-based decision-making.

4. Committee reports
- Planning commissions and governance bodies have emphasised decentralised planning and local-level implementation.
- Recommendations focus on better integration, local capacity, and accountability in public administration.

5. Conclusion
Multi-level planning is essential for inclusive governance, balancing national goals with regional diversity and local development realities.
