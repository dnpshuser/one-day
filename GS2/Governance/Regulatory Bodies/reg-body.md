### Regulatory Bodies
1. Intro
Regulatory bodies are specialised institutions that supervise sectors such as telecom, finance, energy, and competition to ensure accountability, efficiency, and public interest.

2. Necessity for independence
- Sectoral regulation demands technical expertise and insulation from short-term political pressures.
- Independent regulators reduce rent-seeking and improve policy credibility.
- They protect consumer interests while balancing competition, investment, and innovation.
- Regulatory independence is essential for rule-based governance and market confidence.

3. Evolution
- Regulators emerged as a response to complex markets, growing state capacity, and the need for expert oversight.
- Sectors such as telecom, energy, insurance, and securities developed independent bodies to manage complex regulation.
- The trend reflects modern governance, where specialised knowledge is essential for public regulation.

4. Merger
   1. Justification
   - Merging regulators may reduce duplication, improve coordination, and create economies of scale.
   - Integrated oversight may support cross-sector consistency in digital and infrastructure markets.
   2. Challenges
   - Over-merger may dilute specialised expertise and reduce accountability.
   - Sector-specific risks, market dynamics, and consumer protection needs require domain-specific governance.

5. Committee reports and recommendations
- Governance and reform committees emphasise autonomy, transparency, and accountability of regulators.
- They recommend stronger selection processes, performance reviews, and public stakeholder consultation.
- Independent watchdogs and periodic audits improve legitimacy and reduce regulatory capture.

6. Conclusion
Regulatory bodies are crucial for modern governance, provided they remain autonomous, accountable, and aligned with public interest and constitutional norms.
