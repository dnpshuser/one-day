### SHG
1. Intro
Self-Help Groups are community-based collective institutions that enable women and low-income households to access finance, social support, and livelihood opportunities through collective action.

2. What is it?
- SHGs are small informal groups of people, usually women, who save together and provide mutual support.
- They often function as a grassroots mechanism for financial inclusion, social mobilisation, and empowerment.
- They link households with microfinance, institutions, and public welfare delivery.

3. How are they formed?
- Groups are usually formed through collective motivation, local institutions, and NGO or government facilitation.
- Members contribute regularly, maintain accounts, and access credit through banking linkage.
- Formation depends on trust, community leadership, and periodic capacity-building support.

4. Evolution
- SHGs emerged as a response to limited access to credit and livelihood support among poor women.
- The SHG-bank linkage model transformed them into a major instrument for rural financial inclusion.
- Government and NGO participation expanded the scope from savings to livelihoods, enterprise, and social mobilization.

5. Importance
   1. Potential to break
      - Gender inequality through income generation and socio-economic mobility.
      - Poverty by building savings and enterprise capability.
      - Malnutrition through better household resources and awareness.
   2. Role in developmental activities
   - Strengthen local entrepreneurship, collective bargaining, and access to welfare schemes.
   3. How bank linkage helped women empowerment
   - Financial inclusion enabled women to borrow, invest, and participate in economic decision-making.

6. Challenges
   1. Overall challenges
   - Weak book-keeping, inadequate market linkages, and limited managerial capacity hinder growth.
   2. Social hurdles
   - Caste, class, and patriarchy can restrict leadership and voice within groups.
   3. Cultural hurdles
   - Traditional norms may limit women’s mobility, decision-making, and financial autonomy.

7. Government initiatives
- NABARD, SHG-bank linkage programmes, and state livelihood missions have supported SHG expansion.
- Convergence with rural development, health, and women welfare schemes strengthens impact.

8. Women empowerment
- SHGs create economic independence, confidence, and collective agency for women in public and household spaces.

9. Need for scrutiny
- Regular monitoring is needed to prevent over-indebtedness, diversion of credit, and weak repayment discipline.

10. Conclusion
SHGs are a powerful model of development and women’s empowerment, provided they are supported by financial inclusion, governance, and accountability to ensure sustainable social transformation.
