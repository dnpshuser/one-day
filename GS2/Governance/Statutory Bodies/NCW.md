### National Commission for Women
1. Intro
The National Commission for Women is a statutory body that works toward gender justice, women’s rights, and the effective implementation of legal safeguards for women.

2. Would constitutionalization help to get greater gender justice?
- Constitutional status could strengthen legal authority, independence, and policy influence.
- It may improve visibility and decision-making power on women’s issues.
- However, effectiveness depends on resources, enforcement capacity, and political will.
- Gender justice requires more than formal constitutionalisation; it needs institutional action and social reform.

3. Working and effectiveness
- It investigates complaints, advises government, and promotes awareness about women’s rights.
- It has contributed to policy reforms in domestic violence, harassment, and women’s safety.
- Yet implementation gaps remain in access to justice, legal literacy, and systemic discrimination.
- The commission plays a useful role but cannot substitute for stronger courts, policing, and social policy.
- A rights-based approach is required to bridge gaps in education, health, safety, and representation.

4. Conclusion
Women’s empowerment and gender equality require both institutional support and structural reform, making the NCW a relevant but insufficient instrument without broader constitutional and social change.
