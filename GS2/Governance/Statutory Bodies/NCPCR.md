### National Commission for Protection of Child Rights
1. Intro
The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights is a statutory watchdog dedicated to ensuring children’s rights, protection, and participation in public policy.

2. Challenges faced by children in the digital era
- Online exploitation, cyberbullying, and data privacy risks expose children to new forms of harm.
- Digital divide and unequal access limit education and opportunity for marginalised children.
- Child labour, trafficking, and exploitation remain linked to socioeconomic vulnerability.
- Mental health concerns and screen dependency are increasing concerns in the digital age.

3. Existing policies
- Child protection laws, juvenile justice provisions, and welfare schemes support child rights.
- Education, health, nutrition, and child welfare programmes aim to improve access and inclusion.
- Policies increasingly recognise child protection as a governance and welfare priority.

4. Measures to tackle challenges
- Strengthen cyber safety, digital literacy, and child-sensitive online regulations.
- Improve school safety, child-friendly justice, and social welfare outreach.
- Expand community-based monitoring and social audits of child protection systems.
- Integrate child rights in health, education, and anti-trafficking policy frameworks.

5. Committee reports
- Reports on child rights and protection stress the need for stronger implementation, coordination, and institutional accountability.
- They underline the importance of balancing technology with safety and dignity.

6. Relevant articles and legal frameworks
- Articles 14, 15, 21A, and 39 of the Constitution anchor child dignity, education, and protection.
- The Juvenile Justice Act and related laws provide legal protection and institutional support.
- Source: Constitution of India; JJ Act; NCPCR reports.

7. Relevant case judgments
- Courts have highlighted the right to education, dignity, and protection from exploitation.
- Judicial decisions reinforce the state’s duty to secure children’s welfare and development.

8. Conclusion
Child rights governance must protect dignity, education, and safety in both physical and digital spaces, advancing constitutional justice and the SDG agenda for children.
